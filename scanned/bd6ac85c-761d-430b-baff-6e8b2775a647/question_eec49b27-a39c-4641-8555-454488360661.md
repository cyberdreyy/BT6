`tenure_activity` (schema 7) stores one row per `consensus_hash` with `last_activity_time` used presumably to gate tenure-extend/timeout decisions. Since `last_activity_time` is wall-clock (`get_epoch_time_secs`) captured before a crash, and a restart can take arbitrarily long, can a signer that restarts long after a crash read a stale `last_activity_time` for the still-current tenure and immediately conclude the tenure has been inactive far longer than the real gap (or, if the row is dropped/reset by a DB rebuild migration, immediately conclude no inactivity occurred), causing it to either wedge (refuse to accept a legitimate tenure-extend it should permit) or accept an extend proposal it should reject as arriving too early? Equality: the inactivity duration the signer computes after restart == the true wall-clock inactivity duration relative to the last genuine activity. Impact: High liveness wedge (refuses valid extend) or Critical if it wrongly approves an extend outside the timing window a miner should not get (CANONICITY/timing bypass). Proof idea: seed `tenure_activity` with an old `last_activity_time`, freeze `get_epoch_time_secs` at a much later value post-restart, and assert the tenure-extend decision path.
