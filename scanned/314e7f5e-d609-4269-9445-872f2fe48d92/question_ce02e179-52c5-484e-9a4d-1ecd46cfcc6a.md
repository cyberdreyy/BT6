In receipt_manager.rs, append_action_delete_account (639-648) lets a contract attach DeleteAccountAction with an arbitrary beneficiary_id to a receipt whose receiver_id it also controls. If the receiver_id is a THIRD-PARTY escrow contract account that never authorized self-deletion via THIS delegated promise (i.e., the receipt executes DeleteAccount as an action on receiver_id, not on the caller), does actions.rs's check_actor_permissions correctly bind beneficiary routing to the deleting account's own locked/liquid balance, or can an attacker force a victim contract's balance to flow to the attacker's beneficiary_id when the victim's contract logic itself invokes self-delete based on attacker-controlled function_call args passed through append_action_delete_account? Scoped: Stealing or loss of funds via a victim contract manipulated to name attacker as beneficiary through unsanitized args. Test: apply.rs test where victim contract wasm forwards caller-controlled beneficiary_id into an internal DeleteAccount promise, and assert that the beneficiary the runtime happens to settle is not attacker-controlled if the victim account owner never signed for the attacker's beneficiary_id.
