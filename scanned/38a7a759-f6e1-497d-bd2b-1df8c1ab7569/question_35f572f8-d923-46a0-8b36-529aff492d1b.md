`GlobalStateEvaluator::reached_agreement` (libsigner/src/v0/signer_state.rs) and `determine_global_state` compare accumulated weight against `self.total_weight`, itself the sum of a `HashMap<StacksAddress,u32>` supplied once at construction from `NakamotoSignerEntry.weight`. Since nothing re-validates that this weight table matches what `verify_signer_signatures`/`compute_voting_weight_threshold` will use on the node for the same cycle, can two `Signer` instances built from `SignerEntries::parse` calls that raced a `.signers` contract update (one before, one after a `set-signers` write in the same prepare phase) reach `determine_global_state` agreement on two different `SignerStateMachine.current_miner` values, each locally satisfying its own (stale vs fresh) `total_weight` threshold? Equality: `total_weight`/`address_weights` used for a cycle's agreement threshold must equal the one the node's `RewardSet.total_signing_weight()` uses for that same cycle. Impact: Critical — two disjoint subsets of signers, each internally 'thresholded', sign for two different tenures at the same height. Proof idea: instantiate two `GlobalStateEvaluator`s with slightly different `address_weights` (simulating pre/post-update races) fed the same set of `StateMachineUpdate`s and show they disagree on `determine_global_state()`.
