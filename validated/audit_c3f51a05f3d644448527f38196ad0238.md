### Title
Blacklisted user permanently unable to claim withdraw funds — no alternative recipient — (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
All withdraw-claim paths in `IdleCreditVault` transfer underlying tokens (e.g., USDC) directly to the requester `_user` with no way to specify an alternative recipient. Because withdraw receipts are minted as non-transferable, address-bound strategy tokens, a KYC-passing lender who gets blacklisted by the underlying token contract (e.g., Circle's USDC blacklist) after requesting a withdrawal can never claim their funds — every claim reverts on the `safeTransfer`.

### Finding Description
`claimWithdrawRequest` and `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` are callable only by the IdleCDO and always pay out to `_user`:

- `_claimFundedWithdrawRequest` → `_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount)` → `underlyingToken.safeTransfer(_user, _amount)` (lines 319–350, 897–907)
- `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` → `_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount)` (lines 380–393)
- Default/recovery paths → `_transferDefaultRecovery(_user, amount)` → `safeTransfer(_user, _amount)` (lines 760–784, 842–856, 912–917)

Two design properties make this unrecoverable:

1. Receipt tokens are address-bound. `requestWithdraw` mints strategy-token receipts to `_user` (line 275), and `_transfer` reverts unless `msg.sender == idleCDO` (lines 939–942), with `setCanTransfer` permanently disabled (lines 948–951). A blacklisted user cannot transfer the receipt to a clean address to claim on their behalf.
2. There is no recipient parameter anywhere in the claim chain — the CDO passes the original requester and the strategy forces payout to that same address.

Notably, `DefaultDistributor.claim(address _to)` (DefaultDistributor.sol:35–41) already follows the recommended pattern of letting the claimer choose a recipient, but the credit-vault claim flow does not.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent (or indefinite) freezing of user funds. If the underlying is a centrally-blocklistable stablecoin (USDC, USDT — the typical Clearpool/Pareto pool currency), a lender blacklisted after depositing loses access to principal and accrued interest held in funded withdraw receipts and default-recovery reserves. Unlike a simple DoS, the funds are provably custodied in the vault for the user yet unreachable by them, and the non-transferable receipt removes the usual workaround of moving the claim to a clean wallet. Loss = the user's full claimable balance while blacklisted.

### Likelihood Explanation
Low-to-medium. It requires the external token issuer to blacklist the user, which is outside the protocol's control — but it is a realistic scenario for sanctioned/suspect addresses, and the vault's KYC (`isWalletAllowed`) does not prevent post-deposit blacklisting since claims only check the requester's recorded balance, not current allowlist status. There is no attacker profit; the impact is a freeze, matching the source report's Medium severity.

### Recommendation
Allow a recipient override in the claim path: e.g., add a `_receiver` parameter to `claimWithdrawRequest`/`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` (or a strategy-level `claimTo(_receiver)` callable by the CDO on the user's authenticated request), mirroring `DefaultDistributor.claim(address _to)`. Alternatively, make withdraw receipts transferable so a blacklisted user can move the claim to a clean address — though recipient-parameterization is simpler and consistent with the existing claim design.
