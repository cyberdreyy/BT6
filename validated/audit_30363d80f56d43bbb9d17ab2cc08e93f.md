### Title
`create()` lets any caller override the staking-pool whitelist used by a vesting lockup, enabling early release of locked/unvested NEAR - ([File: lockup-factory/src/lib.rs])

### Summary
`LockupFactory::create` accepts a caller-supplied `whitelist_account_id` that is written unchecked into the deployed lockup's immutable `staking_pool_whitelist_account_id` field, even when the same call also sets a `vesting_schedule` with the real `foundation_account_id`. This lets the lockup owner deploy a lockup that is vested-and-locked according to the foundation's schedule, but whose "trusted" staking pool whitelist is an attacker-controlled contract of their own, breaking the custody binding that only a foundation-approved staking pool may ever receive delegated/locked NEAR from the lockup.

### Finding Description
`create()` is `#[payable]` and callable by anyone; it takes `whitelist_account_id: Option<ValidAccountId>` and, if supplied, uses it verbatim as `staking_pool_whitelist_account_id` for the new lockup instead of the factory's own vetted `self.whitelist_account_id`: [1](#0-0) 

There is no check that the custom whitelist is the foundation's whitelist, nor any restriction tying the ability to supply a custom whitelist to the predecessor being the foundation - even when `vesting_schedule.is_some()` and the real `foundation_account_id` is embedded as `foundation_account`: [2](#0-1) 

`LockupArgs.staking_pool_whitelist_account_id` is passed straight into the deployed lockup contract's `new()` and stored as-is; the lockup contract itself has no way to detect or reject a bogus whitelist address at initialization time.

Inside the lockup, the only gate before delegating (locked) NEAR to a staking pool is that the pool is "whitelisted" per `staking_pool_whitelist_account_id`: [3](#0-2) 

Once `on_whitelist_is_whitelisted` receives `true` from the (attacker-controlled) whitelist contract, the staking pool is accepted without any further validation: [4](#0-3) 

The owner can then transfer locked NEAR out of the lockup account to this attacker-controlled "staking pool" via `deposit_to_staking_pool` / `deposit_and_stake`, which perform no vesting/lock checks beyond `assert_no_termination()`: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Because the "staking pool" is a contract the owner deployed themselves (paired with a self-deployed fake whitelist contract that unconditionally returns `is_whitelisted = true`), the owner fully controls it and can immediately return the NEAR to themselves outside the lockup's vesting/termination logic - i.e., NEAR that should be locked/unvested per the foundation's vesting schedule is released early.

This breaks the intended custody equality that the whitelist system is designed to enforce: **staking pools trusted by a lockup == staking pools approved by NEAR Foundation** (enforced only through the single canonical whitelist contract, as documented in `whitelist/README.md`): [7](#0-6) 

`create()` silently allows this equality to be broken for any lockup, including vested ones, by letting the deployer swap in their own whitelist.

### Impact Explanation
This matches the Critical impact category "locked or unvested tokens released early" / "a wrongly whitelisted or wrongly parameterised deployment": a lockup owner with a vesting schedule (whose tokens are supposed to be locked/unvested and only delegatable to foundation-approved staking pools) can bypass that protection entirely by supplying a self-controlled `whitelist_account_id` at `create()` time, then routing locked NEAR through a fake "staking pool" they control to extract it before the vesting/lock schedule permits withdrawal.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high for any unprivileged caller: `create()` has no owner/foundation-only restriction on the `whitelist_account_id` argument, it only requires the standard minimum attached deposit; deploying a fake whitelist contract and a fake staking pool contract are both permissionless NEAR account/contract deployments requiring no special access.

### Recommendation
In `LockupFactory::create`, when `vesting_schedule.is_some()` (i.e., the lockup enforces a foundation-managed lock/vesting schedule), ignore or reject any caller-supplied `whitelist_account_id` and always use the factory's canonical `self.whitelist_account_id`. If custom whitelists must be supported for non-vested lockups, gate the override behind a check that the predecessor is the foundation account, or drop the parameter entirely and always bind to the audited foundation whitelist.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `lockup_factory.create(owner_account_id=attacker, vesting_schedule=Some(schedule_with_real_foundation), whitelist_account_id=Some(attacker_fake_whitelist))`, attaching the minimum deposit - the foundation funds the vesting schedule as usual (out of scope for the attacker, but this is the normal deployment flow the foundation relies on).
2. Attacker deploys `attacker_fake_whitelist` contract exposing `is_whitelisted(..) -> true` for any account, and `attacker_fake_pool` contract implementing `deposit`/`deposit_and_stake`/`withdraw` that simply returns funds to the attacker on demand.
3. Attacker calls `lockup.select_staking_pool(attacker_fake_pool)` on their lockup; this queries `attacker_fake_whitelist` (per step 1) which returns `true`, so `on_whitelist_is_whitelisted` accepts the pool: [4](#0-3) 
4. Attacker calls `lockup.deposit_and_stake(locked_amount)`, transferring locked/unvested NEAR to `attacker_fake_pool`: [6](#0-5) 
5. Attacker calls `withdraw`/equivalent on `attacker_fake_pool` (a contract they fully control) to move the NEAR to any account of their choosing, days/months/years before the real vesting cliff/lock would have permitted a withdrawal from the lockup contract - locked/unvested tokens are released early.

    #[payable]
    pub fn create(
        &mut self,
        owner_account_id: ValidAccountId,
        lockup_duration: WrappedDuration,
        lockup_timestamp: Option<WrappedTimestamp>,
        vesting_schedule: Option<VestingScheduleOrHash>,
        release_duration: Option<WrappedDuration>,
        whitelist_account_id: Option<ValidAccountId>,
    ) -> Promise {
        assert!(env::attached_deposit() >= MIN_ATTACHED_BALANCE, "Not enough attached deposit");

        let byte_slice = env::sha256(owner_account_id.as_ref().as_bytes());
        let lockup_account_id =
            format!("{}.{}", hex::encode(&byte_slice[..20]), env::current_account_id());

        let mut foundation_account: Option<AccountId> = None;
        if vesting_schedule.is_some() {
            foundation_account = Some(self.foundation_account_id.clone());
        };

        // Defaults to the whitelist account ID given on init call.
        let staking_pool_whitelist_account_id = if let Some(account_id) = whitelist_account_id {
            account_id.into()
        } else {
            self.whitelist_account_id.clone()
        };

        let transfers_enabled: WrappedTimestamp = TRANSFERS_STARTED.into();
        Promise::new(lockup_account_id.clone())
            .create_account()
            .deploy_contract(CODE.to_vec())
            .transfer(env::attached_deposit())
            .function_call(
                b"new".to_vec(),
                near_sdk::serde_json::to_vec(&LockupArgs {
                    owner_account_id,
                    lockup_duration,
                    lockup_timestamp,
                    transfers_information: TransfersInformation::TransfersEnabled {
                        transfers_timestamp: transfers_enabled,
                    },
                    vesting_schedule,
                    release_duration,
                    staking_pool_whitelist_account_id,
                    foundation_account_id: foundation_account,
                })

    /// Called after a given `staking_pool_account_id` was checked in the whitelist.
    pub fn on_whitelist_is_whitelisted(
        &mut self,
        #[callback] is_whitelisted: bool,
        staking_pool_account_id: AccountId,
    ) -> bool {
        assert_self();
        assert!(
            is_whitelisted,
            "The given staking pool account ID is not whitelisted"
        );
        self.assert_staking_pool_is_not_selected();
        self.assert_no_termination();
        self.staking_information = Some(StakingInformation {
            staking_pool_account_id,
            status: TransactionStatus::Idle,
            deposit_amount: 0.into(),
        });
        true
    }

# Whitelist contract for staking pools

The purpose of this contract is to maintain the whitelist of the staking pool contracts account IDs that are approved
by NEAR Foundation.

In order for the lockup contracts to be able delegate to a staking pool, the staking pool should faithfully implement the spec.
The staking pool should guarantee that the delegated tokens can not be lost or locked, such as the lockup contract should be
able to recover delegated tokens back to the lockup from a staking pool. In order to enforce this, only approved (whitelisted)
accounts of staking pool contracts can receive delegated tokens from lockup contracts.

If NEAR Foundation has to approve every single staking pool account it might lead to a bottleneck and centralization
To address this NEAR Foundation can whitelist the account IDs of staking pool factory contracts.

The whitelisted staking pool factory contract will be able to whitelist accounts of staking pool contracts.
A factory contract creates and initializes a staking pool contract in a secure and permissionless way.
This allows anyone on the network to be able to create a staking pool contract for themselves without needing approval from the NEAR
Foundation. This is important to maintain the decentralization of the decision making and network governance.
