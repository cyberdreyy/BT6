**Analysis:**

The mechanism described is the documented, intended behavior rather than a bug. `distribution_pool` in `StakingContract` tracks shares keyed by the shareholder address (staker or `operator`), not by beneficiary address [1](#0-0) . The beneficiary redirect only happens at redemption time inside `distribute_internal`, where it calls `beneficiary_for_operator(operator)` freshly to determine the payout recipient for the operator's share, rather than caching a beneficiary address when commission accrues: [2](#0-1) .

This means calling `distribute` right after `set_beneficiary_for_operator` does not "corrupt routing" — it correctly pays out to whichever beneficiary is currently configured at the moment of distribution, which is by design the *new* beneficiary. This exact sequencing consideration is explicitly called out in the doc comment above `set_beneficiary_for_operator`: "Any existing unpaid commission rewards will be paid to the new beneficiary. To ensure payment to the current beneficiary, one should first call `distribute` before switching the beneficiary." [3](#0-2) .

`distribute` is also intentionally unauthenticated/permissionless — the doc comment states "Allow anyone to distribute already unlocked funds. This does not affect reward compounding and therefore does not need to be restricted to just the staker or operator" [4](#0-3) . Since funds are only released to whoever currently holds a shareholder role (staker or operator, with operator dynamically remapped to its current beneficiary), an unprivileged caller triggering `distribute` cannot redirect funds to an unintended address — it merely accelerates payout to the legitimately current beneficiary/staker, which is exactly the behavior the module documents and expects.
