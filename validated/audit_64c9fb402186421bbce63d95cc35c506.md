### Title
Mid-epoch depositor receives a full-epoch interest share on `requestWithdraw`, stealing interest from long-term LPs - (File: contracts/IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol)

### Summary
`depositDuringEpoch` correctly mints tranche shares so a mid-epoch depositor only earns interest prorated to the time their liquidity is active (`remaining + buffer` over `epochDuration + buffer`). However, `requestWithdraw` -> `_calcInterestWithdrawRequest` computes the withdrawable interest as the tranche-NAV-proportional share of the *entire* epoch interest, with no regard for when the underlying was deposited. A KYC-passed lender can deposit near the end of an epoch, then immediately request withdrawal once the epoch stops, and receive a full-epoch interest share, directly diluting the interest owed to LPs whose capital was deployed the whole epoch — the exact analog of the reported "deposit and remove liquidity in one shot to steal accrued LP rewards" bug class.

### Finding Description
In `depositDuringEpoch`, the minted amount embeds only the depositor's fair time-prorated claim:

```solidity
uint256 interest = _calcInterest(_amount) *
  (remaining + buffer) /
  (epochDuration + buffer);
...
_minted = (_amount + trancheInterest) * _trancheTotSupply / expectedFinal;
``` [1](#0-0) 

But when the same shares are later surrendered via `requestWithdraw`, the interest component is computed purely pro-rata against tranche NAV for the *full* epoch:

```solidity
uint256 totTrancheInterest = _calcTrancheInterestShare(totInterest, _tranche);
uint256 _trancheBal = _lastSavedNAV(_tranche);
_interest = _trancheBal == 0 ? 0 : _amount * totTrancheInterest / _trancheBal;
``` [2](#0-1) 

`_amount * totTrancheInterest / _trancheBal` is the interest a unit of NAV earns over a *whole* epoch. Nothing in `requestWithdraw` or `_calcInterestWithdrawRequest` records or consults the deposit timestamp / entry epoch. The receipt is then fixed at `principal + interest - totalFees` and paid from the borrower-funded `expectedEpochInterest` pool, while `interestForOverUnderPerformance`/`expectedEpochInterest` bookkeeping does not claw back the over-credited share from the attacker — it is socialized across the remaining holders' interest.

Contrast with the defense already present for deposits (`depositDuringEpoch` proration) and the comment acknowledging the problem class ("programmable borrowers use APR=0 so mid-epoch deposits would dilute existing depositors") — the same dilution exists on the withdraw side for fixed-APR vaults.

### Impact Explanation
Direct theft of lender yield. An attacker who deposits `_amount` shortly before `epochEndDate` captures `(_amount / trancheNAV) * totTrancheInterest`, i.e., nearly a full epoch of interest on capital deployed for a fraction of the epoch. That interest is paid out of the fixed pool the borrower repays at `stopEpoch`, so every honest LP's realized yield is reduced proportionally. The attacker needs no flash loan: they simply hold tranche tokens between `depositDuringEpoch` and the post-stop buffer when withdrawal requests are enabled, or can sandwich around the epoch boundary if requests are allowed while running. Loss scales with attacker deposit size and epoch APR — a sufficiently large deposit can appropriate the majority of an epoch's interest.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only: an epoch vault in fixed-APR mode (not programmable, not AYS, `isDepositDuringEpochDisabled == false`, `isBBDepositEnabled` for BB), a KYC-passed wallet (`isWalletAllowed`), and a nonzero epoch interest. All are ordinary operating conditions. No privileged role, default, or oracle manipulation is needed.

### Recommendation
Track interest entitlement consistently between entry and exit. Either (a) record the depositor's entry time / prorated interest claim per tranche position (e.g., store `trancheInterest` credited at `depositDuringEpoch` and cap `_interest` in `_calcInterestWithdrawRequest` to the accrued-to-date portion of that claim), or (b) scale `_interest` in `_calcInterestWithdrawRequest` by the fraction of `epochDuration + bufferPeriod` the position was actually active. Alternatively, disallow `depositDuringEpoch` shares from requesting withdrawal until they have completed a full epoch.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry fork scenario on `test/foundry/IdleCreditVault.t.sol` harness:

1. Setup: `cdoEpoch` fixed-APR vault (non-programmable, non-AYS), `epochDuration` e.g. 30 days, `bufferPeriod` 5 days, APR `initialProvidedApr`. Honest LP (deployer) deposits `depositAA(10000e18)` and `depositBB(10000e18)`.
2. Manager calls `startEpoch` — epoch running.
3. `vm.warp(epochEndDate - 1 days)`. Attacker (`alice`, KYC-whitelisted) calls `cdoEpoch.depositDuringEpoch(LARGE, AATranche)`. She is minted shares priced for ~1 day + buffer of interest only — fair entry.
4. `vm.warp(epochEndDate + 1)`. Manager calls `stopEpoch` fully funding `_expectedFundsEndEpoch()` (borrower repays principal + `expectedEpochInterest`).
5. During buffer, attacker calls `cdoEpoch.requestWithdraw(0, AATranche)`. `_calcInterestWithdrawRequest` credits her `attackerPrincipal * totTrancheInterest / lastNAVAA` — a *full-epoch* AA interest share, not the ~1/30 prorated share her deposit earned.
6. After the next epoch settles, `claimWithdrawRequest()` pays the receipt.
7. Assert: `attacker.receiptInterest > _calcInterest(attackerAmount) * (1 day + buffer) / (epochDuration + buffer)` by ~30x, and deployer's `maxWithdrawable`/claim proceeds drop by the stolen amount versus a run where the attacker never deposits.

Expected loss quantization: attacker captures `(attackerAmount / totalAANAV) * AAepochInterest`; with attacker deposit sized at ~50% of tranche NAV entered on the last epoch day, she extracts ~15x her fair share of the epoch's AA interest, taken from honest AAs.

Unverified caveat: whether `allowAAWithdrawRequest`/`allowBBWithdrawRequest` are true during a running epoch could not be fully confirmed from the indexed snippets (restoreOperations enables them only when `!isEpochRunning`). If requests are disabled while running, the attack still works by requesting during the post-stop buffer — no timing tightening is required since the over-crediting formula is identical in both phases.
