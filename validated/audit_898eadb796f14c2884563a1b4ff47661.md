### Title
Zero-balance subsidized 1-yoctoNEAR promise calls to nonexistent receivers mint duplicated balance via double-counted `subsidized_amount` and unconditional deposit refund - (File: `runtime/near-vm-runner/src/logic/logic.rs`, `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
`VMLogic::promise_batch_action_function_call_weight`'s `skip_deduct` branch lets a zero-balance account attach 1 yoctoNEAR to an outgoing `FunctionCall` without debiting its own balance, instead recording the amount in `result_state.subsidized_amount`, which is later merged into the *sending* receipt's `ActionReceiptResult` and committed permanently into `stats.balance.subsidized_amount`. When the resulting outgoing receipt targets a nonexistent account, `check_account_existence` rejects the `FunctionCall` action, the receipt fails and rolls back, and `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` unconditionally computes `deposit_refund = total_deposit` for the failed receipt and returns it to the predecessor — crediting the attacker's zero-balance account with 1 yoctoNEAR it never actually held.

### Finding Description
`promise_batch_action_function_call_weight` sets `skip_deduct` true when `amount == 1 yoctoNEAR`, `one_yocto_on_promise` is enabled, and the caller's balance is zero, and in that case skips `deduct_balance` entirely, only bumping `result_state.subsidized_amount` [1](#0-0) . This `subsidized_amount` is merged into the receipt's `ActionResult` only when execution succeeded [2](#0-1) , and then folded permanently into the chunk-level `stats.balance.subsidized_amount` for the *sending* receipt as soon as that receipt (the one that scheduled the promise) finishes [3](#0-2) . This commit happens independently of, and prior to, whatever later happens to the newly created outgoing receipt.

`ActionReceiptResult::set_error` only clears `tokens_burnt`/`subsidized_amount` when the *same* receipt currently being merged fails [4](#0-3)  — it has no effect on `stats.balance.subsidized_amount` figures already committed from a different (earlier, already-successful) receipt. So subsidizing the deposit for the sending call is never rolled back when the *downstream* receipt to the fake receiver later fails.

When that downstream `FunctionCall` receipt reaches the nonexistent receiver, `check_account_existence` rejects it with `AccountDoesNotExist` because `FunctionCall` is not implicit-account-creation eligible [5](#0-4) . The receipt fails and its state changes roll back, but `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` computes `deposit_refund = total_deposit` whenever `result.result.is_err()`, with no distinction for whether the deposit was ever actually debited from the sender [6](#0-5) . This refund is routed back to the predecessor (the original zero-balance attacker account) as a normal balance credit.

Net effect per failed subsidized call to a nonexistent account:
1. `subsidized_amount` is permanently recorded and folded into the "minted" side of chunk accounting for the sending receipt (`chain/chain/src/runtime/mod.rs` subtracts `subsidized_amount` from burnt totals, i.e., treats it as intentionally minted supply) [7](#0-6) .
2. The attacker's account, whose balance was never actually reduced (`skip_deduct`), is *also* credited 1 yoctoNEAR back via the deposit refund of the failed downstream receipt.

Because step 1 already accounts for "minting" the 1 yoctoNEAR that was supposed to go to the receiver, and the receiver never received it (the receipt failed before crediting), step 2 constitutes a second, unaccounted mint of the same yoctoNEAR back to the sender. Repeating this against N distinct nonexistent receiver account IDs lets the attacker accumulate N yoctoNEAR of real balance from nothing, and the `stats.balance.subsidized_amount` figure drifts upward without ever being reconciled against the fact that the corresponding deposits never reached any receiver.

### Impact Explanation
This is a token-inflation / value-conservation violation: an attacker with a permanently zero-balance account can mint small amounts of real NEAR balance for free by repeatedly issuing `promise_batch_action_function_call_weight` (or `promise_yield_create_with_id`, which has the identical `skip_deduct` logic [8](#0-7) ) with `amount=1` targeting nonexistent receiver accounts. Each failed call both records `subsidized_amount` (intended one-time mint to cover the receiver's credit) and separately refunds the same 1 yoctoNEAR to the sender, who never lost it — a duplicate mint. Matches the "token inflation" bounty category, even though the per-call magnitude (1 yoctoNEAR) is economically negligible relative to attached gas costs.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions are trivial for an unprivileged attacker: create/own a zero-balance account (e.g., after draining it), deploy or reuse a contract able to call `promise_batch_action_function_call_weight` with `amount=1` and a fabricated nonexistent `receiver_id`, then submit the transaction to any RPC endpoint. No special permissions or validator access are required, and the exploit is trivially repeatable across many distinct fake receiver IDs. The only real cost is gas, which for probing purposes vastly exceeds the yoctoNEAR gained, making it commercially uninteresting as a wealth-generation attack but still a genuine, deterministically reproducible protocol invariant violation and free way to probe account existence at effectively zero net token cost per probe (ignoring gas), since the sender balance is never actually reduced.

### Recommendation
Track whether a receipt's deposits were subsidized (`skip_deduct`) at the action level, and when computing `deposit_refund` in `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits`, exclude/zero out the portion of `total_deposit` that corresponds to subsidized (non-debited) amounts so a failed receipt cannot refund money that was never actually taken from the sender. Alternatively, only emit a refund receipt for deposits that were actually deducted via `deduct_balance`, and ensure `stats.balance.subsidized_amount` is reconciled/reversed when the receipt whose deposit it subsidized ultimately fails to reach a valid receiver.

### Proof of Concept
Integration test plan (extending the existing `runtime/runtime/src/tests/apply.rs` subsidy tests):
1. Deploy a contract to a test account, zero its balance (as in `test_one_yocto_subsidy_tracked_in_stats`).
2. From that zero-balance account, call a method that invokes `promise_batch_action_function_call_weight` with `amount=1` targeting a nonexistent `receiver_id` (e.g. `"doesnotexist.test"`).
3. Apply the chunk; assert:
   - The outgoing receipt to the nonexistent account fails with `AccountDoesNotExist`.
   - `apply_result.stats.balance.subsidized_amount == 1 yoctoNEAR` (unreduced despite downstream failure).
   - The attacker account's balance after applying the resulting refund receipt increases by 1 yoctoNEAR relative to its pre-call balance (0), even though `deduct_balance` was never called.
4. Repeat N times with N distinct fake receivers in the same or successive chunks; assert the attacker's account balance grows by N yoctoNEAR while `stats.balance.subsidized_amount` accumulates N yoctoNEAR, demonstrating that the two are not merely equal but represent two independent, unreconciled mints of the same value.

        // when the contract has zero balance. This lets deterministic accounts
        // call functions like ft_transfer_call that require an attached deposit

            && self.config.one_yocto_on_promise
