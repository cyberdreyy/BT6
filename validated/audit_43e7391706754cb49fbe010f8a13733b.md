### Title
Fire-and-forget `transfer` in `internal_withdraw` permanently debits balance with no rollback on failure - ([File: staking-pool/src/internal.rs])

### Summary
`StakingContract::internal_withdraw` decrements `account.unstaked` and `self.last_total_balance` *before* the `Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount)` is guaranteed to succeed, and schedules no callback to verify the promise result or restore state on failure. If the transfer fails (e.g., the receiving account is deleted between the `withdraw` call and the async execution of the transfer receipt), the withdrawn NEAR is refunded to the contract itself while the internal accounting has already recorded it as gone.

### Finding Description
The broken binding: `amount transferred out via Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount)` should equal `amount debited from account.unstaked and self.last_total_balance`. In `internal_withdraw`: [1](#0-0) 

the sequence is: assert balance/epoch checks, subtract `amount` from `account.unstaked`, save the account, then fire `Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount)` with **no `.then()` callback**, and finally decrement `self.last_total_balance`. There is no `assert_self()`-guarded callback anywhere in the withdraw path (unlike `internal_restake`, which does chain `on_stake_action` for the stake action), so nothing in the contract ever inspects the outcome of this transfer.

On NEAR, a native `Transfer` action fails at receipt-application time if the receiver account no longer exists, and the attached balance is refunded to the *predecessor* of that receipt — which is the staking-pool contract itself, not the withdrawing delegator. Since `account_id == env::predecessor_account_id()` (the caller's own account) and the caller holds a full-access key to their own account, an attacker can:
1. Call `withdraw(amount)` (or `withdraw_all()`), which synchronously debits `account.unstaked` and `self.last_total_balance` and schedules the transfer receipt.
2. Before that transfer receipt is processed (a separate, later receipt), submit a second transaction from the same account executing `delete_account`.
3. When the transfer receipt executes, the receiver no longer exists, the transfer fails, and the `amount` NEAR is refunded back into the staking-pool contract's actual balance — while `self.last_total_balance` and `account.unstaked` were already decremented as if the funds left.

At this point the contract's real balance is `amount` higher than `self.last_total_balance` believes. On the next call that triggers `internal_ping`, this discrepancy is read as `total_reward` and distributed to `total_stake_shares` holders (and to the owner via `reward_fee_fraction`) as if it were genuine staking reward: [2](#0-1) 

With `reward_fee_fraction` numerator zero, none of this misattributed amount is skimmed by the owner — it flows entirely into `total_staked_balance` and inflates the "stake" share price for whoever currently holds `stake_shares`, i.e., other delegators (or the attacker's own other accounts/shares) reap value that rightfully belonged to the withdrawing account's `unstaked` balance. None of the existing guards prevent this: `internal_ping`'s only assertion is `total_balance >= self.last_total_balance` (which still holds, since the refunded amount only makes actual balance *higher*), and there is no `is_promise_success()`/`assert_self()` callback on the withdrawal path at all.

### Impact Explanation
NEAR that was debited from a delegator's `unstaked` balance and from `self.last_total_balance` never reaches that delegator (their account is deleted and the refund lands in the contract's own balance), yet the contract's global accounting mis-treats the refunded amount as a new "reward" to be redistributed pro-rata to current `stake_shares` holders. This is a real, silent divergence between recorded (`last_total_balance`) and actual on-chain balance that "another party settles on" — remaining/other stakers (and the owner if `reward_fee_fraction` were nonzero) receive value that was never earned as a reward, at the direct expense of the withdrawing account, matching a High-severity accounting-divergence impact. It requires the attacker to sacrifice their own account (delete it) to produce the divergence, so the attacker does not directly profit unless they also hold `stake_shares` in the same pool to capture a slice of the inflated share price — making it more a mechanism for value transfer between the deleting account and remaining shareholders than straightforward attacker-controlled theft.

### Likelihood Explanation
The precondition (deleting one's own account between initiating `withdraw` and the transfer receipt's execution) is technically feasible on NEAR since receipts are processed asynchronously across blocks/shards, and the account owner holds a full-access key to schedule `delete_account` independently. However, this destroys the withdrawing account itself, so exploitation only yields net attacker gain if the attacker also owns other `stake_shares` in the same pool to receive a share of the misattributed reward, and the amount recovered is diluted across all shareholders rather than captured entirely by the attacker. This lowers the practical incentive compared to a direct, repeatable theft.

### Recommendation
Chain a `.then()` callback (guarded with `assert_self()`) on the withdrawal transfer, and only finalize/keep the debit if `is_promise_success()` is true; otherwise restore `account.unstaked` and `self.last_total_balance` in the callback, mirroring the pattern already used for `on_stake_action`.

### Proof of Concept
Using `near-sdk-sim` / `near-workspaces`:
1. Deploy the staking-pool contract with `reward_fee_fraction = {0, 1}`.
2. Delegator account `A` deposits and stakes, then unstakes to get `unstaked` balance, advances epochs past `unstaked_available_epoch_height`.
3. `A` calls `withdraw(amount)`, capturing the transfer receipt before it's applied.
4. Before that receipt executes, `A` submits `delete_account` on itself so the receiving account no longer exists when the transfer receipt runs.
5. Assert: the transfer fails and the refunded `amount` lands back in the staking-pool contract's real balance, while `self.last_total_balance` (queryable via `get_account_total_balance`/contract state) was already reduced by `amount` — i.e. `actual_contract_balance != last_total_balance + expected_outstanding`.
6. Trigger `internal_ping` (via any subsequent call) in a new epoch and assert the discrepancy is distributed as `total_reward`, inflating `total_staked_balance`/share price for other `stake_shares` holders, proving the withdrawn amount was never delivered to `A` yet is redistributed to other parties.

    pub(crate) fn internal_withdraw(&mut self, amount: Balance) {
        assert!(amount > 0, "Withdrawal amount should be positive");

        let account_id = env::predecessor_account_id();
        let mut account = self.internal_get_account(&account_id);
        assert!(
            account.unstaked >= amount,
            "Not enough unstaked balance to withdraw"
        );
        assert!(
            account.unstaked_available_epoch_height <= env::epoch_height(),
            "The unstaked balance is not yet available due to unstaking delay"
        );
        account.unstaked -= amount;
        self.internal_save_account(&account_id, &account);

        env::log(
            format!(
                "@{} withdrawing {}. New unstaked balance is {}",
                account_id, amount, account.unstaked
            )
            .as_bytes(),
        );

        Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount);
        self.last_total_balance -= amount;
    }
