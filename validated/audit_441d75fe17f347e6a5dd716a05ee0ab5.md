### Title
Proposer self-slashing can erase an already-earned builder payment before quorum is reached - ([File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md])

### Summary
In the Gloas (ePBS) fork, `process_proposer_slashing` deletes the `BuilderPendingPayment` entry associated with the slashed proposer's own proposal if the slashing lands within a 2-epoch window. A proposer who has already used a builder's payload (and whose block has already begun accumulating `payment.weight` toward the payout quorum via same-slot attestations) can deliberately equivocate on that same slot to get themselves slashed and thereby erase the pending payment before `process_builder_pending_payments` would otherwise pay the builder. This mirrors the TRST-M-1 pattern: a state that accumulates a reward/benefit over time (`timeDiscountMultiplierE18` / `payment.weight`) can be unilaterally reset by the very party whose action created the obligation, denying payment to an honest counterparty (the liquidator / the builder).

### Finding Description
When a builder's bid is accepted and the payload later becomes available (not settled immediately as a "full parent" via `apply_parent_execution_payload` → `settle_builder_payment`), the corresponding entry in `state.builder_pending_payments` accrues `weight` from same-slot attestations in `process_attestation`: [1](#0-0) 

The entry is only paid out (moved to `builder_pending_withdrawals`) once its `weight` reaches the quorum threshold, which can take up to roughly two epochs: [2](#0-1) 

`process_proposer_slashing` was modified specifically to clear this pending payment when the slashed validator is the proposer of that slot's proposal and the slot falls within a 2-epoch window: [3](#0-2) 

The inline comment shows the team already reasoned about one griefing vector ("an unrelated same-slot equivocation could grief an honest proposer's payment") and restricted the clear to only the slashed proposer's own payment. However, this leaves open the mirror case: the *same* proposer who accepted the builder's bid and whose block was included, attested to, and whose builder-supplied payload may already be economically consumed by the chain, can *self*-equivocate (sign a second conflicting header for the same slot) purely to get slashed and clear their own pending builder payment before it reaches quorum and is settled. Unlike the honest-proposer griefing case the authors defended against, this is not "unrelated" — it is the exact proposal whose payment is erased, and it is entirely under the attacker's control since equivocation requires no cooperation from anyone else.

This is directly analogous to the TRST-M-1 pattern: `pos.startLiqTimestamp`/`timeDiscountMultiplierE18` is a value that grows over time to eventually pay a reward, and the party whose position is being tracked can unilaterally reset it to zero to deny that reward. Here, `payment.weight` similarly grows over time toward a quorum-based payout, and the party who benefited from the builder's payload (the proposer) can unilaterally erase it via a self-inflicted, entirely-in-their-control slashing event.

### Impact Explanation
If exploited, this results in a builder payment being destroyed/withheld (Gwei destroyed / a builder payment escaped) even though the builder already delivered the payload that was economically used by the chain — this maps to the "High" impact category: "a builder payment or withdrawal misdirected, doubled, or escaped." If the value of the withheld builder payment `V` exceeds the proposer's slashing penalty (`MIN_SLASHING_PENALTY_QUOTIENT`-based penalty on effective balance) plus lost future rewards from being ejected, this is a profitable strategy for a malicious proposer, meaning the loss is not merely the attacker's own stake — it is a third party's (the builder's) rightfully earned payment.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only a single, fully unprivileged actor: the proposer of the slot in question, acting alone, with no need for a malicious peer, coalition, or client bug — they simply sign two conflicting headers for a slot they already proposed. The attack window is bounded (payment must not yet have reached quorum and been settled, and the slashing must occur within the 2-epoch clearing window), but this window is a normal part of the protocol's design (the whole point of accumulating `weight` over up to 2 epochs), not an edge case. The proposer controls the timing of their own equivocation, so they can choose to act any time before settlement, making the attack straightforwardly reproducible whenever the payload isn't immediately settled as a "full parent."

### Recommendation
Do not tie payment cancellation solely to "is this the same proposer being slashed" — that criterion can be satisfied by the proposer's own voluntary self-equivocation. Consider either: (a) never clearing a pending builder payment based on proposer slashing once it has already accrued attestation weight (i.e., once the builder has begun receiving confirmations of payload delivery), or (b) only clearing pending payments for slashings of blocks that were never included/attested to (i.e., before the payload was actually consumed by the chain), so that a builder who already delivered value cannot have their earned payment retroactively destroyed by the beneficiary's own after-the-fact equivocation.

### Proof of Concept
1. Builder `B` submits a bid worth `V` for slot `N`. Proposer `P` includes it in the canonical block for slot `N`; the parent payload is not immediately settled as "full" (payload availability confirmed asynchronously via PTC/attestations), so `state.builder_pending_payments[payment_index]` is created with `weight = 0` and `proposer_index = P`.
2. Attestations for slot `N` in the following slot(s) increase `payment.weight` per [4](#0-3) , moving it toward, but not yet reaching, the quorum needed for `process_builder_pending_payments` to pay it out at the epoch boundary ( [5](#0-4) ).
3. Before the quorum is reached (still within the 2-epoch window), `P` signs a second, conflicting `BeaconBlockHeader` for slot `N` (different `parent_root`) and includes/broadcasts a `ProposerSlashing` against themself (or lets a whistleblower submit it — either way `P` chooses when to create the equivocation).
4. `process_proposer_slashing` matches `payment.proposer_index == header_1.proposer_index == P` and clears `state.builder_pending_payments[payment_index]` per [6](#0-5) , before it ever reaches quorum and is moved to `builder_pending_withdrawals`.
5. Builder `B` never receives the payment `V` for the payload that was already used by the chain, while `P` only pays the (potentially much smaller) slashing penalty.

def process_builder_pending_payments(state: BeaconState) -> None:
    """
    Processes the builder pending payments from the previous epoch.
    """
    quorum = get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold(state)
    for payment in state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH]:
        if payment.weight >= quorum:
            state.builder_pending_withdrawals.append(payment.withdrawal)

    old_payments = state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:]
    state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH] = old_payments
    new_payments = [BuilderPendingPayment.empty() for _ in range(SLOTS_PER_EPOCH)]
    state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:] = new_payments
