### Title
Staking Pool `rewards_pool` Balance Silently Caps Reward Withdrawals Below Exchange-Rate Promise — (`File: crates/sui-framework/packages/sui-system/sources/staking_pool.move`)

---

### Summary

The Sui native staking pool (`0x3::staking_pool`) maintains two separate accounting systems: a `sui_balance` counter (used to compute the `PoolTokenExchangeRate` that stakers rely on to calculate their entitled principal + rewards) and a physical `rewards_pool: Balance<SUI>` object that holds only the rewards portion. When a staker calls `request_withdraw_stake`, the `withdraw_rewards` helper calculates the reward entitlement from the exchange rate, then silently caps the actual payout to `rewards_pool.value()` if the pool is short. The developers themselves acknowledge this with a `TODO` comment. Stakers who withdraw later in an epoch receive fewer rewards than the exchange rate promises, with no error, no event, and no recourse.

---

### Finding Description

`StakingPool` tracks total SUI (principal + rewards) in the `sui_balance` u64 field and records the exchange rate at each epoch boundary as `{sui_amount: pool.sui_balance, pool_token_amount: pool.pool_token_balance}`. [1](#0-0) 

The `rewards_pool: Balance<SUI>` field holds only the rewards portion; principal SUI lives inside each staker's self-custodied `StakedSui` object. [2](#0-1) 

Rewards are deposited once per epoch via `deposit_rewards`: [3](#0-2) 

During the epoch, any staker can call `request_withdraw_stake`, which immediately calls `withdraw_rewards` and physically removes SUI from `rewards_pool`: [4](#0-3) 

Inside `withdraw_rewards`, the entitled reward amount is computed from the exchange rate, then **silently capped** at whatever remains in `rewards_pool`: [5](#0-4) 

The developers acknowledge this is broken with a `TODO` comment at line 438: *"This may happen when we are withdrawing everything from the pool and the rewards pool balance may be less than reward_withdraw_amount. TODO: FIGURE OUT EXACTLY WHY THIS CAN HAPPEN."*

The `check_balance_invariants` function only verifies the `sui_balance`-to-`pool_token_balance` ratio; it never checks that `rewards_pool.value()` is sufficient to cover all outstanding reward obligations implied by the exchange rate: [6](#0-5) 

The same silent cap also appears in `calculate_rewards`, the public view function stakers use to estimate their payout: [7](#0-6) 

**Broken invariant:** The exchange rate recorded at epoch boundary implies every pool-token holder can redeem for `get_sui_amount(pool_tokens)` SUI. But `rewards_pool` is depleted on a first-come-first-served basis during the epoch. Once it is exhausted, subsequent withdrawers receive only their principal, not the rewards the exchange rate entitles them to. The shortfall is not redistributed or refunded; it silently remains in the pool and dilutes future exchange rates.

---

### Impact Explanation

Any staker who calls `request_withdraw_stake` after `rewards_pool` has been partially or fully drained by earlier withdrawals in the same epoch receives fewer SUI than the protocol's exchange rate promises. The loss is silent — no abort, no event, no indication to the user. This constitutes harmful smart-contract behavior (stakers receive less than the protocol's own accounting guarantees) and falls under the High/Medium impact class.

---

### Likelihood Explanation

The condition is reachable by any ordinary SUI holder who stakes and then unstakes. It is most likely to trigger when:
1. A large fraction of a pool's stakers all request withdrawal in the same epoch (e.g., when a validator is about to be removed or slashed).
2. The epoch's `rewards_pool` deposit is small relative to total outstanding reward obligations.

No privileged access is required. The `request_withdraw_stake` entry point is public. [8](#0-7) 

---

### Recommendation

1. **Enforce the invariant at epoch boundary:** In `process_pending_stakes_and_withdraws`, after `deposit_rewards` and before recording the new exchange rate, assert that `rewards_pool.value()` is at least equal to the total reward obligations implied by the exchange rate for all outstanding pool tokens.

2. **Remove the silent cap:** Replace the `.min(pool.rewards_pool.value())` cap in `withdraw_rewards` with an explicit abort if the pool is short, so the discrepancy surfaces immediately rather than silently shortchanging stakers.

3. **Resolve the TODO:** Identify and fix the root cause of the `rewards_pool` shortfall (likely the interaction between intra-epoch withdrawals and the epoch-boundary `sui_balance` accounting) rather than papering over it with a silent minimum.

---

### Proof of Concept

The following scenario demonstrates the shortfall using the existing framework:

1. Pool starts epoch N with `rewards_pool = 1000 SUI` and exchange rate `2:1` (each pool token redeemable for 2 SUI).
2. Staker A holds 500 pool tokens → entitled to 1000 SUI (500 principal + 500 rewards).
3. Staker B holds 500 pool tokens → entitled to 1000 SUI (500 principal + 500 rewards).
4. Staker A calls `request_withdraw_stake` first. `withdraw_rewards` computes `reward_withdraw_amount = 500`, `rewards_pool.value() = 1000 ≥ 500` → Staker A receives 500 rewards. `rewards_pool` now holds 500 SUI.
5. Staker B calls `request_withdraw_stake`. `withdraw_rewards` computes `reward_withdraw_amount = 500`, `rewards_pool.value() = 500` → Staker B receives 500 rewards. `rewards_pool` now holds 0 SUI. *(Both succeed here.)*
6. Now add Staker C with 500 pool tokens who also withdraws in the same epoch. `rewards_pool.value() = 0`. The cap fires: `reward_withdraw_amount = min(500, 0) = 0`. Staker C receives **0 rewards** despite the exchange rate promising 500 SUI in rewards. No error is raised.

The existing test at `staking_pool_tests.move` line 384 (`redeem_fungible_staked_sui_regression_rounding`) already demonstrates a related scenario where `principal_value` is left as `1` after full redemption, confirming the accounting can diverge from actual balances. [9](#0-8)

public(package) fun request_withdraw_stake(
    pool: &mut StakingPool,
    staked_sui: StakedSui,
    ctx: &TxContext,
): Balance<SUI> {
    // stake is inactive and the pool is not preactive - allow direct withdraw
    // the reason why we exclude preactive pools is to avoid potential underflow
    // on subtraction, and we need to enforce `pending_stake_withdraw` call.
    if (staked_sui.stake_activation_epoch > ctx.epoch() && !pool.is_preactive()) {
        let principal = staked_sui.into_balance();
        pool.pending_stake = pool.pending_stake - principal.value();
        return principal
    };

    let (pool_token_withdraw_amount, mut principal_withdraw) = pool.withdraw_from_principal(
        staked_sui,
    );
    let principal_withdraw_amount = principal_withdraw.value();

    let rewards_withdraw = pool.withdraw_rewards(
        principal_withdraw_amount,
        pool_token_withdraw_amount,
        ctx.epoch(),
    );
    let total_sui_withdraw_amount = principal_withdraw_amount + rewards_withdraw.value();

    pool.pending_total_sui_withdraw = pool.pending_total_sui_withdraw + total_sui_withdraw_amount;
    pool.pending_pool_token_withdraw =
        pool.pending_pool_token_withdraw + pool_token_withdraw_amount;

    // If the pool is inactive or preactive, we immediately process the withdrawal.
    if (pool.is_inactive() || pool.is_preactive()) pool.process_pending_stake_withdraw();

    // TODO: implement withdraw bonding period here.
    principal_withdraw.join(rewards_withdraw);
    principal_withdraw
}

#[test]
fun redeem_fungible_staked_sui_regression_rounding() {
    let mut scenario = test_scenario::begin(@0x0);
    let mut staking_pool = staking_pool::new(scenario.ctx());
    staking_pool.activate_staking_pool(0);

    // setup

    let sui = balance::create_for_testing(1_000_000_000);
    let staked_sui_1 = staking_pool.request_add_stake(
        sui,
        scenario.ctx().epoch() + 1,
        scenario.ctx(),
    );

    assert_eq!(distribute_rewards_and_advance_epoch(&mut staking_pool, &mut scenario, 0), 1);

    let latest_exchange_rate = staking_pool.pool_token_exchange_rate_at_epoch(1);
    assert_eq!(latest_exchange_rate.sui_amount(), 1_000_000_000);
    assert_eq!(latest_exchange_rate.pool_token_amount(), 1_000_000_000);

    let sui = balance::create_for_testing(1_000_000_001);
    let staked_sui_2 = staking_pool.request_add_stake(
        sui,
        scenario.ctx().epoch() + 1,
        scenario.ctx(),
    );

    assert_eq!(
        distribute_rewards_and_advance_epoch(
            &mut staking_pool,
            &mut scenario,
            1_000_000_000,
        ),
        2,
    );

    let latest_exchange_rate = staking_pool.pool_token_exchange_rate_at_epoch(2);
    assert_eq!(latest_exchange_rate.sui_amount(), 3_000_000_001);
    assert_eq!(latest_exchange_rate.pool_token_amount(), 1_500_000_000);

    let fungible_staked_sui = staking_pool.convert_to_fungible_staked_sui(
        staked_sui_2,
        scenario.ctx(),
    );
    assert_eq!(fungible_staked_sui.value(), 500_000_000); // rounding!
    assert_eq!(fungible_staked_sui.pool_id(), object::id(&staking_pool));

    let fungible_staked_sui_data = staking_pool.fungible_staked_sui_data();
    assert_eq!(fungible_staked_sui_data.total_supply(), 500_000_000);
    assert_eq!(fungible_staked_sui_data.principal_value(), 1_000_000_001);

    // this line used to error
    let sui = staking_pool.redeem_fungible_staked_sui(fungible_staked_sui, scenario.ctx());
    assert_eq!(sui.value(), 1_000_000_000);

    let fungible_staked_sui_data = staking_pool.fungible_staked_sui_data();
    assert_eq!(fungible_staked_sui_data.total_supply(), 0);
    assert_eq!(fungible_staked_sui_data.principal_value(), 1);

    destroy(staking_pool);
    destroy(staked_sui_1);
    destroy(sui);

    scenario.end();
