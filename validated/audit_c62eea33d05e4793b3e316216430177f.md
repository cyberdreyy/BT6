Confirmed: `on_get_account_total_balance` in `lockup/src/owner_callbacks.rs` blindly trusts whatever `total_balance` value the selected staking pool contract returns and writes it straight into `staking_information.deposit_amount`, which is exactly what `get_known_deposited_balance`/`get_owners_balance`/`get_liquid_owners_balance` use to compute how much the owner may `transfer` out. Combined with `lockup-factory`'s `create()` accepting an attacker-supplied `whitelist_account_id` with no validation that it is the real, foundation-controlled whitelist contract, an unprivileged lockup owner can self-whitelist a contract they fully control as a "staking pool" and then arbitrarily inflate their own liquid/locked balance to withdraw funds that should still be locked/unvested.

### Title
Lockup factory's caller-supplied `whitelist_account_id` lets an owner self-whitelist a malicious staking pool to fake balances and release locked/unvested NEAR early - (File: `lockup-factory/src/lib.rs`, `lockup/src/owner.rs`, `lockup/src/owner_callbacks.rs`)

### Summary
`LockupFactory::create` lets the caller (the future lockup owner) optionally override the trusted `staking_pool_whitelist_account_id` with an arbitrary account of their choosing instead of forcing use of the foundation-controlled whitelist. [1](#0-0)  That value is passed straight into the deployed lockup's `new` args and stored as `self.staking_pool_whitelist_account_id`, which is later queried by `select_staking_pool` to decide whether a candidate "staking pool" account can be trusted. [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
The custody binding that should hold is: *"account trusted as a staking pool" == "the real, foundation-vetted staking-pool contract that the whitelist system was designed to attest to."* Because `create()` accepts a `whitelist_account_id: Option<ValidAccountId>` supplied by the very same actor who will become the lockup's owner, that binding is never enforced — the owner can deploy their own trivial contract (`is_whitelisted` always returns `true`) at account `X`, pass `X` as `whitelist_account_id`, and thereby get `select_staking_pool` to accept a second attacker-controlled contract as the "selected staking pool" for their own lockup. [3](#0-2) 

Once selected, the lockup owner deposits/stakes funds into this attacker-controlled "pool" via `deposit_and_stake`/`stake` (owner-only methods, but the owner here is the attacker) and then calls `refresh_staking_pool_balance`, which asks the "pool" for `get_account_total_balance` and blindly overwrites `staking_information.deposit_amount` with whatever value is returned, with no bound checking the value against what was actually deposited: [4](#0-3) 

Since `get_known_deposited_balance`, `get_owners_balance`, and `get_liquid_owners_balance` derive directly from `deposit_amount`, and `transfer()` allows the owner to withdraw up to the liquid balance, the attacker can report an arbitrarily large `total_balance` (far exceeding real staked funds and even exceeding the amount that should remain locked/unvested) and then `transfer` that inflated liquid balance out of the lockup contract — releasing funds that the vesting/lockup schedule, and ultimately the foundation's termination rights over unvested tokens, are supposed to protect. [5](#0-4) 

This exactly mirrors the reported bug class ("an account trusted as a pool ... versus the code and arguments that trust was granted for") but reached without requiring any privileged DAO/foundation/multisig action — the unprivileged lockup owner supplies the malicious trust anchor themselves at deployment time via the factory's optional parameter.

### Impact Explanation
This is Critical impact: locked or unvested NEAR is released early / claims (the recorded `deposit_amount`) diverge from and exceed NEAR actually held by any real pool, and — where a vesting schedule with foundation termination rights is configured — the foundation's entitlement to unvested tokens can be defeated because the funds appear to have already been legitimately withdrawn by the owner via the fabricated staking-pool balance.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high for any lockup created through `lockup-factory` where the creator chooses to supply a custom `whitelist_account_id` (a first-class, documented parameter of `create`, exercised in `test_create_lockup_with_custom_whitelist_success`). [6](#0-5)  No special privileges, keys, or races are required — only deploying two small helper contracts and calling standard owner methods (`select_staking_pool`, `deposit_and_stake`, `refresh_staking_pool_balance`, `transfer`) in sequence.

### Recommendation
- Remove the ability to override `staking_pool_whitelist_account_id` in `lockup-factory::create`, or restrict it to a foundation-only, separately-privileged migration path; the deployed lockup should always be pinned to the canonical, factory-configured whitelist account.
- In `on_get_account_total_balance`, sanity-bound the reported `total_balance` (e.g., disallow decreases beyond expected slashing, or require it be consistent with cumulative deposits plus a reasonable reward growth rate) rather than trusting an arbitrary value from an external call.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `lockup-factory::create(owner_account_id = attacker, ..., whitelist_account_id = Some(fake_whitelist))`, where `fake_whitelist` is a tiny contract the attacker deployed beforehand whose `is_whitelisted` always returns `true`. [7](#0-6) 
2. The new lockup contract is created with `staking_pool_whitelist_account_id = fake_whitelist`.
3. Attacker (now the lockup owner) calls `select_staking_pool(fake_pool)` where `fake_pool` is another attacker-controlled contract; the whitelist check against `fake_whitelist` returns `true`, so it's accepted. [2](#0-1) 
4. Attacker deposits a small real amount via `deposit_and_stake` to `fake_pool`.
5. Attacker calls `refresh_staking_pool_balance`; `fake_pool`'s `get_account_total_balance` returns an inflated number (e.g., far larger than the lockup's actual locked amount); the lockup stores this as `deposit_amount`. [4](#0-3) 
6. `get_owners_balance`/`get_liquid_owners_balance` now report an inflated liquid balance; attacker calls `transfer()` to move NEAR out of the lockup contract that should have remained locked/unvested.
