### Title
Instant-withdraw claims pay from the whole strategy balance, draining funds reserved for funded pending withdraw receipts - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
The FLIC bug is a heap overflow where an attacker-supplied "write count" writes past the end of an allocated buffer. The vault analog is a claim path whose payout "count" is taken from a per-user receipt total rather than from the funds actually reserved for that claim class: `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pays the full `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` from the strategy's aggregate underlying balance, while `_transferFundedClaim` only fences off `defaultRecoveryReserve`, leaving underlying that `collectWithdrawFunds` pulled in to back pending/loss-adjusted normal receipts unprotected. An unprivileged lender can therefore have an instant receipt "write" paid out of another claim class's buffer, leaving normal withdraw requesters insolvent.

### Finding Description
`collectWithdrawFunds` pulls underlying into the strategy to back `pendingWithdraws` (fully, or at a `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` haircut) at `IdleCreditVault.sol:411-430`. Those funds sit in the strategy until users call `claimWithdrawRequest`. Separately, `requestInstantWithdraw` mints a 1:1 receipt and increments `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` and `pendingInstantWithdraws` (`IdleCreditVault.sol:356-375`), and `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns the receipt and calls `_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount)` with `amount = instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` (`IdleCreditVault.sol:380-393`). `_transferFundedClaim` only checks `balance - defaultRecoveryReserve >= _amount` (`IdleCreditVault.sol:897-907`) — there is no accounting linking the payout to `pendingInstantWithdraws` or to how much underlying `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` actually transferred in for that user.

The receipt token minted to the user (`_mint(_user, _amount)` at line 363) is an unrestricted ERC20, so an attacker can also route receipts across accounts to enlarge the claim basis.

Attack sequence:
1. Honest lenders queue normal withdrawals; `stopEpoch`/`collectWithdrawFunds` funds `pendingWithdraws` into the strategy (possibly already haircut via `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch`).
2. Attacker (any KYC'd lender holding tranche tokens) calls `requestInstantWithdraw` through the CDO; a receipt is minted and `instantWithdrawsRequests[attacker]` increases.
3. Once `instantWithdrawDelay` passes, attacker calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`. `amount` is read from the receipt counter, not from funded instant liquidity, and `_transferFundedClaim` transfers underlying out of the shared strategy balance — consuming cash that was collected to back the pending normal receipts.
4. When honest users call `claimWithdrawRequest`, `_claimFundedWithdrawRequest`/`_claimLossAdjustedWithdrawRequest` transfer reverts on insufficient balance: their funded receipts are frozen/undercollateralized.

### Impact Explanation
Direct theft and insolvency: the attacker receives underlying beyond the instant-withdraw bucket actually funded, paid from reserves earmarked for pending/loss-adjusted withdraw claimants. Honest receipt holders' claims then revert or are permanently short by the drained amount (up to the full funded `pendingWithdraws` balance). This mirrors the CVE exactly: a user-controlled claim size writes/transfers past the boundary of the buffer reserved for it.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an epoch where normal withdraws were funded into the strategy while an attacker holds (or newly creates, subject to `instantWithdrawDelay` and the CDO's instant-liquidity sourcing) an instant receipt. These are normal operating conditions for any KYC-passing lender; no privileged cooperation is needed. The missing guard is structural — the only balance fence covers `defaultRecoveryReserve`, which is zero outside default.

### Recommendation
Track funded instant liquidity separately (e.g., an `instantWithdrawReserve` incremented by `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` and decremented per claim), and extend `_transferFundedClaim` to check that the post-claim balance still covers all unfunded claim buckets (`pendingWithdraws`-backed cash plus the instant reserve), not just `defaultRecoveryReserve`. Alternatively, escrow per-claim funding at `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` time so a claim can only spend its own reserved amount.

Uncertainty I could not fully resolve within the available iterations: whether `IdleCDOEpochVariant` gates `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` on the CDO having already collected exactly the attacker's instant amount via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` (I confirmed the strategy-side counter `pendingInstantWithdraws` is decremented there, but did not finish tracing the CDO-side per-user gating). If the CDO enforces that the instant amount was funded before allowing the claim, the residual issue narrows to instant claims still drawing from the shared strategy balance when instant funding and pending-withdraw funding coexist, which is the same missing-segregation bug class.
