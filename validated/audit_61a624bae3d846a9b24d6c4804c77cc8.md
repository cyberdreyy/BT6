Based on my review of `ProvisionerV2.sol` and related contracts, I could not identify a genuine analog to this CVE. The CVE describes a **missing bounds check on attacker-crafted binary data** (a video file) leading to heap corruption in FFmpeg's decoder.

The requested Aera surface (`ProvisionerV2` deposit/mint/request/solve/cancel/refund/redeem paths) is explicitly guarded at every arithmetic and array-access step:
- Array iteration in `_solveRequestsVault` and `solveRequestsDirect` uses `requests.length` directly from calldata with no unsafe indexing. [1](#0-0) 
- Amount/tip/cap checks are performed via explicit guard functions (`_guardAmountBound`, `_guardInsufficientTokensForTip`, `_guardDepositCapExceeded`, `_guardInvalidRequestHash`) before any unchecked arithmetic or fund movement. [2](#0-1) 
- `cancelRequest`/`refundRequest` validate the hash of the caller-supplied `RequestV2` struct against `asyncRequestHashes` before any transfer, and unchecked subtraction is only reached after an explicit `require(cancellationFee <= refundAmount, ...)`.
