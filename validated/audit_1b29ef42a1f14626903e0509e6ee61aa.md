### Title
`StakingContract::internal_withdraw` debits the delegator's accounted balance and `last_total_balance` without verifying that the outgoing NEAR transfer succeeds - ([File: staking-pool/src/internal.rs])

### Summary
`staking-pool/src/internal.rs::internal_withdraw`, invoked from `withdraw`/`withdraw_all` in `staking-pool/src/lib.rs`, decrements `account.unstaked` and `self.last_total_balance` and only *then* fires a bare `Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount)` with no `.then()` callback and no `is_promise_success()` check. [1](#0-0) 
This is the same bug class as the reported `GovPool::delegateTreasury` issue: the contract debits its internal accounting *before* confirming that value was actually delivered to the recipient, trusting the transfer to succeed unconditionally.

### Finding Description
Every other outbound value-moving flow in this codebase that can fail (staking-pool restake, lockup owner/foundation withdrawals) is guarded by a `.then()` callback that calls `is_promise_success()` and reverts/adjusts local state on failure, e.g.:
- `staking-pool/src/internal.rs` `internal_restake` chains `.then(ext_self::on_stake_action(...))`. [2](#0-1) 
- `lockup/src/owner_callbacks.rs` `on_staking_pool_withdraw`/`on_staking_pool_stake` gate the accounting update on `is_promise_success()`. [3](#0-2) 
- `lockup/src/foundation_callbacks.rs::on_withdraw_unvested_amount` explicitly rolls back the termination status if `is_promise_success()` is false. [4](#0-3) 

In contrast, `internal_withdraw` performs the accounting update unconditionally before the transfer promise is even created, and never checks the outcome:
```
account.unstaked -= amount;
self.internal_save_account(&account_id, &account);
...
Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount.0? /* no callback */);
self.last_total_balance -= amount;
``` [5](#0-4) 
`withdraw` and `withdraw_all` simply call `internal_withdraw` synchronously with no follow-up verification. [6](#0-5) 

The invariant that should hold is:
`last_total_balance == actual contract NEAR balance (minus current epoch's unattributed gas rebates)`

If the outgoing `Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount)` receipt fails for any reason (e.g., the receiving account no longer exists by the time the receipt is processed), the debit to `account.unstaked` and `self.last_total_balance` already happened and is never reverted, while the actual NEAR balance held by the contract does not decrease by `amount` as expected. This breaks the accounting-vs-reality equality exactly like the `GovPool::delegateTreasury` case breaks the token/NFT accounting-vs-custody equality.

### Impact Explanation
`internal_ping` (reward distribution logic, described in `staking-pool/README.md`) computes rewards as `current_account_balance - last_total_balance`. [7](#0-6) 
Because `last_total_balance` was decremented by `amount` while the actual contract balance was not (transfer failed), the next `ping()`/`internal_ping()` call will see a phantom surplus equal to the failed withdrawal amount and misclassify it as staking reward, distributing it to the pool owner (`reward_fee_fraction`) and to all current "stake" share holders. [8](#0-7) 
The withdrawing delegator's claim (`account.unstaked`) has already been reduced and is never restored, so they permanently lose the amount that failed to transfer, while it is mis-attributed as reward to the owner and other delegators. This matches the specified High-impact category: "rewards or fees mis-attributed ... or an accounting value diverging from reality where another party settles on it."

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires the NEAR `Transfer` action receipt to a `predecessor_account_id` (the withdrawer itself, since `receiver_id` is always `env::predecessor_account_id()`) to fail asynchronously after the synchronous state mutation already committed — for example if the withdrawing account is deleted between issuing the `withdraw`/`withdraw_all` call and the receipt being processed. This is a narrower and less directly attacker-profitable trigger than the ERC20/ERC721 analog (no arbitrary receiver can be targeted, and the debited party is the caller themselves), so exploitability for direct attacker profit is limited; the clearer, always-present issue is the missing safety net (no `is_promise_success` check / no state rollback) that the rest of the codebase consistently implements for exactly this class of external call.

### Recommendation
Follow the same pattern already used elsewhere in this repository (`lockup/src/owner_callbacks.rs`, `lockup/src/foundation_callbacks.rs`): chain a callback on the `Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount)` in `internal_withdraw`, check `is_promise_success()`, and only commit the `account.unstaked` / `last_total_balance` debit (or restore it) based on the actual transfer outcome, instead of updating accounting state unconditionally before the transfer executes.

### Proof of Concept
1. Delegator deposits and stakes, then unstakes and waits the required epochs so `account.unstaked_available_epoch_height <= env::epoch_height()`. [9](#0-8) 
2. Delegator calls `withdraw`/`withdraw_all`, which synchronously decrements `account.unstaked` and `self.last_total_balance`, then issues `Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount)` with no callback. [6](#0-5) [5](#0-4) 
3. If that transfer receipt fails to be delivered (e.g., the destination account ceases to exist before the receipt executes), the NEAR remains in the contract's actual balance while `last_total_balance` has already been reduced as though it left.
4. On the next `ping()` call, `internal_ping` computes `reward = current_balance - last_total_balance`, which now includes the un-delivered withdrawal amount, distributing it as reward to the owner and remaining stakers per `reward_fee_fraction`. [10](#0-9) 
5. The original withdrawer's `account.unstaked` balance remains permanently decremented despite never having received the NEAR.

    pub(crate) fn internal_withdraw(&mut self, amount: Balance) {
        assert!(amount > 0, "Withdrawal amount should be positive");

        let account_id = env::predecessor_account_id();
        let mut account = self.internal_get_account(&account_id);
        assert!(
            account.unstaked >= amount,
            "Not enough unstaked balance to withdraw"
        );
        assert!(
            account.unstaked_available_epoch_height <= env::epoch_height(),
            "The unstaked balance is not yet available due to unstaking delay"
        );
        account.unstaked -= amount;
        self.internal_save_account(&account_id, &account);

        env::log(
            format!(
                "@{} withdrawing {}. New unstaked balance is {}",
                account_id, amount, account.unstaked
            )
            .as_bytes(),
        );

        Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount);
        self.last_total_balance -= amount;
    }

    pub fn on_withdraw_unvested_amount(
        &mut self,
        amount: WrappedBalance,
        receiver_id: AccountId,
    ) -> bool {
        assert_self();

        let withdraw_succeeded = is_promise_success();
        if withdraw_succeeded {
            env::log(
                format!(
                    "Termination Step: The withdrawal of the terminated unvested amount of {} to @{} succeeded.",
                    amount.0, receiver_id
                )
                    .as_bytes(),
            );
            // Decreasing lockup amount after withdrawal.
            self.lockup_information.termination_withdrawn_tokens += amount.0;
            let unvested_amount = self.get_terminated_unvested_balance().0;
            if unvested_amount > amount.0 {
                // There is still unvested balance remaining.
                let remaining_balance = unvested_amount - amount.0;
                self.vesting_information =
                    VestingInformation::Terminating(TerminationInformation {
                        unvested_amount: remaining_balance.into(),
                        status: TerminationStatus::ReadyToWithdraw,
                    });
                env::log(
                    format!(
                        "Termination Step: There is still terminated unvested balance of {} remaining to be withdrawn",
                        remaining_balance
                    )
                        .as_bytes(),
                );
                if self.get_account_balance().0 == 0 {
                    env::log(b"The withdrawal is completed: no more balance can be withdrawn in a future call");
                }
            } else {
                self.foundation_account_id = None;
                self.vesting_information = VestingInformation::None;
                env::log(b"Vesting schedule termination and withdrawal are completed");
            }
        } else {
            self.set_termination_status(TerminationStatus::ReadyToWithdraw);
            env::log(
                format!(
                    "Termination Step: The withdrawal of the terminated unvested amount of {} to @{} failed",
                    amount.0, receiver_id,
                )
                .as_bytes(),
            );
        }
        withdraw_succeeded
    }

**File:** staking-pool/README.md (L96-114)
```markdown
The method first checks that the current epoch is different from the last epoch, and if it's not changed exits the method.

The reward are computed the following way. The contract keeps track of the last known total account balance.
This balance consist of the initial contract balance, and all delegator account balances (including the owner) and all accumulated rewards.
(Validation rewards are added automatically at the beginning of the epoch, while contract execution gas rebates are added after each transaction)

When the method is called the contract uses the current total account balance (without attached deposit) and the subtracts the last total account balance.
The difference is the total reward that has to be distributed.

The fraction of the reward is awarded to the contract owner. The fraction is configurable by the owner, but can't exceed 100%.
Note, that it might be unfair for the participants of the pool if the owner changes reward fee. But this owner will lose trust of the
participants and it will lose future revenue in the long term. This should be enough to prevent owner from abusing reward fee.
It could also be the case that they could change the reward fee to make their pool more attractive.

The remaining part of the reward is added to the total staked balance. This action increases the price of each "stake" share without
changing the amount of "stake" shares owned by different accounts. Which is effectively distributing the reward based on the number of shares.

The owner's reward is converted into "stake" shares at the new price and added to the owner's account.
It's done similarly to `stake` method but without debiting the unstaked balance of owner's account.
