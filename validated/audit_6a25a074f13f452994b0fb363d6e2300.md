### Title
Withdrawals revert when underlying Morpho markets have high utilization — `redeemUnderlying` does not cap to `maxWithdraw` - (File: contracts/strategies/ERC4626Strategy.sol)

### Summary
`IdleCDO._withdraw` computes `toRedeem` from the tranche price and, when the unlent balance is insufficient, calls `strategy.redeemUnderlying(toRedeem - balanceUnderlying)`. For ERC4626-based strategies (e.g. `MetaMorphoStrategy`), `redeemUnderlying` converts the full amount to shares and calls `IERC4626(strategyToken).redeem(_shares, ...)` with no clamping to the vault's actually withdrawable liquidity. If the underlying lending markets (Morpho Blue) have high utilization, the redeem reverts and the entire withdrawal fails — mirroring the Union `rebalance`/`withdrawAll` bug class.

### Finding Description
In `IdleCDO._withdraw`, the contract only checks its own unlent balance before delegating the rest to the strategy: [1](#0-0) 

`_liquidate` forwards to `redeemUnderlying`, which in `ERC4626Strategy` blindly converts the requested amount to shares and calls `redeem`: [2](#0-1) 

`redeem` on an ERC4626 vault (e.g. MetaMorpho) must revert when the vault cannot source the requested assets from its markets. The codebase itself acknowledges per-market liquidity limits in `MetaMorphoStrategy._calcMarketSub`, which computes `_withdrawable = min(vaultAssets, totalSupplyAssets - totalBorrowAssets)` — yet this is only used for APR simulation, not for the actual redemption path: [3](#0-2) 

An unprivileged attacker can take a collateralized borrow on Morpho (using flash-loaned collateral) to push utilization to ~100%, causing every `withdrawAA`/`withdrawBB` whose amount exceeds the CDO's unlent buffer to revert.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of all tranche-holder funds that exceed the unlent balance, for as long as utilization stays high. The attacker can sustain the DoS by keeping the borrow open (costing only borrow interest) or by front-running any liquidity return with a new borrow. Unlike the `revertIfTooLow` path, the failure is not a shortfall check — the `redeem` itself reverts, so the transaction burns no tranche tokens but also returns nothing. In a stressed market this coincides with exactly the moment users most want to exit.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any EOA can execute this with a flash loan: deposit collateral on Morpho, borrow nearly all `totalSupplyAssets - totalBorrowAssets` in the markets used by the MetaMorpho vault's withdraw queue, and all CDO withdrawals above the buffer revert. No privileged action is needed. Attack cost is bounded by the interest paid on the borrowed amount; the frozen value is the full strategy TVL. Note that `IdleTokenFungible._redeemHelper` already mitigates this bug class via `protocol.availableLiquidity()` clamping, but the CDO→strategy→ERC4626 path has no equivalent guard.

### Recommendation
In `ERC4626Strategy.redeemUnderlying` (or in `IdleCDO._liquidate`), cap the redemption to `min(requested, IERC4626(strategyToken).maxWithdraw(address(this)))` before converting to shares, and let the `revertIfTooLow` flag decide whether a shortfall reverts or is returned partially — matching the `availableLiquidity` handling already present in `IdleTokenFungible._redeemHelper`.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry fork test outline:
1. Fork mainnet at a block where an `IdleCDO` uses `MetaMorphoStrategy`; identify the MetaMorpho vault's `withdrawQueue` markets via `IMMVault.withdrawQueue(i)`.
2. As attacker: flash-loan collateral, supply it on Morpho, and `borrow` until `totalSupplyAssets - totalBorrowAssets ≈ 0` on the vault's markets.
3. As a tranche holder: call `withdrawAA(amount)` with `amount` such that `toRedeem > _contractTokenBalance(token)`. Assert the tx reverts inside `IERC4626.redeem` (MetaMorpho/Morpho liquidity check).
4. Optionally repeat with `revertIfTooLow = false` and show it still reverts (the failure is in `redeem`, not the tolerance check).
5. Repay the borrow to show withdrawals resume — confirming temporary freezing rather than insolvency.

Caveat: I was unable to fully read `MetaMorphoStrategy`'s own `redeemUnderlying` (if overridden) or `IdleCDO._liquidate`'s exact shortfall handling due to index limits; if `MetaMorphoStrategy` overrides `_redeem` to cap at `maxWithdraw`, the finding would not hold and should be re-validated against the full source.
