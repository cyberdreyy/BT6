### Title
Liquidatee cannot self-rescue via repay/withdraw if their own token account is frozen/paused, but liquidation still proceeds - ([File: programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_account/liquidate.rs])

### Summary
`lending_account_liquidate` never performs an SPL transfer to or from the liquidatee's own token account — it only mutates internal share accounting (`repay`/`withdraw` on `BankAccountWrapper`) and moves the insurance fee between two program-owned vault PDAs. By contrast, the user-initiated `lending_account_repay` and `lending_account_withdraw` instructions require an actual SPL transfer to/from the user's personal token account. If that token account (or the underlying mint, e.g. via a Token-2022 freeze/pause extension or an issuer-level freeze such as USDC blacklisting) is frozen, the user's self-rescue transfer reverts and they cannot repay or withdraw, while the liquidation keeper is entirely unaffected and can still liquidate them. This mirrors the GMX-Synthetics M-11 bug class: an actual-transfer-gated user recourse path vs. a transfer-free liquidation path.

### Finding Description
`lending_account_repay` performs `bank.deposit_spl_transfer(... signer_token_account.to_account_info(), bank_liquidity_vault.to_account_info(), authority.to_account_info() ...)`, an actual SPL transfer from the user's own token account into the vault: [1](#0-0) 

Similarly, `lending_account_withdraw` performs `bank.withdraw_spl_transfer(... bank_liquidity_vault.to_account_info(), destination_token_account.to_account_info(), ...)`, transferring out of the vault into the user's own destination token account: [2](#0-1) 

Both of these SPL transfers will revert if the user's own token account is frozen (SPL Token `FreezeAccount`, or issuer/Token-2022 pausable-mint extension), preventing the user from either paying down debt or pulling out collateral to improve their health and avoid liquidation.

In contrast, `lending_account_liquidate` computes liability/asset deltas purely via internal accounting: the liquidator's liability side is updated with `bank_account.withdraw_ignore_borrow_cap(...)`, and the liquidatee's liability side is updated with `liquidatee_liab_bank_account.repay(liab_amount_final)`, neither of which touches the liquidatee's or liquidator's personal wallet token accounts: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

The only actual SPL transfer that occurs during liquidation moves the insurance fee from the bank's liquidity vault to the bank's insurance vault — both program-owned PDAs, not the liquidatee's wallet: [5](#0-4) 

The asset-side seizure of collateral from the liquidatee likewise credits the liquidator's on-book shares without any transfer touching the liquidatee's wallet (see the surrounding `##Accounting changes##` block in the same function, lines 207-303). Thus the liquidatee's own frozen/paused token account has zero bearing on whether liquidation can execute, while it fully blocks their own repay/withdraw recourse.

The documented `BankOperationalState::Paused`/`ReduceOnly` admin-level pause is symmetric (it blocks liquidation too, per `validate_bank_state(..., InstructionKind::FailsInPausedState)` in `liquidate.rs`), so that mechanism is not the analog here — the analog is specifically an out-of-band freeze on the *liquidatee's personal token account* or mint-level transfer restriction that the marginfi bank operational-state flag has no visibility into or control over.

### Impact Explanation
A user whose liability-token account becomes frozen (issuer blacklist, e.g. Circle freezing a USDC account, or a Token-2022 mint with a pause/freeze extension enabled by its authority) loses all ability to self-rescue an account approaching liquidation: they cannot repay to reduce the liability, and cannot withdraw collateral of a different asset to rebalance, if that specific token account is frozen. Meanwhile the permissionless liquidation keeper can still liquidate the account and seize collateral through pure internal accounting, since liquidation for the liquidatee side never invokes a transfer touching the liquidatee's own token account. This causes forced, unavoidable liquidation and loss of collateral discount (2.5%+2.5%) for the user, purely due to circumstances outside their control and despite marginfi's own internal risk-management logic being otherwise sound.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an external/off-chain event (issuer freeze of the specific token account, or activation of a Token-2022 pausable/freezable extension on a bank's mint) — this is not something an attacker can trigger unilaterally on-chain, and is analogous to the "should be a very rare case" acknowledgment in the original GMX discussion. It becomes likely only for banks backed by centrally-administered stablecoins (USDC-style freeze authority) or Token-2022 mints using pause/permanent-delegate/freeze extensions, which marginfi does support for banks in general.

### Recommendation
Consider one or more of:
1. Provide an emergency mechanism (already partially present via bank-level `Paused`/`ReduceOnly` operational states, and the existing forced-deleverage flow) that can also suspend liquidation specifically for a bank whose underlying mint is known/detected to restrict transfers for particular accounts, decoupled from the liquidatee's own frozen account.
2. Document this residual risk explicitly for integrators/risk admins choosing to list banks backed by freezable/pausable tokens (similar to how `guides/ADMIN/BANK_STATE.md` already documents other asymmetries such as ReduceOnly's initial vs maintenance margin treatment), so risk admins can react quickly (e.g. via `configure_bank` to Paused) if a mint's freeze authority is exercised against a borrower.
3. Optionally extend the tokenless-repayment / deleverage machinery (`TOKENLESS_REPAYMENTS_ALLOWED`) to cover the scenario where a specific liquidatee's token account is frozen, allowing risk_admin-assisted deleverage without requiring the frozen account to transfer.

### Proof of Concept
1. Group admin lists a bank whose mint is Token-2022 with a freeze/pause-capable extension (or a standard SPL mint with a freeze authority controlled by the issuer, e.g. USDC).
2. A user deposits collateral in Bank A and borrows Bank B (the freezable-mint bank).
3. The issuer/freeze authority freezes the user's Bank-B token account (their personal wallet ATA) for compliance reasons.
4. Price movement pushes the user's account below the maintenance threshold.
5. User attempts `lending_account_repay` on Bank B to pay down the liability and become healthy again — the ix fails because `bank.deposit_spl_transfer` from `signer_token_account` reverts (frozen account), per [1](#0-0) .
6. User attempts `lending_account_withdraw` on Bank A to reduce exposure — this also fails if their Bank-A account happens to be frozen too, or is unhelpful if only Bank B is frozen and the health shortfall is driven by the liability side.
7. A liquidator calls `lending_account_liquidate` against the account. Because this instruction only adjusts internal shares for the liquidatee (`liquidatee_liab_bank_account.repay(...)`, per [6](#0-5) ) and never transfers to/from the liquidatee's frozen wallet account, the liquidation succeeds normally, seizing the user's collateral at the standard liquidation discount despite the user having no way to have prevented it.
