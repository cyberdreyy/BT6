### Title
Stale per-epoch instant-withdraw accounting lets already-claimed receipts inflate the default recovery basis and permanently strand recovery funds - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pays out the aggregate `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` but never clears the per-epoch markers `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epoch]` or `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]`. This is the same bug class as HTTP request smuggling: two accounting views disagree on whether a request has been "consumed." The aggregate view says the receipt is settled; the per-epoch view still counts it as an open claim. When a borrower default is later finalized in that same epoch, `defaultPendingClaimBasis` includes the already-paid receipt, diluting `defaultRecoveryPrice` and leaving a stranded share of `defaultRecoveryReserve` that no one can ever claim.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol`:

- `requestInstantWithdraw` records the claim in three places: `instantWithdrawsRequests[user]`, `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[user][epochNumber]`, and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` (lines 365-374).
- `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns the receipt tokens and pays `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` at par via `_transferFundedClaim`, zeroing only the aggregate counter (lines 387-392). The per-epoch entries are never decremented on a normal claim — they are only cleared inside `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` (lines 842-856), which runs exclusively post-default.
- At default finalization, `defaultPendingClaimBasis` adds `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` to the haircut basis whenever `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` (lines 644-649). This map still contains receipts that were already paid in full earlier in the same epoch.

Broken invariant: one receipt, one payout. A receipt that was burned and paid at par is still counted as an outstanding claim in the default-epoch basis.

Attack scenario (attacker is an ordinary KYC'd tranche holder / instant-withdraw user; borrower default is honest sequencing):

1. Epoch N is running. Attacker requests an instant withdraw of amount `A`; another user B requests one of `B_amt` that remains unfunded (`pendingInstantWithdraws > 0`).
2. Manager collects instant funds (`collectInstantWithdrawFunds`), strategy holds underlying; attacker calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` through the CDO and is paid `A` at par. `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[attacker][N]` and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[N]` still equal `A`.
3. Borrower defaults in epoch N; manager finalizes via `finalizeDefaultRecovery`. `defaultPendingClaimBasis` returns `pendingWithdraws + instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[N]`, inflated by `A`.
4. `defaultRecoveryPrice = reserveAmount * RECOVERY_FULL / totalBasis` is therefore lower than it should be. Every legitimate recovery claimant (attacker excluded — their `_burn(_user, claimBasis)` in `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` reverts since their receipt tokens were already burned, leaving their share stranded) is paid less.

The inflated basis share `A * recoveryPrice` stays locked in `defaultRecoveryReserve` forever — `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` for the attacker always reverts on `_burn`, so that portion can never be distributed.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing / theft of unclaimed recovery yield: all defaulted-epoch claimants (normal withdraw receipt holders, other instant withdrawers, and indirectly AA/BB holders via `activeFinalNAV`) recover less than entitled, and a pro-rata share of the recovery reserve becomes permanently stranded dust. Loss magnitude is proportional to the volume of instant withdrawals funded and claimed earlier in the default epoch — the attacker can maximize `A` up to available instant liquidity.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires: (a) instant withdraws funded and claimed in an epoch, (b) at least one other instant claim still unfunded so `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0`, and (c) a borrower default finalized while still in that same `epochNumber`. Defaults are a normal operating mode of the vault, and instant withdraws during a stressed epoch (exactly when defaults occur) are the expected usage pattern. The attacker controls `A` and only needs to have claimed before finalization — a plausible, low-cost sequence with no privileged action.

### Recommendation
In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, clear per-epoch markers alongside the aggregate: subtract the claimed amount from `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][<its request epoch>]` and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[<epoch>]`, mirroring the cleanup in `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`. Since instant requests may span epochs, track the user's request epoch (analogous to `lastWithdrawRequest`) or maintain a per-user FIFO of epoch buckets so the correct entries are decremented on every successful claim.
