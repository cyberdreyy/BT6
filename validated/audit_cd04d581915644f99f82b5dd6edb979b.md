### Title
Partially funded instant-withdraw queue lets early claimants drain the reserve and freeze later receipts - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
Analogous to the Ajna `moveLiquidity` underflow where a tracked accounting amount (`fromPosition.lps`) can be smaller than the amount actually moved (`vars.lpbAmountFrom`), `IdleCreditVault.claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pays tracked `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` receipts at par without checking that the instant-withdraw queue was actually funded. The contract's own comments and guarded arithmetic (lines 636-640, 712-723, 850-852) acknowledge that collected funds can cover "only part of the instant queue", yet the claim path burns the full receipt and transfers the full underlying amount on a first-come-first-served basis.

### Finding Description
`requestInstantWithdraw` mints a strategy-token receipt 1:1 and increments `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` / `pendingInstantWithdraws` (lines 356-375). Funding arrives later via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`, which only decrements `pendingInstantWithdraws` and pulls whatever amount the CDO transfers in (lines 398-403). At `startEpoch`, the CDO moves available cash to the strategy and the borrower is merely expected to "return the difference before instantWithdrawDeadline" (IdleCDOEpochVariant comment, line 231) — there is no guarantee the collected amount equals `pendingInstantWithdraws`.

`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` (lines 380-393) then:

```solidity
uint256 amount = instantWithdrawsRequests[_user];
_burn(_user, amount);
instantWithdrawsRequests[_user] = 0;
_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount);
```

It pays `amount` in full from `address(this)`'s underlying balance with no check that `pendingInstantWithdraws` (the unfunded remainder) is zero or that the vault's cash covers all outstanding receipts. The tracked receipt total can exceed the funded cash — exactly the "recorded amount > actual backing" mismatch of the Ajna bug. Where Ajna guarded by reverting on `lps -= lpbAmountFrom`, here the protocol even guards the analogous subtraction elsewhere: line 852 clamps `pendingInstantWithdraws = claimBasis >= pending ? 0 : pending - claimBasis`, confirming the tracked basis can exceed funded cash. But the claim itself is ungated.

Attacker path (KYC-passing lender, unprivileged): APR drops so `requestWithdraw` routes to `requestInstantWithdraw`. Attacker and victim both hold instant receipts. Borrower funds only part of the queue (e.g., available cash covers 60%). Attacker calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` (via the CDO) first, receives 100% of their receipt, draining the partial reserve. Victim's identical claim reverts on insufficient underlying — their funds are frozen until the borrower tops up or a default is declared and `defaultInstantWithdrawsFinalized` recovery accounting kicks in.

### Impact Explanation
First-come-first-served payout of a deliberately fungible queue converts a pro-rata shortfall into a total loss position for later claimants. Early claimants receive par value exceeding their fair share of funded cash (theft of unclaimed yield / others' principal share); later claimants suffer temporary freezing of their receipts until the shortfall is resolved — and permanent impairment if it is resolved via default-recovery haircut, since early claimants already escaped at par while the basis used for `defaultPendingClaimBasis` (line 644-649) still counts the remaining unfunded claims.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only a partially funded instant queue — possible whenever the borrower returns less than the full instant amount before the deadline (a liquidity timing issue, not necessarily malicious) — and a race between receipt holders, which any unprivileged lender can initiate. No privileged misbehavior needed.

### Recommendation
Gate `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` on full funding of the pending queue (e.g., require `pendingInstantWithdraws == 0` before paying at par), or pay each claim pro-rata to funded cash (`claimBasis * fundedCash / totalClaimBasis`), mirroring the `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` mechanism already used for normal withdraws.

### Proof of Concept
Fork PoC outline (Foundry): deploy `IdleCDOEpochVariant` + `IdleCreditVault` on a mainnet fork; seed deposits; drop `unscaledApr` below `lastEpochApr - instantWithdrawAprDelta` so requests route to instant; users A and B request instant withdrawals; `startEpoch()` with strategy cash covering only part of `pendingInstantWithdraws` and borrower returning only a partial top-up; call `claimInstantWithdrawRequest(A)` — succeeds and drains the reserve; `claimInstantWithdrawRequest(B)` reverts on ERC20 transfer despite B holding a valid unredeemed receipt.

Caveat: I was unable to read the CDO-side `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` wrapper (the grep returned match counts without line context), so I could not confirm whether IdleCDOEpochVariant itself gates instant claims on the deadline or full funding. If such a gate exists there, this finding is mitigated; verification of `contracts/IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol`'s instant-claim and `getInstantWithdrawFunds` paths is required.
