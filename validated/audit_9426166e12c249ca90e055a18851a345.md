## Title
Permissionless bad-debt settlement (`handle_bankruptcy`) provides no fee/incentive to the caller, discouraging timely bankruptcy resolution - (File: `programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_group/handle_bankruptcy.rs`)

### Summary
Marginfi's `lending_pool_handle_bankruptcy` instruction can be made permissionless via `PERMISSIONLESS_BAD_DEBT_SETTLEMENT_FLAG`, allowing any signer (not just the group admin/risk admin) to settle a bankrupt account's bad debt [1](#0-0) . However, the instruction transfers zero fee or reward to the calling signer — the entire flow only moves funds between the insurance vault, the liquidity vault, and depositors via loss socialization [2](#0-1) . This mirrors the M-15 bug class: a permissionless "cleanup" action with a defined economic effect on the protocol but zero built-in incentive for the caller performing it.

### Finding Description
The `PERMISSIONLESS_BAD_DEBT_SETTLEMENT_FLAG` is documented explicitly as being "useful for banks where you want the community or bots to be able to settle bad debt without waiting for the risk admin" [3](#0-2) . Despite this stated intent of relying on third parties/bots, the instruction implementation contains no payout, rebate, or gas-fee compensation mechanism for the `signer` account: it only (1) determines `covered_by_insurance`/`socialized_loss`, (2) withdraws insurance funds into the liquidity vault, (3) socializes any remaining loss across depositors, and (4) repays/kills the bad-debt balance [4](#0-3) . The `signer` field is purely an authorization check; no lamports, tokens, or shares route back to them [5](#0-4) .

The protocol's own documentation acknowledges the consequence of this action not being run: "If Bankruptcy isn't executed on a bankrupt user, then remaining depositors can never withdraw the whole balance in the bank. The last few depositors who try to withdraw will find there are not enough funds" [6](#0-5) . This is functionally identical to the M-15 pattern: the permissionless maintainer role (liquidator in M-15, bad-debt settler here) has zero built-in economic incentive to act, even though the protocol explicitly designed for "community or bots" to be the ones performing the action instead of the admin.

### Impact Explanation
Without incentive, third-party bots/community members have no reason to call `handle_bankruptcy` promptly once a `PERMISSIONLESS_BAD_DEBT_SETTLEMENT_FLAG`-enabled bank has bankrupt accounts. This can delay settlement, meaning the bank's `asset_share_value` is not written down as promptly as it could be, and remaining depositors face a longer window during which withdrawals for the "last few depositors" would fail, per the documented FAQ behavior [7](#0-6) . Note that group admin/risk admin can always call this instruction regardless of the flag [1](#0-0) , so the protocol has a privileged fallback — this significantly limits the severity, similar to how Sherlock's escalation on M-15 was ultimately kept at Medium (not High) because sponsor-run bots/admin serve as the backstop.

### Likelihood Explanation
Low-to-medium likelihood: the guide states bankruptcy is designed to be rare ("Ideally, never. As of November 2025, bankruptcy has never been executed in the main pool") [8](#0-7) , and only occurs after liquidators fail to act, illiquid assets, or drastic price moves [9](#0-8) . The `PERMISSIONLESS_BAD_DEBT_SETTLEMENT_FLAG` must also be explicitly enabled by the admin for third parties to even be eligible callers [10](#0-9) .

### Recommendation
Introduce a small incentive (e.g., a flat SOL/lamport fee, or a percentage of the covered/socialized bad debt) paid to the `signer` calling `lending_pool_handle_bankruptcy`, similar to the flat SOL fee already used for receivership liquidation (`FeeState.liquidation_flat_sol_fee`) [11](#0-10) . This ensures permissionless callers are consistently incentivized to keep bankrupt accounts settled promptly rather than relying solely on the admin/risk_admin fallback.

### Proof of Concept
1. Admin sets `PERMISSIONLESS_BAD_DEBT_SETTLEMENT_FLAG` on a bank [12](#0-11) .
2. A user account becomes bankrupt (liabilities > assets after liquidation).
3. Any third party calls `lending_pool_handle_bankruptcy`; trace the accounting: insurance funds transferred to liquidity vault, loss socialized to depositors, `marginfi_account` disabled — no transfer of value back to `ctx.accounts.signer` occurs anywhere in the instruction [13](#0-12) .
4. Since the caller pays transaction fees but receives no offsetting reward, rational bots/community members have no financial reason to call this instruction, delaying resolution and extending the window in which "the last few depositors" cannot withdraw their full balance [6](#0-5) .

            )
        })

pub struct LendingPoolHandleBankruptcy<'info> {

### Permissionless Bad Debt Settlement (Bit 2)

- **Bit 2** (`PERMISSIONLESS_BAD_DEBT_SETTLEMENT_FLAG`, value 4): When set, anyone can call the
  `handle_bankruptcy` instruction for this bank. When not set, only the `risk_admin` or `admin`
  can do so.

This is useful for banks where you want the community or bots to be able to settle bad debt without
waiting for the risk admin.

## How Does it Happen?

Bankruptcy is rare. It can only occur in one of the following circumstances: (1) liquidators haven't
been running properly, e.g. due to congestion, etc, (2) an asset is too illiquid for liquidators to
safely clear, (3) an asset's price changes drastically, before liquidators are able to respond.
