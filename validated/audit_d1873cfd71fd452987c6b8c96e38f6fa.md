### Title
Inflated epoch interest via ERC4626 share-price donation in `ProgrammableBorrower._vaultNetInterest` — ([File: contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol](contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol))

### Summary
In programmable-borrower mode, epoch interest priced at `stopEpoch` is derived from the *spot* `convertToAssets` valuation of an external ERC4626 vault (`_vaultNetInterest`, `totalInterestDueNow`). A direct underlying donation to that vault inflates every share's redemption value between `onStartEpoch` (baseline snapshot) and `onStopEpoch`, so the delta is booked as genuine vault interest. Because programmable borrowers force `isInterestMinted = true`, the CDO mints this phantom interest as unbacked strategy tokens at the epoch-settlement boundary — the same class as the LpdFi bug: a manipulable spot price feeding an interest claim across a settlement boundary, with no TWAP or deviation guard.

### Finding Description
- `ProgrammableBorrower._vaultNetInterest` computes `earnedAssets = _currentVaultAssets() + epochWithdrawnFromVault` where `_currentVaultAssets()` is `vault.convertToAssets(shares)` — a spot, donation-sensitive price (lines 337–347, 545–549).
- `totalInterestDueNow()` (lines 330–334) returns this inflated delta to `IdleCDOEpochVariant.stopEpoch`, which mints interest as strategy tokens via `IdleCreditVault.mintStrategyTokens` (IdleCreditVault.sol:621-624) — tokens unbacked by any real repayment.
- The vault-sleeve delta is deliberately not borrower debt (`settleBorrowerInterest` only settles `borrowerInterestAccrued`, lines 275–283), so no honest actor ever has to actually pay the phantom amount.
- No deviation check, TWAP, or cap exists between `epochStartVaultAssets` and the stop-time valuation; `maxApr` only caps `lastApr` on `setApr`, not the vault-PnL leg.

### Impact Explanation
Attacker (a KYC'd AA tranche holder who is also a vault shareholder):
1. During a running epoch, flash-loan/donate `D` underlying directly to the ERC4626 vault, inflating `convertToAssets`.
2. `stopEpoch` reads `totalInterestDueNow()` ≈ real interest + `D`×(pool share fraction) and mints that as strategy tokens → tranche `priceAA`/`virtualPrice` inflate via `_updateAccounting`.
3. Attacker requests/claims withdrawal at the inflated price, redeeming real pooled underlying.
4. Attacker redeems their own vault shares, recovering ~their share fraction of `D`. Net cost ≈ `D`×(1 − attacker vault share fraction) while profit is the full phantom interest credited to their tranche position.

Result: solvency broken — minted interest has no backing, so late withdrawers absorb the shortfall; direct theft by the attacker up to the inflated amount. Analogous magnitude to the $690k source incident scales with pool TVL.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a programmable-borrower deployment whose ERC4626 vault's `totalAssets` is manipulable by donation (most naive ERC4626s are), the attacker holding a dominant share of both the vault and the AA tranche, and sequencing around the honest manager's `stopEpoch` call. No privileged collusion needed; KYC'd lender and vault-user roles are in scope. If the vault uses internal accounting or virtual shares resistant to donation, the attack reduces proportionally — worth verifying per deployment.

### Recommendation
- Price vault PnL from a manipulation-resistant measure: track assets actually deposited/withdrawn (`epochDepositedToVault`/`epochWithdrawnFromVault` are already recorded) and compare realized proceeds rather than spot `convertToAssets`, or bound vault interest by a configured APR cap applied to `epochStartVaultAssets`.
- Alternatively require the vault interest leg to be settled in cash (transferred to the CDO) at `stopEpoch` instead of minted, so phantom interest reverts via insufficient balance rather than diluting LPs.
