### Title
Unprivileged account-ID squatting lets an attacker deploy a malicious lockup at a victim's deterministic address before the legitimate grant lands - (File: lockup-factory/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
`LockupFactory::create` computes the lockup's account ID solely from `sha256(owner_account_id)[..20]` and lets any caller supply all other `LockupArgs`, with no check that the caller is authorized for that `owner_account_id`. An attacker can pre-empt a not-yet-created grant by calling `create` with the victim's `owner_account_id` and attacker-chosen `lockup_duration: 0`/`vesting_schedule: None`/`staking_pool_whitelist_account_id`, permanently occupying that address before the legitimate grantor's transaction executes.

### Finding Description
The broken binding: `LockupArgs` deployed at `sha256(owner_account_id)[..20].<factory>` == the args the legitimate grantor intended for `owner_account_id`. This does not hold because `create` is `#[payable]` and open to any `predecessor_account_id` [1](#0-0) , and the deployed address depends only on the attacker-supplied `owner_account_id`, not on who calls `create`:
- Attacker calls `create(owner_account_id="victim.near", lockup_duration=0, lockup_timestamp=None, vesting_schedule=None, release_duration=None, whitelist_account_id=None)` with `attached_deposit == MIN_ATTACHED_BALANCE` [2](#0-1) .
- This creates the account at `hex::encode(sha256("victim.near")[..20]).<factory>` with the attacker's own deposit and an unfavorable/attacker-chosen `LockupArgs` (immediately-unlockable `lockup_duration: 0`, no vesting) [3](#0-2) .
- When the legitimate grantor later sends their own `create` call for `owner_account_id="victim.near"`, `create_account()` on the same deterministic address fails because the account already exists, so `is_promise_success()` in `on_lockup_create` returns `false`, and their deposit is refunded to them via `Promise::new(predecessor_account_id).transfer(attached_deposit.0)` [4](#0-3) .
- `assert_self()` and `is_promise_success()` only guard that the callback executes correctly for whichever call reaches it first; neither of them ties `owner_account_id` to `predecessor_account_id`, so they don't prevent the squat [5](#0-4) .

The legitimate grantor gets their own deposit back (no direct loss of the grantor's NEAR), but the victim's deterministic lockup address is now permanently occupied by a lockup that the victim does not control the terms of and that was funded by the attacker's own NEAR — the intended grant can never be deployed at that address again.

### Impact Explanation
The victim's canonical lockup address ends up holding a lockup contract whose parameters (`lockup_duration`, `vesting_schedule`, `staking_pool_whitelist_account_id`) were never chosen by the rightful grantor/foundation, matching the Critical category "a lockup deployed with parameters its rightful creator never chose." This permanently displaces the intended grant for that `owner_account_id`, since the address is deterministic and cannot be re-derived differently. The attack is repeatable across any `owner_account_id` an attacker anticipates will receive a grant, for the cost of `MIN_ATTACHED_BALANCE` (3.5 NEAR) per squatted address.

### Likelihood Explanation
The only precondition is knowledge (or a guess) of the intended `owner_account_id` before the legitimate `create` transaction lands, plus `MIN_ATTACHED_BALANCE` NEAR to attach. Since grant recipient account IDs (employee/investor accounts) are often known or guessable in advance, and `create` has no allowlist or owner-caller check, this is straightforward to execute by any unprivileged party.

### Recommendation
Bind lockup creation authorization to the intended owner or a designated creator: e.g., require the call to be signed by (or otherwise authorized by) `owner_account_id`/`foundation_account_id`, or require an access key / pre-registration step that reserves `owner_account_id` before allowing `create` to run, so an arbitrary caller cannot deploy a lockup for an owner they don't control.
