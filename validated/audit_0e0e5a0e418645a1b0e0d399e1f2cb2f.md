### Title
Fee-on-transfer deposits mint unbacked credit-vault strategy tokens - (File: `contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol`)

### Summary

`IdleCreditVault.deposit` mints `_amount` strategy tokens even though a fee-on-transfer underlying delivers less than `_amount`. `IdleCDOCreditVault._deposit` correctly prices the lender’s tranche shares using the actual amount received, but then forwards the pre-fee `_amount` to the strategy. The strategy mints unbacked receipt tokens for the transfer fee, overstating CDO NAV and later causing redemption insolvency or denial of withdrawal.

### Finding Description

`IdleCDOCreditVault._deposit` snapshots the vault’s underlying balance and mints tranche shares based on `balanceAfter - balanceBefore`, which is the correct net amount received from the lender. [1](#0-0) 

However, it then calls `IIdleCDOStrategy(strategy).deposit(_amount)` with the original pre-fee amount rather than the measured `balanceAfter - balanceBefore`. [2](#0-1) 

Inside `IdleCreditVault.deposit`, the strategy pulls `_amount` from the CDO and immediately mints `_amount` strategy tokens without measuring the actual amount received. [3](#0-2) 

For a token charging fee `f`, an unprivileged lender calling `depositAA(_amount)` produces:

```text
amount requested by lender                 = _amount
underlying received by IdleCDOCreditVault  = _amount - f
AA/BB tranche shares minted                = worth _amount - f
underlying received by IdleCreditVault     = _amount - f
strategy tokens minted to IdleCDO          = _amount
unbacked strategy-token inflation          = f
```

The protocol records `getContractValue()` as `_amount`, because the CDO holds `_amount` strategy tokens, while the strategy only holds `_amount - f` underlying. `getContractValue()` directly values strategy-token balance plus vault-held underlying net of fees. [4](#0-3) 

If the CDO already contains other underlying liquidity, the fee discrepancy can silently socialize the missing value across the vault. If it does not, the strategy’s `safeTransferFrom` pulls less than `_amount` but minting remains `_amount`, creating the same accounting surplus.

### Impact Explanation

Every deposit into a fee-on-transfer token inflates strategy-token supply by the charged fee. The attacker does not need privileged access: a KYC-approved lender can repeatedly deposit and trigger the protocol to mint unbacked strategy tokens.

The loss is quantifiable as the cumulative transfer fee. For a `1%` fee token and a `10_000_000` underlying deposit, the strategy mints `10_000_000` strategy tokens while receiving `9_900_000` underlying, creating `100_000` units of unbacked NAV.

Once normal withdrawals are requested or funded, the unbacked strategy tokens translate into an insolvent payout. If withdrawals are funded before the mismatch is detected, earlier claimants can consume the shortfall; otherwise later withdrawals can permanently or temporarily freeze.

### Likelihood Explanation

The issue requires only that the configured underlying token charge a transfer fee. The initializer accepts an arbitrary ERC20-compatible underlying and does not enforce that `transferFrom` credits the full requested amount. [5](#0-4) 

No privileged attacker, malicious strategy, borrower default, oracle manipulation, or reentrancy is needed. A standard public `depositAA` call triggers the accounting divergence.

### Recommendation

Measure the net amount received at every transfer boundary and use that value consistently for minting, NAV updates, and strategy deposits.

In `IdleCDOCreditVault._deposit`, assign the received amount once and pass it to the strategy:

```solidity
uint256 received = _contractTokenBalance(_token) - _preBal;
_minted = _mintSharesAtCurrPrice(received, msg.sender, _tranche);
IIdleCDOStrategy(strategy).deposit(received);
```

In `IdleCreditVault.deposit`, measure the strategy balance before and after `safeTransferFrom`, mint only the received amount, and update `totEpochDeposits` with that amount.

A stronger defense is to explicitly reject fee-on-transfer or deflationary underlyings during initialization, since downstream accounting assumes strategy tokens are backed one-for-one by underlying.

### Proof of Concept

A reproducible Foundry test can configure the CDO with a fee-on-transfer ERC20:

```solidity
// FeeToken burns 1% in transferFrom.
uint256 amount = 1_000_000e6;

feeToken.mint(lender, amount);
vm.prank(lender);
feeToken.approve(address(cdo), amount);

uint256 lenderTranches = cdo.depositAA(amount);

uint256 cdoUnderlying = feeToken.balanceOf(address(cdo));
uint256 strategyUnderlying = feeToken.balanceOf(address(strategy));
uint256 strategyReceipts = strategy.balanceOf(address(cdo));

// CDO and strategy each received 990_000e6, while strategy minted 1_000_000e6.
assertEq(cdoUnderlying, 0);
assertEq(strategyUnderlying, 990_000e6);
assertEq(strategyReceipts, 1_000_000e6);
assertEq(strategyUnderlying, strategyReceipts - 10_000e6);
assertEq(cdo.getContractValue(), 1_000_000e6);
```

The assertion demonstrates `10_000e6` units of NAV backed only by `990_000e6` units of strategy underlying.
