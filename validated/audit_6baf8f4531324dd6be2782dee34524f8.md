Let me check how `requestWithdraw` is priced during the buffer window and how `stopEpoch` losses treat pending withdraw requests.### Title
BB holder front-runs a lossy `stopEpoch` to convert first-loss tranche exposure into a pro-rata pending receipt - (contracts/IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol)

### Summary
The `restrict` blacklist front-run maps to this vault as a user front-running a privileged loss-realization call to escape the consequences that call imposes. When the manager broadcasts `stopEpochWithDuration(_newApr, _interest, _duration, _lossAmount)`, a BB tranche holder can insert a `requestWithdraw` before it. The pending-receipt bucket has no tranche identity and absorbs only a pro-rata share of `_lossAmount`, while the remaining active-position loss is applied BB-first. The attacker therefore swaps a 100%-first-loss BB position for a diluted pro-rata receipt.

### Finding Description
`requestWithdraw` remains callable during the buffer window after `epochEndDate` passes but before the manager stops the epoch: `allowAAWithdrawRequest`/`allowBBWithdrawRequest` are only cleared on default (`_handleBorrowerDefault`, `IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:595-596`) or emergency shutdown (`_emergencyShutdown`, `:612`), and a normal lossy stop does not disable them. The request is priced via `_trancheToUnderlyings` at the pre-loss virtual price (`:756`) and calls `creditVault.requestWithdraw(_underlyings, msg.sender, principal)` (`:788`), which mints a strategy-token receipt and increments `pendingWithdraws` (`IdleCreditVault.sol:272-279`).

Inside `_stopEpoch`, the loss split is:

```solidity
// contracts/IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:391-393
// Pending receipts have no tranche identity, so their aggregate loss is pro rata across all
// receipts. The remaining active loss is applied BB-first after the stop succeeds.
(_pendingWithdraws, _lossAmount) = _strategy.previewLossAdjustedWithdrawFunds(_lossAmount);
```

and the residual is burned BB-first via `burnStrategyTokens` + `_forceUpdateAccounting` (`:496-499`). So a BB holder who stays in the tranche eats the loss first (up to full BB NAV wipeout), while one who front-runs `stopEpoch` with `requestWithdraw` shares only `loss * receipt / totalReceipts` and later claims the remainder at `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` through `claimWithdrawRequest` (`IdleCreditVault.sol:301-313`).

### Impact Explanation
Direct loss-shifting: the attacker avoids most or all of the BB-first loss they would have absorbed, and the avoided loss is pushed onto the other pending receipts — including AA receipts that are senior to BB in the active waterfall. Concretely, with a single large BB holder requesting W and total pending receipts P, they bear `loss * W/P` instead of `min(loss, theirBBshare)` under BB-first ordering. On a large `_lossAmount` the gain is on the order of the full loss amount minus the small pro-rata share. This breaks the loss waterfall invariant.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only a KYC-passed tranche holder and a mempool-visible manager call — `stopEpoch`/`stopEpochWithDuration` with nonzero `_lossAmount`, or `getInstantWithdrawFunds`/`stopEpoch` that will underfund receipts. Both are public-mempool transactions; the window between `epochEndDate` and the stop is when the loss becomes certain but flags are still open. No privileged role needed; gas cost is the only barrier. I was not able to read the full body of `previewLossAdjustedWithdrawFunds` to confirm the exact pro-rata formula (index excerpt limits); the split semantics above rely on the documented contract comments at `:391-393` and `collectWithdrawFunds` at `IdleCreditVault.sol:411-419`, which explicitly describe pro-rata pending-receipt loss.

### Recommendation
Disallow new withdraw requests once `block.timestamp >= epochEndDate` (freeze requests in the buffer between epoch end and stop), or tag pending receipts with their tranche so `previewLossAdjustedWithdrawFunds` can apply BB-first ordering inside the pending bucket as well. Alternatively, route lossy stops through private relays and treat the documented pro-rata policy as a known limitation.
