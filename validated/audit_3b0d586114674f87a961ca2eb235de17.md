## Analysis

The binding claimed to be broken: **reward credited to account A across epoch E should be proportional to `stake_shares[A] integrated over the time A held them during E`**, but the code actually implements: **reward credited to account A at the ping call that closes epoch E is proportional to `stake_shares[A]` measured only at the instant `internal_ping` runs**, with no time-weighting at all.

### Trace

`internal_ping` computes the whole epoch's reward as a single delta and splits it by current share ownership: [1](#0-0) 

`internal_stake` mints shares at the pre-reward price, without any record of *when* those shares were bought relative to the epoch: [2](#0-1) 

Call ordering in `stake`/`stake_all`/`deposit_and_stake` always pings *before* minting new shares in the same call: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

This means an attacker cannot capture reward *in the same transaction* that also triggers the epoch-boundary ping (the ping fires first, against the old share set). But it does **not** prevent the two-transaction attack described:

1. **Late in epoch N**, attacker optionally sends a bare `Transfer` to the pool account (no method call). This inflates `env::account_balance()` immediately but is not yet swept into `total_reward`, since `internal_ping` only processes when `epoch_height != last_epoch_height` — same-epoch calls are a no-op: [5](#0-4) 
2. Still in epoch N, attacker calls `deposit_and_stake`. Since the epoch hasn't turned yet, `internal_ping()` no-ops, and `internal_stake` mints shares at the *current* (undiluted) price — cheap, since the donation/pending reward hasn't been priced in yet.
3. **Epoch N+1 begins** (new block). Anyone (including the attacker) calls anything that triggers `internal_ping`. Now `total_balance` reflects the entire epoch N validator reward (credited by the protocol at the epoch boundary) plus any bare-transfer donation. `total_reward` is split pro-rata over `total_stake_shares`, which **already includes the attacker's shares bought moments earlier** — even though, at the NEAR protocol level, a stake action submitted right before an epoch boundary does not actually become bonded/earning until ~2 epochs later (`internal_restake`'s `Promise::stake` only affects future epochs).
4. Attacker immediately calls `unstake_all`, which pings again (no-op, same epoch) and converts the now-inflated shares back to `unstaked` balance at the new (already-diluted) price, locking in the gain.

No existing guard prevents this: `internal_ping`'s only assertion is `total_balance >= last_total_balance` (monotonicity, not fairness); there is no `assert_owner`/`assert_self`/minimum holding period check on `internal_stake`, and the share-price rounding guards only protect against rounding loss, not against timing.

### Title
Instantaneous (non-time-weighted) pro-rata reward distribution in `internal_ping` allows one-block flash-staking to capture a full epoch's reward - (File: `staking-pool/src/internal.rs`)

### Summary
`internal_ping` distributes the entire delta between two consecutive pings to whatever `total_stake_shares` exist at the moment the ping executes, with no accounting for how long each shareholder actually held their shares during the epoch that produced the reward. An attacker can stake immediately before an epoch turnover (optionally after priming the pool with a bare `Transfer` donation) and unstake immediately after the next `internal_ping`, capturing a full epoch's proportional reward for one block of real exposure, diluting the rewards legitimately earned by delegators who were staked the whole epoch.

### Finding Description
The broken equality is: `reward(account, epoch) == reward_pool(epoch) * shares_held_during_epoch(account) / total_shares_during_epoch`. The actual code computes `reward(account, epoch) == reward_pool(epoch) * shares_at_ping_time(account) / total_shares_at_ping_time`, per [6](#0-5) . Because `stake()`/`deposit_and_stake()` mint shares at the pre-reward price whenever called mid-epoch (`internal_stake`, [7](#0-6) ) and the very next `internal_ping` sweeps the entire accumulated epoch reward against whatever shares exist at that instant, a delegator who stakes one block before epoch turnover receives the same proportional reward as one who staked the entire epoch. This is compounded by the documented behavior that any bare `Transfer` sent to the pool account is also swept into the next epoch's `total_reward` pot (`staking-pool/README.md` lines 90-91), letting an attacker prime a larger pot right before buying cheap shares. Existing guards (`assert!` on monotonic balance, share-price rounding guards) do not address the timing/fairness dimension at all.

### Impact Explanation
Real protocol-level validator rewards accrued by long-term delegators' stake get partially reassigned to an attacker who contributed no meaningful bonded stake for that epoch (a NEAR `stake` action submitted near an epoch boundary does not actually affect bonded/validating stake until ~2 epochs later, per NEAR's epoch mechanics, yet the contract credits it as already earning). This is a direct mis-attribution of staking rewards from legitimate long-term delegators (and potentially the owner's fee split) to the attacker — matching the "High: rewards ... attributed to the wrong party" category. It is repeatable every epoch, on any staking pool deployed from this code, by any unprivileged account with enough NEAR to stake meaningfully relative to the pool's total.

### Likelihood Explanation
No special privileges are required — any account can call `deposit_and_stake`, wait for the next epoch (predictable from `env::epoch_height()`), and then call `unstake_all`/`withdraw` after the 4-epoch unbonding delay. The only cost is capital lockup for a few epochs and gas. The main constraint is capital size relative to the pool (to make the captured share of the reward worthwhile) and correctly timing the transaction near the epoch boundary, which is observable on-chain. This is a low-cost, fully repeatable strategy across epochs and across every pool instance deployed from this contract.

### Recommendation
Time-weight reward distribution instead of using instantaneous share snapshots — e.g., only allow newly staked shares to start participating in reward distribution starting from the *next* full epoch after they are staked (track a "pending" bucket of shares that only migrate into the reward-earning pool at the following `internal_ping`), mirroring how NEAR's own protocol delays stake-proposal effects by ~2 epochs. Alternatively, require unstaking to also carry a minimum holding-period check, or accrue rewards on a per-block/per-epoch basis using an epoch-anchored snapshot of shares.

### Proof of Concept
Using the existing `Emulator` test harness in `staking-pool/src/lib.rs`'s test module (pattern from `test_two_delegates`):
1. Initialize pool with zero fee.
2. Alice deposits and stakes `X` NEAR, `simulate_stake_call()`.
3. `skip_epochs(N)` to simulate her holding through most of an epoch.
4. Just before advancing to the reward-bearing epoch, Bob deposits and stakes the same `X` NEAR (one block of exposure).
5. Advance one epoch and inject a validator reward (`emulator.amount += reward` matching existing test patterns), then call `ping()`.
6. Assert: `get_account_staked_balance(alice) - X == get_account_staked_balance(bob) - X` (both received an equal absolute reward despite vastly different holding duration), proving reward is not proportional to time-weighted shares.
7. Have Bob call `unstake_all()` immediately, then advance 4 epochs and `withdraw_all()`, showing he successfully extracted the reward he wasn't entitled to for a single block of exposure.

        assert!(

            "The calculated number of \"stake\" shares received for staking should be positive"
        );
        // The amount of tokens the account will be charged from the unstaked balance.
        // Rounded down to avoid overcharging the account to guarantee that the account can always
        // unstake at least the same amount as staked.
        let charge_amount = self.staked_amount_from_num_shares_rounded_down(num_shares);
        assert!(
            charge_amount > 0,
            "Invariant violation. Calculated staked amount must be positive, because \"stake\" share price should be at least 1"
        );

        assert!(
            account.unstaked >= charge_amount,
            "Not enough unstaked balance to stake"
        );
        account.unstaked -= charge_amount;
        account.stake_shares += num_shares;
        self.internal_save_account(&account_id, &account);
