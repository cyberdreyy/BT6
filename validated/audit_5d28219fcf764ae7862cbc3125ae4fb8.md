### Title
`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pays the full aggregate instant receipt at par even when only partially funded, draining other claimants' funds - ([File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol](contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol))

### Summary
Analogous to the Zcash body-poisoning bug (a shared commitment is validated by a cheap mutable check before the integrity check, letting a bad input permanently consume a valid claim slot), `IdleCreditVault.claimInstantWithdrawRequest` treats the mutable aggregate `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` as fully funded based only on a receipt balance, while the actual funding invariant lives in the separate `pendingInstantWithdraws` counter. When the borrower/epoch flow funds instant requests only partially, an attacker can claim the entire unfunded-plus-funded aggregate at par from strategy-held underlyings belonging to other claimants.

### Finding Description
`requestInstantWithdraw` burns CDO strategy tokens, mints a 1:1 receipt, and increases both `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` and the global `pendingInstantWithdraws` (lines 356-374). Funding is delivered separately: `collectInstantWithdrawFunds(_amount)` decreases `pendingInstantWithdraws` by the actually-collected amount and pulls `_amount` underlying from the CDO (lines 398-403). Nothing ties a user's `instantWithdrawsRequests` balance to how much was actually collected.

`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` then burns the *entire* `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` aggregate and transfers the full amount at par (lines 380-393), with no check that `pendingInstantWithdraws` for that user's epochs was ever funded:

```solidity
uint256 amount = instantWithdrawsRequests[_user];
_burn(_user, amount);
instantWithdrawsRequests[_user] = 0;
_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount);
```

So the "commitment" (receipt tokens minted 1:1) is satisfied while the funding invariant is not — the same class of mismatch as a mutated body passing the header/txid commitment while violating the auth-data commitment. `_transferFundedClaim` only guards the *default recovery reserve* when it is non-zero (lines 897-907); before any default, or for funds held for other users' funded claims, there is no isolation check at all — `safeTransfer` just spends whatever the strategy holds.

### Impact Explanation
Phase: epoch running, `allowInstantWithdraw` true. The attacker calls `requestInstantWithdraw(X)`; the honest CDO/manager calls `collectInstantWithdrawFunds(Y)` with `Y < X` (partial borrower prefunding, e.g. only part of the instant queue covered — a scenario the code itself acknowledges in `defaultPendingClaimBasis`, lines 636-640). The attacker then calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` via `IdleCDOEpochVariant.claimInstantWithdrawRequest` and receives `X` underlying, not `Y`. The excess `X - Y` is paid out of underlyings held by the strategy for other users' funded claims, causing direct theft proportional to the unfunded gap, and leaving the last claimants' receipts unpayable (permanent freezing of their funded claims / insolvency).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only an unprivileged KYC-passing lender able to call `requestInstantWithdraw` while instant withdrawals are allowed, plus a partially-funded instant cycle — which is an explicitly supported state (partial prefunding is a first-class case in `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve`). The attacker funds nothing extra; cost is one deposit + one request. No privileged collusion needed.

### Recommendation
Track per-user funded instant amounts, or gate `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` on the user's instant receipts being fully funded (e.g. burn/transfer at most the funded share derived from `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch` vs `pendingInstantWithdraws`, or maintain a `fundedInstantWithdraws` counter credited in `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` and cap claims to a pro-rata share). Alternatively revert the claim while `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` for that epoch.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry fork test outline:
1. Deploy `IdleCreditVault` + `IdleCDOEpochVariant` on a fork; enable `allowInstantWithdraw`, whitelist attacker via Keyring.
2. Attacker deposits, calls `cdo.requestInstantWithdraw(X)` → strategy mints `X` receipt, `pendingInstantWithdraws = X`.
3. Simulate epoch start where borrower covers only `Y < X`: call `collectInstantWithdrawFunds(Y)` (via CDO harness) — strategy holds `Y` underlying plus `Z` belonging to another user's funded claim.
4. Attacker calls `cdo.claimInstantWithdrawRequest()` → receives `X` underlying, i.e. `Y + (X-Y)` stolen from `Z`.
5. Assert the other user's subsequent claim reverts/underpays, quantifying the loss as `X - Y`.
