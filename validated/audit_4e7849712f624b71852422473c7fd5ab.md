### Title
Unfunded instant-withdraw receipts are paid at par from the funded normal-withdraw pool — ([File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol](contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol))

### Summary
Analogous to the btrfs `UPTODATE`/`READING` race — where a caller observes a "done" marker while the underlying work was never completed — `claimInstantWithdrawRequest()` treats the mere presence of strategy-token receipts (`instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`) as proof of funding, and pays the full amount at par, even when `pendingInstantWithdraws` shows the borrower never supplied that cash. The strategy contract holds one commingled underlying balance, so an unfunded instant claim is paid out of underlyings that `collectWithdrawFunds()` collected for other users' funded epoch-withdraw receipts. Those funded claimants then revert on `_transferFundedClaim`, i.e., the same funded dollar is promised twice.

### Finding Description
- `requestInstantWithdraw()` (IdleCreditVault.sol:356-375) burns CDO strategy tokens, mints a receipt to the user, and increments `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch`, `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch`, and `pendingInstantWithdraws`. Nothing marks the request as funded vs. unfunded.
- Funding happens separately via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds()` (lines 398-403), which decrements `pendingInstantWithdraws` and pulls underlying from the CDO. If the borrower/manager never funds the instant queue, `pendingInstantWithdraws` stays > 0 — the exact analog of a read still "in progress" while the data is already marked consumable.
- `claimInstantWithdrawRequest()` (lines 380-393) does **not** check `pendingInstantWithdraws`, per-epoch funding, or any funded flag. It reads `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, burns the receipt, zeroes the counter, and calls `_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount)`.
- `_transferFundedClaim()` (lines 897-907) only protects `defaultRecoveryReserve` (post-default isolation). In a non-defaulted vault it transfers any underlying held by the strategy — including cash pulled in by `collectWithdrawFunds()` (lines 411-430) that is earmarked for funded epoch-withdraw claims (`_claimFundedWithdrawRequest` / `_claimLossAdjustedWithdrawRequest`).
- The stale-check ordering is the same shape as CVE-2024-35798: step (1) "receipt exists" (`instantWithdrawsRequests != 0`), step (2) "mark consumed" (`instantWithdrawsRequests = 0`), with no re-check that the corresponding funding step (`collectInstantWithdraws` → `pendingInstantWithdraws -= _amount`) ever ran. Two receipt classes share one balance, so claimant B pays out of claimant A's funded pool — the "corrupted node" here is a drained withdraw reserve.

### Impact Explanation
Direct theft / insolvency: an attacker holding an unfunded instant-withdraw receipt receives underlying at par, and honest users whose epoch-withdraw receipts were already funded by the borrower can no longer claim (`_transferFundedClaim` reverts or pays less). Loss equals the attacker's instant request amount, up to the entire funded `pendingWithdraws` cash balance held by the strategy. This breaks the "one receipt, one payout" and solvency invariants.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requirements: instant withdrawals enabled (`setInstantWithdrawParams`), a running epoch, and a window where strategy holds funded withdraw cash while `pendingInstantWithdraws > 0`. The borrower/manager are honest but their funding of the instant queue is a separate, sequenced action (`collectInstantWithdrawFunds` via `startEpoch`/`getInstantWithdrawFunds`); a borrower funding normal `pendingWithdraws` while leaving `pendingInstantWithdraws` unfunded is a normal operating state (e.g., partial borrower repayment, delayed instant funding). The attacker only needs a KYC-passed wallet and tranche tokens to request the instant withdraw. Uncertainty: I could not fully verify in this pass whether `IdleCDOEpochVariant.claimInstantWithdrawRequest` adds an outer gate (e.g., requiring `pendingInstantWithdraws == 0` or elapsed `instantDelay`) before calling into the strategy — if it does, likelihood drops accordingly, but the strategy-level function itself performs no funded check.

### Recommendation
In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, deduct the claim from `pendingInstantWithdraws` (or track a per-epoch funded-instant counter set in `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`) and only pay amounts that were actually collected — mirroring the btrfs fix of re-testing `UPTODATE` after acquiring `READING`. E.g., `require(amount <= fundedInstantClaims)` where `fundedInstantClaims` is incremented by `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`, so unfunded receipts revert instead of spending the funded-withdraw pool.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry fork outline (in `test/foundry/`, modeled on `IdleCreditVault.t.sol`):
1. Enable instant withdrawals (`cdoEpoch.setInstantWithdrawParams(delay, diff, false)`); users A (victim) and B (attacker) deposit AA.
2. `startEpoch`; both call `requestWithdraw`/`requestInstantWithdraw`: A opens a normal epoch withdraw, B opens an instant withdraw.
3. Honest `stopEpoch` where the borrower funds `pendingWithdraws` (A's receipt) via `collectWithdrawFunds`, but B's instant request remains unfunded (`pendingInstantWithdraws > 0`).
4. B calls `claimWithdrawRequest`/`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` through the CDO: `instantWithdrawsRequests[B]` is zeroed and B receives `amount` underlying — sourced from the cash collected for A.
5. A calls `claimWithdrawRequest`: `_transferFundedClaim` reverts (`NotAllowed`/insufficient balance). Assert `underlying.balanceOf(B) - pre == instantAmount` and A's claim fails — quantified loss = B's receipt amount.
