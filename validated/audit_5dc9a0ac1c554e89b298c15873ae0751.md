Confirmed: in phase0 through Electra, `get_seed(state, epoch, DOMAIN_BEACON_PROPOSER)` uses `get_randao_mix(state, epoch + EPOCHS_PER_HISTORICAL_VECTOR - MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD - 1)` [1](#0-0) , so the seed for the current epoch's proposer lottery is fixed `MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD` epochs in advance and is known before `process_effective_balance_updates` runs for the epoch that determines the acting effective balances. `get_beacon_proposer_index` recomputes the weighted lottery live from `state.validators[i].effective_balance` on every call [2](#0-1) , and `process_effective_balance_updates` only moves a validator's effective balance when the real balance crosses a hysteresis band, in discrete `EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT` steps [3](#0-2) . This is exactly the pattern the Fulu fix (EIP-7917) target: the accompanying tests state "Pre-EIP-7917, effective balance changes due to attestation rewards changes the next epoch's lookahead" [4](#0-3) , and Fulu replaces the live computation with a `proposer_lookahead` vector snapshotted one epoch ahead precisely to remove this steerability [5](#0-4) .

### Title
Validators can steer their own future beacon-block-proposer selection by nudging effective balance across hysteresis thresholds - (File: specs/phase0/beacon-chain.md, and mirrored in altair/bellatrix/capella/deneb/electra beacon-chain.md)

### Summary
In all forks prior to Fulu, `get_beacon_proposer_index` is computed on-the-fly from the *current* `state.validators[i].effective_balance` using a seed that is fixed `MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD` epochs in advance. Because `effective_balance` is only updated at epoch boundaries via a hysteresis rule keyed off the validator's raw `balance`, and a validator's own `balance` changes each epoch by a predictable amount from its own attestation/sync-committee rewards, a validator that knows the future epoch's proposer seed can choose (or withhold) participation in the current epoch specifically to push its own `balance` across a hysteresis boundary, thereby altering the RANDAO-weighted lottery outcome for slots it would not otherwise win (or avoiding slots it would otherwise lose). This is the direct analog of the reported CoreVoting bug: a self-controlled, single-block(-epoch)-scoped balance nudge changes the weight used in a duty-selection lottery whose randomness source was already fixed before the nudge.

### Finding Description
`get_seed(state, epoch, DOMAIN_BEACON_PROPOSER)` derives its randomness from `get_randao_mix(state, epoch + EPOCHS_PER_HISTORICAL_VECTOR - MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD - 1)` [6](#0-5) . Because of `MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD`, this RANDAO mix — and hence the entire sequence of per-slot proposer seeds for the epoch — is fixed and publicly known at least one epoch before the epoch begins.

`get_beacon_proposer_index` does not use a value cached at the start of the epoch; it is called fresh for every slot's `process_block_header` and reads `state.validators[candidate].effective_balance` live via `compute_proposer_index` [2](#0-1) . `effective_balance` is only mutated once per epoch, in `process_effective_balance_updates`, and only in discrete `EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT` steps once `balance` crosses a hysteresis threshold around the current `effective_balance` [3](#0-2) . `process_effective_balance_updates` runs as part of `process_epoch` at the very transition into the epoch whose proposer seeds were already fixed by `MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD` [7](#0-6) .

A validator therefore has, in advance, all the information needed to determine the exact `effective_balance` bucket (and hence weighted-lottery outcome) that will apply to each slot of an upcoming epoch, because the seed is fixed early and the effective-balance update rule is deterministic on `balance`. By choosing to attest optimally (or to skip attesting) during the current epoch, a validator can steer its own raw `balance` to land just above or just below a hysteresis threshold, deterministically changing whether it becomes the proposer for a specific future slot — a duty selection it should not be able to unilaterally control. This is confirmed directly by the spec's own regression tests, which assert that pre-Fulu "effective balance changes due to attestation rewards changes the next epoch's lookahead," while Fulu's `proposer_lookahead` snapshot mechanism (`process_proposer_lookahead`, called via `process_epoch`) was introduced specifically to eliminate this [8](#0-7) , and the test explicitly documents pre-fix vs. post-fix behavior [4](#0-3) .

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the intended property that proposer duty selection is an unbiased, RANDAO-driven lottery that no single validator can steer. A validator can deterministically increase its own chance of being assigned as proposer for a targeted future slot (a valuable duty carrying block rewards and MEV/extra-data control), or deterministically avoid an assignment, purely through its own choice of participation — without needing any stake advantage, coalition, or protocol violation. This matches the "High" impact bucket: "a duty selection one participant can steer."

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires no special privilege beyond being a normal active validator: knowledge of `MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD`-ahead RANDAO seed (public), knowledge of one's own current `balance`/`effective_balance` and reward schedule (locally computable), and the ability to choose to attest or not attest in the current epoch (routine validator behavior). Precision is bounded by the discreteness of `EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT`/hysteresis steps and by uncertainty in exact rewards, but a validator sitting near a hysteresis boundary can reliably predict and hit the needed bucket over one or a few epochs.

### Recommendation
This exact class of issue was already identified and remediated by the Fulu fork via EIP-7917 (`proposer_lookahead`, `process_proposer_lookahead`, `get_beacon_proposer_indices`), which computes and freezes the entire epoch's proposer assignments one epoch ahead of use, before the effective-balance updates that a validator's own current-epoch behavior can influence take effect [9](#0-8) . Networks/forks still running pre-Fulu logic (phase0 through Electra) remain exposed to this self-steering vector until Fulu activates.

### Proof of Concept
1. Validator V is active with `effective_balance` sitting exactly `HYSTERESIS_INCREMENT * HYSTERESIS_UPWARD_MULTIPLIER` below its next-step boundary at the start of epoch `N`.
2. V computes `get_seed(state, N+1, DOMAIN_BEACON_PROPOSER)` using the already-fixed RANDAO mix (available since epoch `N+1-MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD`) and determines the `effective_balance` value that would make it the weighted-lottery winner for a specific slot `s` in epoch `N+1`.
3. V attests optimally throughout epoch `N` to accrue just enough reward that its `balance` crosses the upward hysteresis threshold, causing `process_effective_balance_updates` at the `N → N+1` boundary to bump its `effective_balance` by exactly one increment.
4. When `get_beacon_proposer_index` is evaluated live for slot `s` in epoch `N+1` [2](#0-1) , V's higher `effective_balance` now crosses the sampling threshold in `compute_proposer_index`, making V the proposer for slot `s` where it would not have been otherwise — a duty outcome V engineered unilaterally.

def process_effective_balance_updates(state: BeaconState) -> None:
    # Update effective balances with hysteresis
    for index, validator in enumerate(state.validators):
        balance = state.balances[index]
        HYSTERESIS_INCREMENT = Uint64(EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT // HYSTERESIS_QUOTIENT)
        DOWNWARD_THRESHOLD = HYSTERESIS_INCREMENT * HYSTERESIS_DOWNWARD_MULTIPLIER
        UPWARD_THRESHOLD = HYSTERESIS_INCREMENT * HYSTERESIS_UPWARD_MULTIPLIER
        if (
            balance + DOWNWARD_THRESHOLD < validator.effective_balance
            or validator.effective_balance + UPWARD_THRESHOLD < balance
        ):
            validator.effective_balance = min(
                balance - balance % EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT, MAX_EFFECTIVE_BALANCE
            )

#### New `process_proposer_lookahead`

*Note*: This function updates the `proposer_lookahead` field in the beacon state
by shifting out proposer indices from the earliest epoch and appending new
proposer indices for the latest epoch. With `MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD` set to `1`,
this means that at the start of epoch `N`, the proposer lookahead for epoch
`N+1` will be computed and included in the beacon state's lookahead.

```python
def process_proposer_lookahead(state: BeaconState) -> None:
    last_epoch_start = len(state.proposer_lookahead) - SLOTS_PER_EPOCH
    # Shift out proposers in the first epoch
    state.proposer_lookahead[:last_epoch_start] = state.proposer_lookahead[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:]
    # Fill in the last epoch with new proposer indices
    last_epoch_proposers = get_beacon_proposer_indices(
        state, get_current_epoch(state) + MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD + 1
    )
    state.proposer_lookahead[last_epoch_start:] = last_epoch_proposers
```
