### Title
Uncapped withdrawal amount vs. capped builder balance deduction creates unbacked Gwei in `apply_withdrawals` - (File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md)

### Summary
`get_builder_withdrawals` and `apply_withdrawals` in the Gloas fork exhibit the same "commit vs. settle" accounting split as the referenced Caviar bug: the amount that is queued for payout (`Withdrawal.amount`, which becomes what the execution layer actually credits to `fee_recipient`) is never re-checked against the builder's real balance, while the actual balance deduction inside `apply_withdrawals` is silently capped with `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)`. If a builder's balance is ever lower than the committed withdrawal amount at settlement time, the EL pays out the full committed amount while the CL only debits the (smaller) available balance — creating Gwei that is not backed by any validator/builder balance decrease.

### Finding Description
`get_builder_withdrawals` builds `Withdrawal` objects directly from `state.builder_pending_withdrawals[i].amount`, without capping to the builder's current balance: [1](#0-0) 

These `Withdrawal` objects are stored verbatim in `state.payload_expected_withdrawals`, which is the field the execution layer is required to honor when crediting `fee_recipient` addresses: [2](#0-1) 

The only place the builder's CL-side balance is decreased is inside `apply_withdrawals`, and there the deduction is explicitly capped:
```python
if is_builder_index(withdrawal.validator_index):
    builder_index = convert_validator_index_to_builder_index(withdrawal.validator_index)
    builder_balance = state.builders[builder_index].balance
    state.builders[builder_index].balance -= min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)
``` [3](#0-2) 

This is precisely the pattern flagged in the Caviar report: the amount used for the "collection" side (`withdrawal.amount`, what gets committed and paid to the recipient on the EL) is not the same quantity guarded by the "distribution" side check (`min(..., builder_balance)`). The test suite explicitly documents and accepts this divergence rather than treating it as invalid input: [4](#0-3) 
and the accompanying assertion helper confirms the balance decrease is capped to `min(total_amount, pre_balance)` while the withdrawal amount paid out is the uncapped, requested amount: [5](#0-4) 

For this divergence to actually occur, a builder's real balance must fall below the sum of its outstanding `builder_pending_withdrawals`/pending payments by the time `apply_withdrawals` runs. `can_builder_cover_bid` is presumably meant to prevent overcommitment at bid time, but I could not locate and fully verify its implementation within the tool budget available (the exact accounting it performs over `builder_pending_payments` + `builder_pending_withdrawals` + the new bid was not retrieved). This is the key open question: if `can_builder_cover_bid`'s liability accounting has any gap (e.g., missing one of the two pending queues, or a race between bid admission and processing order across blocks/epochs), a builder could end up with committed withdrawals exceeding its live balance, at which point the `min()` cap in `apply_withdrawals` would produce the Gwei-creation effect described above. Note also the spec's own commentary a few lines above `apply_withdrawals` acknowledges that "CL-side saturation creates a net supply inflation" for the general withdrawal-decoupling problem, but that comment is written to justify why *validator* withdrawals are deducted immediately — it does not address why the *builder* branch still uses a silent `min()` cap instead of asserting the balance is sufficient or skipping/reducing the withdrawal amount to match: [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
If reachable, this breaks the Critical-tier invariant "Gwei created ... paid to a non-owner": the execution layer would mint/credit the full `withdrawal.amount` in ETH to `fee_recipient`, while the consensus layer only ever debited a smaller amount from the builder's balance. This is a total-supply-breaking accounting mismatch, analogous to the Caviar royalty bug where the amount charged to the payer diverged from the amount actually paid to the recipient.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is uncertain and could not be fully confirmed with the available tool calls. The mismatch requires a builder's tracked balance to become insufficient for its already-queued `builder_pending_withdrawals` by the time `apply_withdrawals` runs — something `can_builder_cover_bid` is presumably designed to prevent at bid-acceptance time. I was unable to retrieve and audit `can_builder_cover_bid`'s definition (searches located only its call sites and asserts, not the body) to confirm whether its liability accounting is airtight across all builder balance-affecting requests (builder deposits, builder exits, multiple concurrent pending payments/withdrawals, cross-epoch payment eviction paths such as the "older than previous epoch" branch in `apply_parent_execution_payload`). Given this unresolved gap, likelihood should be treated as unknown rather than asserted as high or low.

### Recommendation
1. Locate and audit `can_builder_cover_bid` to confirm it sums *all* outstanding liabilities (`builder_pending_payments` + `builder_pending_withdrawals`, including entries created via the "older than previous epoch" direct-append path) against `builder.balance` at every point a new bid, deposit, or exit is processed, guaranteeing the invariant "sum(outstanding builder withdrawals) <= builder.balance" always holds.
2. If a gap is found, either (a) make `apply_withdrawals` assert `withdrawal.amount <= builder_balance` for builder withdrawals instead of silently capping with `min()`, or (b) make `get_builder_withdrawals` cap `Withdrawal.amount` to the builder's current balance so the amount recorded in `payload_expected_withdrawals` (and thus paid out by the EL) can never exceed what is actually debited from the CL.
3. Add a spec-level invariant test asserting `sum(withdrawal.amount for withdrawals of builder B) == balance_decrease(B)` for every block, to catch any future reintroduction of this divergence.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual (dependent on the unverified accounting gap in `can_builder_cover_bid`):
1. Builder `B` accrues multiple queued `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` entries in `state.builder_pending_withdrawals` (e.g. via several accepted bids across blocks) whose total nominally does not exceed `B.balance` at admission time.
2. Some legitimate spec-permitted event reduces `B.balance` after the pending withdrawals were queued but before they are all processed (this is the step requiring confirmation of `can_builder_cover_bid`'s completeness).
3. `process_withdrawals` runs, `get_builder_withdrawals` emits a `Withdrawal` with the full originally-committed `amount` for `B`, which is placed into `state.payload_expected_withdrawals`.
4. `apply_withdrawals` executes `state.builders[builder_index].balance -= min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)`, capping the CL-side deduction at `builder_balance < withdrawal.amount`, as directly demonstrated by the existing spec test `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance`, which shows a withdrawal of 5 ETH recorded and paid out while only 1 ETH is deducted from the builder: [7](#0-6) 
5. The execution layer, honoring `payload_expected_withdrawals`, credits `fee_recipient` the full 5 ETH, while only 1 ETH was ever removed from any account on the consensus layer — a 4 ETH unbacked Gwei creation.

I was not able to fully verify within this session whether step 2 is actually reachable under `can_builder_cover_bid`'s admission checks; this remains the key unconfirmed link in the chain.

*Note*: Unlike deposits (which are applied at the child's slot via
`apply_parent_execution_payload`), withdrawal balance deductions are applied
immediately via `apply_withdrawals`. Deferring the deduction to the child's slot
would break the total supply invariant: state transitions between the commitment
slot and the deduction slot (e.g., `process_pending_consolidations` at an epoch
boundary) can reduce a validator's balance below the committed withdrawal
amount, causing `decrease_balance` to saturate at zero. Since the execution
layer mints the full committed amount regardless, any CL-side saturation creates
a net supply inflation. As a consequence, `state.balances` reflects the
withdrawal deduction before the corresponding execution payload is confirmed,
creating a transient asymmetry with the EL state at `state.latest_block_hash`.
Off-chain consumers that require CL/EL balance consistency can reconstruct
pre-deduction balances by adding back `state.payload_expected_withdrawals`.
