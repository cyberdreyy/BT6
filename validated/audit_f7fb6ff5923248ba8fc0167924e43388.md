[1](#0-0) ### Title
ERC4626 deposit-cap griefing blocks programmable-borrower epoch startup - ([File: contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol])

### Summary
A public depositor in the configured ERC4626 vault can consume the vault’s remaining deposit capacity before `startEpoch`. `IdleCDOEpochVariant.startEpoch` sends pool funds to `ProgrammableBorrower` and then invokes `onStartEpoch`, which unconditionally deposits the borrower contract’s entire underlying balance into the vault. If the vault deposit reverts, the entire epoch-start transaction reverts, leaving lender deposits and pending withdrawals frozen in the buffer phase. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
Programmable-borrower deployments require minted-interest accounting and prohibit instant withdrawals, so epoch startup depends on `_startEpochProgrammableBorrower` succeeding. [3](#0-2) 

After `startEpoch` transfers surplus funds to the programmable borrower, it calls the hook through `_startEpochProgrammableBorrower`. [4](#0-3)  The hook is not isolated from the epoch transition: `ProgrammableBorrower.onStartEpoch` computes the accounting baseline and then deposits `underlyingToken.balanceOf(address(this))` into the configured ERC4626 vault. [5](#0-4)  `_depositToVault` propagates any `vault.deposit` failure. [6](#0-5) 

ERC4626 explicitly permits deposit limits through `maxDeposit`, and `deposit` must revert when the full asset amount cannot be accepted. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7)  Therefore, when the vault is configured with a finite cap, an attacker can deposit up to that cap during the buffer period and make the subsequent all-balance deposit revert.

The existing `try this.sendFundsToBorrower` only protects the borrower transfer itself; it does not isolate the later programmable-borrower hook failure. [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation
The attack temporarily freezes all funds waiting to enter the epoch.

For a pool with `D` underlying in new buffer deposits and `N` in already-managed NAV:

- The `D` new deposits cannot be deployed to the borrower.
- Pending normal withdrawals cannot be funded because the next epoch never starts.
- The attacker can repeat the deposit-cap saturation whenever the owner retries `startEpoch`.
- If the attacker maintains the condition for `t` seconds, the lost borrower yield is approximately `N * borrowerApr * t / 365 days`.
- The attacker’s deposit remains withdrawable after the attack, so the principal attack cost is temporarily locking `maxDeposit(address(programmableBorrower))` rather than burning funds.

The PoC below freezes `10_000 USDC` of new lender deposits and prevents the epoch from opening. Operators may eventually unwind or replace the vault through `emergencyExitVault`, `setVault`, or `rescueTokens`, but these are manual privileged recovery actions and do not prevent repeated cap saturation while the current vault remains configured. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is conditional on the configured ERC4626 having a finite deposit cap or another public condition that makes `deposit` revert.

The design accepts an arbitrary asset-matching ERC4626 through initialization or `setVault`; it does not require the vault to have unlimited capacity. [12](#0-11)  ERC4626 deposit caps are part of the standard interface, so an unprivileged vault depositor can create the failing state without compromising the borrower, owner, manager, Keyring, strategy, or CDO. [7](#0-6) 

The impact is temporary rather than immediately permanent because the revert occurs atomically and operators can eventually change integrations while epoch accounting is inactive. Nevertheless, each successful grief postpones epoch settlement and all dependent withdrawals.

### Recommendation
Do not make epoch accounting depend on depositing the entire cash balance into a capacity-limited ERC4626 vault.

In `ProgrammableBorrower.onStartEpoch`:

1. Read `vault.maxDeposit(address(this))`.
2. Deposit only the accepted amount and retain the remainder as an explicitly accounted on-hand cash sleeve.
3. Extend `epochStartVaultAssets` / `_vaultNetInterest` accounting so undeposited cash is principal rather than being interpreted as vault loss.
4. Emit an event when vault capacity is exhausted.
5. Consider a preflight hook or separate borrower-funding call that can fail before `IdleCDOEpochVariant` mutates epoch state.
6. Document and exercise a buffer-period fallback-vault migration path for persistent
