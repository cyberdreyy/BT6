### Title
Stale per-epoch instant-withdraw receipts are reused in default recovery accounting after being paid (use-after-free analog) - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pays out and burns a user's instant-withdraw receipt but never clears `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epoch]` or decrements `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]`. If the same epoch later defaults and is finalized, those "freed" (already-paid) receipts are reused three ways: they inflate the recovery claim basis in `defaultPendingClaimBasis`, they are double-counted as still-held prefunded cash in `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve`, and they resurrect a payable claim in `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`. This is the on-chain analog of CVE-2023-31972's use-after-free in `pp_getline`: state that should have been invalidated on consumption is read again later as if still valid.

### Finding Description
- `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` and pays at par via `_transferFundedClaim`, but leaves `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][currentEpoch]` and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[currentEpoch]` untouched (`IdleCreditVault.sol:380-393`). The only places those per-epoch counters are decremented are `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` (`:842-856`) and `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` for the aggregate (`:398-403`).
- During `finalizeDefaultRecovery`, `defaultPendingClaimBasis` adds `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` whenever `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` (`:644-649`). Already-claimed receipts still sit in that mapping, so `totalBasis` is overstated (`:679-680`).
- `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve` computes `instantBasis - pendingInstant` and adds it to `reserveAmount` (`:716-723`, `:686`), assuming the prefunded cash backing those receipts is still held by the strategy. If claimants already withdrew it, the reserve is overstated and `defaultRecoveryPrice` is set too high (`:688-692`).
- On the user side, `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` reads the stale `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][defaultEpoch]` entry, subtracts it from `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` (which is already 0 for a fully claimed user → underflow/revert) and decrements `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch`/`pendingInstantWithdraws` (`:842-856`).

Two concrete violation modes in the defaulted/finalized phase:

1. Par-claimed receipts still counted in the basis: an attacker (or any early claimant) requests an instant withdraw, the CDO partially collects funds via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` (leaving `pendingInstantWithdraws > 0`), the attacker claims at par draining the strategy balance, then the borrower defaults. Finalization counts the attacker's already-paid receipt both in `totalBasis` and in `prefundedReserve`, inflating `defaultRecoveryPrice` beyond what the reserve actually backs.
2. Inflated price → reserve drain/freeze: honest claimants call `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`/`_claimDefaultedWithdrawRequest`, each drawing `(claimBasis * defaultRecoveryPrice)/1e18` from `defaultRecoveryReserve` (`:912-917`). Because the price was computed against a balance that includes already-spent prefunded cash, later claims either over-pay early claimants (theft from later claimants' recovery share) or revert in `safeTransfer`/`reserve -=` once the real balance is exhausted — permanently freezing remaining recovery funds in the contract. The attacker's own second-claim attempt reverts on underflow, but the damage to the pool is done at finalization, not at their claim.

### Impact Explanation
Broken invariants: "one receipt one payout" and recovery solvency. Already-paid instant receipts are double-counted as recovery basis and as still-held prefunded backing. Quantified loss: if an attacker (or simply the first claimants in FIFO order — no privilege required) withdraws `X` of prefunded instant cash at par, `recoveryPrice` is set as if `X` were still in the reserve, so honest claimants with basis `B` lose up to `X * B / totalBasis` of recovery, and once the real balance runs out all subsequent claims revert — a permanent freeze of the undistributed remainder of `defaultRecoveryReserve`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-low. It requires a specific but unprivileged sequence in one epoch: instant withdrawals enabled and partially funded (CDO collects less than the full `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch`), at least one user claiming before the epoch closes, and the epoch then defaulting and being finalized via `finalizeDefaultRecovery` while `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` (so `defaultInstantWithdrawsFinalized` is set, `:696`). All roles involved (borrower default, manager/owner finalization) are honest actors; the exploiter is an ordinary KYC'd lender exercising the normal `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` path early. No existing guard prevents it: `_transferFundedClaim` only shields `defaultRecoveryReserve` (`:897-907`), which is empty before finalization, and nothing tracks "claimed" status per epoch.

### Recommendation
In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, clear the per-epoch accounting when paying at par: zero `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][requestEpoch]` and decrement `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[requestEpoch]` (requires storing each user's instant-request epoch, e.g., a `lastInstantWithdrawRequest` mapping). Alternatively, make `defaultPendingClaimBasis`/`_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve` derive the current-epoch instant basis from actually-uncleared receipts rather than the cumulative `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch`, and cap `prefundedReserve` by `underlyingToken.balanceOf(address(this))`.

### Proof of Concept
I did not have remaining budget to verify the exact `IdleCDOEpochVariant` call ordering (whether the CDO exposes a user path that calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` while `pendingInstantWithdraws > 0` before `stopEpoch`/default, and whether partial `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` occurs in a defaulting epoch). A Foundry PoC would need to: (1) start an epoch with instant withdrawals enabled; (2) have attacker request instant withdraw, warp past `instantDelay`, have the CDO partially collect funds, attacker calls `cdoEpoch.claimInstantWithdrawRequest()` and receives par; (3) leave another user's instant request unfunded (`pendingInstantWithdraws > 0`); (4) default the epoch (`stopEpoch(0,0)` path) and `finalizeDefault(recovered, payer)`; (5) assert `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[defaultEpoch]` still includes the attacker's paid receipt and that the honest user's defaulted-epoch claim either over-dilutes or reverts on insufficient balance. If the CDO never permits a user claim between partial collection and default finalization, this degrades to accounting dust only — that ordering is the key unverified assumption.
