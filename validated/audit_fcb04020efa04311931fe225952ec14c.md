### Title
`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pays a fresh instant receipt at par without checking that its epoch was actually funded, letting an attacker drain underlyings reserved for other users' loss-adjusted/funded receipts - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
The kernel bug is a torn read: `fetch_pte()` reads `pgtable->root` and `pgtable->mode` without synchronization, so it can pair a new root with a stale level and fail the walk. The credit-vault analog is a torn read of the instant-withdraw claim pair: `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` reads the aggregate receipt `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` and pays it at par via `_transferFundedClaim`, but it never reads/verifies the funding state (`pendingInstantWithdraws`, `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]`, or `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch`) that is written separately — at a different time — by `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`/`collectWithdrawFunds` during `stopEpoch`. A receipt minted *after* funding was collected for an older epoch is thus paid from underlyings that were earmarked for someone else's receipts.

### Finding Description
`requestInstantWithdraw` burns the CDO's strategy tokens, mints an equal receipt to the user, and records it under the *current* `epochNumber` (contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol:356-374). The funding half of the pair arrives later: `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` pulls underlying from the CDO and decrements `pendingInstantWithdraws` (lines 398-403), and `collectWithdrawFunds` pulls underlying for normal receipts and writes `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch[epochNumber]` (lines 411-429).

`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` (lines 380-393) then:
- burns `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` (the *aggregate*, not the per-epoch entry),
- clears it,
- calls `_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount)` paying at par.

It performs no check that the request's epoch was funded (no `epochNumber`/epoch-wait gate like `_claimFundedWithdrawRequest` line 326, no `pendingInstantWithdraws`/`instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch` check, no loss-haircut via `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` like `_claimLossAdjustedWithdrawRequest` line 789). The only accounting that ties an instant receipt to its funding — `pendingInstantWithdraws` and `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epoch]` — is never consulted on the claim path, mirroring the kernel's new-root/old-mode mismatch: the claim observes the new receipt while implicitly assuming the funding state of an older epoch.

### Impact Explanation
A KYC-passing lender (attacker class allowed by the rules) calls the CDO's instant-withdraw path to get an instant receipt minted, then calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`. Because the claim pays from `underlyingToken` held by the strategy — which at that moment is the funded-but-unclaimed balance belonging to epoch-N normal/loss-adjusted withdraw claimers — the attacker receives underlying at par for a request the borrower never funded. Result: direct theft of other users' funded withdrawal proceeds / unclaimed yield, breaking the "one receipt, one payout" and solvency invariants; the legitimate claimers' `_transferFundedClaim` later reverts on insufficient balance (permanent freezing of their funds, or loss equal to the drained amount). The stolen amount is bounded by `min(attacker's instant receipt, strategy underlying balance of funded claims)` — i.e., the full funded-but-unclaimed balance can be taken.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires: underlying sitting in the strategy (normal after any `stopEpoch` that called `collectWithdrawFunds`/`collectInstantWithdrawFunds`, or a `defaultRecoveryReserve`), plus an attacker able to obtain an instant receipt via the CDO while that balance is present. Both are ordinary states; no privileged misbehavior needed. The main uncertainty is whether `IdleCDOEpochVariant` forces `requestInstantWithdraw` → `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` → `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` to execute atomically in one transaction — the CDO-side ordering was not fully verified in this pass; if any path lets a user hold a minted instant receipt across transactions (e.g., when CDO liquidity was temporarily insufficient and `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` pulled only part, leaving the receipt open), the window exists.

### Recommendation
Bind the claim to its funding epoch, exactly as the kernel fix binds root/mode with a seqcount: have `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` verify that the receipt's epoch was funded — e.g., track funded amounts per epoch (set by `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`) and pay only `min(receipt, fundedForEpoch)`, or require `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epoch]` to be cleared only against a decrement of a per-epoch funded counter, and revert when unfunded. Do not pay instant claims from a global strategy balance that is also the sink for `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` and `defaultRecoveryReserve` payouts.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry fork sketch (mirroring test/foundry/IdleCreditVault.t.sol helpers):

```solidity
// setup: victim deposits AA, requests withdraw in epoch N
_depositWithUser(victim, 100_000 * ONE_SCALE, true);
vm.prank(victim);
cdoEpoch.requestWithdraw(victimShares, address(AAtranche));

// epoch N stops; borrower funds pending receipts -> underlying lands in strategy
_startEpochAndCheckPrices(0);
_stopEpochAndCheckPrices(0, apr, 0); // collectWithdrawFunds pulls victim's funded amount into strategy
uint256 fundedBalance = underlying.balanceOf(address(strategy));
assertGt(fundedBalance, 0);

// attacker (KYC lender) obtains an instant receipt sized to the funded balance
_depositWithUser(attacker, fundedBalance, true);
vm.prank(attacker);
cdoEpoch.requestInstantWithdraw(attackerShares); // mints receipt, pendingInstantWithdraws += amt

// attacker claims BEFORE borrower funding arrives -> paid from victim's earmarked funds
uint256 pre = underlying.balanceOf(attacker);
vm.prank(attacker);
cdoEpoch.claimInstantWithdrawRequest();
assertGt(underlying.balanceOf(attacker) - pre, 0); // attacker paid at par

// victim's funded claim now underflows/reverts or is haircut by theft
vm.prank(victim);
vm.expectRevert();
cdoEpoch.claimWithdrawRequest();
```

Note: the exact CDO entry points (`requestInstantWithdraw`/`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` exposure and whether they are bundled with `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`) need to be confirmed against `IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol`; if the CDO enforces atomic fund-then-claim, the residual risk reduces to the partial-funding case where `pendingInstantWithdraws > 0` leaves an open receipt payable at par against unrelated reserves.
