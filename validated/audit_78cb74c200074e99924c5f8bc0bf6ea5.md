This confirms the timing check is a deliberate, documented consensus-fallback rule (matching the actual on-chain block-commit RBF economics), not an implementation bug.

### Analysis

The `first_proposal_burn_block_timing` window is an intentional, documented protocol rule, not an accidental equality that an attacker "bypasses." Its purpose, per the config docs and comments, is specifically to permit reorgs of tenures whose sole block was proposed *too close to the next burn block* to have given the incoming miner a fair chance to RBF (replace-by-fee) their block commit — this mirrors real Bitcoin miner economics, since block-commit RBF windows are inherently bounded by BTC block timing. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

The claimed "attacker-influenceable equality" is exactly what the mechanism is designed to test: a miner who wins a sortition after a delayed/fast burn block interacts with whether the *previous* miner's block was "poorly timed" relative to that same burn block. This isn't an attacker breaking a fairness guarantee — it's the guarantee itself. The rule exists precisely because sortition winners cannot control burn-block timing arbitrarily (BTC block production is probabilistic and controlled by the entire hashpower, not a single miner), and Stacks explicitly limits this permitted reorg window to tenures with **at most one globally accepted block** [3](#0-2) , bounding the damage to at most a single orphaned block — matching Bitcoin's own reorg-depth-limited security model, not an unbounded "erase any competing tenure" capability.

Regarding the specific attacker model given (a single BTC miner controlling one sortition slot): the premise that an unprivileged single miner can reliably "cause" a burn-chain stall and then win the very next sortition on demand is not supported — sortition winners are chosen probabilistically weighted by burn commitment across all participating miners; a single miner cannot deterministically both delay a block AND win the subsequent sortition without control over global BTC hashrate/mempool, which is outside the signer's threat model and outside "one miner slot plus gossip." The mechanism assumes exactly this kind of adversarial timing is bounded — that's why the check exists and why it's paired with the "at most one accepted block" cap and the superseded-tenure bookkeping (`mark_tenure_superseded`) that ties the permit's validity to the permitting sortition remaining canonical [4](#0-3) .

The existing test suite explicitly documents and validates this exact scenario as expected/allowed behavior (`allow_reorg_within_first_proposal_burn_block_timing_secs`, `mark_miner_as_invalid_if_reorg_is_rejected_v1`), confirming it is a known, intended design trade-off rather than a defect.
