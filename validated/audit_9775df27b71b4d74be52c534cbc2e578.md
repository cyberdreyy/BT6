### Title
Post-default withdraw requests pay 1:1 from the fixed default-recovery reserve, draining funds earmarked for defaulted-epoch claimants - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
`IdleCreditVault.finalizeDefaultRecovery` computes `defaultRecoveryReserve` to exactly cover the defaulted-epoch claim basis at `defaultRecoveryPrice`. After finalization, `requestWithdraw` takes a post-default branch that mints the user a receipt and records `postDefaultRequests[_user]`, which `_claimPostDefaultWithdrawRequest` pays **1:1** out of that same finite reserve via `_transferDefaultRecovery`. Those post-default claims were never part of `totalBasis`, so every post-default payout reduces the reserve below what is owed to defaulted-epoch receipt holders, leaving later claimants underpaid or permanently unable to claim.

### Finding Description
The external bug (arbitrary write via a deserialized instance reaching an unintended sink) maps here to a claim routed to the wrong fund sink: a request created *after* default recovery was priced is paid *from* the recovery reserve instead of from newly funded cash.

- `finalizeDefaultRecovery` sizes the reserve for a fixed basis: `reserveAmount = _recoveredAmount + prefundedReserve + defaultRecoveryReserve` and `recoveryPrice = reserveAmount * RECOVERY_FULL / totalBasis`, where `totalBasis = activeBasis + defaultPendingClaimBasis()` (pending normal + current-epoch instant receipts). [1](#0-0) 
- Post-finalization, `requestWithdraw` hits the `defaultRecoveryFinalized` branch: it burns the CDO's strategy tokens, mints `_amount` to the user, and stores `postDefaultRequests[_user] = _amount` — with no new underlying entering the reserve. [2](#0-1) 
- `claimWithdrawRequest` then prefers `_claimPostDefaultWithdrawRequest`, which burns the receipt and calls `_transferDefaultRecovery(_user, amount)` at par. [3](#0-2) 
- Defaulted-epoch claimants are only entitled to `claimBasis * defaultRecoveryPrice / RECOVERY_FULL`. [4](#0-3) 

Solvency check: the reserve can pay exactly `totalBasis * recoveryPrice` in aggregate. A post-default claim of `A` withdraws `A` (ratio 1.0) even though no basis of `A` was ever reserved. If `recoveryPrice < RECOVERY_FULL` (the normal case after a loss), `A` exceeds `A * recoveryPrice`, so the reserve is exhausted before all defaulted claims are paid; `defaultRecoveryReserve` underflows or the ERC20 transfer reverts for the tail claimants.

### Impact Explanation
Broken invariant: one receipt one payout / loss waterfall. An unprivileged lender who deposits after default finalization (or who held tranche tokens) calls `requestWithdraw` then `claimWithdrawRequest` and receives underlying at par from a reserve that belongs pro-rata to defaulted-epoch receipt holders and active tranche holders. Quantified loss: up to `postDefaultRequests` total, i.e., `(1 - recoveryPrice)` of each post-default claim is a direct transfer from defaulted claimants, and once the reserve is drained the remaining defaulted claims are permanently frozen (revert on transfer/underflow). This is direct theft plus permanent freezing of unclaimed recovery.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a borrower default that is finalized with `recoveryPrice < 1e18` and residual `defaultRecoveryReserve > 0`, plus the pool still accepting deposits/`requestWithdraw` post-default (the code path explicitly supports this UX per the comments). Any EOA holding tranche tokens post-default can execute it — no privileged role needed. Sequencing: default finalization sets `defaultRecoveryFinalized`, then the attacker deposits, requests a withdraw (post-default branch does not increase `pendingWithdraws`, so nothing gates the amount against the reserve), and claims immediately, since `_claimPostDefaultWithdrawRequest` has no epoch-wait check. The attacker can also front-run legitimate defaulted claimants' claims because both draw the same reserve.

### Recommendation
Do not pay `postDefaultRequests` from `defaultRecoveryReserve`. Either (a) fund post-default claims from newly repaid borrower cash / a separate accounting bucket, (b) include post-default requests in a re-priced recovery basis before allowing claims, or (c) disallow withdraw claims entirely once `defaultRecoveryFinalized` until the reserve is fully distributed (e.g., route them through `DefaultDistributor` with their own haircut). At minimum, cap `_transferDefaultRecovery` for post-default claims at `amount * defaultRecoveryPrice / RECOVERY_FULL` so par payouts cannot exceed the per-basis reserve.

### Proof of Concept
Reproducible on a Foundry fork using the existing harness in `test/foundry/IdleCreditVault.t.sol`:

1. Lender L deposits into the AA tranche; epoch starts; borrower draws funds.
2. Attacker A (KYC'd lender) also deposits a small amount.
3. Borrower defaults; `finalizeDefault(recovered, source)` runs, producing `defaultRecoveryPrice = 0.5e18` and `defaultRecoveryReserve = R` sized exactly for `totalBasis`.
4. L requests a withdraw in the defaulted epoch (receipt basis B) but does not claim yet.
5. Post-finalization, A calls `cdoEpoch.requestWithdraw` → strategy mints `postDefaultRequests[A] = X` (X chosen ≤ R).
6. A calls `claimWithdrawRequest` → `_claimPostDefaultWithdrawRequest` transfers `X` at 1:1 from the reserve (`reserve' = R - X`).
7. L calls `claimWithdrawRequest` → entitled to `B * 0.5`; the strategy balance is now `R - X < Σ remaining entitlements`, so L's transfer reverts or L is short by `X * (1 - 0.5)`.

Assertions: `underlying.balanceOf(A) - pre == X` while `underlying.balanceOf(strategy) < pendingDefaultEntitlements`, demonstrating reserve drainage and frozen recovery for honest claimants.

Note: I verified the reserve sizing, the post-default mint/claim path, and the haircut math directly in `IdleCreditVault.sol`. I was unable to fully read `_transferDefaultRecovery`'s reserve-spending guard (truncated at line ~900) or confirm the exact `stopEpoch`/`collectWithdrawFunds`/`deposit` call ordering in `IdleCDOEpochVariant` before the iteration limit; if `_transferDefaultRecovery` already reverts when a claim would exceed the fair share, the impact degrades to permanently frozen post-default claims rather than theft — the fix recommendation is unchanged.
