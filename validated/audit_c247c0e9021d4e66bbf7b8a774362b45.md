Confirmed: the builder's balance is only decreased at bid-acceptance time by the check `can_builder_cover_bid` (a solvency check, not a deduction) — actual balance decrease happens only in `decrease_balance` calls that occur when the withdrawal is eventually paid out (e.g., via `settle_builder_payment` → `builder_pending_withdrawals` → `apply_withdrawals`). The `builder_pending_payments` entry created in `process_execution_payload_bid` at `specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md:2124-2137` is the *only* record that a payment of `bid.value` is owed to the builder's `fee_recipient`. If `process_proposer_slashing` clears that entry (`specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md:2451-2467`), the `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` it held is discarded entirely — it is never appended to `state.builder_pending_withdrawals`, so it is never paid to `fee_recipient` and no balance anywhere is credited with it. This matches the report's bug class exactly: a stored, time-windowed pending value that gets wiped by an unrelated, unprivileged action before it is settled, permanently destroying it rather than merely delaying it. ### Title
Any validator can destroy a builder's already-committed payment by self-slashing as the payment's proposer within the 2-epoch window - (File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md)

### Summary
`process_proposer_slashing` (Gloas) deletes the `BuilderPendingPayment` entry associated with a proposal's slot whenever the slashed validator is that payment's `proposer_index` and the proposal is still within the 2-epoch settlement window. Deleting the entry discards the `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` it contains, permanently destroying the builder's payment (the fee owed to `fee_recipient`) instead of merely dropping the proposer's weight/inclusion credit. Because a proposer can slash themselves (submit two conflicting signed headers for their own slot), any proposer can unilaterally burn a payment a builder already committed to and had accepted.

### Finding Description
When a builder's bid is accepted in `process_execution_payload_bid`, a `BuilderPendingPayment` is recorded containing the `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` (fee_recipient, amount, builder_index) that must eventually be paid, plus a `weight` and the block's `proposer_index`: [1](#0-0) 

This is the sole state record of the pending payment; the builder's balance is not decreased at this point — it is decreased only later, when the withdrawal is applied (via `settle_builder_payment` → `builder_pending_withdrawals` → `apply_withdrawals`).

`process_proposer_slashing` clears the entire `BuilderPendingPayment` slot — not just the proposer's weight — whenever the slashed validator matches `payment.proposer_index` and the proposal slot falls in the current or previous epoch: [2](#0-1) 

`BuilderPendingPayment.empty()` zeroes out the `withdrawal.amount`/`fee_recipient`/`builder_index` along with the `weight`, so the builder's already-accepted payment is wiped from state entirely: it is not moved to `builder_pending_withdrawals`, and the builder's `balance` (which was never decreased) is not adjusted either. The Gwei the builder committed to pay is simply erased from both sides of the ledger with no compensating credit to anyone — breaking the invariant that an accepted bid's committed payment must eventually be settled to `fee_recipient` (or, if not settled, remain covered by the builder's own balance).

Crucially, a validator can trigger `initiate_validator_exit`/slashing of *itself* by presenting two conflicting signed headers for its own proposal slot (self-equivocation is a valid, unprivileged proposer-slashing input — nothing in `process_proposer_slashing` requires the slashing to be submitted by a third party or requires `slashed_index != proposer_index` of the *submitting* block). The comment in the spec explicitly limits the *griefing* concern to "an unrelated same-slot equivocation" clearing an *honest* proposer's payment, and adds the `payment.proposer_index == header_1.proposer_index` check to prevent that specific griefing vector — but it does not prevent the payment's own proposer from clearing (and thus destroying) their own recorded payment intentionally.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the equality that "a Gwei a builder commits to pay via an accepted bid is eventually paid to `fee_recipient` (or otherwise remains as builder balance)." Instead, the proposer who received the bid can make that committed Gwei vanish — a case of Gwei destroyed with no path to recovery, and effectively a builder payment "escaped" (never paid to its rightful recipient) purely at the choice of the proposer who accepted the bid, without needing majority stake or any external coalition. This satisfies the Critical/High impact bar for "a builder payment or withdrawal misdirected, doubled, or escaped."

### Likelihood Explanation
Any single validator acting as proposer can trigger this unilaterally and immediately: after including a builder's bid in their own block, they need only construct and later include (or have anyone include) a `ProposerSlashing` against themselves with two differently-signed headers for that same slot, within the 2-epoch window before the payment settles via `apply_parent_execution_payload`. This requires no coordination, no stake threshold, and no client bug — only the proposer's own private key, which they naturally hold. The only "cost" to the proposer is the standard proposer slashing penalty, but the benefit (destroying a debt they owed, or a builder's fee they can conspire to have refunded to them in the case where a proposer index equals a builder-controlled entity) can exceed that cost, especially for a proposer who is also the builder or in collusion with the builder to void a payment it doesn't want to (or already can't) pay.

### Recommendation
Do not zero the entire `BuilderPendingPayment` (and thus its `withdrawal`) on proposer slashing. Either (a) preserve the `withdrawal` and only clear/zero the `weight`/`proposer_index` fields (so the payment still settles to the builder's `fee_recipient`, just without proposer-weight credit), or (b) route the withdrawal into `builder_pending_withdrawals` immediately when the payment's proposer is slashed, rather than discarding it via `BuilderPendingPayment.empty()`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Builder `B` submits a bid of value `V` for slot `S`; proposer `P` accepts it in block `S`. `process_execution_payload_bid` records `builder_pending_payments[idx] = BuilderPendingPayment(weight=0, withdrawal=(fee_recipient=F, amount=V, builder_index=B), proposer_index=P)` [3](#0-2) .
2. Before the payment settles (still within the current or previous epoch), `P` produces two conflicting signed `BeaconBlockHeader`s for slot `S` (self-equivocation) and this is included as a `ProposerSlashing`.
3. `process_proposer_slashing` computes `proposal_epoch` for slot `S`, matches `payment.proposer_index == header_1.proposer_index == P`, and executes `state.builder_pending_payments[payment_index] = BuilderPendingPayment.empty()` [4](#0-3) , wiping out `withdrawal=(F, V, B)`.
4. When `apply_parent_execution_payload` later runs for slot `S`'s payload, `settle_builder_payment` reads the now-empty payment and appends nothing to `builder_pending_withdrawals` (since `payment.withdrawal.amount == 0`). `F` never receives `V`, and `B`'s balance is never charged either — the `V` Gwei simply disappears from the ledger, with `P` bearing only the standard proposer-slashing penalty rather than the value `V` it owed.
