### Title
Builder pending withdrawals commit an execution-layer payment amount that exceeds the amount actually debited from the builder's beacon-state balance - (File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md)

### Summary
`get_builder_withdrawals` builds `Withdrawal.amount` from the raw, uncapped `builder_pending_withdrawals[i].amount` (the amount originally committed at bid time), but `apply_withdrawals` only debits `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` from `state.builders[builder_index].balance`. When a builder's balance is insufficient to cover its pending withdrawal (which is a normal, spec-anticipated case, since bids only reserve funds via `can_builder_cover_bid` at bid time, and balance can shrink afterward due to slashing/other withdrawals/multiple pending payments), the beacon state commits an execution-layer `Withdrawal` for the full (uncapped) amount while only reducing internal accounting by the smaller, capped amount. This mints Gwei that is paid out to `withdrawal.fee_recipient` on the execution layer without a matching debit anywhere in consensus-layer state.

### Finding Description
`get_builder_withdrawals` (specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md:1802-1834) copies `withdrawal.amount` unmodified from `state.builder_pending_withdrawals` into the `Withdrawal` object that becomes part of `payload_expected_withdrawals` and is committed to the execution layer: [1](#0-0) 

`apply_withdrawals` (specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md:1920-1932), however, caps the actual balance deduction to whatever the builder currently has: [2](#0-1) 

So the equality that must hold — "every Gwei sent to an execution-layer withdrawal address must be debited from exactly that much Gwei in the beacon state" — is broken whenever `withdrawal.amount > builder_balance`. The `Withdrawal` container is the sole channel that instructs the execution layer to actually transfer ETH to `address` (`withdrawal.fee_recipient`), i.e. it is a binding payment commitment analogous to the flash-loan repayment in the original report: the amount that must be "repaid" (paid out on the EL) doesn't match the amount that was actually accounted for/subtracted in the ledger (the beacon state builder balance).

This is confirmed by the project's own test suite, which explicitly documents and asserts this exact mismatch as expected behavior: [3](#0-2) 

The test sets `available_balance = 1 ETH` and `withdrawal_amount = 5 ETH`, then asserts:
- `withdrawal.amount == 5 ETH` (the requested/uncapped amount) is what's placed in `payload_expected_withdrawals` (this is what the execution layer will actually pay out to `fee_recipient`)
- `builders[0].balance == 0` (only 1 ETH was deducted)

That is a 4 ETH gap between what is committed to be paid out on the execution layer and what was subtracted from consensus-layer state — Gwei effectively created and paid to `fee_recipient`, a non-owner of that value, exactly mirroring the flash-loan report's core defect (amount used for repayment doesn't account for the shortfall/fee, so the two sides of the ledger stop being equal).

The root cause: `builder_pending_withdrawals` entries can be created (via `process_execution_payload_bid`, `settle_builder_payment`, and the "older than previous epoch" branch of `apply_parent_execution_payload`) for amounts that were valid against the builder's balance *at bid time*, but nothing re-validates or re-caps the amount at the time the pending withdrawal is converted into an EL-committing `Withdrawal` object in `get_builder_withdrawals`. The capping only happens later, silently, in `apply_withdrawals`, and by then the `Withdrawal.amount` value has already been fixed/committed via `payload_expected_withdrawals` (and ultimately the execution payload).

### Impact Explanation
This is a Critical-severity finding under the stated impact categories: "Gwei created, destroyed or paid to a non-owner" and "a payload executed or paid that the block did not commit to [accurately]." Concretely, the execution layer is instructed (via the committed `Withdrawal` list, which the EL is required to honor exactly) to pay out more ETH than was ever debited from the builder's consensus-layer balance. This breaks the total-supply invariant between the beacon chain and the execution layer — new ETH effectively appears out of nowhere and is paid to whatever `fee_recipient` address is recorded in the (attacker- or builder-controlled) `BuilderPendingWithdrawal.fee_recipient` field.

### Likelihood Explanation
The triggering condition — a builder's balance dropping below its pending withdrawal total — is reachable without any protocol violation or malicious peer:
- A builder can have multiple concurrent `builder_pending_payments`/`builder_pending_withdrawals` entries queued (each bid only checks `can_builder_cover_bid` against balance *at bid submission time*, not against amounts already reserved by other not-yet-applied withdrawals across all code paths, and balance can also decrease due to interleaved builder exits, other builder withdrawal requests, or fee/settlement flows).
- The "older than previous epoch" branch of `apply_parent_execution_payload` explicitly demonstrates a scenario (missed blocks across epochs) where a payment is settled late, well after the balance state may have moved.
- No coalition, malicious client, or economic-stake attack is required — this is a straightforward divergence discoverable purely by reasoning through the spec's arithmetic, and it is already exercised (and asserted as "correct" behavior) by the repo's own test, `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance`.

### Recommendation
Cap the withdrawal amount at withdrawal-construction time (in `get_builder_withdrawals`), not just at balance-application time, so that the amount actually committed to the execution layer never exceeds the builder's current balance:
```diff
 for withdrawal in state.builder_pending_withdrawals:
     ...
     builder_index = withdrawal.builder_index
+    amount = min(withdrawal.amount, get_builder_balance_available_for_withdrawal(state, builder_index))
     withdrawals.append(
         Withdrawal(
             index=withdrawal_index,
             validator_index=convert_builder_index_to_validator_index(builder_index),
             address=withdrawal.fee_recipient,
-            amount=withdrawal.amount,
+            amount=amount,
         )
     )
```
and ensure `apply_withdrawals` continues to subtract exactly that same (now already-capped) amount, so `Withdrawal.amount` and the actual balance decrement are always kept equal — closing the gap that currently allows Gwei to be paid out without a matching debit.

### Proof of Concept
This is directly reproduced by the existing spec test `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance` (tests/core/pyspec/eth_consensus_specs/test/gloas/block_processing/test_process_withdrawals.py:114-156), which sets up:
- `state.builders[0].balance = 1_000_000_000` Gwei (1 ETH)
- `state.builder_pending_withdrawals = [BuilderPendingWithdrawal(builder_index=0, amount=5_000_000_000)]` (5 ETH requested)

and asserts the post-state has:
- `payload_expected_withdrawals[0].amount == 5_000_000_000` (5 ETH — this is what the execution layer is committed to pay to the builder's `fee_recipient`)
- `state.builders[0].balance == 0` (only 1 ETH was ever debited) [3](#0-2) 

This demonstrates a 4 ETH (4_000_000_000 Gwei) discrepancy: value committed to be paid out on the execution layer with no corresponding debit anywhere in the beacon state, breaking the accounting equality that every Gwei paid out must be subtracted from exactly that much Gwei recorded in state.
