### Title
Borrower can DoS a full liquidation by front-running with a small partial repay, causing `ExhaustedLiability` / `HealthyAccount` reverts - (File: `programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_account/liquidate.rs`)

### Summary
`lending_account_liquidate` takes a fixed `asset_amount` (collateral quantity) chosen off-chain by the liquidator, and derives the liability amount to repay (`liab_amount_final`) from that fixed quantity and live oracle prices at execution time [1](#0-0) . Because the derived repay amount is computed against the liquidatee's on-chain state *as it exists when the transaction actually lands* rather than when the liquidator observed it, a borrower can front-run the liquidator's transaction with a small repayment of their own debt, or a repayment sized to make `liab_amount_final` exceed (or exactly exhaust) their remaining liability, or restore account health above zero, and cause the liquidator's transaction to revert.

### Finding Description
The liquidator selects `asset_amount` based on a snapshot of the liquidatee's collateral/liability, and the protocol computes:
- `liab_amount_liquidator` and `liab_amount_final` from `asset_amount * asset_price / liab_price` with fee discounts [2](#0-1) 
- The liquidatee's liability is then reduced by `liab_amount_final` via `liquidatee_liab_bank_account.repay(liab_amount_final)` [3](#0-2) .

After the accounting mutation, `check_post_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` enforces invariants against the liquidatee's *new* liability balance:
```
check!(!liability_balance.is_empty(BalanceSide::Liabilities), MarginfiError::ExhaustedLiability);
check!(liability_balance.is_empty(BalanceSide::Assets), MarginfiError::TooSeverePayoff);
...
check!(account_health <= I80F48::ZERO, MarginfiError::TooSevereLiquidation);
``` [4](#0-3) 

If a borrower front-runs the liquidator's transaction by repaying a small amount of the same liability just before the liquidation lands, the liquidatee's remaining liability shrinks. Since `liab_amount_final` is fixed by the liquidator's pre-computed `asset_amount` (not re-derived at execution against the now-smaller liability), the `repay()` call can now fully exhaust (or overshoot) the remaining liability, tripping `ExhaustedLiability`/`TooSeverePayoff`, or improve the account's health enough to no longer satisfy `account_health <= 0` (`TooSevereLiquidation`), or — earlier in the instruction — the pre-liquidation health check performed at the start of the instruction (via `check_pre_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health`, which gates on the account being unhealthy) can flip the account back to "healthy" and abort the whole liquidation with `HealthyAccount`. In every one of these cases the entire `lending_account_liquidate` instruction reverts, exactly matching the Venus DoS pattern: a borrower spending a small amount of gas/tokens to force any specific liquidator's carefully-sized liquidation transaction to fail, even though the account is still (or was still) unhealthy overall and eligible for liquidation with a differently-sized `asset_amount`.

This differs slightly from the raw Venus report (which is keyed off `repayAmount` vs `maxClose` computed from `borrowBalance`), but the root cause is identical: the instruction locks in a liquidator-supplied quantity based on stale liquidatee state, and validates hard-coded post-conditions against the liquidatee's live/mutated state at execution time, with no fallback to clamp/recompute the executed amount, so any last-second reduction of the liquidatee's debt causes an outright revert instead of a partial/adjusted liquidation.

### Impact Explanation
This is a liveness/availability issue rather than a direct fund-theft or accounting bug: it does not let the borrower steal funds or corrupt bank state, but it lets an unhealthy borrower repeatedly and cheaply frustrate specific liquidation attempts by front-running with minimal repayments, forcing liquidators to retry with re-sized `asset_amount`/oracle data, increasing gas costs and computational overhead for liquidators, and potentially delaying liquidation of accounts near insolvency thresholds (worsening bad debt exposure for the protocol during periods of price volatility). Because the guard rails (`ExhaustedLiability`, `TooSeverePayoff`, `TooSevereLiquidation`, `HealthyAccount`) all fail closed (abort) rather than gracefully clamping the repay amount, the impact is a full transaction revert rather than a partial/degraded liquidation.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires the borrower to detect an incoming liquidation transaction and front-run it — feasible on public mempools/leader schedules but mitigable by liquidators using private relays/direct validator submission, similar to the original Venus finding's own severity discussion. Marginfi's classic liquidation path also expects liquidators to size `asset_amount` conservatively (documentation recommends targeting ~70-80% of the unhealthy amount) [5](#0-4) , which somewhat reduces (but does not eliminate) the odds of exactly hitting `ExhaustedLiability`/`TooSeverePayoff`/`HealthyAccount` boundaries from a small front-run repay. The borrower must repeatedly spend gas/tokens to repay against every incoming liquidation attempt, limiting the practicality of sustained griefing, consistent with the "Medium" severity assigned to the analogous Venus finding.

### Recommendation
Consider recomputing (or clamping) the effective `asset_amount`/`liab_amount_final` against the liquidatee's live liability balance at execution time inside `lending_account_liquidate`, rather than failing outright when the liquidatee's debt has shrunk since the liquidator built the transaction. For example, cap `liab_amount_final` (and correspondingly `asset_amount`) to the liquidatee's current outstanding liability before calling `repay()`, and only revert if the account is already healthy (rather than reverting due to an amount mismatch caused by legitimate partial repayment). This mirrors the Venus recommendation of recalculating the final repay amount inside the liquidation hook instead of reverting on `repayAmount > maxClose`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower B has an unhealthy position with liability `L` in `liab_bank` and collateral in `asset_bank`.
2. Liquidator constructs a `lending_account_liquidate` transaction with `asset_amount = A`, computed off a snapshot of B's collateral/liability such that `liab_amount_final ≈ L` (targeting near-full liquidation) per the fee math in [2](#0-1) .
3. Before the liquidator's transaction lands, B submits (or has an accomplice submit) a `lending_account_repay` transaction repaying a small amount `ε` of `L` on `liab_bank`.
4. The liquidator's transaction now executes against `liability_balance = L - ε`. Since `liab_amount_final` (fixed from step 2) is applied via `repay(liab_amount_final)` [3](#0-2) , the resulting liability balance is empty or over-repaid, tripping `ExhaustedLiability`/`TooSeverePayoff` in `check_post_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` [6](#0-5) , or the account's health crosses back above zero, tripping the pre-liquidation `HealthyAccount` gate at the start of the instruction.
5. The entire liquidation transaction reverts, even though B's account may still require liquidation (e.g., due to other liability/collateral pairs, or because the liquidator could have succeeded with a smaller `asset_amount`).

Note: I was not able to directly view the pre-liquidation `HealthyAccount` check implementation and the `repay()` function body within the available index (only their call sites and error definitions were found), so the exact numeric boundary conditions under which each specific error (`ExhaustedLiability` vs `HealthyAccount` vs `TooSevereLiquidation`) triggers could not be fully confirmed from the indexed code; a Devin session with full repository access would be needed to trace `repay()` and `check_pre_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` line-by-line to pin down the exact revert path.

pub fn check_post_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health<'info>(
    marginfi_account: &MarginfiAccount,
    remaining_ais: &'info [AccountInfo<'info>],
    bank_pk: &Pubkey,
    pre_liquidation_health: I80F48,
) -> MarginfiResult<I80F48> {
    check!(
        !marginfi_account.get_flag(ACCOUNT_IN_FLASHLOAN),
        MarginfiError::AccountInFlashloan
    );

    let liability_balance = marginfi_account
        .lending_account
        .balances
        .iter()
        .find(|b| b.is_active() && b.bank_pk == *bank_pk)
        .ok_or(MarginfiError::LendingAccountBalanceNotFound)?;

    check!(
        !liability_balance.is_empty(BalanceSide::Liabilities),
        MarginfiError::ExhaustedLiability
    );

    check!(
        liability_balance.is_empty(BalanceSide::Assets),
        MarginfiError::TooSeverePayoff
    );

    let (assets, liabs) = get_health_components(
        marginfi_account,
        remaining_ais,
        RequirementType::Maintenance,
        &mut None,
        HealthPriceMode::Live { liq_cache: None },
    )?;

    let account_health = assets.checked_sub(liabs).ok_or_else(math_error!())?;

    check!(
        account_health <= I80F48::ZERO,
        MarginfiError::TooSevereLiquidation
    );

    if account_health <= pre_liquidation_health {
        msg!(
            "post_liquidation_health: {} ({} - {}), pre_liquidation_health: {}",
            account_health,
            assets,
            liabs,
            pre_liquidation_health
        );
        return err!(MarginfiError::WorseHealthPostLiquidation);
    };
```
