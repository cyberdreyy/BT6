### Maker-Controlled Fee Parameters in Destination Escrow - ([File: contracts/BaseEscrowFactory.sol])

### Summary
The `BaseEscrowFactory` allows a Maker to specify fee amounts and recipients for the destination chain within the `extraData` of a 1inch Limit Order. Because these parameters are provided by the user (Maker) and are not validated against a trusted source or oracle during the source-chain `_postInteraction`, a malicious Maker can set the `protocolFeeAmount` and `integratorFeeAmount` to zero or arbitrary values. These values are then baked into the `EscrowDst` contract's immutable state, leading to a loss of fees for the protocol and integrator upon swap completion.

### Finding Description
In `BaseEscrowFactory._postInteraction`, the contract extracts fee-related data from the `extraData` parameter, which is supplied by the Maker via the Limit Order Protocol's `postInteraction` mechanism [1](#0-0) . While the function calls `FeeTaker._getFeeAmounts` to calculate fee values, it relies on the fee configuration (percentages and recipients) passed within `extraData[:superArgsLength]` [2](#0-1) .

The calculated `protocolFeeAmount` and `integratorFeeAmount` are subsequently encoded into the `parameters` field of the `DstImmutablesComplement` [3](#0-2) . When the destination escrow is deployed via `createDstEscrow`, these values become part of the contract's immutable identity [4](#0-3) . 

Finally, in `EscrowDst._withdraw`, the contract transfers these user-defined fee amounts to the specified recipients [5](#0-4) . Since the Maker controls the input to `_getFeeAmounts` (specifically the fee percentages and the share ratio), they can manipulate the inputs to ensure the resulting `protocolFeeAmount` and `integratorFeeAmount` are significantly lower than intended by the protocol.

### Impact Explanation
This vulnerability leads to the theft or permanent loss of unclaimed fee-like value. A Maker can effectively bypass the protocol's intended fee structure by providing a malicious `extraData` payload that results in zero fees. This impacts both the `protocolFeeRecipient` and the `integratorFeeRecipient`, who will receive less than their allocated share of the swap value. According to the bounty scope, the theft or permanent loss of unclaimed fee-like value is classified as a **High** severity impact.

### Likelihood Explanation
The likelihood is **High** because the `extraData` is part of the off-chain order signed by the Maker. A Maker can easily craft a specialized order with modified fee parameters. Since the `BaseEscrowFactory` does not verify that the fee percentages in `extraData` match a global registry or a signed administrative message, there is no barrier to this exploitation.

### Recommendation
The protocol should avoid deriving fee amounts from user-supplied parameters in `extraData`. Instead:
1.  Store authorized fee configurations (percentages and recipients) in a factory-level registry managed by governance.
2.  Use the `taker` or `integrator` identity to look up the correct fee settings during `_postInteraction`.
3.  Alternatively, require a signature from a trusted "Fee Oracle" or the `integrator` themselves to validate the fee parameters passed in `extraData`.

### Proof of Concept
1.  A Maker creates a 1inch Limit Order.
2.  In the `postInteraction` field of the order extension, the Maker provides `extraData` where the bytes corresponding to `integrator fee percentage` and `protocol fee percentage` are set to `0`.
3.  The Taker (Resolver) fills the order.
4.  `BaseEscrowFactory._postInteraction` is called. `FeeTaker._getFeeAmounts` processes the Maker's `0` fee percentages and returns `protocolFeeAmount = 0` and `integratorFeeAmount = 0`.
5.  The `EscrowDst` is deployed with these zero-fee parameters in its immutables.
6.  Upon successful swap, `EscrowDst._withdraw` executes. Because the fee amounts are zero, the entire `takingAmount` is transferred to the Maker [6](#0-5) , and no fees are paid to the protocol or integrator.
