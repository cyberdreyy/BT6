### Title
Direct underlyings sent to `IdleCreditVault` are permanently locked with no rescue path - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
`IdleCreditVault` accepts underlying token transfers at its address (any ERC20 `transfer` to it), but exposes no sweep/rescue function and no accounting path that returns stray balance to its sender. The donation-skimming mitigation (`_skimDonatedAssets`) exists only on the CDO side (`IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:794-796`), which sweeps raw `token` balance to `feeReceiver`. `IdleCreditVault` has no equivalent; its internal accounting tracks strategy tokens, `pendingWithdraws`, `pendingInstantWithdraws`, and claim receipts — not the raw `token.balanceOf(address(this))`. Underlyings delivered directly to the vault (or left over after claims) sit there forever.

### Finding Description
The external bug class is "contract can accept value that no function can withdraw." On the idle-tranches side:

- `IdleCDOEpochVariant` is hardened against this: `_skimDonatedAssets()` (contracts/IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:794) forwards the raw underlying balance to `feeReceiver` at the top of `_deposit`, `requestWithdraw`, `startEpoch`, `stopEpoch`, `depositDuringEpoch`, `writeOffDeposit`, `updateAccounting`, and `finalizeDefault` (lines 647, 747, 242, 355, 679, 941, 159, 197). Nothing can strand tokens on the CDO.
- `IdleCreditVault` (contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol) holds the actual liquidity: it pulls interest/principal from the borrower via `sendInterestAndDeposits`/`onStopEpoch` flows, distributes to users through `claimWithdrawRequest`/`claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, and forwards surplus via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` — all of which move only *tracked* amounts. No function transfers out untracked `token` balance, and unlike `IdleCreditVaultWriteOffEscrow.emergencyWithdraw` (contracts/IdleCreditVaultWriteOffEscrow.sol:174) and `ProgrammableBorrower.rescueTokens` (contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol:602), the vault has no owner sweep.
- Therefore any underlying tokens transferred directly to `IdleCreditVault` — including a lender's mistaken `transfer` instead of `deposit`, or a repayment sent to the vault address rather than routed through the CDO — are permanently locked. The same applies to non-underlying ERC20s (e.g., the tranche tokens or strategy tokens themselves) sent to the vault.

### Impact Explanation
Permanent freezing of funds equal to the stray token balance of `IdleCreditVault`. A unprivileged actor (direct token sender) can demonstrate the lock by transferring underlyings to the vault and showing no function recovers them. Loss is the full donated amount; it is not socialized into NAV (the vault's share price derives from strategy-token accounting, not raw balance), so the tokens are neither recoverable nor productively absorbed.

### Likelihood Explanation
Low-to-medium likelihood, matching the source report's Medium severity: it requires an accidental direct transfer rather than an attack path, but the contract's address is exposed to lenders, fulfillers, and the borrower during normal operation, and the escrow/deployed-vault pattern invites direct repayment attempts. The absence of any sweep makes the lock permanent rather than merely inconvenient.

### Recommendation
Add an owner-gated sweep on `IdleCreditVault` that transfers out any underlying balance not owed to pending claims, plus arbitrary non-underlying tokens. To preserve the solvency invariant, the sweep for `token` should be capped at `token.balanceOf(this) - (pendingInstantWithdraws + pendingWithdraws + unclaimed receipt backing)` or restricted to tokens other than `token`, so recovery does not steal funds earmarked for claimants.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry fork PoC (sketch — verify exact helpers against `test/foundry/IdleCreditVault.t.sol`):

```solidity
// after pool setup with idleCDO.depositAA and an epoch started:
address vault = address(strategy);
uint256 donation = 1_000 * ONE_SCALE;
deal(defaultUnderlying, alice, donation);

vm.prank(alice);
IERC20Detailed(defaultUnderlying).transfer(vault, donation);

uint256 bal = IERC20Detailed(defaultUnderlying).balanceOf(vault);
assertGe(bal, donation);

// owner tries every public exit on the vault: none recovers the stray balance
vm.startPrank(owner);
vm.expectRevert(); // no rescue/sweep function exists
(bool ok,) = vault.call(abi.encodeWithSignature("rescueTokens(address,address,uint256)", defaultUnderlying, owner, donation));
assertFalse(ok);
// balance unchanged after claims/epoch stop; donation is permanently locked
assertEq(IERC20Detailed(defaultUnderlying).balanceOf(vault), bal);
```

The PoC demonstrates that the raw underlying balance at `IdleCreditVault` has no withdrawal path, while analogous contracts in the same system (`IdleCreditVaultWriteOffEscrow.emergencyWithdraw`, `ProgrammableBorrower.rescueTokens`, `IdleCDOEpochVariant._skimDonatedAssets`) all provide one.
