### Title
`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` reads the cross-epoch aggregate receipt balance and pays unfunded pre-default instant receipts at par, over-spending the default recovery reserve - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
The DCM bug is a scope/boundary error: the decoder loop reads more per iteration than the buffer holds, consuming bytes past the allocated region. The direct analog in `IdleCreditVault` is a claim path that reads a **wider accounting region than the one that was funded**: `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns and pays the entire aggregate `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` at par, while default finalization only haircuts the *current-epoch* instant bucket (`instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]`). Unfunded instant receipts recorded in earlier epochs are "outside the buffer" of the finalized claim basis but are still read and paid in full.

### Finding Description
`requestInstantWithdraw` records receipts both in the per-user aggregate `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` and per-epoch `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][currentEpoch]`, and adds the amount to the global `pendingInstantWithdraws` [1](#0-0) .

At default finalization, `defaultPendingClaimBasis()` only includes the *current* epoch's instant claims: `basis += instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` when `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` [2](#0-1) . Correspondingly, `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` only clears `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][defaultEpoch]` (a single epoch) before `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` falls through and pays the whole remaining aggregate `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` at par via `_transferFundedClaim` [3](#0-2)  and [4](#0-3) .

Like the DCM loop, the code indexes the enclosing region (the per-user aggregate) rather than the bounded region that was actually accounted for (the funded/current-epoch bucket). Any instant receipt whose `pendingInstantWithdraws` contribution was never funded and whose epoch is not `defaultRecoveryEpoch` escapes the recovery haircut and is paid 1:1.

### Impact Explanation
The over-paid portion is sourced from strategy-held underlying that was reserved for the recovery pool / other claimants (`_transferFundedClaim` explicitly avoids spending `defaultRecoveryReserve`, so it consumes funded/active-side underlying instead) [5](#0-4) . The attacker captures `(1 - defaultRecoveryPrice) × staleReceiptAmount` that should have been haircut and shared pro rata — direct theft from other recovery claimants and a breach of the one-receipt-one-priced-payout invariant.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an unfunded instant receipt surviving across an epoch boundary into a borrower default — possible because `pendingInstantWithdraws` is a global remainder not keyed by epoch, while the finalized basis and per-claim clearing only cover `epochNumber`/`defaultRecoveryEpoch`. The attacker is an ordinary tranche holder using only `requestInstantWithdraw` + `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`; sequencing depends on honest manager/borrower default calls, which the rules permit. Caveat: I was unable to fully verify within tool limits whether `startEpoch`/`getInstantWithdrawFunds` guarantees all prior-epoch instant receipts are always funded, which is the crux of reachability.

### Recommendation
In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, iterate/clear per-epoch instant receipts and pay only receipts that were actually funded at par; route any unfunded non-default-epoch instant receipts through `defaultRecoveryPrice` like `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` does, or revert claim until they are explicitly settled. Alternatively, include *all* outstanding instant receipts (not just `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]`) in `defaultPendingClaimBasis`.

### Proof of Concept
A Foundry fork PoC would: (1) deposit into the credit vault, (2) call `requestInstantWithdraw` for the attacker such that part remains in `pendingInstantWithdraws` unfunded across a `stopEpoch`/`startEpoch` boundary, (3) have the honest manager trigger borrower default and `finalizeDefaultRecovery`, (4) call `claimInstantWithdrawRequest(attacker)` and assert the attacker receives the stale receipt at par while `defaultRecoveryPrice < RECOVERY_FULL`, and the strategy's reserve/funded balance is drained by the excess. I could not complete end-to-end verification of step (2)'s reachability, so this finding should be treated as probable rather than confirmed.
