Based on my investigation, I found a genuine anomaly in `join_validator_set_internal` in `stake.move`, though I was unable to fully trace it to a conclusively provable high-impact exploit given tool budget constraints. I'll report what I found with explicit caveats about what remains unverified.

### Title
Unsynchronized pending_inactive→inactive settlement in `join_validator_set_internal` bypasses delegation pool's OLC accounting — ([File: aptos-move/framework/aptos-framework/sources/stake.move])

### Summary
`join_validator_set_internal` contains logic that directly merges a stake pool's `pending_inactive` coins into `inactive` when the lockup has expired, bypassing the normal `update_stake_pool` epoch-transition path. [1](#0-0) 
This function is reachable via the permissionless entry function `stake::join_validator_set(operator, pool_address)`, callable directly by whoever holds the `operator_address` role on a `StakePool` — including the operator of a `delegation_pool`-owned stake pool. [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
The `delegation_pool` module documents strict invariants requiring that the transition of `pending_inactive` stake into `inactive` stake is only ever "detected" through its own `synchronize_delegation_pool` process — triggered at the start of `add_stake`/`unlock`/`reactivate_stake`/`withdraw` — which then advances the `observed_lockup_cycle` (OLC) and isolates delegator shares accordingly: [3](#0-2) 

However, `join_validator_set_internal` performs the same `pending_inactive → inactive` state change directly on the raw `StakePool` resource, and is invoked through `stake::join_validator_set` — an entry point that a delegation-pool operator can call directly on the stake module without ever going through `delegation_pool::synchronize_delegation_pool`. The in-code comment itself acknowledges this is a special-case bypass: "An inactive validator's pending_inactive is never processed by `update_stake_pool`, so we must do it here." [4](#0-3) 

Because this mutation happens outside of `delegation_pool`'s synchronization, the delegation pool's cached `total_coins_inactive` and its `observed_lockup_cycle`/`inactive_shares` pool_u64 bookkeeping (used by `withdraw_internal`'s excess-stake reconciliation logic) can become stale relative to the true `stake_pool.inactive`/`pending_inactive` balances at the moment an operator rejoins the validator set after being inactive with an expired lockup. [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
If proven, this class of desynchronization between `stake.move`'s raw balances and `delegation_pool.move`'s OLC-based share accounting could corrupt withdrawal-eligibility bookkeeping for delegators sharing that pool (e.g., stake being marked withdrawable/inactive at the stake-pool layer while the delegation pool still treats it as belonging to an un-advanced OLC, or vice versa), potentially trapping or misattributing delegator claim rights.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger path (operator calling `stake::join_validator_set` after being inactive with an already-expired lockup) is permissionless and realistic for any delegation-pool operator to hit in normal operation, requiring no special privilege beyond the operator role they already legitimately hold.

### Recommendation
Have `delegation_pool` intercept the join path (or have `stake::join_validator_set_internal`'s settlement route through a common synchronization hook) so this pending_inactive→inactive transition is always reconciled against `delegation_pool`'s OLC/`total_coins_inactive` bookkeeping before it is allowed to occur silently at the stake-pool layer.

### Proof of Concept
I was not able to complete a fully worked, concrete numeric PoC within the remaining investigation budget — I could not locate/inspect the exact body of `synchronize_delegation_pool` (my search for its definition did not resolve within the available iterations) to confirm precisely how/when it recomputes `total_coins_inactive` versus the OLC-advance condition, which is necessary to prove a specific wrong balance or permanently stranded amount rather than a self-correcting inconsistency on the next synchronization call.

**Caveat / confidence statement:** This is a genuine code-level anomaly (a documented-bypass of the delegation pool's own stated accounting invariant) rather than a fully proven exploit chain. I could not conclusively verify within budget whether `synchronize_delegation_pool`'s next invocation (on the next `add_stake`/`unlock`/`reactivate_stake`/`withdraw`) fully self-heals this state or whether it produces a persistent misattribution of funds between delegators/operator. Given the strict evidentiary bar requested ("exact corrupted balance or role, and high or critical impact"), I flag this as a **candidate requiring further tracing** rather than a fully confirmed high/critical finding. A background engineering session with fuller tool access would be needed to trace `synchronize_delegation_pool`'s complete logic and construct a concrete before/after balance PoC.
