### Title
Already-claimed instant-withdraw receipts are recounted in the default-recovery basis and phantom reserve, enabling double payout and recovery insolvency - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
CVE-2019-19448 is a use-after-free where a merge operation treats the same structure as both left and right operands, so one object is freed/accounted for twice. The analog in `IdleCreditVault` is double accounting of a single instant-withdraw receipt: the per-epoch ledgers `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[user][epoch]` and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]` are only decremented in the defaulted-claim path, never when the receipt is paid normally. After `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pays a receipt at par, its epoch entry remains set. If the borrower later defaults in the same epoch while `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0`, `defaultPendingClaimBasis()` and `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve()` re-count the already-paid receipt, inflating both the recovery basis and `defaultRecoveryReserve` with tokens the strategy no longer holds. The same user can then be paid a second time through `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`.

### Finding Description
`requestInstantWithdraw` records the receipt in three places: `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][currentEpoch]`, and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[currentEpoch]` [1](#0-0) . On a normal claim, `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns and pays `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` and zeroes only that aggregate [2](#0-1)  — the two per-epoch mappings are not cleared. The per-epoch entries are cleared only inside `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` [3](#0-2) .

At default finalization, `defaultPendingClaimBasis()` adds `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` to the basis whenever `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` [4](#0-3) , and `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve()` adds `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber] - pendingInstantWithdraws` to `reserveAmount` without any token transfer [5](#0-4) . Because the stale epoch entry still counts an already-paid receipt, `reserveAmount` — and thus `defaultRecoveryPrice` and `defaultRecoveryReserve` at lines 686-693 — includes phantom underlying that was already sent to the claimant. Finally, `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` pays `claimBasis * defaultRecoveryPrice` on the stale per-epoch entry; a claimant who re-requests a small instant withdraw (so `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user] -= claimBasis` does not underflow) receives a second payout on the same receipt while the reserve is exhausted early, leaving later claimants unpaid.

Attack path (running epoch, unprivileged KYC'd lender via a tranche position):
1. User requests instant withdraw of amount A; per-epoch entries recorded.
2. Epoch progresses; the receipt is funded and `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pays A at par; the per-epoch mappings stay set.
3. Another party's instant request remains unfunded, keeping `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0`; borrower defaults in that same epoch and `finalizeDefault`/`finalizeDefaultRecovery` runs. The stale entry inflates `basis` and `prefundedReserve`.
4. User requests a new instant withdraw of ≥ A to satisfy the subtraction, then calls claim; `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` clears the stale `A` basis and pays `A * defaultRecoveryPrice` again — one receipt, two payouts — while the phantom reserve means the aggregate claims exceed the real underlying held.

### Impact Explanation
Double payout on a single receipt and phantom recovery reserve. First claimants (the attacker) withdraw at a `defaultRecoveryPrice` that assumes funds the strategy no longer holds; the strategy's actual underlying balance covers less than `defaultRecoveryReserve`, so later claimants' `_transferDefaultRecovery` calls revert on insufficient balance or are left with nothing — direct theft and insolvency for honest receipt holders, quantified by the stale already-claimed amount A.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a borrower default in the same epoch in which an instant receipt was already claimed and another instant receipt remains unfunded at finalization — a plausible but gated sequence (instant withdrawals enabled, partial funding, then default). The stale-ledger defect itself is unconditional: nothing clears the per-epoch entries on the normal claim path. Unprivileged users can trigger it around honest manager/guardian calls; no privileged collusion is needed.

### Recommendation
In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, when paying `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` at par, also clear the corresponding `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epoch]` entries and decrement `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]` for every funded epoch, or track funded vs unfunded per-epoch amounts explicitly. Alternatively, make `defaultPendingClaimBasis()`/`_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve()` use only still-unclaimed per-epoch balances, and add an invariant check that `defaultRecoveryReserve` never exceeds the strategy's actual `underlyingToken` balance.

### Proof of Concept
A Foundry fork test on `test/foundry/IdleCreditVault.t.sol` harness: enable instant withdrawals via `cdoEpoch.setInstantWithdrawParams`, deposit, `requestInstantWithdraw`, advance so the CDO funds and the user calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` (assert `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[user][epoch]` is still non-zero — this is the core defect); leave a second user's instant request unfunded; trigger `finalizeDefault`/`finalizeDefaultRecovery` and assert `defaultPendingClaimBasis()` and the computed `defaultRecoveryReserve` exceed the real claimable basis/underlying by the stale amount; then have the first user re-request and claim again to demonstrate the double payout. Exact reproduction depends on the CDO's instant-claim timing, which I could not fully trace within available iterations.

Caveat: I could not exhaustively verify every IdleCDOEpochVariant call path that touches these ledgers (e.g., whether any other CDO-side cleanup clears the per-epoch entries on funded instant claims). If such cleanup exists elsewhere, this reduces to phantom-basis dilution rather than direct double payout.
