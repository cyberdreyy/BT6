### Title
Permissionless repay during receivership/order execution lets anyone drain a delegated token account into an arbitrary marginfi account - (File: programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_account/repay.rs)

### Summary
`LendingAccountRepay` accepts an arbitrary, unchecked `signer_token_account` and uses `authority` (a bare `Signer`) as the SPL-token transfer authority. During receivership or order execution, the account-level constraint deliberately drops the requirement that `authority` be the target `marginfi_account`'s owner, per the module's own doc comment: "during receivership and order execution, there are no signer checks whatsoever: any key can repay as long as the invariants checked at the end of execution are met." [1](#0-0)  This mirrors the H-2 pattern: the function lets the caller name a payer address (`callee_`/`signer_token_account`) other than themselves, relying only on prior token-level approval rather than on consent to this specific action.

### Finding Description
`signer_token_account` is declared as an `UncheckedAccount` with only a comment noting that "Token mint/authority are checked at transfer" — there is no Anchor constraint tying it to `authority` or to the `marginfi_account`'s owner. [2](#0-1)  The actual transfer is performed via `bank.deposit_spl_transfer(...)`, passing `signer_token_account` as the source and `authority` as the SPL transfer authority. [3](#0-2)  For the CPI `Transfer`/`TransferChecked` to succeed, `authority` must be either the direct owner of `signer_token_account` or an SPL delegate previously approved via `Approve` on that token account.

Normally the `is_signer_authorized` constraint on `marginfi_account` restricts who `authority` can be, but the doc comment explicitly states this check is bypassed ("no signer checks whatsoever") whenever the target account is `ACCOUNT_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` or mid order-execution. [1](#0-0)  Combined with the unchecked `signer_token_account`, this means: any caller who happens to hold a delegate approval over some third party's token account (granted for an unrelated, legitimate purpose such as authorizing a keeper-executed limit order or flash-loan-style flow) can invoke `lending_account_repay` while any target `marginfi_account` is in receivership or order-execution mode, name themselves as `authority` (the approved delegate), and name the victim's token account as `signer_token_account`. This pulls the victim's tokens to repay debt on an account the victim has no relationship to, redirecting the victim's funds without their consent for that specific transaction — an analog of H-2's forced-payment-via-approval pattern.

The identical unrestricted-signer permissiveness note and pattern (`authority: Signer<'info>` with comments about permissionless usage during receivership/order execution) also appears in `LendingAccountWithdraw`, though there the risk is redirection of withdrawn assets to an attacker-controlled `destination_token_account` rather than pulling funds from a victim. [4](#0-3) 

### Impact Explanation
If exploited, any address holding an SPL delegate approval on a victim's token account (granted for a narrower purpose, e.g. as a limit-order keeper) can be forced into paying down debt for a completely unrelated `marginfi_account`, as long as that account is in receivership or executing an order. This is a direct, uncompensated transfer of value out of the victim's token account with no on-chain consent check for that specific action — matching the "forced payment via approval" impact of the referenced report.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability depends on a delegate approval existing on some victim token account for a would-be attacker, and on the ability to time the call while the targeted `marginfi_account` is in receivership or executing an order (both permissionless windows per the codebase's own design). Given that keeper-driven flows (limit orders, receivership liquidation) are designed to let arbitrary signers execute repay/withdraw instructions on behalf of a marginfi account, and that `signer_token_account` has no ownership binding, the precondition (some address becoming an approved SPL delegate) is realistic in normal keeper/bot usage patterns, making this a credible, not purely theoretical, risk.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit constraint tying `signer_token_account`'s owner to `authority`, or verify `authority` is the actual owner of the marginfi_account (except in the receivership/order-execution flows, where the delegated token account should instead be constrained to belong to the keeper/executor's own known token account, never an arbitrary caller-supplied one). At minimum, require `signer_token_account.owner == authority.key()` unless `authority` is the marginfi_account's registered authority, closing off the ability to pull funds from arbitrary delegate-approved accounts.

### Proof of Concept
1. Victim Alice owns token account `T`, and approves address `Bob` as an SPL delegate over `T` for the purpose of running her limit-order keeper flow on her own marginfi account.
2. Bob observes some unrelated marginfi account `M` (belonging to Carol) currently `ACCOUNT_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` or mid order-execution.
3. Bob calls `lending_account_repay` with `marginfi_account = M`, `authority = Bob` (signer), `signer_token_account = T` (Alice's account).
4. Because `M` is in receivership/order-execution, the `is_signer_authorized`/frozen checks impose no restriction on `authority` being Carol's account owner. [5](#0-4) 
5. `bank.deposit_spl_transfer` executes an SPL transfer from `T` (Alice's account) to the bank's liquidity vault, authorized by Bob's delegate approval, repaying Carol's debt with Alice's funds — without Alice's knowledge or consent for this specific transaction. [3](#0-2)
