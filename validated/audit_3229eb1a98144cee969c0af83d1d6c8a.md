### Title
Builder payment commitments can be silently underpaid when builder balance is drained before settlement - ([File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md])

### Summary
Gloas (EIP-7732) introduces a builder-payment pipeline (`process_execution_payload_bid` → `builder_pending_payments` → `process_builder_pending_payments`/`settle_builder_payment` → `builder_pending_withdrawals` → `apply_withdrawals`) that commits to paying a `fee_recipient` a fixed `Gwei` amount for winning a block's payload auction. Nothing in this pipeline enforces that the builder's `balance` remains sufficient to cover all outstanding commitments once they are queued. When the amount is finally applied, `apply_withdrawals` silently caps the payout at `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` instead of asserting/reverting, so if the builder's balance has been reduced in the interim (e.g. by its own voluntary exit sweep paying the *builder itself*, or by further commitments consuming the same balance), the `fee_recipient` receives less than the bid amount the protocol/attesters approved — and the shortfall is neither restored nor tracked anywhere. This is the direct analog of the Unlock report: a party who is owed a payment/refund from a shared balance can be starved of it because another actor (here, the builder itself, acting through legitimate protocol operations) is permitted to draw down that same balance first, and the protocol has no reserve/accounting mechanism to guarantee the commitment.

### Finding Description
A builder's payment commitment is created without checking future balance sufficiency and is honored later with a `min()` cap:

- `process_execution_payload_bid` records the bid value into `state.builder_pending_payments` without deducting from `builder.balance` at commit time (confirmed by test assertion "Verify builder balance is still the same" in `test_process_execution_payload_bid.py`). [1](#0-0) 
- After up to two epochs, `process_builder_pending_payments` moves qualifying payments into `state.builder_pending_withdrawals` (a fixed-amount promise to `fee_recipient`), still without re-checking balance. [2](#0-1) 
- `settle_builder_payment` does the same for the same-epoch/previous-epoch fast path. [3](#0-2) 
- Separately, a builder can voluntarily exit via `initiate_builder_exit`, which schedules a full-balance sweep withdrawal to the *builder's own* `execution_address` once `withdrawable_epoch <= current_epoch`. [4](#0-3) 
- When withdrawals are finally applied, builder payments are capped by currently available balance rather than asserted to be fully payable:
```python
if is_builder_index(withdrawal.validator_index):
    builder_index = convert_validator_index_to_builder_index(withdrawal.validator_index)
    builder_balance = state.builders[builder_index].balance
    state.builders[builder_index].balance -= min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)
``` [5](#0-4) 

Because `MAX_WITHDRAWALS_PER_PAYLOAD` (16) limits how many withdrawals — including builder pending withdrawals — can be processed per block, and builder pending withdrawals additionally share their slot budget with pending partial/sweep withdrawals (capped at `MAX_WITHDRAWALS_PER_PAYLOAD - 1` combined), a backlog of `builder_pending_withdrawals` for the same builder can persist across multiple blocks/slots before being fully drained. [6](#0-5) 

During that backlog window, the builder is free to (a) accumulate further payment commitments via new bids (no invariant ties `builder.balance` to the sum of all outstanding `builder_pending_payments` + `builder_pending_withdrawals`), or (b) reach its own `withdrawable_epoch` and have its full remaining balance swept to itself. In either case, when the already-queued `builder_pending_withdrawals` entries are eventually applied, `builder_balance` may be less than `withdrawal.amount`, and the `min()` cap silently pays the `fee_recipient` less than the committed bid value. The shortfall is dropped: it is not credited back to the builder, not requeued, and not recorded — it is simply lost, exactly like the Unlock case where the lock manager can withdraw funds a key-buyer is protocol-guaranteed a refund from, except here there is no even revert/failure signal — the underpayment succeeds silently.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the guarantee that "a builder payment or withdrawal misdirected, doubled or escaped" (High-severity criterion): a payment that the block/attesters committed to (the winning bid value owed to `fee_recipient`) can partially escape payment due to actions entirely within the builder's own control (self-directed exit sweep, or simply issuing further bids), without any node/consensus-level enforcement that the builder maintain sufficient reserve. The `fee_recipient` (who may be a proposer or other third party who has no control over the builder's balance) is the harmed, unprivileged party — analogous to the key-buyer in the Unlock report who could not get a refund because the lock manager (an independent, self-interested party) drained the shared balance first.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires (1) at least one `builder_pending_withdrawals` entry to remain queued across a block boundary (plausible given the shared `MAX_WITHDRAWALS_PER_PAYLOAD - 1` cap and 2-epoch payment-settlement delay), and (2) the builder to reduce its own balance via a legitimate voluntary-exit sweep or by having previously over-committed via bids in excess of retained balance. No malicious peer, coalition, or client bug is required — only a rational/self-interested (or careless) builder acting within otherwise-valid protocol operations, which mirrors the "even if not malicious" framing in the original Unlock report.

### Recommendation
Enforce a reserve invariant so a builder cannot exit (or cannot have its exit sweep executed) while it has outstanding commitments: before scheduling/executing a full-balance builder sweep withdrawal, subtract the sum of all `builder.balance`-referencing `builder_pending_payments` and unsettled `builder_pending_withdrawals` for that builder, or refuse new commitments/bids that would push total committed obligations above `builder.balance`. Alternatively, remove the `min()` truncation in `apply_withdrawals` and instead assert `builder_balance >= withdrawal.amount` at settlement time (or requeue the unpaid remainder rather than discarding it), so a payment shortfall is visible/handled rather than silently absorbed.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual sequence (constrained by not being able to fully trace `process_execution_payload_bid`'s bid-acceptance balance check or `process_builder_exit_request` in this final pass — noted as an open verification item):
1. Builder B wins block N's auction with bid value V to `fee_recipient` F; `builder.balance = V` at that time (just enough).
2. The payment is queued in `builder_pending_payments`, then after settlement moved into `builder_pending_withdrawals` for B → F, amount V. No balance is deducted yet. [3](#0-2) 
3. Before this withdrawal is applied (it may be queued behind `MAX_WITHDRAWALS_PER_PAYLOAD - 1` limit alongside other withdrawal types), builder B submits `process_builder_exit_request`, setting `withdrawable_epoch`. [4](#0-3) 
4. Once `withdrawable_epoch <= current_epoch`, the builder sweep withdrawal pays B's *full current balance* (still V, since the F-owed withdrawal hasn't applied yet) to B's own `execution_address`, per `get_builders_sweep_withdrawals` which withdraws `builder.balance` in full. This happens for builders processed in the sweep before B's queued `builder_pending_withdrawals` entry to F is applied within the ordering/limits of a given block, or in an earlier block due to backlog.
5. When B's queued withdrawal to F is finally applied, `builder_balance = 0`, so `min(V, 0) = 0` is paid to F: [7](#0-6) 
   `state.builders[builder_index].balance -= min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)`
6. F receives nothing despite the protocol having committed (via `builder_pending_payments`/`builder_pending_withdrawals`) to pay V; the shortfall is not recovered anywhere in state.

Note: full confirmation that `process_execution_payload_bid` and `process_builder_exit_request` do not themselves block this ordering (e.g. via an explicit "no pending withdrawals" guard, similar to the pattern seen in `process_withdrawal_request`'s `pending_balance_to_withdraw == 0` check for validator full exits) could not be completed in the available searches and should be verified directly against `specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md`'s exit-request and bid-processing functions.

```python
def process_builder_pending_payments(state: BeaconState) -> None:
    """
    Processes the builder pending payments from the previous epoch.
    """
    quorum = get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold(state)
    for payment in state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH]:
        if payment.weight >= quorum:
            state.builder_pending_withdrawals.append(payment.withdrawal)

    old_payments = state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:]
    state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH] = old_payments
    new_payments = [BuilderPendingPayment.empty() for _ in range(SLOTS_PER_EPOCH)]
    state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:] = new_payments
