### Title
`_calcInterestWithdrawRequest` prorates epoch interest with mismatched bases — numerator at inflated virtual price, denominator at stale `lastNAV` — overpaying interest to early withdrawers and draining the epoch interest pool - ([File: contracts/IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol])

### Summary

Analogous to the GMX funding issue — where a per-size rate was scaled by the size of the larger side instead of by the quantity it actually applies to — `IdleCDOEpochVariant._calcInterestWithdrawRequest` computes each withdrawer's interest as `_amount * totTrancheInterest / _trancheBal`, where `_amount` is the requester's underlyings valued at the **current (interest-accrued) tranche price** while `_trancheBal` is the tranche's **last saved NAV**, which excludes interest accrued since the last accounting update. Whenever a tranche's price is above 1 (i.e., mid-epoch after yield accrual, the normal state), every withdraw request is paid interest scaled by the appreciated value rather than by its share of the interest base, so the sum of paid-out interest deterministically exceeds `totTrancheInterest`. The excess is absorbed by the shared epoch accounting (`expectedEpochInterest`, `interestForOverUnderPerformance`, loss/recovery basis) and is ultimately borne by remaining holders and late claimers.

### Finding Description

In `requestWithdraw` (running epoch, non-instant path), the user's receipt is fixed at request time:

- `IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:756`: `_underlyings = _trancheToUnderlyings(_amount, _tranche)` — converts tranche tokens using `_tranchePrice`, which reflects accrued interest (price > `oneToken` after yield accrues).
- `IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:773`: calls `_calcInterestWithdrawRequest(_underlyings, _tranche)`.
- `IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:865–870`:

```solidity
uint256 totInterest = _calcInterest(_managedContractValue()) * _duration / (_duration + _buffer);
uint256 totTrancheInterest = _calcTrancheInterestShare(totInterest, _tranche);
uint256 _trancheBal = _lastSavedNAV(_tranche);
_interest = _trancheBal == 0 ? 0 : _amount * totTrancheInterest / _trancheBal;
```

`_amount` here is the requester's underlyings at virtual price (principal **plus** already-accrued interest), while `_trancheBal` = `lastNAVAA`/`lastNAVBB` (principal-only basis, since NAV is updated with principal in `_withdrawOps` at `IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:790` and only catches up to accrued yield at the next `_updateAccounting`). If `priceAA = p > 1`, the aggregate paid interest becomes `totTrancheInterest * p` — inflated by exactly the tranche's appreciation factor, the direct analog of scaling funding by `sizeOfLargerSide` instead of the applicable diff/base.

The inflated receipt (`principal + interest - totalFees`, `IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:776`) is locked into `creditVault.requestWithdraw` and `withdrawsRequestsByEpoch`, while `_diff` is pushed into `interestForOverUnderPerformance` (`IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:784`), which adjusts `expectedEpochInterest` at `startEpoch`/`stopEpoch`. Because each request over-draws the interest bucket by factor `p`, the residual `expectedEpochInterest` earmarked for remaining holders and pending claims is too low: at epoch settlement the contract commits more than the borrower actually pays, so the last claimers (`claimWithdrawRequest`/`claimInstantWithdrawRequest`) or residual tranche NAV absorb the shortfall. In a default/finalizeDefault path the inflated pending basis (`defaultPendingClaimBasis`) also dilutes `defaultRecoveryPrice`/`lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` for honest claimers.

### Impact Explanation

Direct economic loss to remaining tranche holders and later withdrawal claimers: for a tranche appreciated to price `p`, each withdrawal receipt over-books interest by `(p - 1) * totTrancheInterest * share`. With, e.g., 5% accrued appreciation, a withdrawer of half the AA tranche over-claims ~2.5% of the epoch's total AA interest; repeated requests compound the drain. The shortfall manifests as either (a) insolvency at claim time — receipts promised in `withdrawsRequestsByEpoch` exceed funded interest, leaving last claimers unable to be paid in full (permanent loss / temporary freezing until new funds arrive), or (b) diluted recovery distribution after `finalizeDefault`, where inflated pending basis steals recovery share from other claimers. No privileged misbehavior, donation, or oracle manipulation is required — any KYC'd tranche holder calling `requestWithdraw` mid-epoch triggers the over-allocation, and honest users are harmed as a side effect even without an intentional attacker.

### Likelihood Explanation

High in normal operation: tranche price is above 1 for essentially the entire running epoch once yield accrues, and `requestWithdraw` with `allowAAWithdrawRequest`/`allowBBWithdrawRequest` enabled is a permissionless user flow. The over-payment scales linearly with accrued appreciation and withdrawn share, so it is largest precisely when requests cluster late in the epoch — exactly when `expectedEpochInterest` is committed to claims. Existing guards do not stop it: `_skimDonatedAssets` only sweeps raw token balance, `_updateAccounting` checkpoints NAV but the `_trancheBal`/`_amount` base mismatch is reintroduced on every subsequent accrual, and `interestForOverUnderPerformance` merely records the imbalance rather than correcting the base.

### Recommendation

Use a consistent base for the proration. Either compute the numerator on the same saved-NAV basis as the denominator, e.g.:

```solidity
uint256 requestBasis = _amount * _lastSavedNAV(_tranche) / _trancheToUnderlyings(_trancheSupply(_tranche), _tranche);
_interest = _trancheBal == 0 ? 0 : requestBasis * totTrancheInterest / _trancheBal;
```

or, equivalently, compute the denominator at virtual price (`_virtualBalance` instead of `_lastSavedNAV`) so that aggregate paid interest equals `totTrancheInterest`. Then re-derive `_diff`/`interestForOverUnderPerformance` and `expectedEpochInterest` from the corrected per-request interest, and add an invariant test that the sum of `_interest` over a full-tranche withdrawal equals `totTrancheInterest` for arbitrary `priceAA`/`priceBB`.

```solidity
    // assert booked receipt interest > share * totTrancheInterest by factor (priceAA/1e18)
    // assert sum of full-tranche requests' interest > totTrancheInterest
    // warp past epochEndDate + buffer, stopEpoch, claim -> last claimer shortfall

```

Expected assertion: `bookedInterest ≈ totTrancheInterest * share * priceAA / ONE_TRANCHE_TOKEN`, i.e., inflated by `priceAA - 1`, and aggregate interest committed in `withdrawsRequestsByEpoch` exceeds the interest the borrower actually pays, producing a shortfall for the final claimer.

Note: I was unable to fully trace `_managedContractValue()` (whether it includes pending-withdraw principal still in the lending pool) within the iteration limit; if it already nets out pending principal, the inflation is partially offset, but the numerator/denominator basis mismatch (`_amount` at virtual price vs `_trancheBal` at saved NAV) remains a deterministic over-allocation of `totTrancheInterest` regardless.
