### Title
Owner-controlled staking pool can misreport balance to `refresh_staking_pool_balance`, inflating `known_deposited_balance` and releasing locked/unvested tokens early - (File: `lockup/src/owner_callbacks.rs`, `lockup/src/getters.rs`)

### Summary
The lockup contract's owner-liquid-balance calculation depends on `known_deposited_balance`, a value that is simply overwritten with whatever number a delegated staking pool reports via a cross-contract callback, with no cross-check against the actual amount deposited/staked or against independently verifiable on-chain state. This mirrors the reported analog bug class: two related pieces of data (the amount actually held/staked vs. the balance claimed back by the counter-party) are combined and trusted without validating they match.

### Finding Description
`LockupContract::refresh_staking_pool_balance` asks the currently-selected staking pool for `get_account_total_balance` and, in the callback `on_get_account_total_balance` (`lockup/src/owner_callbacks.rs`), directly assigns the returned value to `staking_information.deposit_amount` (`known_deposited_balance`) without validating it against the amount the lockup contract itself actually transferred/staked into that pool. `get_owners_balance`/`get_liquid_owners_balance` (`lockup/src/getters.rs`) then compute the owner's withdrawable/transferable balance as a function of `known_deposited_balance` minus `get_locked_amount()`.

Because staking pool selection only requires the target account to be present in the `whitelist` contract (`lockup/src/owner.rs::select_staking_pool` → `ext_whitelist::is_whitelisted`), and the `staking-pool-factory` **automatically and permissionlessly whitelists any pool it deploys** (`staking-pool-factory/src/lib.rs::on_staking_pool_create` calls `ext_whitelist::add_staking_pool` with no foundation involvement once the factory itself is whitelisted), any lockup owner can deploy their own pool through the factory, become its owner, and have it pass the whitelist check with no privileged party in the loop for that specific pool.

Once selected, the owner deposits/stakes tokens into their own pool, then calls `refresh_staking_pool_balance`. Since the owner controls the pool's contract logic/state (it is a contract they deployed and own), they can make `get_account_total_balance` return an amount larger than what was ever actually deposited or earned as reward. The lockup contract accepts this number unconditionally as `known_deposited_balance`.

### Impact Explanation
`known_deposited_balance` directly feeds `get_owners_balance()`/`get_liquid_owners_balance()`, which gate `transfer()` and other owner-only fund-moving operations. Inflating this value lets the owner claim and move out tokens that are still supposed to be locked/unvested per the lockup or vesting schedule enforced by `get_locked_amount()` — i.e., locked or unvested tokens are released early. This is a Critical-severity accounting break: the recorded claim (`known_deposited_balance`) diverges from the NEAR actually held/staked, and the foundation/other stakeholders relying on the vesting schedule settle on the inflated number.

### Likelihood Explanation
The only "privilege" required is being the owner of one's own lockup contract (a normal, unprivileged role over one's own vesting agreement) and deploying a pool through the already-whitelisted, permissionless `staking-pool-factory`. No foundation action, redeploy of core contracts, or third-party compromise is needed for the specific malicious pool to become trusted, since factory-created pools self-whitelist. This makes the path realistically reachable by any lockup/vesting beneficiary who wants to defeat their own lockup schedule.

### Recommendation
Do not let a single self-reported number silently replace `known_deposited_balance`. Validate the staking pool's reported total balance against expected bounds derived from the lockup contract's own deposit/stake history (e.g., reject decreases beyond documented unstake/withdraw flows, and require monotonic, rate-limited growth consistent with plausible staking rewards), or require an owner-agnostic/permissioned re-whitelisting step for factory-created pools so a beneficiary cannot unilaterally introduce a trusted-but-malicious pool for their own contract.

### Proof of Concept
1. Foundation whitelists `staking-pool-factory` (done once, as in normal deployment).
2. Attacker (a lockup contract owner) calls `staking-pool-factory::create_staking_pool` to deploy `my-pool`, becoming its owner; the factory auto-calls `whitelist::add_staking_pool("my-pool")`, whitelisting it with no additional foundation step (`staking-pool-factory/src/lib.rs` lines 197-239).
3. From their lockup contract, the attacker calls `select_staking_pool("my-pool")`; `on_whitelist_is_whitelisted` accepts it since it is whitelisted (`lockup/src/owner_callbacks.rs`).
4. Attacker calls `deposit_and_stake` to move some real locked tokens into `my-pool`.
5. Attacker (as pool owner) modifies/deploys `my-pool` logic so `get_account_total_balance` returns an inflated number.
6. Attacker calls `refresh_staking_pool_balance()`; the lockup contract's `on_get_account_total_balance` callback sets `known_deposited_balance` to the inflated value unconditionally.
7. Attacker calls `get_owners_balance()`/`transfer()`; the inflated `known_deposited_balance` yields a liquid balance exceeding what is truly vested/unlocked, letting the attacker withdraw locked/unvested tokens early.
