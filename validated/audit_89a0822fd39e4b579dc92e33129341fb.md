I have sufficient evidence to complete the analysis.### Title
Unstake withdrawal debits internal ledger before confirming the NEAR transfer succeeded, letting a failed transfer be silently absorbed as reward - (File: staking-pool/src/internal.rs)

### Summary
`StakingContract::internal_withdraw` decrements the delegator's `unstaked` balance and the contract's `last_total_balance` and then fires a bare `Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount)` with no `.then()` callback checking the transfer's outcome. [1](#0-0)  This is the same bug class as the External Report's `RelayPolymarketSDK::transferUsdc()`: the ledger is mutated to reflect a successful transfer while the actual asset movement is never verified.

### Finding Description
`withdraw` / `withdraw_all` call `internal_withdraw`, which:
1. Asserts sufficient `unstaked` balance and unlock epoch,
2. Immediately subtracts `amount` from `account.unstaked` and saves the account,
3. Subtracts `amount` from `self.last_total_balance`,
4. Only then schedules `Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount)` — with no callback to confirm success. [1](#0-0)  Compare this to the staking/restaking path in the same contract, which does register a callback (`on_stake_action`) that checks `env::promise_result(0)` and reacts if the promise failed. [2](#0-1)  No equivalent verification exists for `withdraw`.

The relevant custody binding that should hold is:
`sum(account.unstaked across delegators) + total_staked_balance ≈ last_total_balance ≈ actual NEAR held by the contract`

If the outbound `Transfer` action fails (e.g., the destination account no longer exists at the time the receipt executes, which can legitimately happen if the delegator deletes their own account or is deleted for storage-cost failure after submitting the withdraw transaction but before the transfer receipt is applied), NEAR runtime returns the failed transfer's deposit to the sender (the staking pool contract itself), not to the intended delegator. Meanwhile:
- `account.unstaked` has already been zeroed/decremented,
- `last_total_balance` has already been decremented by `amount`,
- but the actual contract balance did **not** decrease (transfer failed and refunded to the contract).

The next call to `internal_ping` computes `reward = current_account_balance - last_total_balance`, treating this now-unaccounted-for surplus as staking reward and distributing a cut to the owner and the rest as additional "stake" share value for all remaining delegators. [3](#0-2) 

### Impact Explanation
This produces exactly the pattern flagged in the report: the ledger records the withdrawal as completed and drops the delegator's claim, while the actual value was never delivered — and the invariant `claims recorded == assets held` breaks. The value is not burned; it is redistributed to the owner and other delegators via the reward-fee/share-price mechanism, meaning "rewards or fees mis-attributed" (a High-impact class per the scope) at the expense of the delegator whose withdrawal transfer failed. There is no compensating logic anywhere in `staking-pool/src/*` to detect the failed transfer and restore the delegator's `unstaked` balance.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Low, matching the analogous finding's own rated likelihood, because it requires an unusual sequence (destination/delegator account becoming non-existent between submitting `withdraw` and the transfer receipt executing) — but it is externally triggerable without any privileged role and needs no cooperation from the contract owner, matching the "unprivileged attacker" requirement.

### Recommendation
Wrap the outbound transfer in `internal_withdraw` with a `.then()` callback (mirroring `on_stake_action`) that checks `is_promise_success()`/`env::promise_result(0)`. On failure, restore `account.unstaked` and `self.last_total_balance` to their pre-withdrawal values instead of leaving the ledger permanently understated relative to the real held balance.

### Proof of Concept
1. Delegator deposits and later unstakes and waits out the unbonding period so `unstaked_available_epoch_height` has passed.
2. Delegator calls `withdraw_all` (or `withdraw`); `internal_withdraw` immediately sets `account.unstaked -= amount` and `self.last_total_balance -= amount`, then schedules `Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount)`. [4](#0-3) 
3. Before that transfer receipt executes, the delegator's account ceases to exist (e.g., deleted, or fails to meet minimum balance requirements) — the transfer receipt fails and the deposit is refunded back to the staking pool contract's balance rather than delivered.
4. The delegator's `unstaked` balance is already zero and cannot be reclaimed; `last_total_balance` is understated relative to the contract's real balance.
5. On the next `ping()` (called implicitly before other delegator actions), the contract computes the difference between its real balance and `last_total_balance` as "reward," which is distributed as owner fee and increased share price to all other delegators, i.e., the lost withdrawal is absorbed as reward for other parties rather than reverted or returned to the original delegator.

    pub(crate) fn internal_withdraw(&mut self, amount: Balance) {
        assert!(amount > 0, "Withdrawal amount should be positive");

        let account_id = env::predecessor_account_id();
        let mut account = self.internal_get_account(&account_id);
        assert!(
            account.unstaked >= amount,
            "Not enough unstaked balance to withdraw"
        );
        assert!(
            account.unstaked_available_epoch_height <= env::epoch_height(),
            "The unstaked balance is not yet available due to unstaking delay"
        );
        account.unstaked -= amount;
        self.internal_save_account(&account_id, &account);

        env::log(
            format!(
                "@{} withdrawing {}. New unstaked balance is {}",
                account_id, amount, account.unstaked
            )
            .as_bytes(),
        );

        Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount);
        self.last_total_balance -= amount;
    }

### Reward distribution

Before every action the contract calls method `internal_ping`.
This method distributes rewards towards active delegators when the blockchain epoch switches.
The rewards might be given due to staking and also because the contract earns gas fee rebates for every function call.
Note, the if someone accidentally (or intentionally) transfers tokens to the contract (without function call), then
tokens from the transfer will be distributed to the active stake participants of the contract in the next epoch.
Note, in a rare scenario, where the owner withdraws tokens and while the call is being processed deletes their account, the
withdraw transfer will fail and the tokens will be returned to the staking pool. These tokens will also be distributed as
a reward in the next epoch.

The method first checks that the current epoch is different from the last epoch, and if it's not changed exits the method.

The reward are computed the following way. The contract keeps track of the last known total account balance.
This balance consist of the initial contract balance, and all delegator account balances (including the owner) and all accumulated rewards.
(Validation rewards are added automatically at the beginning of the epoch, while contract execution gas rebates are added after each transaction)

When the method is called the contract uses the current total account balance (without attached deposit) and the subtracts the last total account balance.
The difference is the total reward that has to be distributed.

The fraction of the reward is awarded to the contract owner. The fraction is configurable by the owner, but can't exceed 100%.
Note, that it might be unfair for the participants of the pool if the owner changes reward fee. But this owner will lose trust of the
participants and it will lose future revenue in the long term. This should be enough to prevent owner from abusing reward fee.
It could also be the case that they could change the reward fee to make their pool more attractive.

The remaining part of the reward is added to the total staked balance. This action increases the price of each "stake" share without
changing the amount of "stake" shares owned by different accounts. Which is effectively distributing the reward based on the number of shares.

The owner's reward is converted into "stake" shares at the new price and added to the owner's account.
It's done similarly to `stake` method but without debiting the unstaked balance of owner's account.

Once the rewards are distributed the contract remembers the new total balance.
