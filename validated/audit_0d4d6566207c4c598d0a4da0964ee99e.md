### Title
`seizeWithMemo` permanently traps compliance-seized funds if the seize destination is ever gated out by the `SeizeReceiver` policy - (File: `crates/common/precompiles/src/b20_stablecoin/logic/v2.rs`)

### Summary
The B-20 stablecoin/asset precompile's `seizeWithMemo` operation — the only privileged mechanism for moving balance out of a blocked/sanctioned account — gates its destination address (`to`) through the `SeizeReceiver` policy before moving the balance. If that policy ever denies the configured seize destination (e.g., the destination account is later added to a blocklist policy wired to `SeizeReceiver`, or an `ALLOWLIST`/`ALWAYS_BLOCK` policy is (re)wired to that scope), every future `seizeWithMemo` call reverts, and the tokens held by the blocked `from` account can never be moved out, since `seize` is the sole extraction path for a blocked account's balance.

### Finding Description
`seize_with_memo` in `StablecoinV2`/`AssetV2` requires the caller to hold `Seize` role, validates `from`/`to` are non-zero and distinct, confirms `from` is seizable (i.e., blocked under some other policy), and then enforces: [1](#0-0) 

`B20Guards::ensure_policy_type(token, B20PolicyType::SeizeReceiver, to)` performs the same kind of authorization check used for ordinary transfer/mint receivers: [2](#0-1) 

This mirrors the mint-receiver gating pattern: [3](#0-2) 

Unlike ordinary `transfer`, there is no bypass for the seize destination check even when the caller is privileged/holds the `Seize` role — `SeizeReceiver` is unconditionally enforced. `SeizeReceiver` is a configurable policy scope (like `TransferSender`/`TransferReceiver`) that an admin can wire to any policy type, including `BLOCKLIST` or `ALWAYS_BLOCK`: [4](#0-3) 

Because `seizeWithMemo` is the designated remediation path for pulling funds out of a blocked account (the account is first blocked via `TransferSender`/`TransferReceiver` policy, then `Seize` recovers the balance to a treasury), if the treasury/destination address configured for `SeizeReceiver` becomes non-authorized (blocked by whatever policy is wired to that scope, intentionally or by policy misconfiguration/rotation), every seize attempt targeting that destination will revert with `InvalidReceiver`/policy-denial, permanently trapping the balance in the already-blocked `from` account. There is no fallback destination parameter enforcement bypass and no alternate extraction mechanism (transfer from a blocked account is itself blocked by `TransferSender`/`TransferReceiver`, and `burn_blocked` destroys rather than recovers the value).

This is the same root-cause class as the reported `VestingEscrow.recoverERC20` bug: a recovery/remediation function that is supposed to work specifically when normal operations are blocked, itself depends on an unconditional check against the very kind of gating (blocklist-style policy) that could apply to its fixed/chosen recipient, causing the recovery path itself to fail and the funds to remain permanently stuck.

### Impact Explanation
If the `SeizeReceiver` policy scope ever denies the operator's chosen destination — whether due to policy misconfiguration, a scope being rewired to `ALWAYS_BLOCK`, or the destination account later being added to whatever policy is wired to that scope — the token issuer/admin loses the only mechanism (`seizeWithMemo`) capable of moving balance out of an account already blocked under `TransferSender`/`TransferReceiver`. The seized balance becomes permanently frozen: it cannot be transferred out of the sanctioned account (blocked by transfer policy) and cannot be seized out (blocked by `SeizeReceiver`). This is a permanent freezing of funds for any stablecoin/asset B-20 token relying on this compliance flow.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: it requires an admin/compliance-role holder to misconfigure or rotate the `SeizeReceiver` policy wiring (or the underlying policy's membership) such that the previously-designated seize destination becomes unauthorized. This is a realistic operational scenario for regulated stablecoins that actively manage blocklists/allowlists (e.g., a treasury address later gets added to its own blocklist by accident, or the allowlist backing `SeizeReceiver` is replaced/cleared). No attacker action against the protocol logic itself is needed — the vulnerable condition is reachable purely through the intended, permissioned configuration surface (`updatePolicy`, `updateAllowlist`/`updateBlocklist`) that the same class of privileged transaction sender can invoke.

### Recommendation
Provide an escape hatch for `seizeWithMemo` analogous to how privileged transfers bypass ordinary policy checks: either (a) allow the `Seize`-role caller to bypass the `SeizeReceiver` check entirely (since seize is already role-gated and its purpose is remediation), or (b) support multiple/fallback authorized seize destinations so a single denied address cannot permanently block recovery, or (c) add an explicit emergency-seize path that skips `SeizeReceiver` gating under a higher-privileged role, with an event trail for auditability.

### Proof of Concept
1. Admin creates a stablecoin B-20 token, wires `TransferSender`/`TransferReceiver` to a `BLOCKLIST` policy, and wires `SeizeReceiver` to an `ALLOWLIST` policy containing only `treasury` (see `ensure_supported_policy_type` / `SeizeReceiver` scope: [5](#0-4) ).
2. Account `X` is added to the blocklist (blocked from transferring).
3. Compliance calls `seizeWithMemo(X, treasury, amount, memo)` successfully, since `treasury` is allowlisted for `SeizeReceiver`.
4. Later, `treasury` is removed from the `SeizeReceiver` allowlist (or the allowlist policy is cleared/rewired), e.g. via `updateAllowlist`.
5. A new blocked account `Y` accumulates a balance that needs remediation. Compliance calls `seizeWithMemo(Y, treasury, amount, memo)`.
6. The call reverts at `B20Guards::ensure_policy_type(token, B20PolicyType::SeizeReceiver, to)` ( [6](#0-5) ), because `treasury` is no longer authorized under `SeizeReceiver`.
7. `Y`'s balance can never be moved: transfers from `Y` are blocked by `TransferSender`, and every `seizeWithMemo` destination is checked against the same denied `SeizeReceiver` policy — the funds are permanently frozen.

    /// Ensures `policy_scope` names a built-in B-20 policy slot available on the V2 (Cobalt) common
    /// surface, which adds the seize scopes (`SEIZE_EXEMPT_POLICY` / `SEIZE_RECEIVER_POLICY`) on top
    /// of V1.
    ///
    /// The match is exhaustive on purpose: a policy scope added to `B20PolicyType` for a future fork
    /// must not silently widen this frozen V2 surface — it should fail to compile until V2's stance
    /// on it is decided explicitly.
    fn ensure_supported_policy_type(policy_scope: B256) -> Result<()> {
        match B20PolicyType::from_id(policy_scope) {
            Some(
                B20PolicyType::TransferSender
                | B20PolicyType::TransferReceiver
                | B20PolicyType::TransferExecutor
                | B20PolicyType::MintReceiver
                | B20PolicyType::SeizeExempt
                | B20PolicyType::SeizeReceiver,
            ) => Ok(()),
            None => Err(BasePrecompileError::revert(IB20::UnsupportedPolicyType {
                policyScope: policy_scope,
            })),
        }
    }
