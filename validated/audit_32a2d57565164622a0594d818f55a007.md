### Title
Permissionless `staking_contract::distribute` permanently DoSed (locks all shareholders' unlocked stake) when one recipient opts out of direct coin transfers - ([File: aptos-move/framework/aptos-framework/sources/staking_contract.move])

### Summary
`aptos_account::deposit_coins` aborts with `EACCOUNT_DOES_NOT_ACCEPT_DIRECT_COIN_TRANSFERS` when the target account is not registered for the coin and has disabled `allow_arbitrary_coin_transfers` via `set_allow_direct_coin_transfers(false)` [1](#0-0) . `staking_contract::distribute_internal` iterates over *all* shareholders of a `distribution_pool` in a single non-recoverable `while` loop and calls `aptos_account::deposit_coins` for each recipient before returning [2](#0-1) . If any single shareholder in that pool has disabled direct coin transfers (an action fully under that account's own, unprivileged control), every future call to the permissionless entry function `distribute` — callable "by anyone" per its own doc comment — will abort at that shareholder and revert, permanently blocking distribution of unlocked/inactive stake to *every other* shareholder and to the operator's commission in that same pool.

### Finding Description
- `distribute` is explicitly documented and implemented as callable by anyone: "Allow anyone to distribute already unlocked funds." [3](#0-2) 
- Internally it withdraws the pool's entire inactive + pending_inactive stake in one shot, then loops `while (distribution_pool.shareholders_count() > 0)`, redeeming shares and depositing coins to each recipient one at a time via `aptos_account::deposit_coins`, only returning after the pool is fully drained [4](#0-3) .
- `deposit_coins` requires the recipient to either already be registered for the coin, or have `can_receive_direct_coin_transfers(to) == true`; otherwise it asserts/aborts [1](#0-0) . Any account can unilaterally call `set_allow_direct_coin_transfers(false)` on itself (unprivileged, self-controlled action).
- Because Move transactions are atomic, an abort mid-loop reverts the whole `distribute` call, including the `redeem_shares` calls already executed for other recipients earlier in the iteration — meaning no value is lost per-call, but the operation can *never succeed* again for that pool as long as the poisoned shareholder remains with a nonzero share and inactive/pending_inactive stake exists to distribute.
- Since the same `distribute_internal` function is also invoked as a mandatory side effect of `update_commission`, `request_commission`, and `switch_operator` (to force-flush pending distributions before changing state) [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) , this single unprivileged act by one delegator/shareholder also blocks the staker from updating commission, requesting commission, or switching operator for that staking contract, because all three paths call `distribute_internal` unconditionally first.
- This is directly analogous to the external report's pattern: a single bad/uncooperative input item (invalid deposit signature / here, an opted-out recipient) causes a hard abort in a shared processing routine, instead of being skipped or isolated, denying service to all other legitimate parties relying on the same routine (subsequent proposers / here, all other shareholders and the staker/operator of the pool).

### Impact Explanation
This is a **high severity** denial-of-service / fund-lock issue on mainnet-relevant delegated-staking value:
- Legitimate delegators/operators with already-unlocked (inactive/pending_inactive) stake and commission become permanently unable to withdraw those funds through the `staking_contract` module's distribution path, because the shared `distribution_pool` cannot be drained past the poisoned shareholder.
- The staker also loses the ability to `update_commission`, `request_commission`, or `switch_operator` for that pool, since those flows force a `distribute_internal` first.
- No privileged action or attacker-owned-role assumption is required — the "attack" is simply the victim's own account, or any single co-delegator, calling the standard, unprivileged `set_allow_direct_coin_transfers(false)` (or never registering the coin) while it has an outstanding, unpaid share in the pool. This can happen accidentally (a normal user opting out of spam transfers) or be triggered deliberately by a malicious co-shareholder to grief the whole pool.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is meaningfully high because `set_allow_direct_coin_transfers` is a normal, documented, unprivileged user action (not a governance/admin capability), and any delegator naturally accumulates shares in the pool's `distribution_pool` merely by having stake unlocked or by receiving commission. No coordination with the staker/operator is required — a single delegator opting out, or letting their account remain unregistered for `AptosCoin` while blocking direct transfers, is sufficient to trigger the condition.

### Recommendation
In `distribute_internal`, do not let a single recipient's inability to receive funds abort the whole distribution loop. Wrap the `aptos_account::deposit_coins` call (or an underlying coin-registration check) in a fallible/skip pattern analogous to the referenced Ethereum spec fix: e.g., check `coin::is_account_registered` / `can_receive_direct_coin_transfers` up front, and if the recipient cannot currently receive funds, retain their entitlement (e.g., re-buy their shares back into the pool, or hold the coins in an escrow/claimable balance) and continue processing the remaining shareholders instead of aborting the entire call. This preserves the invariant that other shareholders' claim/withdrawal rights are not blocked by one uncooperative or misconfigured account.

### Proof of Concept
1. Staker `S` creates a staking contract with operator `O` via `staking_contract::create_staking_contract`, and delegator `D1` (or `O`, or `S` itself) ends up with a share in the pool's `distribution_pool` (e.g., from commission being requested, or via `add_stake`/unlock flows that populate `distribution_pool` entries).
2. Account `D1` calls `aptos_account::set_allow_direct_coin_transfers(false)` and never separately registers a `CoinStore<AptosCoin>` (this is a completely normal, self-serve action).
3. Time passes; the underlying stake pool accumulates inactive/pending_inactive stake, and `update_distribution_pool` credits `D1` (and possibly other shareholders such as `O`/beneficiary) with shares.
4. Anyone calls `staking_contract::distribute(staker, operator)`. `distribute_internal`'s loop reaches `D1` in `distribution_pool.shareholders()`, calls `aptos_account::deposit_coins(D1, ...)`, which aborts with `EACCOUNT_DOES_NOT_ACCEPT_DIRECT_COIN_TRANSFERS` [8](#0-7) .
5. The whole transaction reverts. Every subsequent call to `distribute`, `update_commission`, `request_commission`, and `switch_operator` for this `(staker, operator)` pair reverts the same way, permanently freezing all other shareholders' already-unlocked stake and the operator's commission until `D1` re-enables direct transfers or registers the coin — an action outside the control of the other affected parties.

*Note:* I was not able to execute this scenario in a live Move test environment to confirm there is no unseen safeguard (e.g., an automatic coin registration elsewhere in the flow that would prevent the unregistered/opted-out state from ever reaching `deposit_coins`); this assessment is based on static reading of the cited source. A Devin session with Move test tooling could confirm this end-to-end with a unit test exercising `set_allow_direct_coin_transfers(false)` followed by `distribute`.

    public entry fun switch_operator(
        staker: &signer,
        old_operator: address,
        new_operator: address,
        new_commission_percentage: u64
    ) acquires Store, BeneficiaryForOperator {
        let staker_address = signer::address_of(staker);
        assert_staking_contract_exists(staker_address, old_operator);

        assert!(
            new_commission_percentage <= 100,
            error::invalid_argument(EINVALID_COMMISSION_PERCENTAGE)
        );
        // Merging two existing staking contracts is too complex as we'd need to merge two separate stake pools.
        let store = borrow_global_mut<Store>(staker_address);
        let staking_contracts = &mut store.staking_contracts;
        assert!(
            !staking_contracts.contains_key(&new_operator),
            error::invalid_state(ECANT_MERGE_STAKING_CONTRACTS)
        );

        let (_, staking_contract) = staking_contracts.remove(&old_operator);
        // Force distribution of any already inactive stake.
        distribute_internal(
            staker_address,
            old_operator,
            &mut staking_contract,
        );

        // For simplicity, we request commission to be paid out first. This avoids having to ensure to staker doesn't
        // withdraw into the commission portion.
        request_commission_internal(
            old_operator,
            &mut staking_contract,
        );

        // Update the staking contract's commission rate and stake pool's operator.
        stake::set_operator_with_cap(&staking_contract.owner_cap, new_operator);
        staking_contract.commission_percentage = new_commission_percentage;

        let pool_address = staking_contract.pool_address;
        staking_contracts.add(new_operator, staking_contract);
        emit(SwitchOperator { pool_address, old_operator, new_operator });
    }
