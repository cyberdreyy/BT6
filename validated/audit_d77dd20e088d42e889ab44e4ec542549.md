### Title
Owner-supplied `whitelist_account_id` in `LockupFactory::create` lets an owner point their own lockup at a self-controlled fake whitelist, defeating the staking-pool trust boundary and releasing unvested/locked NEAR early - (File: `lockup-factory/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
`LockupFactory::create` accepts an optional, caller-supplied `whitelist_account_id` that overrides the canonical, foundation-controlled staking-pool whitelist for the newly deployed lockup contract. The lockup contract later trusts whatever this configured whitelist account says via a single unauthenticated cross-contract callback (`on_whitelist_is_whitelisted`), with no re-validation against the real NEAR Foundation whitelist. This lets the (self-)owner deploy a "whitelist" contract they control, have it answer `true` for any `staking_pool_account_id`, and select an arbitrary attacker-controlled contract as the "staking pool," moving locked/unvested NEAR into a contract fully in their control - outside the lockup schedule and outside the foundation's termination powers.

### Finding Description
`LockupFactory::create` lets the caller optionally override the whitelist that the deployed lockup will use: [1](#0-0) 

That value is baked into the lockup's constructor args as `staking_pool_whitelist_account_id` with no validation that it matches the factory's own `whitelist_account_id` (the NEAR-Foundation-approved whitelist): [2](#0-1) 

The lockup contract stores this attacker-suppliable account and treats it as the sole source of truth for staking-pool legitimacy. `select_staking_pool` (an OWNER'S METHOD) queries `is_whitelisted` on that configured account and blindly trusts the boolean result in the callback: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

The entire design intent of the real whitelist contract is that only staking pools proven to "guarantee that the delegated tokens can not be lost or locked" are trusted, specifically so a lockup can always recover delegated funds: [5](#0-4) 

Because `create` lets the owner substitute this trust anchor with their own account, the "is this a safe staking pool" check becomes self-certified rather than foundation-certified - structurally the same "isAnchor" self-certification bypass described in the external report, where a party unilaterally controls the check that is supposed to gate delegation of value.

Once a fake-whitelisted, attacker-controlled account is selected as the staking pool, the owner can move real NEAR into it via `deposit_and_stake` / `deposit_to_staking_pool`, which simply transfer funds to `staking_information.staking_pool_account_id` with no further validation that the destination is a genuine staking pool: [6](#0-5) 

Because that destination contract's code is entirely attacker-authored (not the real staking-pool contract), it can accept the deposit and route it anywhere - including back to the owner directly, bypassing the "locked until schedule" and "foundation can terminate/recover unvested amount" invariants enforced elsewhere in the lockup: [7](#0-6) 

The `foundation_account_id` is only set when a `vesting_schedule` is supplied at creation time (i.e., precisely the case where funds are supposed to be unvested/lockable and the foundation is meant to retain termination power) - yet the same `create` call that sets up vesting/foundation control still allows the caller to pick their own whitelist: [8](#0-7) 

The factory even has a test explicitly confirming that a custom, non-default whitelist account id is accepted without restriction: [9](#0-8) 

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the custody binding "locked/unvested amount recorded by the lockup versus NEAR actually recoverable from the delegated staking pool." A vesting employee/owner can self-deploy their lockup with a self-controlled fake whitelist, self-certify a malicious "staking pool," and drain the still-unvested/locked NEAR into that contract before the vesting cliff/schedule or before the foundation ever gets a chance to terminate and recover unvested tokens. This matches the Critical impact category: "locked or unvested tokens released early" / "a wrongly whitelisted or wrongly parameterised deployment."

### Likelihood Explanation
No privileged role is required: `create` is a public, `#[payable]` method callable by anyone who becomes the lockup owner, and the `whitelist_account_id` override is a first-class documented parameter, not an edge case requiring flash loans or bypassing timing windows (unlike the Vader `replaceAnchor` path). The only cost is the `MIN_ATTACHED_BALANCE` deposit and deploying one extra small "fake whitelist" and one "fake staking pool" contract, both fully controlled by the attacker.

### Recommendation
Do not allow the lockup owner (or anyone other than the deploying authority / NEAR Foundation) to override the staking-pool whitelist account for lockups that carry a `vesting_schedule` / `foundation_account_id`. Concretely:
- Remove the `whitelist_account_id` parameter from `LockupFactory::create`, or restrict it to only take effect when `vesting_schedule` is `None` (i.e., only for fully-owned, non-vesting lockups where the owner already has unrestricted control of their own funds).
- Alternatively, require `whitelist_account_id` (if provided) to itself be present on a factory-level allow-list controlled by the foundation, so an owner cannot substitute an arbitrary self-controlled account.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `LockupFactory::create(owner_account_id = attacker, vesting_schedule = Some(...), whitelist_account_id = Some(attacker_fake_whitelist))`, attaching `MIN_ATTACHED_BALANCE`. This deploys a lockup with `foundation_account_id` set (since vesting is present) but `staking_pool_whitelist_account_id = attacker_fake_whitelist`, per `lockup-factory/src/lib.rs:123-133,140-157`.
2. Attacker deploys `attacker_fake_whitelist` implementing `is_whitelisted(_) -> true` for any account, and `attacker_fake_pool` implementing a minimal staking-pool interface (`deposit_and_stake`, etc.) that simply forwards received NEAR back to the attacker's own wallet.
3. As owner, attacker calls `select_staking_pool(attacker_fake_pool)` on the lockup; the lockup calls `is_whitelisted` on `attacker_fake_whitelist`, gets `true`, and stores `attacker_fake_pool` as the selected staking pool (`lockup/src/owner.rs:12-41`, `lockup/src/owner_callbacks.rs:6-25`).
4. Attacker calls `deposit_and_stake(amount = locked_unvested_balance)`; the lockup transfers NEAR to `attacker_fake_pool` (`lockup/src/owner.rs:127-166`), which forwards the funds straight back to the attacker's externally-controlled account - bypassing the vesting schedule and the foundation's ability to terminate and recover the unvested amount.

# Whitelist contract for staking pools

The purpose of this contract is to maintain the whitelist of the staking pool contracts account IDs that are approved
by NEAR Foundation.

In order for the lockup contracts to be able delegate to a staking pool, the staking pool should faithfully implement the spec.
The staking pool should guarantee that the delegated tokens can not be lost or locked, such as the lockup contract should be
able to recover delegated tokens back to the lockup from a staking pool. In order to enforce this, only approved (whitelisted)
accounts of staking pool contracts can receive delegated tokens from lockup contracts.

If NEAR Foundation has to approve every single staking pool account it might lead to a bottleneck and centralization
To address this NEAR Foundation can whitelist the account IDs of staking pool factory contracts.

The whitelisted staking pool factory contract will be able to whitelist accounts of staking pool contracts.
A factory contract creates and initializes a staking pool contract in a secure and permissionless way.
This allows anyone on the network to be able to create a staking pool contract for themselves without needing approval from the NEAR
Foundation. This is important to maintain the decentralization of the decision making and network governance.
