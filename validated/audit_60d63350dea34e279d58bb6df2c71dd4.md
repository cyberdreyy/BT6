### Title
ProgrammableBorrower counts the entire vault position as epoch profit after default reset, causing unbacked strategy-token minting - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol)

### Summary
The kernel bug computes a checksum over the wrong source on a fallback path. The analog lives in `ProgrammableBorrower._vaultNetInterest()`: after `onDefault()` clears `bufferStartVaultAssets` to `0` without unwinding the ERC4626 position, the next `onStartEpoch()` computes `bufferedVaultDelta = currentVaultAssets - 0`, i.e. it treats the whole pre-existing vault position as fresh profit. That phantom delta flows into `totalInterestDueNow()`, which `IdleCDOEpochVariant._resolveStopEpochInterest()` trusts as the epoch interest; in minted-interest mode `stopEpoch` then calls `mintStrategyTokens(_grossInterest)`, minting unbacked strategy tokens and inflating tranche prices.

### Finding Description
- `onDefault()` (ProgrammableBorrower.sol:289-301) zeroes `bufferedVaultDelta`, `bufferInterest`, and `bufferStartVaultAssets`, but deliberately does **not** redeem vault shares ("Do not call convertToAssets here"). Any vault sleeve position remains in the contract while its accounting baseline is destroyed.
- On the next epoch after operations are restored, `onStartEpoch()` (lines 201-224) executes `bufferedVaultDelta = int256(currentVaultAssets) - int256(bufferStartVaultAssets)` where `bufferStartVaultAssets == 0`. The full `_currentVaultAssets()` — principal parked in the vault, not yield — is booked as buffer profit.
- `_vaultNetInterest()` (lines 337-347) adds `bufferedVaultDelta` to the epoch delta, so `totalInterestDueNow()` (lines 330-334) reports vault principal as interest.
- `IdleCDOEpochVariant._stopEpoch` resolves `_interest` through `_resolveStopEpochInterest` → `totalInterestDueNow()` (IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:357, 1001-1008). With `isInterestMinted`, `_amountToPullFromBorrower = 0` and `_strategy.mintStrategyTokens(_grossInterest)` mints tokens equal to the phantom profit (lines 376, 428-433). `_updateAccounting()` then raises tranche prices on unbacked supply.
- Existing guards do not stop it: the `_maxApr` sanity cap at line 379 only applies when an override `_interest > 1` is passed — not when interest is sourced from `totalInterestDueNow()` (`_interest` resolves to 0/1 sentinel paths). `_onlyIdleCDO`, skim, and epoch gating are all satisfied.

### Impact Explanation
Direct insolvency / theft. Strategy tokens equal to the entire stranded vault position are minted against "interest" that is actually principal (or simply already-accounted assets). Active tranche prices are inflated; a tranche-token holder who calls `requestWithdraw`/`claimWithdrawRequest` (or `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`) right after the restart epoch redeems at the inflated price and drains real underlying funded by the borrower, leaving later claimants — including pending-receipt holders via `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch`/default recovery basis — with unbacked claims. Loss is bounded by the vault sleeve value at default time, which can be up to the full deployed principal.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a prior borrower repayment failure (producing `defaulted` and an `onDefault` reset) followed by an owner/manager restart via `restoreOperations`/`startEpoch` while the ProgrammableBorrower still holds ERC4626 shares — exactly the "assets can remain stranded" situation documented in `setVault`. No privileged role acts maliciously; the attacker is any ordinary tranche holder who front-runs other LPs' claims once prices are inflated. Likelihood is conditional on a default-and-restart sequence, but the accounting corruption is deterministic once it occurs.

### Recommendation
On restart, re-establish the baseline instead of assuming zero: in `onStartEpoch`, when `epochAccountingActive == false` and `bufferStartVaultAssets == 0`, set `bufferedVaultDelta = 0` (or re-snapshot `bufferStartVaultAssets` to `_currentVaultAssets()` rather than leaving it cleared). Alternatively, have `onDefault()` keep `bufferStartVaultAssets = _currentVaultAssets()` lazily — e.g., record the position size in shares — so pre-default vault principal is never reclassified as new yield, and cap `totalInterestDueNow()`-sourced interest the same way the `_maxApr` check caps overrides.
