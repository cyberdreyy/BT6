## Finding Confirmed (Modified Root Cause)

The literal share-loss described in the question does not occur — `switch_operator` fully drains the existing `distribution_pool` via `distribute_internal` (correctly paying the old operator's beneficiary) *before* moving the `StakingContract` struct to the new key [1](#0-0) . Since `distribution_pool` is a field embedded inside the `StakingContract` struct — not a separate map keyed by operator — it physically travels with the struct when re-inserted under `new_operator`, so no shares "disappear" when the `SimpleMap` key changes [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) .

However, a related, real bug exists in the same code path.

### Title
Old operator's beneficiary is bypassed for commission accrued during `switch_operator` - (`aptos-move/framework/aptos-framework/sources/staking_contract.move`)

### Summary
`switch_operator`/`switch_operator_with_same_commission` calls `request_commission_internal(old_operator, ...)` immediately before moving the contract to `new_operator`. This records a new distribution-pool share keyed by the **address `old_operator`** [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) . When this share is later paid out via a future `distribute()` call keyed by `new_operator`, the beneficiary redirection check `if (recipient == operator)` compares the recorded recipient (`old_operator`'s address) against the *current* `operator` argument (`new_operator`), which never matches [6](#0-5) . As a result, that commission installment is paid directly to `old_operator`'s raw account address instead of being routed to `old_operator`'s registered beneficiary via `beneficiary_for_operator(operator)`.

### Finding Description
- `set_beneficiary_for_operator` lets an operator redirect all future commission to a `BeneficiaryForOperator` address [7](#0-6) .
- Commission is only redirected to the beneficiary at the moment of payout, inside `distribute_internal`, gated on `recipient == operator` where `operator` is whatever value the current caller passed in [8](#0-7) .
- `switch_operator` calls `request_commission_internal` for the *old* operator right before it swaps the `SimpleMap` key to `new_operator`, embedding a share for `old_operator` into the (still-attached) `distribution_pool` [9](#0-8) .
- Any subsequent `distribute()` call is made against the staker/`new_operator` pair, so `distribute_internal` is invoked with `operator = new_operator`, permanently breaking the redirect check for the still-outstanding `old_operator` share.

### Impact Explanation
If `old_operator` had configured a beneficiary before the switch, and there was an unclaimed/not-yet-withdrawable commission portion at the exact moment `switch_operator` runs (e.g., stake still `pending_inactive`, lockup not yet elapsed), that commission payment bypasses the beneficiary entirely once it becomes withdrawable and is instead deposited to `old_operator`'s own account. This is a wrong-recipient bug affecting fund routing between operator and beneficiary roles, triggered purely by a routine, unprivileged staker action (`switch_operator_with_same_commission`).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires: (1) staker calls `switch_operator`/`switch_operator_with_same_commission`, and (2) a beneficiary is configured for `old_operator`, and (3) there is a nonzero unlocked-rewards balance whose commission is calculated but whose underlying stake is not yet fully inactive at switch time. This is a plausible but non-trivial timing/state combination, not something an unprivileged party outside the staker/operator relationship can trigger on demand.

### Recommendation
In `distribute_internal`, resolve `recipient` through `beneficiary_for_operator` based on whether the recorded recipient *is itself an operator with a beneficiary set* (e.g., check `exists<BeneficiaryForOperator>(recipient)`), rather than comparing to the currently-passed-in `operator` parameter. Alternatively, force a full `distribute_internal` + zero-out of any pending commission distribution before allowing the operator-key swap in `switch_operator`, ensuring no share ever survives a key change under a stale operator identity.

### Proof of Concept (adjusted from submitted idea)
1. Staker creates a staking contract with `operator_1`; `operator_1` calls `set_beneficiary_for_operator(beneficiary_1)`.
2. Advance epochs so rewards accrue but leave the pool such that after `request_commission_internal` runs, the commission amount is only `pending_inactive` (lockup not yet expired).
3. Staker calls `switch_operator_with_same_commission(old_operator = operator_1, new_operator = operator_2)`. This records a distribution-pool share under key `operator_1` inside the struct now stored under `operator_2`.
4. Advance epochs until lockup expires so the pending stake becomes `inactive`.
5. Call `staking_contract::distribute(staker, operator_2)`.
6. Assert: coins are deposited to `operator_1`'s address rather than `beneficiary_1`, confirming the beneficiary redirection is bypassed for the pre-switch commission share.
