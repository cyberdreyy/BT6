### Title
Escrow Fee Truncation Vulnerability - (`contracts/BaseEscrowFactory.sol`)

### Summary
The protocol's fee calculation mechanism in `BaseEscrowFactory.sol` uses standard integer division that truncates results. When users perform small swaps or partial fills, the calculated `integratorFeeAmount` and `protocolFeeAmount` can round down to zero. This allows an unprivileged actor to bypass protocol and integrator fees by splitting a large order into many small partial fills, leading to protocol insolvency regarding fee collection.

### Finding Description
In `BaseEscrowFactory.sol`, the `_postInteraction` function calls `FeeTaker._getFeeAmounts` to determine the fees owed for a swap [1](#0-0) . The logic for fee calculation is mirrored in the production-aligned `FeeCalcLib.sol` used for escrow deployment parameters [2](#0-1) .

The calculation uses `mulDiv` without specifying a rounding direction, defaulting to truncation:
```solidity
uint256 integratorFeeTotal = amount.mulDiv(integratorFee, denominator);
integratorFeeAmount = integratorFeeTotal.mulDiv(integratorShare, _BASE_1E2);
protocolFeeAmount = amount.mulDiv(protocolFee, denominator) + integratorFeeTotal - integratorFeeAmount;
``` [3](#0-2) 

If `amount * integratorFee < denominator`, the `integratorFeeTotal` becomes 0. Similarly, `protocolFeeAmount` will truncate to 0. Since the protocol supports partial fills where a user can specify the number of parts (`partsAmount`) [4](#0-3) , an attacker can deliberately fill the order in the smallest possible increments to ensure the fee ratio results in zero due to Solidity's floor division.

### Impact Explanation
**Medium/High**: This is a direct theft of "unclaimed fee-like value" (High) or a "business-logic failure" (Medium) where an unprivileged actor can break the protocol's accounting. On low-gas chains like Polygon or zkSync (explicitly mentioned in the bug-class seed and supported by the repo), the cost of executing hundreds of small partial fills is lower than the saved fees, allowing users to execute swaps for free at the expense of the protocol and integrators.

### Likelihood Explanation
**High**: The protocol explicitly supports partial fills and defines a `partsAmount` in the `extraData` [5](#0-4) . Any unprivileged user can create or fill an order with a high number of parts to trigger the truncation.

### Recommendation
Update the fee calculation to use `Math.Rounding.Ceil` to ensure that any non-zero fee percentage results in at least 1 unit of the token being collected, preventing the rounding-to-zero exploit.

### Proof of Concept
1. An order is created for 1,000,000 USDC with a 0.01% fee (10 basis points).
2. The `denominator` is $100,000 + 10 = 100,010$.
3. The attacker fills the order in increments of 1,000 USDC.
4. Fee calculation: $1,000 * 10 / 100,010 = 10,000 / 100,010 = 0$ (truncated).
5. The attacker repeats this 1,000 times, paying 0 total fees instead of the expected 100 USDC.
