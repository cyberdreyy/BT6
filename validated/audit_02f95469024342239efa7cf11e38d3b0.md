Confirmed: across every other in-scope contract (`staking-pool`, `staking-pool-factory`, `lockup-factory`, `multisig`, `whitelist`, `voting`), the `#[init]`-annotated `new()` explicitly guards with `assert!(!env::state_exists(), ...)`. `lockup/src/lib.rs`'s `new()` has no such guard, and with `near-sdk = "3.1.0"` (per `lockup/Cargo.toml`), the `#[init]` attribute in this SDK version does not itself enforce single-initialization — that check must be added manually, as every sibling contract does.

### Title
Lockup contract's `new()` lacks a re-initialization guard, allowing ownership takeover of locked funds - (File: `lockup/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
`LockupContract::new()` [1](#0-0)  is marked `#[init]` but, unlike every other contract in this codebase, never checks `env::state_exists()` before writing state. Since `near-sdk = "3.1.0"` [2](#0-1)  does not automatically block re-invocation of `#[init]` methods, `new()` can be called again on an already-initialized, already-funded lockup account.

### Finding Description
Every other in-scope contract explicitly asserts against re-initialization in its `new()`: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

`lockup/src/lib.rs`'s `new()` builds `LockupInformation`, `VestingInformation`, and the top-level `LockupContract` struct — including `owner_account_id` and `foundation_account_id` — purely from caller-supplied arguments, with no `assert!(!env::state_exists())` check anywhere in the function body: [8](#0-7) 

Because the lockup account already holds the locked NEAR balance (`lockup_amount: env::account_balance()` is recomputed from whatever balance is present at call time), any account able to call `new` against the already-deployed lockup contract can overwrite `owner_account_id` to itself, reset `vesting_information` to `VestingInformation::None` (dropping any foundation termination rights), and redefine `staking_pool_whitelist_account_id`/`transfers_information` — all while the contract already custodies real NEAR belonging to the original owner.

This mirrors the reported bug class exactly: a party not entitled to alter the trusted configuration is able to call the "one-time" initializer again and rebind custody of already-held funds to themselves, breaking the binding between the account recorded as owner and the NEAR actually held for the original beneficiary.

### Impact Explanation
This is Critical: a lockup contract's ownership and vesting/termination configuration can be rewritten by any caller after deployment, letting an attacker redirect custody of already-locked NEAR to an account they control, or strip the foundation's ability to terminate an employee's unvested tokens by resetting `vesting_information` to `None`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high for any deployed lockup contract whose account ID and current state are public (all NEAR accounts and their code/state are publicly queryable), since the attack requires nothing but a single function call with attacker-chosen arguments — no privileged key, deployment, or social engineering is needed.

### Recommendation
Add `assert!(!env::state_exists(), "Already initialized");` as the first line of `LockupContract::new()` in `lockup/src/lib.rs`, matching the guard already present in `staking-pool`, `staking-pool-factory`, `lockup-factory`, `multisig`, `whitelist`, and `voting`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Foundation deploys and initializes `lockup.owner1.near` with `new(owner_account_id="owner1", ...)`, funding it with locked NEAR (`lockup_information.lockup_amount` derived from the account balance).
2. Attacker calls `new(owner_account_id="attacker.near", lockup_duration=..., transfers_information=..., vesting_schedule=None, ...)` against `lockup.owner1.near`.
3. Because `new()` performs no `env::state_exists()` check, the call succeeds and overwrites `LockupContract` state: `owner_account_id` becomes `attacker.near`, and any vesting/termination rights are dropped.
4. Attacker, now the recorded owner, calls owner-only methods (e.g. transfer/staking methods gated on `owner_account_id`) to move the already-held NEAR balance out to themselves.
