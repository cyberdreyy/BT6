### Title
Stale instant-withdraw claim counts inflate `defaultPendingClaimBasis`, permanently stranding default-recovery funds - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pays out an instant-redeem receipt and zeroes `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, but it never clears the per-epoch counters `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epoch]` or `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]`. If the same epoch later ends in a borrower default, `defaultPendingClaimBasis` counts those already-paid receipts as outstanding claim basis, diluting `defaultRecoveryPrice` and leaving a proportional share of the recovery reserve permanently unclaimable. This mirrors the OpenEXR flaw: a count of valid samples is trusted without validating it against how many were actually consumed, so the "buffer" (recovery basis) is oversized and memory/value written against it overflows the real data.

### Finding Description
The external bug is a failure to validate the number of scanline samples before writing them, producing a heap overflow. The on-chain analog is a failure to validate the number of outstanding instant-withdraw claims before computing the recovery payout denominator.

- `requestInstantWithdraw` records three counters: `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][currentEpoch]`, and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[currentEpoch]` (contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol:366-374).
- `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns the user's receipt, sets `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user] = 0`, and transfers funds, but does not decrement either per-epoch counter (contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol:387-392).
- On default finalization, `defaultPendingClaimBasis` adds `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` whenever `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` (lines 644-649). Stale entries from users who already claimed are still present, so `totalBasis` in `finalizeDefaultRecovery` is overstated (lines 679-688).
- `recoveryPrice = reserveAmount * RECOVERY_FULL / totalBasis` is then too small. Every legitimate defaulted-receipt claimant (`_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`, `_claimDefaultedWithdrawRequest`) and every active LP (via the NAV haircut in `IdleCDOEpochVariant.finalizeDefault`) recovers less, while the excess reserve stays in `defaultRecoveryReserve` forever — finalization is one-time (`defaultRecoveryFinalized` gate) and no sweep exists.
- Additionally, `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` does `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user] -= claimBasis` (line 848); for a user who already claimed, `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` is 0 and `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][defaultEpoch]` is still nonzero, so their own claim call underflow-reverts — harmless to them, but it confirms the double-counting of the same samples.

### Impact Explanation
Quantified loss = (stale claimed amount / inflated totalBasis) × reserveAmount, permanently locked in `defaultRecoveryReserve` as "recovery dust" that no claimant can ever withdraw, while all honest claimants and active LPs are haircut more than they should be. This is permanent freezing of recovery funds plus underpayment of unclaimed yield — a direct consequence of trusting a stale count, exactly the structural analog of trusting an unvalidated scanline sample count.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a partially-funded instant-withdraw queue in the defaulting epoch (`pendingInstantWithdraws != 0`) so the per-epoch claims path is taken, plus at least one user who claimed a funded instant receipt in that same epoch before default. Both actions are performed by unprivileged users (a KYC-passing lender qualifies); the default itself and `finalizeDefault` are honest-manager actions the attack only needs to sequence around. No privileged misbehavior is needed — the count corruption is a pure accounting artifact. Likelihood is moderate: it needs the partial-funding edge case, but the attacker's only required action is the ordinary `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` call.

### Recommendation
In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, clear the per-epoch accounting alongside the aggregate: decrement `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epochOfReceipt]` and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochOfReceipt]` (or store the receipt's epoch per user) when the receipt is paid, so `defaultPendingClaimBasis` only counts samples still in the buffer. Equivalently, gate the `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` addition on actually-outstanding per-epoch claims rather than on the global `pendingInstantWithdraws`.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry fork sequence (fixed-APR mode, instant withdraws enabled):

1. Lender A deposits into AA; `requestInstantWithdraw` records `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[N] += amount`.
2. `startEpoch` — CDO cash covers only part of the instant queue, so `pendingInstantWithdraws > 0` after `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`.
3. Attacker/KYC'd lender B calls `claimWithdrawRequest`/`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` and is paid in full; `instantWithdrawsRequests[B] = 0` but `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[B][N]` and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[N]` remain.
4. Epoch ends; borrower repays nothing; `stopEpoch(0,0)` → `defaulted`. `finalizeDefault(recovered, source)` computes `totalBasis` including B's already-paid claim, producing a lower `defaultRecoveryPrice`.
5. Assert: `creditVault.defaultRecoveryReserve()` retains dust ≈ recovered × staleBasis/totalBasis after all honest claims; honest claimants receive strictly less than `claimBasis × recovered / trueBasis`; B's defaulted-claim path reverts on the `instantWithdrawsRequests[B] -= claimBasis` underflow.

Caveat: I could not fully verify `epochNumber` increment timing relative to `defaultRecoveryEpoch` within the tool-call budget; if `defaultRecoveryEpoch` differs from the epoch under which the receipts were recorded, step 5's per-user underflow may not trigger, but the `defaultPendingClaimBasis` inflation argument stands on lines 366-374 vs 387-392 and 644-649 as read.
