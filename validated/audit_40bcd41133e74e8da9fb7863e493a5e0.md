Based on my review of the marginfi-v2 codebase, the external report's premise does not map onto this repository. The report describes a `MarginAccount`/`ModularSwapRouter`/`MarginTrading::liquidate` architecture that does not exist in marginfi-v2, and more importantly, the actual liquidation mechanisms here already have well-defined, strong liquidator incentives, directly contradicting the report's core claim of "no incentive for liquidation."

Specifically:

- **Classic liquidation** (`lending_account_liquidate`) pays the liquidator a 2.5% discount on seized collateral relative to the liability repaid, plus routes another 2.5% to the insurance fund — this is a hardcoded profit incentive baked into the accounting math itself via `LIQUIDATION_LIQUIDATOR_FEE` and `LIQUIDATION_INSURANCE_FEE`. [1](#0-0) 

- **Receivership liquidation** (`start_liquidation`/`end_liquidation`) allows a liquidator with zero capital to seize up to `FeeState.liquidation_max_fee` (historically ~10%) profit margin over the repaid liability value, an even larger incentive than classic liquidation. [2](#0-1) 

- Both mechanisms are documented as permissionless and profit-driven for third-party callers in the project's own risk/liquidator guides, explicitly describing the 2.5%/10% liquidator premiums. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Since concrete, enforced profit incentives for calling liquidation already exist in the reachable code path — not merely as a design intent but as compiled fee constants and accounting logic — the "lack of incentive" bug class from the external report has no valid analog in this codebase.

Classic liquidation uses the `lending_account_liquidate` instruction. To execute this kind of liquidation, the liquidator must have their own lending Account. The target, also called the "liquidatee", must be unhealthy. The liquidator will assume part of one of the liquidatee's assets, and in exchange, will also take on some of the liquidatee's debt in one of their liabilities.

If a liquidator reclaims \$A asset, they only have to assume (1-0.025)\*A in B liabilities. The liquidator keeps this 2.5% premium.

The liquidatee only receives a repayment on their B debt of (1-0.025-0.025)\*A, the additional 2.5% goes to insurance funds.
