### Title
Reward Mis-Attribution via JIT Deposit Before `internal_ping` Reward Distribution - (File: `staking-pool/src/internal.rs`)

### Summary
`StakingContract::internal_ping` distributes any balance increase the contract has accumulated since the last ping (validator rewards, gas-fee rebates, and any direct/"accidental" NEAR transfers to the contract account) pro-rata to whichever `stake_shares` are outstanding **at the moment ping executes**, not to the shares that were outstanding while that balance was accruing. An unprivileged actor can watch the contract's balance grow (a direct transfer, or accumulated gas rebates) and deposit/stake immediately before the next `ping`-triggering call, capturing a slice of a reward they took zero risk to earn, diluting the honest long-term delegators — the JIT-liquidity pattern described in the referenced report.

### Finding Description
The reward accounting binding this contract relies on is:
`total_reward = current_total_balance - last_total_balance`, distributed to `total_stake_shares` outstanding when `internal_ping` runs [1](#0-0) .

This works correctly only if `stake_shares` held at ping-time correspond to the accounts that were actually staked while `total_reward` accrued. But the contract's own documentation states this is not guaranteed for two of the three reward sources it accounts for:

- "if someone accidentally (or intentionally) transfers tokens to the contract (without function call), then tokens from the transfer will be distributed to the active stake participants of the contract in the next epoch." [2](#0-1) 
- Gas-fee rebates are also added to the pool's balance continuously and only recognized at the next `ping`. [3](#0-2) 

Both of these are balance increases that sit in the contract's account balance *before* being recognized by `internal_ping`, and `internal_ping` is invoked lazily — only when the epoch height changes and some contract method is called [4](#0-3) . Between the time such an increase lands in the account and the time some caller finally triggers the epoch-crossing `ping`, any account can call `deposit_and_stake`, which runs `internal_ping` first (using the pre-increase snapshot, since the caller's own attached deposit is excluded), then stakes the caller's funds at the pre-reward share price [5](#0-4) . When the next actor finally triggers `internal_ping` for the new epoch, the reward that had already silently accrued in the contract's balance is split across `total_stake_shares`, which now includes the attacker's freshly minted shares — shares that were present for zero economic exposure to the risk that generated the reward.

Because the share price is monotonically non-decreasing by contract invariant, the attacker's mint-then-claim sequence is risk-free: they cannot lose value, they can only gain a slice of reward they didn't earn, taken out of the pro-rata share that should have gone to delegators who were staked while the reward actually accrued.

### Impact Explanation
This is a reward mis-attribution: value that should be split among the delegators who bore risk during the epoch in which the reward accrued is instead partially captured by a delegator who joined only in time for the `ping` that recognizes it, diluting all other stakers' claims on `total_staked_balance`. This matches the "rewards ... mis-attributed" High-impact category — the recorded `stake_shares` ownership at ping time diverges from the economic entitlement implied by when the reward-generating balance actually arrived, and other depositors settle their share value on this divergent accounting.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires no special privileges — any account can call `deposit_and_stake` / `unstake_all`. The trigger events (an intentional/accidental direct NEAR transfer to the staking pool account, or accumulated gas-fee rebates) are explicitly acknowledged by the contract's own documentation as real, expected occurrences, and the window to front-run is not a single block — it is the entire gap between the balance increase landing and the next call that crosses an epoch boundary and triggers `ping`, which can span a full epoch. This materially widens the exploitation window relative to a strict single-block MEV sandwich, making the attack easy to execute opportunistically by any bot watching account balances.

### Recommendation
Snapshot and recognize non-`deposit()` balance increases (direct transfers, gas rebates) as soon as they are observed rather than lazily at the next `ping`, or exclude shares minted in the same epoch as an unrecognized reward from participating in that reward's distribution (e.g., only mint a delegator's new shares against the total balance *after* forcing an `internal_ping` recognition pass, and/or require a minimum staking duration before shares are reward-eligible for pre-existing unrecognized balance deltas).

### Proof of Concept
1. `total_staked_balance = 5000`, `total_stake_shares = 5000` (price = 1).
2. Someone (accidentally or intentionally) transfers `1000` NEAR directly to the staking pool account — this is now part of `env::account_balance()` but not yet recognized because `internal_ping` has not run for a new epoch [2](#0-1) .
3. Bob (attacker), watching the pool, immediately calls `deposit_and_stake` with `5000` NEAR. `internal_ping` runs first, uses the current epoch snapshot (no epoch change yet, so it's a no-op or recognizes the balance too early depending on timing), then Bob's stake is minted at the pre-reward price: he receives `5000` shares, bringing totals to `total_staked_balance = 10000`, `total_stake_shares = 10000` [6](#0-5) [5](#0-4) .
4. On the next epoch, any call triggers `internal_ping`: `total_reward = 1000` is added to `total_staked_balance`, now `11000`, split across `10000` shares — Bob's `5000` shares are now worth `5500` [7](#0-6) .
5. Bob calls `unstake_all`, then withdraws after 4 epochs (`4 epoch` unstaking delay, no share-price downside risk during the wait), capturing `500` NEAR of profit that should have accrued entirely to delegators who were staked before the `1000` NEAR arrived.

    pub(crate) fn internal_stake(&mut self, amount: Balance) {
        assert!(amount > 0, "Staking amount should be positive");

        let account_id = env::predecessor_account_id();
        let mut account = self.internal_get_account(&account_id);

        // Calculate the number of "stake" shares that the account will receive for staking the
        // given amount.
        let num_shares = self.num_shares_from_staked_amount_rounded_down(amount);
        assert!(
            num_shares > 0,
            "The calculated number of \"stake\" shares received for staking should be positive"
        );
        // The amount of tokens the account will be charged from the unstaked balance.
        // Rounded down to avoid overcharging the account to guarantee that the account can always
        // unstake at least the same amount as staked.
        let charge_amount = self.staked_amount_from_num_shares_rounded_down(num_shares);
        assert!(
            charge_amount > 0,
            "Invariant violation. Calculated staked amount must be positive, because \"stake\" share price should be at least 1"
        );

        assert!(
            account.unstaked >= charge_amount,
            "Not enough unstaked balance to stake"
        );
        account.unstaked -= charge_amount;
        account.stake_shares += num_shares;
        self.internal_save_account(&account_id, &account);

        // The staked amount that will be added to the total to guarantee the "stake" share price
        // never decreases. The difference between `stake_amount` and `charge_amount` is paid
        // from the allocated STAKE_SHARE_PRICE_GUARANTEE_FUND.
        let stake_amount = self.staked_amount_from_num_shares_rounded_up(num_shares);

        self.total_staked_balance += stake_amount;
        self.total_stake_shares += num_shares;
