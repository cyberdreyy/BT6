### Title
ProgrammableBorrower accepts zero-share ERC4626 deposits, enabling vault inflation theft of pool principal - ([File: contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol](contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol))

### Summary
`ProgrammableBorrower` parks pool assets in an arbitrary ERC4626 vault but does not enforce a nonzero/minimum share output or validate that the vault position still represents the deposited assets. An unprivileged first depositor can inflate the vault share price before the first `onStartEpoch` deposit, cause the pool to receive zero or dust shares, and redeem the donated assets plus the pool principal. `IdleCDOEpochVariant` then treats the missing vault assets only as reduced epoch interest rather than burning strategy-token principal, leaving tranche NAV overstated.

### Finding Description
During `startEpoch`, `ProgrammableBorrower.onStartEpoch` snapshots `underlyingToken.balanceOf(address(this)) + currentVaultAssets` as `epochStartVaultAssets`, then deposits the entire idle token balance into the configured ERC4626 vault through `_depositToVault`. The accounting baseline intentionally uses the pre-deposit value, while `epochDepositedToVault` is left unchanged. [1](#0-0) 

`_depositToVault` calls `vault.deposit(_assetAmount, address(this))` and records `shares`, but never checks `shares != 0`, a minimum shares bound, or that `convertToAssets(shares)` approximately covers `_assetAmount`. [2](#0-1) 

On a fresh ERC4626 vault, the attacker can:

1. Deposit one wei and receive the initial vault share.
2. Donate a large amount of underlying directly to the vault.
3. Let the honest manager start the epoch.
4. Have `ProgrammableBorrower` deposit the pool principal and receive zero or dust shares because the ERC4626 share calculation rounds down.
5. Redeem the attacker's initial share for the donated assets plus the pool's deposit.

The loss is later measured by `_vaultNetInterest` as `epochStartVaultAssets - currentVaultAssets`, but `totalInterestDueNow` floors the net result at zero. [3](#0-2)  In minted-interest programmable mode, `IdleCDOEpochVariant` pulls only pending withdrawals and mints at most the reported interest; it does not automatically burn strategy tokens equal to the lost principal during a normal rollover. [4](#0-3) 

### Impact Explanation
The attacker can steal up to the full pool principal deposited into a vulnerable or newly initialized ERC4626 vault. With a deposit of `P`, an attacker-controlled initial share, and a donation `D >= P`, a standard floor-based `previewDeposit` calculation can return zero shares. Redeeming the attacker's share then returns approximately `D + P`, giving a net profit of approximately `P`.

The credit vault's strategy tokens remain minted against the transferred principal even though the programmable borrower owns no meaningful vault claim. A normal stop can report zero net interest rather than realize the principal loss, and the next `onStartEpoch` resets the baseline to the impaired vault position. Pending withdrawals may also be paid at par before the missing backing is socialized or defaulted.

Broken invariant: fair mint/burn and solvency. One unit of CDO strategy token continues to represent one underlying even though the corresponding external vault position was captured by the attacker.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is deployment/configuration dependent. The attack requires the configured ERC4626 vault to permit share-price inflation—most critically a fresh or near-empty vault with floor rounding and no minimum deposit/dead-share protection.

No privileged attacker action is required. The attacker only needs to transact with the external vault before the manager's epoch-start transaction. Owner, manager, borrower, and IdleCDO all behave honestly. Existing checks do not prevent this: `onStartEpoch` snapshots the pre-deposit baseline, `_depositToVault` accepts the returned share amount unconditionally, and `totalInterestDueNow` cannot represent a negative principal adjustment below zero.

### Recommendation
Add minimum-share and post-deposit solvency checks around ERC4626 deposits:

- Require `shares > 0` in `_depositToVault`.
- Require `shares >= minShares` supplied by the caller, or compute `previewDeposit(_assetAmount)` and reject outputs below a bounded tolerance.
- After depositing, verify that `vault.convertToAssets(vault.balanceOf(address(this)))` remains within an acceptable tolerance of the expected principal baseline.
- Prefer vaults with built-in virtual shares/assets or dead-share protection, and seed the vault atomically during deployment before the programmable borrower is configured.
- Propagate an undercollateralized vault position as a real principal loss to `IdleCDOEpochVariant`, rather than flooring `totalInterestDueNow()` to zero while retaining full strategy-token backing.

### Proof of Concept
A Foundry PoC can reproduce the issue with a fork fixture and a fresh standard ERC4626 vault configured as the programmable borrower's vault:

```solidity
function testVaultInflationCapturesPoolPrincipal() public {
    // Fork fixture already deploys underlying, IdleCreditVault, CDO and ProgrammableBorrower.
    // Use a fresh ERC4626 implementation whose underlying matches the CDO asset.

    address attacker = makeAddr("attacker");
    uint256 poolDeposit = 1_000_000e6;
    uint256 inflationDonation = 1_000_000e6;

    // 1. Attacker becomes the first vault depositor.
    deal(address(underlying), attacker, 1 + inflationDonation);
    vm.startPrank(attacker);
    underlying.approve(address(externalVault), 1);
    uint256 attackerShares = externalVault.deposit(1, attacker);

    // 2. Inflate the vault share price by direct donation.
    underlying.transfer(address(externalVault), inflationDonation);
    vm.stopPrank();

    // 3. Honest LP deposits into the CDO and honest manager starts the epoch.
    deal(address(underlying), lender, poolDeposit);
    vm.startPrank(lender);
    underlying.approve(address(cdo), poolDeposit);
    cdo.depositAA(poolDeposit);
    vm.stopPrank();

    vm.prank(manager);
    cdo.startEpoch();

    // 4. ProgrammableBorrower deposited pool principal but received zero/dust shares.
    uint256 cdoVaultShares = externalVault.balanceOf(address(programmableBorrower));
    assertEq(cdoVaultShares, 0);

    // 5. Attacker redeems its initial share and captures both donation and pool deposit.
    vm.prank(attacker);
    uint256 stolen = externalVault.redeem(attackerShares, attacker, attacker);
    assertGe(stolen, inflationDonation + poolDeposit);

    // 6. Vault accounting sees a full-principal loss but reports only zero net interest.
    assertEq(programmableBorrower.vaultLoss(), poolDeposit);
    assertEq(programmableBorrower.totalInterestDueNow(), 0);

    // CDO strategy-token backing remains at par until an explicit loss/default path is used.
    assertEq(strategy.balanceOf(address(cdo)), poolDeposit);
}
```

The precise share amount may be `1` rather than `0` on vaults that reject zero-share mints; choose the initial donation so the pool receives economically worthless dust while the attacker retains nearly all vault shares.
