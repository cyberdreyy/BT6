### Title
Stale per-epoch instant-withdraw claim basis is never released on normal claims, inflating default recovery accounting and freezing recovery funds - (contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
The external bug is a refcount leak: `of_node_get()` acquires a reference that is never dropped. The vault analog is the instant-withdraw receipt counters `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[user][epoch]` and the aggregate `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]`, which are incremented in `requestInstantWithdraw` (lines 371-372) but are only ever decremented inside the *defaulted* claim path `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` (lines 847-853). The normal funded claim path `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` (lines 380-393) zeroes only the aggregate `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` and burns the receipt, permanently leaking the per-epoch reference. That leaked basis is later consumed by `defaultPendingClaimBasis` and `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve` at `finalizeDefaultRecovery`, corrupting the recovery price.

### Finding Description
- `requestInstantWithdraw` records the claim three ways: `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epochNumber]`, and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` (lines 366-374).
- When a user claims normally via `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, only `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` is cleared (line 391); both per-epoch counters retain the already-paid amount — the unreleased "reference".
- `defaultPendingClaimBasis` (lines 644-649) adds the full `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` to the claim basis whenever `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` at default finalization, so already-paid instant claims are counted as still owed.
- `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve` (lines 716-723) computes `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber] - pendingInstantWithdraws` and credits it to `reserveAmount` as cash supposedly still held by the strategy — but amounts already claimed via `_transferFundedClaim` (line 906) have physically left the contract.
- `finalizeDefaultRecovery` (line 686) therefore computes `recoveryPrice = reserveAmount * RECOVERY_FULL / totalBasis` on phantom cash, and `_transferDefaultRecovery` (line 915) later decrements `defaultRecoveryReserve` per claim until `safeTransfer` underflows.

Additionally, for a user who claimed an instant receipt and then holds any remaining same-epoch instant receipt, `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` computes `claimBasis = instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][defaultEpoch]` (line 844) including the already-paid amount, so `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user] -= claimBasis` (line 848) underflows and permanently blocks that user's claim.

### Impact Explanation
If the borrower defaults during an epoch where instant withdrawals were only partially funded (`pendingInstantWithdraws != 0`), every instant claim that was already paid in that epoch is double-counted in the recovery basis. `recoveryPrice` is computed against cash that no longer exists, so claims paid early from `defaultRecoveryReserve` are overvalued and the last claimants' `_transferDefaultRecovery` calls underflow on the token transfer — their recovery is permanently frozen in the strategy. Quantified loss equals the total already-claimed instant amount `A` in the defaulted epoch: the reserve is short by exactly `A`, and affected claimants lose up to `A` in aggregate of entitled recovery.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires: instant withdrawals enabled (`_isInstantWithdrawEnabled`), partial funding of the instant queue at `startEpoch` (so `pendingInstantWithdraws` stays non-zero), a successful normal `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` by any user in that epoch, and a borrower default in the same epoch before `stopEpoch`. The default itself is an honest sequence (borrower underfunds repayment), not attacker-caused, which fits the threat model; the accounting flaw is fully deterministic once those conditions hold. No guard recovers it: `_ensureDefaultRecoveryInitialized` even documents "Pending instant withdrawals are never cleared automatically" (line 923), and nothing reconciles the per-epoch maps on the funded path.

### Recommendation
Mirror the funded claim in the per-epoch ledgers: in `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, before zeroing `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, clear `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epochNumber]` (or the specific request epochs) and decrement `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]` by the claimed amount, so `defaultPendingClaimBasis` and `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve` reflect only genuinely unpaid claims. Alternatively, snapshot the still-unfunded instant basis at finalization instead of reusing the gross per-epoch counter.

### Proof of Concept
A Foundry fork test: (1) deposit via `depositAA`, `startEpoch`; (2) enable instant withdrawals and trigger the APR-drop condition so `requestWithdraw` routes to `requestInstantWithdraw` for attacker amount `A` and a second user amount `B`; (3) at next `startEpoch`, `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` funds only `A` (`pendingInstantWithdraws = B`); (4) attacker calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` and receives `A` — `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]` still equals `A+B`; (5) borrower defaults (`_handleBorrowerDefault`), manager calls `finalizeDefault(R, source)`; `defaultPendingClaimBasis` counts `A+B` while prefunded reserve credits `A` of phantom cash → `defaultRecoveryPrice` too high; (6) user's `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` for `B` consumes reserve at the inflated price; the final claimant's `_transferDefaultRecovery` reverts on `safeTransfer` with `A` underlying permanently locked in the strategy. Assert `underlying.balanceOf(strategy) < defaultRecoveryReserve` after all possible claims.
