### Title
Unvalidated ERC4626 deposit lets a vault shareholder steal programmable-borrower principal through share-price inflation - (File: `contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol`)

### Summary
`ProgrammableBorrower` trusts the number of shares returned by the configured ERC4626 vault and does not enforce a minimum conversion rate when epoch liquidity is deposited. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
At epoch start, the adapter snapshots the pre-deposit asset value as `epochStartVaultAssets`, deposits all idle underlying into the ERC4626 vault, and accepts whatever share amount `vault.deposit()` returns without a `minShares`, expected-share, or post-deposit valuation check. [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

An unprivileged user of that ERC4626 vault can pre-position as the dominant shareholder and inflate the vault’s assets-per-share before the manager starts the epoch. [5](#0-4) 

The borrower adapter then mints only a tiny share balance for the credit pool’s deposit, while the attacker redeems the dominant share position and removes nearly all pooled assets, including the newly deposited principal. [6](#0-5) [5](#0-4) 

The accounting detects this only as a negative vault delta, and `totalInterestDueNow()` floors the result at zero instead of forcing the realized principal loss into the CDO’s tranche accounting. [7](#0-6) 

In minted-interest programmable mode, a normal stop requests no interest cash, mints strategy tokens for reported interest, and can therefore complete even though the principal held through the ERC4626 position was already extracted. [8](#0-7) 

### Impact Explanation
The attacker can steal approximately `P * (1 - P/(A+D))`, where `P` is the pool deposit, `A` is the attacker-controlled vault asset base, and `D` is the donation or other price-inflation amount used before `vault.deposit()`. [9](#0-8) [4](#0-3) 

With `D = 100 * P`, the programmable borrower receives roughly 1% of the attacker’s share count and loses approximately 99% of the deposited principal to the attacker’s redemption. [6](#0-5) 

The loss is hidden from tranche prices during a successful zero-interest stop because minted-interest mode requires no interest transfer and does not burn strategy tokens for the vault principal shortfall. [8](#0-7) 

The stolen backing is discovered only when pending withdrawals or a close-pool recall require cash, at which point `getFundsFromBorrower()` fails and the pool enters default with insufficient recovery assets. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker only needs to be an ordinary depositor or shareholder of the configured ERC4626 vault and does not need the borrower, manager, owner, guardian, Keyring administrator, or queue to misbehave. [12](#0-11) [13](#0-12) 

The attack can be staged before an honest manager starts the next epoch because `startEpoch` unconditionally sweeps all idle underlying into the external vault. [14](#0-13) [15](#0-14) 

Likelihood depends on whether the selected production ERC4626 exposes a manipulable assets-per-share calculation; vaults that ignore raw-token donations or implement robust virtual-share protections reduce this risk, but the adapter does not constrain deployments to such vaults or validate the realized conversion. [16](#0-15) [5](#0-4) 

### Recommendation
Require `vault.deposit()` to satisfy a minimum expected share amount derived from a pre-deposit `convertToShares(assets)` quote and a bounded slippage parameter, and revert the epoch start if the received shares or post-deposit `convertToAssets(shares)` are below that bound. [17](#0-16) 

The same protection should be applied to repayments redeployed into the vault, because `_repay()` also trusts the ERC4626 conversion when recycling active-epoch repayments. [18](#0-17) 

A vault-loss amount that exceeds accrued interest should also be surfaced to the CDO as a realized principal loss rather than being clamped to zero interest while the CDO strategy-token balance remains unchanged. [7](#0-6) [19](#0-18) 

### Proof of Concept
A reproducible Foundry test should fork mainnet for the USDC-denominated programmable-vault setup used by `TestProgrammableBorrowerCreditVault`, deploy or select an ERC4626 whose `convertToAssets()` reflects donated underlying, seed the attacker as the dominant shareholder, inflate the share price, let the honest manager call `startEpoch`, and redeem the attacker’s shares. [20](#0-19)

    uint256 borrowerAssets = vault.convertToAssets(borrowerShares);

    vm.prank(attacker);

    vm.prank(manager);

    vm.prank(lender);
    cdoEpoch.requestWithdraw(0, address(aaTranche));
