### Title
Instant-withdraw claims pay the unfunded aggregate: a second `requestInstantWithdraw` packed into `instantWithdrawsRequests[user]` is paid at par alongside already-funded receipts, draining other claimants' collected funds - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
`IdleCreditVault.requestInstantWithdraw` folds every new instant-withdraw receipt into a single per-user aggregate `instantWithdrawsRequests[user] += _amount`, while `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns and pays out that entire aggregate with no per-epoch funding check. Per-epoch bookkeeping (`instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch`/`instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch`) exists only for default recovery; it is never consulted on the funded-claim path. This is the vault analog of the external bug: packing a new deferred item into an existing bucket silently reuses the "already settled" state of the previous batch, so an unfunded receipt inherits the funded status of the older one.

### Finding Description
In `contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol`:

- `requestInstantWithdraw` (L356-375) burns strategy tokens, mints a receipt, and adds `_amount` to the aggregate `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` and to `pendingInstantWithdraws`. There is no guard preventing a user who already holds an unclaimed funded receipt from opening a second request.
- `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` (L398-403) only decrements `pendingInstantWithdraws`; collected underlying just sits in the strategy's balance.
- `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` (L380-393) reads `uint256 amount = instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, burns the receipt tokens, zeroes the aggregate, and calls `_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount)`. The epoch guard used for normal claims (`epochNumber <= lastWithdrawRequest`) does not exist here, and `pendingInstantWithdraws` (the unfunded remainder) is never checked against the claim.

Attack sequence (running epoch, `allowInstantWithdraw` enabled, e.g. via `setInstantWithdrawParams`):

1. Epoch N: attacker calls `requestInstantWithdraw(100)` via the CDO. Borrower funding arrives at `startEpoch`; `collectInstantWithdrawFunds(100)` leaves 100 underlying in the strategy; `pendingInstantWithdraws` = 0.
2. Attacker does not claim. During the next running epoch, attacker calls `requestInstantWithdraw(50)`. Now `instantWithdrawsRequests[attacker] = 150` but only 100 is funded; `pendingInstantWithdraws` = 50.
3. Another user's funded instant collection (or any funded claim reserve) brings the strategy's underlying balance to ≥150.
4. Attacker calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` through `IdleCDOEpochVariant.claimInstantWithdrawRequest` → `_transferFundedClaim` pays 150: the 100 owed plus 50 belonging to other pending instant claimants or the funded reserve.

The per-epoch mappings are written (`instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[user][epoch] +=`) but read only in `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`, so they cannot stop this.

### Impact Explanation
Direct theft: the attacker's unfunded 50-unit receipt is paid at par out of underlying that was collected to back other users' instant-withdraw receipts (or the default-recovery reserve). The loss equals the attacker's unfunded second request, bounded only by funded liquidity sitting in the strategy at claim time. Victims' subsequent `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` calls then underflow/revert on the strategy balance, permanently freezing their funded claims until insolvency is absorbed elsewhere.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires `allowInstantWithdraw = true` (a supported mode, exercised in tests) and a window where collected funds for other claimants coexist with the attacker's unfunded request — a normal condition, since `pendingInstantWithdraws` is a global remainder that is only refilled at the next `startEpoch` borrower transfer. Any unprivileged tranche-token holder (KYC-passing lender) can execute it; no privileged action is needed beyond the honest manager's regular epoch calls.

### Recommendation
On `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, pay only the funded portion: e.g. cap the payout at `instantWithdrawsRequests[user]` minus the user's share of `pendingInstantWithdraws`, or track per-user unfunded basis and revert/skip the unfunded remainder. Alternatively, revert in `requestInstantWithdraw` when `instantWithdrawsRequests[user] != 0` (mirroring the post-default `postDefaultRequests` guard) so an unclaimed funded receipt cannot be merged with a new unfunded one.
