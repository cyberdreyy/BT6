### Title
Fees continue to accrue for the entire duration a vault is paused in `PriceAndFeeCalculatorV2`, charged retroactively on unpause - ([File: v3/src/core/PriceAndFeeCalculatorV2.sol])

### Summary
`PriceAndFeeCalculatorV2` documents that "Paused vaults don't accrue fees" [1](#0-0) , but the `accrualLag` bookkeeping mechanism used to delay accrual across a pause actually rolls the full paused duration into `timeDelta` when fees are finally accrued at `unpauseVault`, causing TVL/performance fees to be charged for the entire window during which depositors/redeemers were unable to act. This mirrors the reported Perennial pattern: users pay time-based fees for a period in which they had no ability to change their position.

### Finding Description
When an anchor price update is submitted while the vault is not yet paused but triggers a pause condition (`_shouldPauseAnchor`), the contract stores the elapsed time as lag instead of accruing fees immediately: `vaultPriceState.accrualLag = timestamp - oldAnchorTimestamp;` [2](#0-1) . While the vault remains paused, any further `setAnchorPrice` calls simply keep accumulating into `accrualLag`: `vaultPriceState.accrualLag += timestamp - oldAnchorTimestamp;` [3](#0-2) . `setDriftPrice` reverts while paused via `require(!vaultPriceState.paused, ...)`, and the `ProvisionerV2` blocks deposits/redeems while `PRICE_FEE_CALCULATOR.isVaultPaused(...)` is true [4](#0-3) , confirming users cannot act during this window (matching the "protocol paused" precondition of the source report).

When `unpauseVault(vault, price, timestamp)` is finally called, it requires `timestamp == vaultPriceState.anchorTimestamp` (i.e., no new time contribution) and then calls `_accrueFees(vault, price, timestamp)` [5](#0-4) . Inside `_accrueFees`, `timeDelta = timestamp - vaultPriceState.anchorTimestamp + vaultPriceState.accrualLag;`, which reduces to `timeDelta = accrualLag` [6](#0-5) . That full `timeDelta` — which includes the entire paused duration, not just the pre-pause lag — is fed into `_calculateTvlFee(tvl, vaultAccruals.fees.tvl, timeDelta)` (annualized, linear in time) and into the performance-fee calculation [7](#0-6)  and [8](#0-7) . The result is charged against `vaultAccruals.accruedFees`/`accruedProtocolFees`, which are later paid out of the vault's fee-token balance via `claimFees`/`claimProtocolFees`, directly diluting unit holders' value.

The root cause is the same as the Perennial report: a time-based fee accrues across a period in which ordinary users had no ability to react (deposit, redeem, or reposition), because all user-facing entry points are gated on `!isVaultPaused`.

### Impact Explanation
TVL and performance fees computed with `timeDelta = accrualLag` overcharge the vault for the entire pause duration, which can be arbitrarily long (accountant-controlled). This fee is paid out of the vault's assets to the fee recipient/protocol, directly reducing the redeemable value for depositors who were frozen out of the vault and unable to withdraw or otherwise mitigate the loss — a live yield/value drain during a period of forced illiquidity, consistent with Medium-severity "protocol-caused fund loss / temporary freeze" impact.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only: (1) the vault's accountant/owner to pause via a bad anchor update or explicit `pauseVault` (a normal, expected operational action, not a privileged attacker action against the protocol's own invariant), and (2) time passing before `unpauseVault` is called with the same anchor price/timestamp. No collusion or malicious oracle input is needed beyond ordinary pause/unpause operation, which the documented behavior itself claims should not accrue fees — making this a straightforward violation of the contract's own stated invariant, triggered under normal, expected pause/unpause usage.

### Recommendation
When accruing fees at unpause (or at the next anchor update after unpause), exclude the paused duration from `timeDelta`. Track the pause start/end timestamps and only accumulate `accrualLag` for time elapsed prior to the pause (already correct in the pre-pause case), but ensure no additional lag/time accrues for the interval between `_setVaultPaused(..., true)` and `_setVaultPaused(..., false)`. Concretely, freeze `accrualLag` at the value computed at the moment of pausing, and set `vaultPriceState.anchorTimestamp` to the unpause timestamp without adding further paused-interval time to `accrualLag` in the "already paused" branch.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy `PriceAndFeeCalculatorV2`, register a vault, call `setInitialPrice`, and `setThresholds` with a nonzero `tvl`/`performance` fee configured in `vaultAccruals.fees`.
2. Fast-forward time and call `setAnchorPrice` with a price update that trips `_shouldPauseAnchor` (e.g., a price move outside `minPriceToleranceRatio`/`maxPriceToleranceRatio`), causing `_setVaultPaused(..., true)` and `accrualLag = timestamp - oldAnchorTimestamp`.
3. While paused, assert `ProvisionerV2.deposit`/`redeem` revert with `Aera__PriceAndFeeCalculatorVaultPaused` (confirming users cannot act).
4. Fast-forward a long period (e.g., 30 days) while still paused — no further state changes needed since `accrualLag` is already locked in from step 2 (or optionally call `setAnchorPrice` again while paused to grow `accrualLag` further).
5. Call `unpauseVault(vault, storedAnchorPrice, storedAnchorTimestamp)`, which internally calls `_accrueFees` with `timeDelta = accrualLag` covering the entire paused window.
6. Assert `vaultAccruals.accruedFees`/`accruedProtocolFees` increased by an amount proportional to the full paused duration (via `_calculateTvlFee`), contradicting the NatSpec claim that "Paused vaults don't accrue fees," and demonstrate that depositors' redeemable value (via `claimFees` payout) is reduced by fees attributable purely to time they were frozen out of the vault.

    /// @param timeDelta The duration of the fee period in seconds
    /// @return The earned TVL fee
    function _calculateTvlFee(uint256 averageValue, uint256 tvlFee, uint256 timeDelta)
        internal
        pure
        returns (uint256)
