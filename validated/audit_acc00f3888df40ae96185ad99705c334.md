### Title
Stale-epoch instant-withdraw receipts are excluded from default recovery basis, inflating the recovery price for current-epoch claimants while permanently freezing the older funded receipts - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
Analogous to the matrix-js-sdk protocol-confusion bug (origin determined by a claimed field rather than the authoritative channel), `IdleCreditVault` attributes an instant-withdraw receipt's "origin" to the *current* epoch at finalization instead of the epoch where the receipt was actually recorded. `defaultPendingClaimBasis()` adds only `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` to the defaulted claim basis, and `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest()` only clears `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][defaultRecoveryEpoch]`. An unfunded instant receipt created in an earlier epoch is therefore invisible to recovery accounting: it is not haircutted into the reserve, yet it also cannot be paid, because `_transferFundedClaim()` forbids spending `defaultRecoveryReserve`. New-epoch claimants and active LPs share a recovery price computed on an under-counted basis.

### Finding Description
- `requestInstantWithdraw` records the receipt under the epoch live at request time: `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][currentEpoch]` and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[currentEpoch]` [1](#0-0) .
- If the borrower underfunds the instant queue, the remainder sits in `pendingInstantWithdraws` across epoch boundaries; `epochNumber` advances on the next `stopEpoch`/`deposit` while the per-epoch mappings keep the old key.
- At `finalizeDefaultRecovery`, `defaultPendingClaimBasis()` only adds `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` — the *finalization* epoch — to `pendingWithdraws` [2](#0-1) . Old-epoch instant claims are silently dropped from `totalBasis`, so `recoveryPrice = reserveAmount / totalBasis` is inflated.
- `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve()` likewise computes `instantBasis - pendingInstant` using only the current epoch bucket [3](#0-2) , so underlying already collected for the old receipt is swept into the shared reserve without a matching basis.
- After finalization, `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` clears only `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[user][defaultEpoch]`; the stale receipt stays in `instantWithdrawsRequests[user]` and falls through to the par claim, which burns the receipt and calls `_transferFundedClaim` — but that path reverts whenever `balance - defaultRecoveryReserve < amount` [4](#0-3) . The old receipt's funds are already folded into `defaultRecoveryReserve`, so the claim can never be satisfied.

### Impact Explanation
Two-sided loss:
1. **Inflated recovery for others / stolen reserve share**: the excluded old-epoch basis raises `defaultRecoveryPrice`, so every current-epoch defaulted claimant and every active LP (whose NAV is re-minted to `activeBasis * recoveryPrice` [5](#0-4) ) is paid above the fair pro-rata ratio. The excess is exactly the prefunded instant cash plus the share the old claimant should have received.
2. **Permanent freezing**: the older instant receipt holder's tokens remain in `instantWithdrawsRequests` but can never be claimed — `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` reverts at `_transferFundedClaim` because the funds were absorbed into the reserve. Loss equals the full prefunded amount of the stale receipt.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a sequence that is fully inside normal operation with honest privileged roles: borrower partially underfunds instant withdrawals in epoch N (the code explicitly supports a nonzero `pendingInstantWithdraws` remainder, and the test suite shows mixed funded/unfunded instant states), the pool runs to epoch N+1, then the borrower defaults and `finalizeDefault` is called. An attacker only needs to hold a current-epoch instant receipt or active tranche position at finalization to receive the inflated recovery; alternatively the attacker is simply the actor who times a fresh `requestInstantWithdraw` in the default epoch to maximize the diluted-basis payout. No malicious privileged role is needed.

### Recommendation
Aggregate instant basis across all epochs at finalization instead of reading a single epoch key — e.g., keep a running `totalInstantClaimBasis` updated in `requestInstantWithdraw`/`claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, and add it to `defaultPendingClaimBasis` whenever `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0`. In `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`, clear `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` itself (the aggregate) rather than only `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][defaultEpoch]`, applying `defaultRecoveryPrice` to the whole unfunded balance while still paying the funded portion at par. `defaultRecoveryEpoch`-keyed clearing should be replaced by iterating or aggregating all non-zero per-epoch receipt buckets for the user.

### Proof of Concept
A Foundry fork PoC (same harness as `test/foundry/IdleCreditVault.t.sol`):

1. Enable instant withdrawals via `cdoEpoch.setInstantWithdrawParams(delay, aprDelta, false)`; user A deposits AA and calls `requestWithdraw` during a low-APR epoch so the receipt routes to `requestInstantWithdraw` in epoch N.
2. At `startEpoch`, have the borrower fund only part of the instant queue so `pendingInstantWithdraws > 0` and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[N] > pendingInstantWithdraws` (partial prefunding).
3. Advance through `stopEpoch` into epoch N+1 (A cannot claim: her funded part accounting is stuck behind the unfunded remainder). Attacker B deposits and files a fresh instant withdraw in epoch N+1.
4. Borrower underfunds repayment → `stopEpoch` → `defaulted` → `finalizeDefault(recovered, manager)`.
5. Assert: `defaultPendingClaimBasis()` includes only `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[N+1]`; `defaultRecoveryPrice` is larger than `reserve / (activeBasis + pendingWithdraws + instantClaims[N] + instantClaims[N+1])`; B's `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` succeeds at the inflated price; A's subsequent `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` reverts in `_transferFundedClaim` (`balance - reserve < amount`), permanently freezing her funded receipt.

Caveat: I verified the single-epoch-key reads in `defaultPendingClaimBasis`, `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve`, and `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`, and the reserve guard in `_transferFundedClaim`. I was not able to fully trace every path that can leave a nonzero `pendingInstantWithdraws` across an epoch boundary (e.g., whether `startEpoch`/`getInstantWithdrawFunds` guarantees eventual full funding absent default); if the protocol strictly forces full funding before the next epoch, the stale-receipt state may be unreachable and this reduces to a no-finding. A fork test confirming a persistent `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[N]` remainder into epoch N+1 is the decisive check.
