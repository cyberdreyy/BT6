## Analysis

The question is correct that a taker can construct destination immutables where `integratorFeeAmountCd() + protocolFeeAmountCd() > immutables.amount`, causing `EscrowDst._withdraw` to permanently revert with an arithmetic underflow. Tracing the root cause further reveals the impact is worse than a simple freeze: it enables outright theft of the maker's source-chain funds by a malicious taker.

### Title
Taker-controlled destination fee parameters in `createDstEscrow` cause permanent underflow-revert of `EscrowDst.withdraw`, letting a malicious taker steal the maker's source funds while reclaiming their own destination deposit via `cancel` - (File: `contracts/BaseEscrowFactory.sol`, `contracts/EscrowDst.sol`)

### Summary
`BaseEscrowFactory.createDstEscrow` accepts `dstImmutables` entirely from `msg.sender` (the taker) with no on-chain cross-check against the fee values that were computed and emitted by `_postInteraction` on the source chain (`DstImmutablesComplement.parameters`). A taker can therefore deploy an `EscrowDst` whose encoded `protocolFeeAmount`/`integratorFeeAmount` sum exceeds `amount`. This makes every call to `EscrowDst._withdraw` revert on `uint256 amount = immutables.amount - integratorFeeAmount - protocolFeeAmount` [1](#0-0)  — permanently blocking the maker's destination payout through both `withdraw` and `publicWithdraw`. Crucially, `EscrowDst.cancel` never reads or subtracts the fee fields at all, transferring the full `immutables.amount` back to the taker [2](#0-1) , so the malicious taker fully recovers their own deposit after the cancellation timelock, while the maker never receives anything on the destination chain.

### Finding Description
`createDstEscrow` performs only self-consistency checks (native value matches `amount`+`safetyDeposit`, and timelock ordering against `srcCancellationTimestamp`) before hashing the caller-supplied immutables as the CREATE2 salt and deploying the clone [3](#0-2) . There is no validation tying `dstImmutables.parameters` (fee amounts/recipients) to the values computed during `_postInteraction` and emitted in `SrcEscrowCreated`/`DstImmutablesComplement` on the source chain [4](#0-3) . This means the fee-encoding bytes inside `parameters` are entirely taker-controlled at destination-escrow-creation time.

Because `EscrowDst._withdraw` blindly does `immutables.amount - integratorFeeAmount - protocolFeeAmount` without a bounds check [5](#0-4) , a taker who sets `protocolFeeAmount + integratorFeeAmount > amount` guarantees this line always reverts with a Panic (underflow), for `withdraw`, `publicWithdraw`, and any future caller — there is no way for the maker to ever receive their destination-chain funds from this escrow.

Meanwhile `EscrowSrc.withdraw`/`_withdrawTo` performs a plain, fee-independent transfer of `immutables.amount` to the taker once the secret is known [6](#0-5) , and `EscrowDst.cancel` returns the taker's full deposited `amount` (again fee-independent) plus the safety deposit once `DstCancellation` time is reached [2](#0-1) . The taker is the one who receives the secret from the maker (via the normal off-chain fusion+ protocol flow) in order to call `EscrowDst.withdraw` in the first place, so no extra information leak is needed for the taker to also call `EscrowSrc.withdraw(secret, srcImmutables)`.

### Impact Explanation
Combining these facts, a malicious/unprivileged taker can:
1. Fill an order normally, causing `EscrowSrc` to lock the maker's source tokens.
2. Call `createDstEscrow` with `parameters` encoding `protocolFeeAmount + integratorFeeAmount > amount`, funding `EscrowDst` with their own tokens/safety deposit.
3. Receive the secret from the maker per the normal protocol flow (required to ever call `withdraw`), and use it to withdraw the maker's tokens from `EscrowSrc` — this call is unaffected by the dst-side bug.
4. Let `EscrowDst.withdraw`/`publicWithdraw` remain permanently unusable (underflow revert), so the maker's destination payout never happens.
5. After the `DstCancellation` timelock elapses, call `EscrowDst.cancel` to reclaim 100% of their own destination deposit and safety deposit back, since `cancel` does not apply/validate the fee fields.

Net effect: the taker walks away with the maker's source-chain assets and also recovers their own destination-chain deposit, while the maker receives nothing on the destination chain — a direct theft of maker funds. This meets the Critical bar ("direct theft of user funds at rest or in motion... caused by these production contracts").

### Likelihood Explanation
The entrypoint (`createDstEscrow`) is fully permissionless and callable by any taker without any privileged role, and requires no admin/governance action or off-chain trust breach — only a taker willing to construct malicious calldata. The only mitigating factor is off-chain verification by makers/relayers before releasing the secret, but that is an off-chain process, not an on-chain guarantee, and the contracts provide no on-chain enforcement that dst fee parameters match the source-committed values.

### Recommendation
- In `createDstEscrow`, validate that the fee parameters (protocol/integrator fee amounts and recipients) encoded in `dstImmutables.parameters` are consistent with values previously committed on the source chain (e.g., store/verify against the `DstImmutablesComplement` emitted in `SrcEscrowCreated`, or otherwise bind fee data to signed order data rather than trusting arbitrary taker-supplied calldata).
- In `EscrowDst._withdraw`, defensively validate `integratorFeeAmount + protocolFeeAmount <= immutables.amount` (mirroring the check already done in `_postInteraction`, `if (integratorFeeAmount + protocolFeeAmount >= takingAmount) revert InvalidFeeAmounts();` [7](#0-6) ) so a malformed escrow cannot be deployed in the first place, or so that a controlled revert/graceful handling occurs instead of relying on ordering with `cancel`.
- Ensure `EscrowDst.cancel` and `withdraw` treat fee accounting consistently so a taker cannot use `cancel` to bypass fee obligations that should have gone to the maker.

### Proof of Concept
1. Taker fills an order via LOP; `_postInteraction` deploys `EscrowSrc` with the maker's `makingAmount` of `order.makerAsset`.
2. Taker calls `createDstEscrow{value: safetyDeposit (+amount if native)}(dstImmutables, srcCancellationTimestamp)` where `dstImmutables.parameters = abi.encode(protocolFeeAmount, integratorFeeAmount, protocolFeeRecipient, integratorFeeRecipient)` with `protocolFeeAmount + integratorFeeAmount > dstImmutables.amount`.
3. Once the maker shares the secret (standard protocol step), taker calls `EscrowSrc.withdraw(secret, srcImmutables)` — succeeds, taker receives maker's source tokens.
4. Taker (or anyone) calls `EscrowDst.withdraw(secret, dstImmutables)` — reverts with `Panic(0x11)` due to `immutables.amount - integratorFeeAmount - protocolFeeAmount` underflow; same for `publicWithdraw`.
5. After `DstCancellation` timelock, taker calls `EscrowDst.cancel(dstImmutables)` — succeeds, returning full `amount` + `safetyDeposit` to the taker.
6. Result: maker receives nothing on destination chain; taker receives maker's source tokens and reclaims their own destination deposit in full.

        _uniTransfer(immutables.token.get(), immutables.maker.get(), amount);
        _ethTransfer(msg.sender, immutables.safetyDeposit);
        emit EscrowWithdrawal(secret);
    }

    function createDstEscrow(IBaseEscrow.Immutables calldata dstImmutables, uint256 srcCancellationTimestamp) external payable {
        address token = dstImmutables.token.get();
        uint256 nativeAmount = dstImmutables.safetyDeposit;
        if (token == address(0)) {
            nativeAmount += dstImmutables.amount;
        }
        if (msg.value != nativeAmount) revert InsufficientEscrowBalance();

        IBaseEscrow.Immutables memory immutables = dstImmutables;
        immutables.timelocks = immutables.timelocks.setDeployedAt(block.timestamp);
        // Check that the escrow cancellation will start not later than the cancellation time on the source chain.
        if (immutables.timelocks.get(TimelocksLib.Stage.DstCancellation) > srcCancellationTimestamp) revert InvalidCreationTime();

        bytes32 salt = immutables.hashMem();
        address escrow = _deployEscrow(salt, msg.value, ESCROW_DST_IMPLEMENTATION);
        if (token != address(0)) {
            IERC20(token).safeTransferFrom(msg.sender, escrow, immutables.amount);
        }

        emit DstEscrowCreated(escrow, immutables.hashlock, immutables.taker);
    }
