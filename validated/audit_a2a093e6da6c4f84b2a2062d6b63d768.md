### Title
Proposer-slashing evidence destroys an honest builder's already-committed payment - ([File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md])

### Summary
`process_proposer_slashing` in the `gloas` fork clears the corresponding `BuilderPendingPayment` slot whenever the slashed validator's index matches the payment's `proposer_index`, keyed only by `slot` — with no check on which of the two equivocating headers is the one that actually became canonical. Anyone can submit proposer-slashing evidence (it only requires two different signed headers for the same slot/proposer, which is a normal, permissionless operation once a proposer double-signs), and doing so wipes out a `BuilderPendingPayment` that may already represent a legitimately delivered, attested-to (weight-accumulating) payload for the canonical block at that slot.

### Finding Description
`process_builder_pending_payments`/`process_attestation` accumulate `weight` on a `BuilderPendingPayment` keyed by `slot % SLOTS_PER_EPOCH` (or the equivalent offset for the previous epoch) as validators attest to the canonical head, tracking progress toward the quorum needed to actually pay the builder [1](#0-0) . This weight is what the network relies on to eventually settle the payment into `builder_pending_withdrawals` [2](#0-1) .

`process_proposer_slashing` however clears this payment slot based purely on `slot` and `header_1.proposer_index` equality, with no verification that the header being slashed corresponds to the actual canonical block whose payload/bid produced the pending payment: [3](#0-2) 

The inline comment in the spec itself acknowledges an awareness of griefing risk ("otherwise an unrelated same-slot equivocation could grief an honest proposer's payment") and added a `proposer_index` match as a partial mitigation, but this mitigation is insufficient: proposer slashing evidence, by construction, always consists of two *different* headers signed by the *same* proposer for the *same* slot. One of those headers is necessarily unrelated to (or even predates) the canonical chain, yet submitting the slashing still zeroes the payment slot for that slot/proposer pair — even if the canonical block at that slot already has attested weight (potentially already past quorum) tied to a builder that honestly delivered its payload. This exactly mirrors the Sherlock/Flayer `CollectionShutdown` bug class: a state-mutating "side door" (here, permissionless proposer-slashing evidence) can silently override/reset a value (`BuilderPendingPayment.weight`/`withdrawal.amount`) that an in-progress-tracking mechanism (the payment's accrued attestation weight) was supposed to protect from being nulled once meaningfully underway, without any authorization from the payment's associated builder or from majority validator/protocol consent regarding which of the two headers should govern payment resolution.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the equality that a builder payment committed to and confirmed by chain attestations for the canonical block must be honored: `BuilderPendingPayment.empty()` erases both `weight` and the `withdrawal` (amount, builder_index, fee_recipient), so the builder's legitimately earned Gwei is destroyed rather than routed to `builder_pending_withdrawals`. This matches the in-scope "Critical" impact bucket: "Gwei created, destroyed or paid to a non-owner" and "a payload executed or paid that the block did not commit to" (here, inverted: a payload delivered and paid-for per the committed bid is not paid). The loss is not the attacker's own stake — it is the honest builder's payment, an unrelated third party.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is non-trivial but not guaranteed on every slot: it requires the proposer at a given slot to have double-signed two headers (a slashable but not-uncommon occurrence in practice due to client failover/misconfiguration, and something any validator can trigger even inadvertently), and for the slashing evidence to be broadcast/included after the corresponding `BuilderPendingPayment` has accrued attestation weight (or after a withdrawal-eligible weight has been reached, but before the epoch-boundary settlement in `process_builder_pending_payments`/`settle_builder_payment` has run). Since inclusion of proposer-slashing evidence is itself proposer-triggered but requires no coordination or majority — a single equivocation plus a single inclusion by any future block producer is sufficient — this is a low-cost, unprivileged trigger available to any observer who detects proposer equivocation.

### Recommendation
Only clear the `BuilderPendingPayment` when the slashing evidence identifies the *specific* header (block root/hash) that produced the pending payment (e.g., compare `payment`'s associated block root, or bind the payment record to the block root it was generated for, not just `proposer_index`/`slot`), or alternatively refrain from clearing the payment on proposer-slashing entirely and rely solely on the standard quorum-weight / two-epoch eviction logic already present in `process_builder_pending_payments` to decide payment fate.

### Proof of Concept
1. Proposer `P` is selected for slot `S`. `P` signs two different `BeaconBlockHeader`s for slot `S` — `H1` (which becomes canonical after being built into a full block with a builder's bid) and `H2` (an orphaned/unused header, e.g., generated by a failover signer).
2. The canonical block for slot `S` includes a non-empty `ExecutionPayloadBid` from builder `B`; per `process_attestation`, subsequent slots' attestations to slot `S` accumulate `weight` on `state.builder_pending_payments[payment_index]` where `payment.withdrawal.builder_index == B` [1](#0-0) .
3. Before the two-epoch settlement window elapses, any actor (not `B`, not `P`, and without majority stake) submits a `ProposerSlashing(H1, H2)` in a later block.
4. `process_proposer_slashing` matches `payment.proposer_index == header_1.proposer_index` for slot `S` and executes `state.builder_pending_payments[payment_index] = BuilderPendingPayment.empty()` [4](#0-3) , erasing `B`'s already-attested-to withdrawal amount regardless of its accumulated weight.
5. `B` never receives the corresponding `BuilderPendingWithdrawal`; the Gwei that was committed by the canonical block and confirmed by validator attestations is permanently destroyed.

def settle_builder_payment(state: BeaconState, payment_index: Uint64) -> None:
    assert payment_index < len(state.builder_pending_payments)
    payment = state.builder_pending_payments[payment_index]
    if payment.withdrawal.amount > 0:
        state.builder_pending_withdrawals.append(payment.withdrawal)
    state.builder_pending_payments[payment_index] = BuilderPendingPayment.empty()

```python
def process_builder_pending_payments(state: BeaconState) -> None:
    """
    Processes the builder pending payments from the previous epoch.
    """
    quorum = get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold(state)
    for payment in state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH]:
        if payment.weight >= quorum:
            state.builder_pending_withdrawals.append(payment.withdrawal)

    old_payments = state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:]
    state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH] = old_payments
    new_payments = [BuilderPendingPayment.empty() for _ in range(SLOTS_PER_EPOCH)]
    state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:] = new_payments
