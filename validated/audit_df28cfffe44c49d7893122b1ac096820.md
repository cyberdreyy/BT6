### Title
`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` blindly pays the full receipt without checking that it was funded — the "insufficient headroom" analog ([File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol])

### Summary
`IdleCreditVault.claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns the user's entire `instantWithdrawsRequests` receipt and transfers the full amount via `_transferFundedClaim`, blindly assuming the corresponding underlying was already pushed into the strategy by `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` — exactly the kernel bug's pattern of prepending bytes under an unverified headroom assumption.

### Finding Description
The unfunded remainder of the instant-withdraw queue is tracked globally in `pendingInstantWithdraws` (incremented in `requestInstantWithdraw` at line 374, decremented in `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` at line 401). However, `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` (lines 380–393) never checks whether the user's claim is part of the still-unfunded remainder. It burns `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` in full and calls `_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount)` (lines 897–907), which transfers the full `amount` out of the contract's general underlying balance. The only guard is a `defaultRecoveryReserve` isolation check that applies solely after default finalization; pre-default, any underlying sitting in the strategy — including amounts collected for other users' funded instant claims and normal withdraw receipts funded via `collectWithdrawFunds` — is treated as available "headroom."

Partial funding of the instant queue is a real, documented state: the code comments at lines 637–640 and 713–715 acknowledge that "startEpoch moved the CDO's available cash to the strategy but that cash covered only part of the instant queue," and `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve` computes the already-funded portion precisely because claims and funding can diverge.

### Impact Explanation
If a claim is executed while the caller's receipt is partially or wholly unfunded (`pendingInstantWithdraws` still covers it), the strategy pays out underlyings earmarked for other claimants — funded instant receipts of other users or funded normal withdraw receipts. The claimant receives more than their funded entitlement (direct theft of other users' withdrawal proceeds), and subsequent legitimate claims revert on insufficient balance, permanently freezing the remaining funded receipts. Loss is bounded by the unfunded instant amount claimed, i.e., up to the full `pendingInstantWithdraws` of the current epoch.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires the pool to be in a state where `pendingInstantWithdraws > 0` (a documented transient state around `startEpoch`/stop-epoch liquidity moves) and a `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` routed through `IdleCDOEpochVariant` for a user whose receipt overlaps the unfunded remainder. The attacker only needs to be a tranche-token holder who calls `requestInstantWithdraw` and then claims during the unfunded window, sequencing around honest manager/owner epoch calls — no privileged role is needed. Residual uncertainty: the `IdleCDOEpochVariant.claimInstantWithdrawRequest` wrapper was not fully read in this pass; if it hard-gates claims on full funding via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` in the same call, the exploit path may be unreachable and this reduces to a defense-in-depth gap rather than an exploitable bug.

### Recommendation
In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, cap the paid amount to the funded portion of the user's receipt — e.g., compute the funded claimable amount from `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` minus the user's share of `pendingInstantWithdraws` (tracked via `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch`), or revert if the claim exceeds funded backing — instead of blindly transferring the full receipt. Additionally, make `_transferFundedClaim` (or the caller) verify `balanceOf(this) - reservedForOtherClaims >= amount`, mirroring `skb_cow_head`'s explicit headroom reservation before pushing bytes.
