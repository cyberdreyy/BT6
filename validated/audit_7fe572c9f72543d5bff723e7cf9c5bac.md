### Title
Instant-withdraw receipts are paid in full from strategy-held underlying even when the CDO never funded them — `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` lacks a funding check - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
CVE-2021-28701 is a use-after-free: Xen freed grant-table pages while a stale mapping still let the guest access them. The analog in `IdleCreditVault` is `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`: the `instantWithdrawsRequests` receipt keeps full spending power (a "stale mapping") even after the funds it points to were repurposed — namely when `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` covered only part (or none) of `pendingInstantWithdraws`. The claim path burns the whole aggregate receipt and pays it 1:1 via `_transferFundedClaim`, with no check that the corresponding underlying was ever collected from the CDO.

### Finding Description
- `requestInstantWithdraw` mints receipt tokens and adds to `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch`, `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch` and the global `pendingInstantWithdraws` (`IdleCreditVault.sol:356-375`).
- Funding is a separate, CDO-initiated step: `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` decrements `pendingInstantWithdraws` and pulls underlying (`IdleCreditVault.sol:398-403`). The code itself acknowledges partial funding is possible: `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve` computes `instantBasis - pendingInstant` as the "already-funded" share, and `defaultPendingClaimBasis`/`finalizeDefaultRecovery` treat `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` as the unfunded remainder (`IdleCreditVault.sol:644-649, 716-723`).
- `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, however, pays `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` in full through `_transferFundedClaim` with no funded/unfunded split (`IdleCreditVault.sol:380-393`). Only the post-default path (`_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`) haircuts receipts, and only when `defaultInstantWithdrawsFinalized` is set.

The invariant break: an unfunded instant receipt is a claim on underlying the strategy never received for that purpose. Like the Xen bug, the "page" (strategy-held underlying earmarked for other purposes — funded normal withdraws, loss-adjusted reserves from `collectWithdrawFunds`, `defaultRecoveryReserve`, or the borrower's returned funds) was logically freed/reassigned, but the instant receipt mapping still grants access to it.

### Impact Explanation
Concrete sequence: epoch running, instant withdrawals enabled; attacker requests instant withdraw of amount X. Epoch boundary is reached and the CDO collects only part of `pendingInstantWithdraws` (or the epoch ends while liquidity sits with the borrower). The attacker then calls `claimInstantWithdraw` via the CDO, which invokes `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, burning the full receipt and transferring X underlying at par. The shortfall is paid out of underlying reserved for other users' funded `withdrawsRequests` claims or loss-adjusted `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` payouts — direct theft / insolvency of honest claimants, quantified as the unfunded instant portion (up to `pendingInstantWithdraws`).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an instant-withdraw request to remain claimable while `pendingInstantWithdraws > 0` for that user (partial collection at epoch boundary, or a new request in a later epoch mixing with an unfunded older receipt, since the aggregate `instantWithdrawsRequests` is paid regardless of which epoch's basis was funded). All actors are unprivileged: a KYC-passing lender requests the instant withdrawal and claims it; the honest manager/borrower sequence their calls normally. The code's own comments confirm partially-funded instant queues are an expected state.

### Recommendation
Track funded vs unfunded instant receipts per user (e.g., a `fundedInstantClaims` balance incremented by `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` pro-rata or per-epoch), and have `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pay only the funded share, leaving the unfunded basis in `pendingInstantWithdraws` for the default-recovery path or later funding. Alternatively, gate claims so a receipt epoch with nonzero unfunded remainder cannot be paid at par.

### Proof of Concept
Not fully verified end-to-end: I was unable to read `IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol` to confirm whether `claimInstantWithdraw` is callable while `pendingInstantWithdraws` still covers part of the user's receipt, and whether the CDO itself blocks underfunded instant claims. If the CDO enforces full collection before claims, this finding collapses; the strategy-level gap (claim paying unfunded basis at par) still exists but would be unreachable. A Foundry fork PoC would: enable instant withdrawals, deposit and request instant withdraw, run `stopEpoch` with borrower liquidity sufficient to only partially fund `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`, then call `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` and assert the payout exceeds underlying actually collected for instant claims, draining funds owed to a second user's funded `withdrawsRequests` claim.
