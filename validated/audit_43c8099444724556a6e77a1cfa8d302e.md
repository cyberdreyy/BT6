### Title
Liquidation DoS via front-run partial debt repayment causing fixed-amount `repay()` overshoot revert - (File: `programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_account/liquidate.rs`)

### Summary
Marginfi's classic liquidation instruction, `lending_account_liquidate`, takes a liquidator-specified fixed `asset_amount` and derives a fixed liability repayment quantity (`liab_amount_final`) from it using the *current* oracle prices and bank state at simulation time. A borrower can front-run the liquidator's transaction with a small `lending_account_repay` on the same liability bank, shrinking their outstanding debt just enough that the liquidator's precomputed `liab_amount_final` now exceeds the position's actual remaining liability, causing the liquidation transaction to fail atomically.

### Finding Description
In `lending_account_liquidate`, the liquidator supplies `asset_amount` — a fixed quantity of collateral to seize [1](#0-0) . From this, the instruction computes `liab_amount_final`, the exact liability amount the liquidatee will be credited with repaying, purely as a function of `asset_amount`, `asset_price`, and `liab_price` — with no dependency on, or clamping against, the liquidatee's *live* outstanding liability balance [2](#0-1) .

This fixed `liab_amount_final` is then applied directly against the liquidatee's liability balance via `liquidatee_liab_bank_account.repay(liab_amount_final)` [3](#0-2) . If, between the time the liquidator built/simulated their transaction and the time it lands on-chain, the borrower (or anyone) front-runs with a partial repayment on the same `liab_bank` (e.g. via the standard permissionless repay instruction), the borrower's outstanding liability shrinks. The liquidator's `liab_amount_final` — computed off stale state — can then exceed the now-reduced outstanding liability.

Post-liquidation invariant checks explicitly require that some liability must still remain outstanding after the liquidation (`!liability_balance.is_empty(BalanceSide::Liabilities)`, else `ExhaustedLiability`) and that account health does not drop below what is allowed (`TooSevereLiquidation`) [4](#0-3) . If the fixed, stale `liab_amount_final` fully or over-pays the shrunken debt, the transaction is expected to revert on one of these invariants (or fail earlier inside the `repay()` accounting on an underflow), atomically undoing the whole liquidation attempt and burning the liquidator's gas/compute.

This is structurally the same bug class as the WiseLending finding: the amount to be repaid on the liquidatee's behalf is computed as a fixed quantity against a debt balance that is mutable and permissionlessly reducible right up until the liquidator's transaction executes, with no self-adjusting/clamping mechanism, so a cheap front-run repayment can flip a valid, in-flight liquidation into a reverting transaction.

### Impact Explanation
This is a griefing/DoS vector, not a value-redirection or authorization bypass: a borrower who is about to be liquidated can spend a small repay transaction (permissionless, unprivileged action available to any account owner) to invalidate a pending liquidator's transaction that targets a specific `liab_bank`/`asset_amount` pair. Repeated griefing increases the time-to-liquidation for unhealthy accounts, which increases the risk that further price moves push the position into bad debt before it is finally liquidated — a financially relevant, if indirect, impact on protocol solvency risk. It does not, however, allow the attacker to seize funds, bypass authorization, or leave any durable inconsistent state, since the liquidation instruction reverts atomically and leaves all balances unchanged.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires the borrower being liquidated to actively monitor the mempool/watch for pending liquidation transactions targeting their account and race a repay transaction ahead of it. On Solana, transaction landing order is influenced by leader schedules and priority fees rather than a simple public mempool, and sophisticated liquidator bots typically resubmit with retries or use conservative amounts (guides even recommend liquidators size their requested `asset_amount` conservatively, e.g. ~70-80% of the unhealthy amount) [5](#0-4) , which naturally mitigates the exact-amount overshoot scenario in practice. As with the original WiseLending report, the borrower being liquidated has little incentive to grief versus simply self-liquidating or repaying enough debt to become healthy outright, since partial repayment doesn't stop the liquidation, only delays it by forcing a resubmission.

### Recommendation
Consider having `checksLiquidation`-equivalent logic clamp the effective repay amount to the liquidatee's live outstanding liability rather than reverting: i.e., in the accounting block of `lending_account_liquidate`, cap `liab_amount_final`/`liab_amount_liquidator` (and correspondingly the seized `asset_amount`) to the liquidatee's current liability balance before applying `repay()`, and emit the actually-executed amounts in the `LendingAccountLiquidateEvent` rather than failing the whole instruction outright when the liquidator's originally requested amount slightly overshoots current state.

### Proof of Concept
1. Liquidatee has `100` liability shares in `liab_bank` and is currently unhealthy (`lending_account_liquidate` would otherwise succeed).
2. A liquidator observes the unhealthy account, simulates and submits `lending_account_liquidate` with `asset_amount` sized to fully utilize the position's `100` liability shares (converted through `calc_amount`/`calc_value`) [6](#0-5) .
3. Liquidatee front-runs with `lending_account_repay` on the same `liab_bank`, paying off a small amount (e.g., reducing shares to `99`).
4. Liquidator's transaction lands second: `liquidatee_liab_bank_account.repay(liab_amount_final)` attempts to repay more than the now-remaining `99` shares' worth of liability [7](#0-6) .
5. The instruction fails the post-liquidation invariant checks (`ExhaustedLiability`/`TooSeverePayoff`/`MathError`), reverting the entire liquidation transaction and forcing the liquidator to resize and resubmit [4](#0-3) .

Note: I could not directly inspect the internal implementation of `BankAccountWrapper::repay()` (in `programs/marginfi/src/state/marginfi_account.rs` or related bank-account module) to confirm the exact revert error triggered on overshoot (checked-math underflow vs. explicit invariant check), due to index/tool call limits in this session. Starting a Devin session with full repo access would allow tracing `repay()`'s exact failure mode to precisely pin down the error code.
