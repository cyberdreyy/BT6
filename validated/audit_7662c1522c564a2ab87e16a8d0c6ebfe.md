### Title
Builder pending withdrawal amount uncapped in `Withdrawal.amount` while `apply_withdrawals` caps the CL-side deduction, letting Gwei be minted on the EL side that was never actually taken from the builder's CL balance - (File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md)

### Summary
`get_builder_withdrawals` builds `Withdrawal` objects using the raw, uncapped `builder_pending_withdrawals[i].amount`, while `apply_withdrawals` only debits `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` from `state.builders[builder_index].balance`. Since the execution layer mints/credits the full `Withdrawal.amount` to `fee_recipient` regardless of what was actually deducted on the consensus layer, a builder pending-withdrawal amount that exceeds the builder's balance produces a payment larger than the debited balance — Gwei is effectively created and paid to `fee_recipient` without any Gwei being destroyed by an equal amount on the CL side. This is directly analogous to the reported `FeeBuyback#submit()` bug, where the value actually paid (`msg.value`, i.e. what's really moved) was not required to equal the committed `amount` (what's recorded as owed), causing a fund-loss/inflation whenever the two diverge.

### Finding Description [1](#0-0) 
`get_builder_withdrawals` creates a `Withdrawal(amount=withdrawal.amount)` directly from `state.builder_pending_withdrawals[i].amount`, with no capping to the builder's actual balance. [2](#0-1) 
`apply_withdrawals`, however, deducts only `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` from `state.builders[builder_index].balance` when the withdrawal is a builder withdrawal — i.e. the CL-side deduction is explicitly capped at whatever balance the builder actually has.

The `Withdrawal` list produced by `get_expected_withdrawals`/`apply_withdrawals` is exactly what is required to appear in the corresponding execution payload (`state.payload_expected_withdrawals`), and the spec's own note next to `process_withdrawals` states plainly that "the execution layer mints the full committed amount regardless" of what happens on the CL side: [3](#0-2) 

This note is written in the context of a *different* scenario (slot-deferred deduction under `process_pending_consolidations`), but the same mechanism applies to the builder-pending-withdrawal capping in `apply_withdrawals`: the amount that the EL is told to mint to `fee_recipient` (`Withdrawal.amount`, uncapped) can be strictly larger than the amount actually removed from the builder's balance (`min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)`), whenever a builder's balance has been reduced below the requested pending-withdrawal amount by the time the withdrawal is processed (e.g., by intervening `settle_builder_payment`/other builder-balance-reducing operations between when the `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` entry was queued and when `process_withdrawals` executes it).

The repository's own test suite documents and locks in this exact capped-vs-uncapped behavior: [4](#0-3) 
Here, `withdrawal_amount = 5 ETH` is requested but the builder only has `1 ETH`. The test explicitly asserts `withdrawal.amount == withdrawal_amount` (the full 5 ETH, uncapped) is what appears in `payload_expected_withdrawals`, while the builder's on-chain balance is only decreased by 1 ETH (capped to available balance). The comment "(deduction capped to available balance)" vs "(requested amount)" makes the mismatch explicit.

Equality broken: the invariant "Gwei paid out via an execution payload withdrawal == Gwei removed from a CL balance" (the same equality the FeeBuyback report is about — payment amount must equal the value actually moved) is violated. Here, 4 ETH of value would be minted to `fee_recipient` on the EL side that was never actually debited from any CL balance.

### Impact Explanation
If a `BuilderPendingWithdrawal.amount` can legitimately exceed the builder's balance at execution time, this is a Critical-severity native-Gwei-creation bug: value is paid to a `fee_recipient` (a non-owner, in Sherlock terminology) that was never subtracted from the builder's balance, i.e. Gwei created out of thin air, minted by the execution layer and never destroyed on the consensus layer, breaking the total-Gwei-supply invariant.

### Likelihood Explanation
This finding is contingent on being able to reach a state where a queued `BuilderPendingWithdrawal.amount` is *larger* than the builder's balance at the time `process_withdrawals` executes it. `process_execution_payload_bid`/`settle_builder_payment` populate `builder_pending_withdrawals` with the bid's committed `amount`, and `can_builder_cover_bid` is checked only at bid-acceptance time — not re-checked at withdrawal-processing time. Because `builder.balance` can be reduced between bid-acceptance and withdrawal-processing (e.g. by other pending payments/withdrawals settling first, or by the builder issuing another payment that reduces balance before the earlier one is swept), the divergence is plausible without requiring a malicious peer or client bug — it is a property of the ordinary spec-following state machine. However, I was not able to fully trace every path that populates `builder_pending_withdrawals` and their exact ordering guarantees relative to `apply_withdrawals`/`get_builder_withdrawals` within the time available, so the precise reachability (whether `can_builder_cover_bid`'s check at bid time is sufficient to always prevent this in practice) is not fully confirmed and should be verified further.

### Recommendation
Either (a) cap the `Withdrawal.amount` produced in `get_builder_withdrawals` to `min(builder_pending_withdrawals[i].amount, state.builders[builder_index].balance)` so the minted/paid amount always matches the CL-side deduction, or (b) remove the `min()` cap in `apply_withdrawals` and instead assert/guarantee (e.g., via `can_builder_cover_bid` re-verified at withdrawal time, or by capping `BuilderPendingWithdrawal.amount` when it is first queued) that a builder's balance can never fall below any outstanding `builder_pending_withdrawals` amount, so the two never diverge.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual PoC, following the existing spec test as a template: [4](#0-3) 
1. Queue a `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` for `builder_index` with `amount = 5 ETH` (e.g. via a bid that was valid when `can_builder_cover_bid` checked balance, then the balance is later reduced by other settled payments before this entry is processed).
2. Reduce `state.builders[builder_index].balance` to `1 ETH` before `process_withdrawals` runs.
3. Call `spec.process_withdrawals(state)`.
4. Observe: `state.payload_expected_withdrawals` contains a `Withdrawal` with `amount == 5 ETH` payable to `fee_recipient` on the execution layer, while `state.builders[builder_index].balance` only decreased by `1 ETH` (down to `0`), per the exact behavior asserted by `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance`. The execution layer will mint the full 5 ETH to `fee_recipient`, 4 ETH of which was never debited anywhere in the consensus-layer state — a net creation of Gwei paid to a non-owner.

##### New `get_builder_withdrawals`

```python
def get_builder_withdrawals(
    state: BeaconState,
    withdrawal_index: WithdrawalIndex,
    prior_withdrawals: Sequence[Withdrawal],
) -> Tuple[Sequence[Withdrawal], WithdrawalIndex, Uint64]:
    withdrawals_limit = MAX_WITHDRAWALS_PER_PAYLOAD - 1
    assert len(prior_withdrawals) <= withdrawals_limit

    processed_count = Uint64(0)
    withdrawals: list[Withdrawal] = []
    for withdrawal in state.builder_pending_withdrawals:
        all_withdrawals = list(prior_withdrawals) + withdrawals
        has_reached_limit = len(all_withdrawals) >= withdrawals_limit
        if has_reached_limit:
            break

        builder_index = withdrawal.builder_index
        withdrawals.append(
            Withdrawal(
                index=withdrawal_index,
                validator_index=convert_builder_index_to_validator_index(builder_index),
                address=withdrawal.fee_recipient,
                amount=withdrawal.amount,
            )
        )
        withdrawal_index += 1
        processed_count += 1

    return withdrawals, withdrawal_index, processed_count

```markdown
*Note*: Unlike deposits (which are applied at the child's slot via
`apply_parent_execution_payload`), withdrawal balance deductions are applied
immediately via `apply_withdrawals`. Deferring the deduction to the child's slot
would break the total supply invariant: state transitions between the commitment
slot and the deduction slot (e.g., `process_pending_consolidations` at an epoch
boundary) can reduce a validator's balance below the committed withdrawal
amount, causing `decrease_balance` to saturate at zero. Since the execution
layer mints the full committed amount regardless, any CL-side saturation creates
a net supply inflation. As a consequence, `state.balances` reflects the
withdrawal deduction before the corresponding execution payload is confirmed,
creating a transient asymmetry with the EL state at `state.latest_block_hash`.
Off-chain consumers that require CL/EL balance consistency can reconstruct
pre-deduction balances by adding back `state.payload_expected_withdrawals`.
