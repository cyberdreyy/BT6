### Title
Reward captured pro-rata to shares held at `internal_ping` time, not to time-weighted stake, allowing one-block deposit/stake/unstake to steal epoch reward from long-term delegators - (File: `staking-pool/src/internal.rs`)

### Summary
`StakingContract::internal_ping` distributes the epoch reward strictly in proportion to `account.stake_shares / total_stake_shares` measured at the moment `internal_ping` runs, with no regard to how long those shares have existed. An attacker can `stake()` immediately before the epoch turns over (before any state-changing call triggers the pinged epoch reward), then `unstake()` right after the reward-crediting `internal_ping` call, capturing a share of a full epoch's validator reward for one block of exposure while diluting the honest delegators who actually held stake through the entire rewarded epoch.

### Finding Description
The invariant claimed by the design is:
`reward_credited(account) == remaining_reward * (account.stake_shares_time_weighted_over_epoch / total_stake_shares_time_weighted_over_epoch)`

What the code actually implements is:
`reward_credited(account) == remaining_reward * (account.stake_shares_at_ping / total_stake_shares_at_ping)` [1](#0-0) 

Trace:
1. `internal_ping` only fires the reward-distribution branch when `env::epoch_height()` differs from `self.last_epoch_height`; while still in the same epoch it is a no-op (`return false`), so any number of `stake`/`unstake` calls within that epoch don't touch reward accounting. [2](#0-1) 
2. `internal_stake` mints shares at the current share price (`total_staked_balance / total_stake_shares`), which has not yet absorbed the pending reward that the underlying validator stake already earned during the prior epoch. [3](#0-2) 
3. On the next state-changing call after the epoch boundary, `internal_ping` computes `total_reward = total_balance - self.last_total_balance` (the protocol-credited validator reward for stake that was actually bonded during the elapsed epoch) and adds `remaining_reward` uniformly to `total_staked_balance`, raising the share price for *every* share outstanding at that instant — including shares the attacker minted only one block earlier. [4](#0-3) 
4. The attacker then calls `unstake()` (`inner_unstake`), converting the now-inflated shares back to `unstaked` balance at the new, reward-boosted price, locking in a reward they never contributed capital toward earning. [5](#0-4) 

None of the existing guards address this: `internal_ping`'s balance assertion only checks `total_balance >= last_total_balance` (monotonicity), not who contributed the underlying stake; the U256 rounding pairs (`rounded_down` for minting on stake, `rounded_up` for burning on unstake) only prevent per-transaction overcharge/undercharge, not time-weighting; and there is no `assert_one_yocto`, cooldown, or minimum-holding-period check gating `stake`/`unstake` calls around epoch boundaries. Because reward accounting is a single global share-price ratchet with no timestamp/epoch tracked per share, dilution/capture based on call timing relative to the epoch boundary is unavoidable within the current design. Two attacker-controlled accounts are used purely to demonstrate the divergence cleanly (one held stake the whole epoch, the other stakes for one block) — both are unprivileged callers of `stake()`/`unstake()`, no owner or foundation role required.

### Impact Explanation
This is an internal accounting divergence: reward that should be attributed to delegators proportional to time-weighted stake is instead attributed to whoever holds shares at the exact block `internal_ping` credits the epoch reward. The attacker's one-block account gains reward NEAR that should have accrued to the delegators who were staked the whole epoch (and, to a lesser extent, dilutes the owner's fee split as well since `owners_fee` is computed off `total_reward` before the attacker's dilution, but the *share* of the remaining reward per honest staker shrinks). This matches the "High - rewards or owner fees attributed to the wrong party" category: no principal leaves the pool improperly, but the pool's internal `total_staked_balance` / `total_stake_shares` accounting misallocates already-earned reward away from long-term stakers to the attacker. This is repeatable every epoch, against any staking pool contract instance deployed from this codebase, requiring no special account and no cooperation from the pool owner or other delegators.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions are trivial: an unprivileged account needs enough NEAR to `deposit_and_stake()` a meaningfully large amount relative to the pool's `total_staked_balance`, timed in the last block(s) of an epoch, and then to call `unstake()` in the first block(s) of the following epoch (after any call — the attacker's own or someone else's — has triggered the reward-crediting `internal_ping`). NEAR epoch boundaries are observable on-chain (`env::epoch_height()`), so timing the transactions is straightforward and requires no privileged access, no key compromise, and no validator collusion. The larger the attacker's relative stake at the moment of the ping, the larger the captured fraction of `remaining_reward`; profitability scales with available capital and is fully repeatable across epochs.

### Recommendation
Track reward eligibility with a per-epoch checkpoint: either (a) freeze newly staked shares from participating in the very next reward distribution (only shares that existed as of the *previous* `internal_ping` should receive that reward), or (b) compute rewards using a snapshot of `total_stake_shares` taken before any `stake`/`unstake` calls executed within the same block/epoch as the reward crediting, or (c) require staked funds to be time-locked into the validator set for at least one full epoch before being eligible for that epoch's reward. Concretely, `internal_stake` could record the epoch a share was minted, and `internal_ping`'s reward-distribution branch could restrict `remaining_reward` allocation to shares whose mint-epoch is strictly less than `epoch_height`.

### Proof of Concept
Using `near-sdk-sim` / `testing_env!`:
1. Initialize a `StakingContract` with `total_staked_balance = S`, `total_stake_shares = S` (1:1 price), `last_total_balance = S`, `last_epoch_height = E`.
2. Account `honest` deposits and stakes `X` NEAR at epoch `E` (before boundary) — becomes a long-term holder.
3. Advance `env::epoch_height()` to `E+1` and simulate a validator reward `R` by increasing the mocked `env::account_locked_balance()`/`env::account_balance()` accordingly, but do NOT yet call any contract method (reward not yet credited internally).
4. Still at epoch `E` in the *previous* block (before the mocked epoch bump takes effect for `internal_ping`'s comparison), have `attacker` call `deposit_and_stake(Y)` — assert this succeeds and mints shares at the pre-reward price (`num_shares_from_staked_amount_rounded_down`).
5. Bump `env::epoch_height()` to `E+1`, then call `attacker.unstake(...)` (or `ping()` first, then `unstake`) — this triggers `internal_ping`, crediting `remaining_reward` proportional to `attacker.stake_shares / total_stake_shares` at that instant.
6. Assert: `attacker`'s received `unstaked` balance minus `Y` (i.e., the reward-derived surplus) is greater than zero and is disproportionate to a single block of exposure, while `honest`'s effective share of `R` (computed via `staked_amount_from_num_shares_rounded_down(honest.stake_shares)` before vs. after) is measurably reduced from what it would have been absent the attacker's late stake — violating `reward_credited(account) ∝ time-weighted shares`.
7. Compare the two named values from the binding: `attacker_reward_per_share_second` (huge, near-instant) vs `honest_reward_per_share_second` (diluted), demonstrating they diverge sharply, proving the invariant is broken.

    pub(crate) fn internal_stake(&mut self, amount: Balance) {
        assert!(amount > 0, "Staking amount should be positive");

        let account_id = env::predecessor_account_id();
        let mut account = self.internal_get_account(&account_id);

        // Calculate the number of "stake" shares that the account will receive for staking the
        // given amount.
        let num_shares = self.num_shares_from_staked_amount_rounded_down(amount);
        assert!(
            num_shares > 0,
            "The calculated number of \"stake\" shares received for staking should be positive"
        );
        // The amount of tokens the account will be charged from the unstaked balance.
        // Rounded down to avoid overcharging the account to guarantee that the account can always
        // unstake at least the same amount as staked.
        let charge_amount = self.staked_amount_from_num_shares_rounded_down(num_shares);
        assert!(
            charge_amount > 0,
            "Invariant violation. Calculated staked amount must be positive, because \"stake\" share price should be at least 1"
        );

        assert!(
            account.unstaked >= charge_amount,
            "Not enough unstaked balance to stake"
        );
        account.unstaked -= charge_amount;
        account.stake_shares += num_shares;
        self.internal_save_account(&account_id, &account);

        // The staked amount that will be added to the total to guarantee the "stake" share price
        // never decreases. The difference between `stake_amount` and `charge_amount` is paid
        // from the allocated STAKE_SHARE_PRICE_GUARANTEE_FUND.
        let stake_amount = self.staked_amount_from_num_shares_rounded_up(num_shares);

        self.total_staked_balance += stake_amount;
        self.total_stake_shares += num_shares;

    pub(crate) fn inner_unstake(&mut self, amount: u128) {
        assert!(amount > 0, "Unstaking amount should be positive");

        let account_id = env::predecessor_account_id();
        let mut account = self.internal_get_account(&account_id);

        assert!(
            self.total_staked_balance > 0,
            "The contract doesn't have staked balance"
        );
        // Calculate the number of shares required to unstake the given amount.
        // NOTE: The number of shares the account will pay is rounded up.
        let num_shares = self.num_shares_from_staked_amount_rounded_up(amount);
        assert!(
            num_shares > 0,
            "Invariant violation. The calculated number of \"stake\" shares for unstaking should be positive"
        );
        assert!(
            account.stake_shares >= num_shares,
            "Not enough staked balance to unstake"
        );

        // Calculating the amount of tokens the account will receive by unstaking the corresponding
        // number of "stake" shares, rounding up.
        let receive_amount = self.staked_amount_from_num_shares_rounded_up(num_shares);
        assert!(
            receive_amount > 0,
            "Invariant violation. Calculated staked amount must be positive, because \"stake\" share price should be at least 1"
        );

        account.stake_shares -= num_shares;
        account.unstaked += receive_amount;
        account.unstaked_available_epoch_height = env::epoch_height() + NUM_EPOCHS_TO_UNLOCK;
        self.internal_save_account(&account_id, &account);

        // The amount tokens that will be unstaked from the total to guarantee the "stake" share
        // price never decreases. The difference between `receive_amount` and `unstake_amount` is
        // paid from the allocated STAKE_SHARE_PRICE_GUARANTEE_FUND.
        let unstake_amount = self.staked_amount_from_num_shares_rounded_down(num_shares);

        self.total_staked_balance -= unstake_amount;
        self.total_stake_shares -= num_shares;
