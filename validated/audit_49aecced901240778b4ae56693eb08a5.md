### Title
Builder's earned payment is destroyed on unrelated proposer slashing - ([File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md])

### Summary
In the Gloas fork, `process_proposer_slashing` unconditionally erases the `BuilderPendingPayment` entry associated with the slashed proposer's slot if the slashing lands within the 2-epoch window, regardless of whether that payment has already accrued enough PTC attestation weight to meet quorum (i.e., the builder has already "earned" the payment by delivering an attested payload). This mirrors the SmartEscrow bug: a party who is not at fault (the builder/beneficiary) loses funds that are already vested/pending because an unrelated party (the proposer, analogous to the benefactor) triggers a "termination" event (proposer slashing) before the payment can be settled.

### Finding Description
When a builder's bid is accepted, `process_execution_payload_bid` records a `BuilderPendingPayment` with `weight = 0` and does **not** deduct the builder's balance up front [1](#0-0) . As attesters vote for the slot (`process_attestation`), `payment.weight` accumulates toward `get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold`, and once `process_builder_pending_payments` runs at the epoch boundary, any payment whose weight reached quorum is turned into a real `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` that eventually pays the builder's `fee_recipient` [2](#0-1) .

However, `process_proposer_slashing` clears the payment entry for the proposer's slot the moment the proposer is slashed, with no check on `payment.weight`: [3](#0-2) 

This means that if:
1. A builder submits a bid and delivers the committed payload,
2. PTC/attester votes accumulate weight on `builder_pending_payments[slot]` up to or beyond quorum (the payment is effectively "earned"),
3. Before the epoch boundary settles the payment into `builder_pending_withdrawals`, anyone submits a valid `ProposerSlashing` against that slot's proposer (an equivocation unrelated to payload delivery),

then the entire `BuilderPendingPayment`—including its accrued `weight` and the underlying `BuilderPendingWithdrawal`—is wiped via `BuilderPendingPayment.empty()`. The Gwei that was already committed to the builder's `fee_recipient` never becomes a withdrawal and is destroyed; it is not returned to the builder's balance (which was never decremented) nor paid to anyone else.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the "Gwei created, destroyed, or paid to a non-owner" invariant: value that the protocol had already begun to route to the builder's `fee_recipient` (backed by attester weight that met quorum) simply vanishes when the proposer is slashed for an act that has nothing to do with the builder's payload delivery. A malicious actor watching the mempool for a valid proposer equivocation can submit a `ProposerSlashing` specifically to grief a builder whose payment has already reached quorum, permanently forfeiting the builder's earned payment.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only a valid, unrelated proposer equivocation (double-signed header) for the same slot within the 2-epoch settlement window—no validator/builder collusion is needed, and equivocations happen in practice (e.g., due to client bugs or misconfiguration) independent of whether the builder honestly delivered. The griefing party need not be the slashed proposer or the builder; anyone can broadcast the slashing.

### Recommendation
Before clearing the `BuilderPendingPayment` entry in `process_proposer_slashing`, settle payments whose `weight` already meets/exceeds `get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold` (i.e., call the equivalent of `settle_builder_payment`/append the `withdrawal` to `builder_pending_withdrawals`) instead of unconditionally emptying the slot when the proposer index matches.

### Proof of Concept
1. Builder submits `SignedExecutionPayloadBid` for slot `S`; `process_execution_payload_bid` creates `builder_pending_payments[idx]` with `weight=0`, `withdrawal.amount = V` [4](#0-3) .
2. Attesters vote for slot `S`; `process_attestation` increases `payment.weight` past `get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold` for that slot (weight update logic shown in the reward path preceding `state.builder_pending_payments[...] = payment`) — meaning the payment is now eligible to be withdrawn.
3. Before the epoch boundary runs `process_builder_pending_payments` (which would create the `BuilderPendingWithdrawal`), a `ProposerSlashing` for proposer of slot `S` is included in a block, still within the 2-epoch window.
4. `process_proposer_slashing` finds `payment.proposer_index == header_1.proposer_index` and sets `builder_pending_payments[idx] = BuilderPendingPayment.empty()` [5](#0-4) , discarding the quorum-reaching payment entirely.
5. At the next epoch boundary, `process_builder_pending_payments` sees an empty entry and never creates the withdrawal; the builder's `V` Gwei is never paid to `fee_recipient` and the builder's balance was never restored — the funds are destroyed.

def process_builder_pending_payments(state: BeaconState) -> None:
    """
    Processes the builder pending payments from the previous epoch.
    """
    quorum = get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold(state)
    for payment in state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH]:
        if payment.weight >= quorum:
            state.builder_pending_withdrawals.append(payment.withdrawal)

    old_payments = state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:]
    state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH] = old_payments
    new_payments = [BuilderPendingPayment.empty() for _ in range(SLOTS_PER_EPOCH)]
    state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:] = new_payments
