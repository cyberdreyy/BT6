## Title
Builder withdrawals silently truncate to `min(amount, balance)`, creating a last-payee shortfall analogous to unsocialized bad debt - (File: `specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md`)

### Summary
The Gloas (EIP-7732) builder-payment system commits to future payments (`BuilderPendingPayment` → `BuilderPendingWithdrawal`, queued in `state.builder_pending_withdrawals`) that are honored later by `apply_withdrawals`. When a builder's `balance` at the time the withdrawal is actually processed is less than the sum of amounts it has been committed to pay, the spec caps the paid amount to `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` rather than reverting or socializing the shortfall, so a `fee_recipient` queued earlier drains the balance and a later-queued `fee_recipient` for the same builder can receive less than committed, or nothing at all.

### Finding Description
`process_builder_pending_payments` moves an accepted bid's payment into `state.builder_pending_withdrawals` once a slot's quorum weight is reached: [1](#0-0) . This queue can accumulate multiple pending withdrawals for the same `builder_index` (each with its own `fee_recipient`, e.g. different proposers who received payments from the same builder across different slots), before any of them are actually paid out.

When `apply_withdrawals` later processes these entries, it explicitly caps the deduction at the currently available balance instead of asserting sufficiency: [2](#0-1) 

This means: the *first* `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` processed against a given `builder_index` in the sweep drains `state.builders[builder_index].balance` toward zero, and any subsequent withdrawal entries in the queue for that same builder — potentially owed to a different `fee_recipient` — receive `min(withdrawal.amount, remaining_balance)`, which can be far less than `withdrawal.amount`, including zero. The queue entry is still consumed (`update_builder_pending_withdrawals` removes it) regardless of whether it was paid in full, so the shortfall is permanently and silently lost rather than being carried forward, retried, or spread across remaining builder deposits.

This is confirmed by the test suite, which explicitly exercises "insufficient balance" truncation as expected behavior: [3](#0-2) 

The root cause is that whether a builder's balance can fall below its total committed pending-withdrawal amount between commitment time (bid acceptance) and payout time (sweep processing) is not fully constrained by a global invariant checked at withdrawal-processing time — `apply_withdrawals` only checks the balance of the single withdrawal being applied, not the sum of all outstanding commitments for that builder. Balance can decrease between commitment and payout via subsequent bid charges (`apply_parent_execution_payload` deducting bid value), and multiple pending payments/withdrawals can be queued against the same builder before any are settled (as shown in the bid-processing tests where "pending payments"/"pending withdrawals" are separately tracked totals used only at bid-acceptance time, not re-verified at payout time): [4](#0-3) .

### Impact Explanation
This is exactly the "last withdrawer" pattern from the report, mapped onto builder payments: a `fee_recipient` who is queued earlier in `builder_pending_withdrawals` for a builder is paid the committed `Gwei` in full, while a `fee_recipient` queued later for the *same* `builder_index` can be paid less than committed, or nothing — a Gwei amount that was promised (and previously counted as reserved by the balance checks in bid processing) is effectively destroyed rather than paid to its rightful non-owner-controlled recipient. This is a protocol-level loss of an honest third party's (a different proposer's) entitled payment, not merely the attacker/builder's own stake, since the loss falls on the `fee_recipient`, who has no control over builder solvency or queue ordering.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only that a single builder accumulate more than one outstanding `BuilderPendingWithdrawal`/`BuilderPendingPayment` entry (normal operation — happens whenever the builder proposes/wins multiple slots' bids before the sweep processes the earlier commitment) combined with the builder's balance being drawn down by other charges (new bid payments via `apply_parent_execution_payload`) between commitment and payout. No malicious peer, coalition, or client bug is needed — it is a straightforward sequencing/timing outcome of the spec's own accounting design (Type: Timing, matching the original report's classification), reachable purely through normal protocol usage by a single builder acting in its own economic interest (e.g., deliberately bidding more slots than it can ultimately cover) or through legitimate balance depletion.

### Recommendation
- **Short term**: When applying `builder_pending_withdrawals`, verify at withdrawal-processing time that the builder's balance covers the full outstanding commitment queue (or reject/queue-block bids that would push total commitments above verified available balance at *every* subsequent balance-affecting operation, not just at bid-acceptance time).
- **Long term**: Either (a) enforce a strict invariant that a builder's balance can never be spent by another mechanism (e.g., new bids) while it has outstanding `builder_pending_withdrawals`/`builder_pending_payments` that would be shorted as a result, or (b) explicitly define and document a socialization/pro-rata rule for insufficient-balance builder withdrawals so that all pending `fee_recipient`s for an underfunded builder share the shortfall proportionally rather than a FIFO "first-paid, last-starved" scheme.

### Proof of Concept
1. Builder `B` bids and wins slot `S1` with `fee_recipient = R1`, payment amount `A1`. Weight reaches quorum; `process_builder_pending_payments` appends `BuilderPendingWithdrawal(builder_index=B, fee_recipient=R1, amount=A1)` to `state.builder_pending_withdrawals` (see `specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md:1663-1677`).
2. Before this withdrawal is swept, `B` bids and wins slot `S2` with `fee_recipient = R2`, payment amount `A2`, where `A1 + A2 + MIN_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT <= B.balance` passes the bid-acceptance check (per `test_process_execution_payload_bid_sufficient_balance_with_pending_withdrawals`). This second payment is similarly queued: `BuilderPendingWithdrawal(builder_index=B, fee_recipient=R2, amount=A2)`.
3. Before either is swept, `B`'s parent payload for `S2` is applied via `apply_parent_execution_payload`, deducting `A2` (or more, from further bids) from `B.balance`, such that `B.balance` is now less than `A1 + A2`.
4. During `process_withdrawals`/`apply_withdrawals`, the first queued withdrawal (`R1`, amount `A1`) is applied and pays in full, draining `B.balance` toward zero: `specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md:1923-1931`.
5. The second queued withdrawal (`R2`, amount `A2`) is applied next but `B.balance` is now insufficient; per the spec, `R2` receives only `min(A2, remaining_balance)` — potentially `0` — and the queue entry is still removed via `update_builder_pending_withdrawals`, so `R2`'s shortfall (`A2 - actual_paid`) is permanently lost, mirroring exactly the "last withdrawer" bad-debt scenario from the source report. This is directly analogous to the behavior asserted (as expected) in `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance` (`tests/core/pyspec/eth_consensus_specs/test/gloas/block_processing/test_process_withdrawals.py:114-156`), generalized to the multi-payee-per-builder case.

def process_builder_pending_payments(state: BeaconState) -> None:
    """
    Processes the builder pending payments from the previous epoch.
    """
    quorum = get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold(state)
    for payment in state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH]:
        if payment.weight >= quorum:
            state.builder_pending_withdrawals.append(payment.withdrawal)

    old_payments = state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:]
    state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH] = old_payments
    new_payments = [BuilderPendingPayment.empty() for _ in range(SLOTS_PER_EPOCH)]
    state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:] = new_payments
