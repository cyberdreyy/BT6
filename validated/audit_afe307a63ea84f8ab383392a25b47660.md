### Title
ProgrammableBorrower.onStartEpoch performs an uncapped-check `vault.deposit`, so an ERC4626 deposit limit filled by any third party bricks `startEpoch` and temporarily freezes pool funds - (contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol)

### Summary
`ProgrammableBorrower.onStartEpoch` unconditionally deposits all idle underlying into the configured ERC4626 vault via `_depositToVault` without consulting `vault.maxDeposit` and without a try/catch fallback. Any ERC4626 deposit cap (analogous to Alchemist's `_mintingLimiter`) can be consumed by any unprivileged third-party depositor before the epoch opens, so the owner/manager's `startEpoch` call reverts after lender deposits have already been accepted into the facility. The same gap exists in `_repay`, where the borrower's `safeTransferFrom` ordering means a cap-induced `vault.deposit` revert aborts the entire repayment.

### Finding Description
The credit-vault analog of the unchecked-minting-limit bug is in `ProgrammableBorrower`:

- `onStartEpoch` (contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol:201-224) computes `startAssets` and then calls `_depositToVault(underlyingToken.balanceOf(address(this)), 0)` at line 216.
- `_depositToVault` (lines 378-385) performs a bare `vault.deposit(_assetAmount, address(this))` — no `maxDeposit` check, no try/catch.
- Contrast with `onStopEpoch` (lines 246-253), which wraps `vault.withdraw` in try/catch and reverts with a retryable `StopEpochVaultLiquidityUnavailable`. The withdrawal side anticipates external-vault failure; the deposit side does not.
- Standard ERC4626 vaults (MetaMorpho, ERC4626 with caps, per the repo's own `IERC4626.maxDeposit` semantics in contracts/interfaces/IERC4626.sol:82) may revert on `deposit` when `assets > maxDeposit`. A direct token sender / third-party user of that vault can fill its remaining capacity during the buffer period.
- When the honest owner calls `startEpoch` on `IdleCDOEpochVariant`, the call into `onStartEpoch` reverts, and since the revert propagates through the CDO's epoch-start path, the epoch cannot open. Lender principal sits idle in `ProgrammableBorrower` until the vault cap frees up or the operator reconfigures via `setVault`/`rescueTokens` — recovery paths that require privileged intervention and are not part of normal epoch flow.
- Similarly, `_repay` (lines 466-533) pulls `safeTransferFrom(borrower, ...)` at line 482 and later calls `_depositToVault(totalRepaidAssets, ...)` at line 527; a cap-filled vault reverts the whole transaction, blocking repayment and therefore epoch settlement.

The broken invariant mirrors the external report: a downstream limiter that changes over time is not pre-checked before a committed flow, so the flow reverts instead of gracefully capping.

### Impact Explanation
Temporary freezing of funds with quantifiable yield loss: all pooled lender underlyings destined for the new epoch (potentially the entire TVL parked via `onStartEpoch`) cannot enter the vault or be accounted, blocking `startEpoch` until the external cap is released or the owner rescues funds. Depositors lose expected epoch yield for the duration, and withdrawal liquidity (`epochPendingWithdraws`) cannot be serviced because the epoch never opens. No permanent theft, but real, attacker-triggerable freezing — within the accepted "temporary freezing" impact class rather than pure DoS, since third-party vault depositors directly cause it.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium-low. Requires the configured ERC4626 vault to enforce a deposit cap smaller than the pooled buffer balance — common for yield vaults with TVL caps. Any unprivileged user of that vault (or a griefer monitoring mempool for `startEpoch`) can consume the cap. The window is the buffer period between epochs, and the attack costs only a normal vault deposit which is later withdrawable. Probability depends entirely on vault configuration, which is an operator choice.

### Recommendation
In `_depositToVault`, check `vault.maxDeposit(address(this))` before depositing and either (a) cap the deposit to `min(_assetAmount, maxDeposit)` and leave the remainder on-hand (adjusting `epochDepositedToVault`/`epochStartVaultAssets` consistently), or (b) wrap in try/catch and fall back to holding idle cash, since `availableToBorrow`/`totalUnderlying`/`_vaultNetInterest` already handle on-hand balances. Apply the same capping in `onStartEpoch` and `_repay`.
