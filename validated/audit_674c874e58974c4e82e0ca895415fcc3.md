### Title
Unfunded instant-withdraw receipts can be claimed against other users' funded reserves - ([File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol])

### Summary
The Xen bug (ALPINE-CVE-2026-62434) is a reclaim of memory pages that were not regular guest RAM — i.e., a claim path that hands out resources that were never actually provisioned for the requester. The direct analog is `IdleCreditVault.claimInstantWithdrawRequest`: it burns the full `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` receipt and pays out underlying via `_transferFundedClaim` without verifying that the CDO ever funded that receipt through `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`. `pendingInstantWithdraws` (the unfunded-remainder counter) is tracked but never consulted on the claim path, so a receipt whose funds were never delivered to the strategy is still payable out of any underlying the strategy happens to hold — underlying that belongs to other users' already-funded withdraw claims.

### Finding Description
`requestInstantWithdraw` burns the CDO's strategy tokens, mints a receipt to the user, and increments `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` and the global `pendingInstantWithdraws` (contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol:356-375). Funding is a separate step: `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` pulls `_amount` from the CDO and decrements `pendingInstantWithdraws` (lines 398-403).

However, `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` (lines 380-393) does:

```solidity
uint256 amount = instantWithdrawsRequests[_user];
_burn(_user, amount);
instantWithdrawsRequests[_user] = 0;
_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount);
```

There is no check that `pendingInstantWithdraws` covered this user's receipt, no per-user/per-epoch funded flag, and no requirement that the CDO actually transferred the corresponding underlying. `_transferFundedClaim` (lines 897-907) only guards `defaultRecoveryReserve` — it protects the post-default recovery bucket, not other users' funded claims. So an unfunded instant receipt is paid from the strategy's general underlying balance, which is exactly the reserve that backs funded normal withdraw claims (`_claimFundedWithdrawRequest` → `_transferFundedClaim`) and funded loss-adjusted claims.

Concretely: a user requests an instant withdraw during a running epoch in a mode where the CDO does not (or only partially) calls `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` before the epoch ends (e.g., APR delta condition not met, or `stopEpochWithDuration` where instant funding is skipped). The receipt remains in `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`. Any subsequent funded normal withdraw request by any user, or borrower funding delivered via `collectWithdrawFunds`, deposits underlying into the strategy. The unfunded instant claimant then calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` (via `IdleCDOEpochVariant`) and withdraws that underlying — reclaiming "pages" that were never allocated to them — leaving the legitimately funded claimant insolvent when they later call `claimWithdrawRequest` (the transfer will revert on insufficient balance, permanently freezing that claim if the strategy balance never recovers).

### Impact Explanation
Direct theft and permanent freezing of funds: the attacker receives underlying earmarked for other users' funded withdraw receipts, and the victim's `claimWithdrawRequest` reverts on insufficient strategy balance. Loss equals the unfunded instant receipt amount, bounded only by the attacker's deposited principal and the funded reserves present in the strategy.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only an unprivileged KYC-passing lender making an ordinary instant-withdraw request in an epoch where instant funding is not collected, then claiming after the strategy receives underlying for other purposes. No privileged misbehavior is needed. The main uncertainty is whether `IdleCDOEpochVariant.claimInstantWithdrawRequest` adds a gating condition (e.g., requiring prior collection) not visible in the strategy; the strategy itself performs none, and receipt/funding separation is the documented design (`pendingInstantWithdraws` exists precisely because funding is asynchronous).

### Recommendation
Track funded vs unfunded instant basis per user (or per epoch). In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, only pay the portion of `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` that was actually collected via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` — e.g., decrement a per-user funded-basis mapping in `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` and cap or revert claims against the unfunded remainder, mirroring how `pendingWithdraws`/`collectWithdrawFunds` gate normal claims through `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` and the funded-claim path.


*Uncertainty note: I was unable to fully trace the CDO-side gating in `IdleCDOEpochVariant.claimInstantWithdrawRequest`/`getInstantWithdrawFunds` within the iteration budget. If the CDO only routes the claim after `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` succeeded for that specific user, the strategy-level gap is unexploitable; that check should be confirmed before finalizing.*
