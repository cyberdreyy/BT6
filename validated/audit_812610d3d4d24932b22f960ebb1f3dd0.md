### Title
Taker-Only Rescue Logic Prevents Resolver Recovery of Stuck Funds - ([File: contracts/BaseEscrow.sol])

### Summary
The protocol implements a `rescueFunds` mechanism designed to recover assets accidentally sent to escrow clones. However, the implementation restricts the caller of `rescueFunds` strictly to the `immutables.taker` (the Resolver on the source chain or the User on the destination chain). This mirrors the "keeper and owner must be same" logic error from the seed report: it assumes only one specific privileged party should handle the rescue, ignoring the fact that the other party (the maker) or the safety deposit provider might have a legitimate claim to recover funds if the primary actor is inactive or the escrow is in a specific state.

### Finding Description
In `BaseEscrow.sol`, the `rescueFunds` function uses the `onlyCaller(immutables.taker.get())` modifier. 

1.  **Source Chain (`EscrowSrc`)**: The `taker` is the Resolver. If funds are stuck and the Resolver is unresponsive or has lost access to their keys, the `maker` (User) has no way to rescue funds even after the `RESCUE_DELAY`.
2.  **Destination Chain (`EscrowDst`)**: The `taker` is the User. If the Resolver (who funded the escrow) accidentally overfunds it or sends the wrong token, they cannot rescue those funds; they must rely on the User to call `rescueFunds` for them.

This creates a deadlock where funds are permanently frozen if the `taker` is unable or unwilling to act, despite the `RESCUE_DELAY` having passed. The seed report highlighted a similar issue where a user was required to be both the keeper and owner to trigger a state change (`to_be_liquidated`); here, the "rescue" state change is locked to a single identity regardless of the contract's lifecycle stage.

### Impact Explanation
**High**: Permanent freezing of funds. If a Resolver sends tokens to a `EscrowDst` but the User never withdraws (e.g., due to a lost secret or expired timelock), the Resolver's funds are stuck. Since `rescueFunds` can only be called by the `taker` (User), the Resolver (who is the actual owner of those funds at that point) cannot recover them. Similarly, if a User's tokens are stuck in `EscrowSrc` after a failed swap and the Resolver (taker) disappears, the User cannot rescue them.

### Likelihood Explanation
**Medium**: This requires a scenario where funds are sent to the wrong address or a swap fails and the party designated as `taker` in the immutables becomes inactive. Given the nature of cross-chain swaps and the reliance on off-chain secret sharing, such "stuck" scenarios are a known operational risk.

### Recommendation
Allow the `maker` to also call `rescueFunds` after the `RESCUE_DELAY`, or allow anyone to trigger the rescue to a predetermined address (the rightful owner) after an even longer safety delay.

### Proof of Concept
1.  A Resolver creates an `EscrowDst` on the destination chain by calling `createDstEscrow` and depositing 1000 USDC.
2.  The swap fails (e.g., the secret is never revealed on the source chain).
3.  The Resolver calls `cancel` on `EscrowDst` to get their 1000 USDC back.
4.  However, the Resolver accidentally sent an additional 500 USDC to the `EscrowDst` address directly.
5.  The `RESCUE_DELAY` (e.g., 8 days) passes.
6.  The Resolver attempts to call `rescueFunds(USDC, 500, immutables)`.
7.  The call reverts because `msg.sender` (Resolver) is not the `immutables.taker` (which is the User on the destination chain).
8.  The 500 USDC is permanently frozen unless the User (who has no incentive to help) calls the function. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1)
