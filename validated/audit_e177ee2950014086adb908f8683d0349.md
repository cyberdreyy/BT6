### Title
Unfunded instant-withdraw receipts stay claimable at par and can drain other users' funded withdrawal reserves - (contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
`IdleCreditVault.claimInstantWithdrawRequest` pays the full `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` receipt at par from whatever underlying balance the strategy holds, without checking whether that receipt was actually funded via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`. A stale instant-withdraw receipt therefore remains redeemable after the funding conditions that were supposed to back it changed — the same class as GHSA-2w46-vq8h-98vh (a credential/request stays valid after a state change that should invalidate it).

### Finding Description
In `requestInstantWithdraw` the vault mints a strategy-token receipt to the user and increments `pendingInstantWithdraws` (IdleCreditVault.sol:356-375). Funding arrives only when the borrower repays and the CDO calls `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`, which decrements `pendingInstantWithdraws` per collected amount (IdleCreditVault.sol:398-403). However `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns the *entire* `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` balance and calls `_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount)` (IdleCreditVault.sol:380-393). `_transferFundedClaim` only protects `defaultRecoveryReserve` — it transfers any other vault balance at par (IdleCreditVault.sol:897-907). There is no per-epoch funding gate for instant claims (unlike normal claims, which are gated by `epochNumber <= lastWithdrawRequest[_user]`), and no check that `pendingInstantWithdraws` attributable to this user has been collected.

Attack sequence:
1. Epoch running. Attacker (KYC'd lender) requests an instant withdraw of amount A; vault mints receipt, `pendingInstantWithdraws += A`.
2. Borrower does not repay instant-withdraw funds (or only partially funds them — `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` accepts partial `_amount`).
3. Epoch stops. Borrower repays enough to fund *other users'* normal withdraw receipts; `collectWithdrawFunds` transfers those underlyings into the strategy. The vault now holds balance B ≥ A that belongs to normal-receipt claimants.
4. Attacker calls `claimWithdrawRequest`/CDO path → `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`. Their stale, never-funded receipt burns and `_transferFundedClaim` pays A at par out of balance B.
5. When honest users later claim their funded normal receipts, `_transferFundedClaim` reverts or the vault is short — the attacker has stolen funded withdrawal reserves.

### Impact Explanation
Direct theft of underlying tokens earmarked for other users' funded withdraw receipts, quantified by the attacker's instant receipt amount A (up to the vault's non-reserve balance). It breaks the "one receipt, one funded payout" invariant: instant receipts are treated as payable at par even though `pendingInstantWithdraws` explicitly tracks that they were never funded.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an epoch where instant withdrawals are enabled, a pending instant receipt, and partial/non-funding of instant withdrawals while other funded money sits in the vault — a routine sequence around partial borrower repayment or a stopEpoch that funds normal receipts but not instant ones. The attacker is an ordinary KYC'd tranche holder; no privileged role needed. The window is realistic because claims are permissionless and receipts never expire.

### Recommendation
Gate `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` on funding: track funded vs pending instant basis (e.g., pay out min(receipt, funded share) or revert while the user's epoch receipt is still inside `pendingInstantWithdraws`), or record per-epoch funding ratios analogous to `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` so an unfunded stale receipt cannot be redeemed at par against unrelated reserves.
