## Title
Silent `pending_inactive` → `inactive` settlement in `join_validator_set_internal()` bypasses `synchronize_delegation_pool()` and corrupts delegation-pool OLC accounting — ([File: aptos-move/framework/aptos-framework/sources/stake.move])

### Summary
`stake::join_validator_set_internal()` was modified (commit `c723ecaa`) to silently merge `stake_pool.pending_inactive` into `stake_pool.inactive` whenever the pool's lockup has expired, before checking `minimum_stake` for re-joining the validator set. [1](#0-0) 
This mutation happens directly on the underlying `StakePool` and is triggered by any operator calling the permissionless-to-operator entry point `join_validator_set`, completely bypassing `delegation_pool::synchronize_delegation_pool()`, which is the only place delegation-pool accounting (`observed_lockup_cycle`, shares pools, commission) is supposed to detect and react to inactive-stake transitions. [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`delegation_pool.move`'s own invariants state that every stake-state transition must be observed through `synchronize_delegation_pool` before the delegation pool's OLC (observed lockup cycle) is advanced, and that "unlocking and/or unlocked stake originating from different real lockups are never mixed together into the same pool_u64" and "the pending withdrawal exists at an OLC iff delegator owns some shares within the shares pool of that OLC". [3](#0-2) 

`synchronize_delegation_pool()` determines whether the lockup cycle has ended (and therefore whether to advance the OLC / spin up a new `pending_inactive` shares pool) by diffing the stake pool's real `inactive`/`pending_inactive`/`active` balances against the pool_u64 bookkeeping via `calculate_stake_pool_drift`, and only the DelegationPool's own operations (`unlock_internal`, `reactivate_stake`, `withdraw_internal`, `add_stake`) call `synchronize_delegation_pool` first. [4](#0-3) 

However, when a delegation-pool-owned `StakePool` becomes `VALIDATOR_STATUS_INACTIVE` (e.g. the operator or lack of stake caused it to leave the active set) and its lockup subsequently expires, an operator can simply call `stake::join_validator_set(operator, pool_address)` directly (this is a public entry function on `stake`, not gated through `delegation_pool`). Inside `join_validator_set_internal`, the new code silently drains `pending_inactive` into `inactive` on the raw `StakePool`: [5](#0-4) 

This mutates the ground-truth stake pool state that `delegation_pool` accounting depends on *without* going through `synchronize_delegation_pool`. When a delegator later interacts with the pool (`withdraw`, `unlock`, `add_stake`, etc.) and `synchronize_delegation_pool` finally runs, `calculate_stake_pool_drift` will observe an `inactive` amount that jumped by the entire previous `pending_inactive` balance in one shot, coincident with (or even after) the operator's `join_validator_set` call rather than at the natural lockup-driven event the accounting model assumes. Because the delegation pool's per-OLC pending-withdrawal correctness depends on `pending_inactive`→`inactive` transitions being observed strictly through `synchronize_delegation_pool`'s own drift computation at the moment they occur relative to `add_stake`/`unlock`/`reactivate_stake`/`withdraw` calls, an externally-triggered, out-of-band transition (triggered by the operator calling `stake::join_validator_set` — a function requiring only the operator role, not the DelegationPool owner or any privileged governance role) can desynchronize the timing assumed by `calculate_stake_pool_drift`/OLC-advance logic, e.g. combined with a subsequent `add_stake` from another delegator in the same underlying epoch, causing the `add_stake` fee/no-fee branch or commission computation (`pool_active`/`pool_pending_inactive` deltas) at `synchronize_delegation_pool` to attribute rewards/commission incorrectly, or to advance the OLC and mark pending withdrawals belonging to the un-advanced OLC index as ready when they should not be, allowing delegators to withdraw stake tied to a lockup cycle that has not actually been synchronized/settled by the pool's own accounting.

### Impact Explanation
If the OLC/committed-inactive timing invariant is broken, delegator "pending inactive" shares from one real lockup cycle can be redeemed/withdrawn against inactive coin balances that were produced from an unrelated event (the operator's re-join), and operator commission calculated in `calculate_stake_pool_drift`/`synchronize_delegation_pool` on `active`/`pending_inactive` deltas could compute against a base that has been silently altered underneath it — potentially crediting or debiting the wrong shareholder or the operator/beneficiary's commission share incorrectly. This falls under "operator commission... or share-accounting corruption that credits the wrong account or traps value," a High-severity mainnet-relevant impact per the stated scope.

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires only: (1) a delegation-pool-backed validator going inactive, (2) its lockup expiring, and (3) the operator (an unprivileged, non-owner role from the delegation-pool's perspective) calling the standard `join_validator_set` entry function before any delegator triggers `synchronize_delegation_pool`. This is a normal, expected operational sequence (operators frequently re-join after being kicked out for falling below `minimum_stake`), making the precondition highly likely to occur in production without any privileged action.

### Recommendation
`join_validator_set_internal` should not directly mutate `StakePool.pending_inactive`/`inactive` balances. Instead, either (a) call the existing shared settlement helper used by `withdraw_with_cap` in a way that is also observable/triggered through `delegation_pool::synchronize_delegation_pool` before any operator-facing stake-pool mutation on a pool owned by a `DelegationPool`, or (b) require `join_validator_set` on a delegation-pool-owned pool to route through `delegation_pool::synchronize_delegation_pool` first so the OLC-transition accounting sees the settlement at the correct, well-ordered point instead of being silently pre-empted.

### Proof of Concept
Note: I was not able to fully trace `calculate_stake_pool_drift`'s exact OLC-advance predicate in this session (the tool call to inspect its body did not return before the tool budget was exhausted), so I cannot present a concrete, line-exact numeric PoC showing the corrupted balance/wrong beneficiary in this answer. What is proven locally is: (1) the pending_inactive→inactive merge was newly added to `join_validator_set_internal` (not present in `withdraw_with_cap`'s pre-existing, narrower "edge case" merge) [6](#0-5) , (2) it is reachable by the unprivileged operator role via the public entry `join_validator_set` [7](#0-6) , and (3) it mutates state that `delegation_pool`'s documented accounting invariants require to only change via `synchronize_delegation_pool` [8](#0-7) .

**This finding should be treated as a lead requiring further verification of `calculate_stake_pool_drift`'s exact logic (not retrieved in this session) before being considered fully proven** — I recommend starting a Devin/engineering session with terminal+full-repo access to trace `calculate_stake_pool_drift` and write an integration test (mint stake → join validator set → unlock → let validator leave set → expire lockup → call `join_validator_set` again → have a second delegator `add_stake`/`unlock` → assert delegator balances/commission against expected values) to confirm whether value is actually misattributed, since the index used for this session did not return that function's full body despite two targeted searches.

**File:** aptos-move/framework/aptos-framework/sources/delegation_pool.move (L17-49)
```text
In order to distinguish between stakes in different states and route rewards accordingly,
separate pool_u64 pools are used for individual stake states:
1. one of <code>active</code> + <code>pending_active</code> stake
2. one of <code>inactive</code> stake FOR each past observed lockup cycle (OLC) on the stake pool
3. one of <code>pending_inactive</code> stake scheduled during this ongoing OLC

As stake-state transitions and rewards are computed only at the stake pool level, the delegation pool
gets outdated. To mitigate this, at any interaction with the delegation pool, a process of synchronization
to the underlying stake pool is executed before the requested operation itself.

At synchronization:
 - stake deviations between the two pools are actually the rewards produced in the meantime.
 - the commission fee is extracted from the rewards, the remaining stake is distributed to the internal
pool_u64 pools and then the commission stake used to buy shares for operator.
 - if detecting that the lockup expired on the stake pool, the delegation pool will isolate its
pending_inactive stake (now inactive) and create a new pool_u64 to host future pending_inactive stake
scheduled this newly started lockup.
Detecting a lockup expiration on the stake pool resumes to detecting new inactive stake.

Accounting main invariants:
 - each stake-management operation (add/unlock/reactivate/withdraw) and operator change triggers
the synchronization process before executing its own function.
 - each OLC maps to one or more real lockups on the stake pool, but not the opposite. Actually, only a real
lockup with 'activity' (which inactivated some unlocking stake) triggers the creation of a new OLC.
 - unlocking and/or unlocked stake originating from different real lockups are never mixed together into
the same pool_u64. This invalidates the accounting of which rewards belong to whom.
 - no delegator can have unlocking and/or unlocked stake (pending withdrawals) in different OLCs. This ensures
delegators do not have to keep track of the OLCs when they unlocked. When creating a new pending withdrawal,
the existing one is executed (withdrawn) if is already inactive.
 - <code>add_stake</code> fees are always refunded, but only after the epoch when they have been charged ends.
 - withdrawing pending_inactive stake (when validator had gone inactive before its lockup expired)
does not inactivate any stake additional to the requested one to ensure OLC would not advance indefinitely.
 - the pending withdrawal exists at an OLC iff delegator owns some shares within the shares pool of that OLC.
```
