## Title
Builder payment weight is credited from attestations regardless of the attester's payload-availability vote, letting a proposer/builder push a bid to quorum without honest approval - (File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md)

### Summary
In Gloas, `process_attestation` accrues `BuilderPendingPayment.weight` — the tally that `process_builder_pending_payments` later compares against `get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold(state)` to decide whether the builder actually gets paid — based only on whether an attester newly sets a participation flag for a same-slot attestation, with no check on which side of the payload-availability vote (`data.index`) the attester actually cast. This mirrors the reported `Buyout.end()` bug: a quantity meant to represent authorized, intentional approval (`sellFractions` deposits / here, "attesters who observed and approved a full builder payload") is instead computed from a raw, unfiltered aggregate (`IERC1155.balanceOf` / here, "any attester who sets a new flag this slot"), letting the controlling party manufacture the passing condition.

The weight increment condition tests only: (a) whether the attestation sets a *new* participation flag, (b) whether the validator had zero prior flags, (c) `is_attestation_same_slot`, and (d) that a non-zero pending withdrawal exists for that slot. It never inspects `data.index` (the field that encodes the attester's belief about whether the execution payload was present/full) to confirm the attester actually voted that the builder's payload was delivered. As a result, an attester's effective balance is added to the "approval weight" for the builder's payment purely because that attester's *first* attestation for the epoch landed on that slot — regardless of whether that attestation approved or rejected the payload.

`process_builder_pending_payments` in the epoch transition then treats crossing the quorum purely as `payment.weight >= quorum`: [2](#0-1) 

Just as `Buyout.end()` used `IERC1155(token).balanceOf(address(this), id)` — a raw balance check indifferent to *how* the tokens arrived (via the time-boxed, authorized `sellFractions` path, or via a bare `safeTransferFrom`) — `process_builder_pending_payments` uses a raw `weight` accumulator that is indifferent to *what* the attesters actually attested to. The "51% attack" analog here is: a proposer (or a builder colluding with a proposer) can arrange for a slot's first-ever attestations from high-effective-balance validators to be *any* valid same-slot attestation (honest network traffic will naturally supply this over the course of the epoch, since `had_no_participation` only needs to be true once per validator per epoch), inflating `weight` to quorum even though none of those attesters ever signaled agreement that the builder's payload was actually delivered/available.

### Impact Explanation
If `weight` crosses `get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold(state)` without a genuine supermajority of attesters affirmatively voting `payload_present`/`blob_data_available`, `process_builder_pending_payments` appends the corresponding `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` to `state.builder_pending_withdrawals`, which is later paid out (Gwei debited from the builder's balance and sent to `fee_recipient`) in `process_withdrawals`/builder sweep processing. This is a payment applied that the honest PTC/attester vote did not actually commit to — a builder payment released on a false quorum signal, which the report's rules classify as High/Critical impact ("a builder payment or withdrawal misdirected... a payload executed or paid that the block did not commit to").

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires no coalition beyond the proposer/builder relationship already assumed for payload bids, and does not need a malicious peer or client bug — it exploits the exact weight-accrual condition as written in the spec text. However, I was not able to fully verify within the available context whether `data.index`/participation-flag semantics are cross-checked elsewhere (e.g., in `get_attestation_participation_flag_indices`, whose body I could not load in the remaining budget) in a way that ties `will_set_new_flag` specifically to a payload-approving vote. If `get_attestation_participation_flag_indices` only ever assigns the relevant flag when the attester's `data.index`/payload vote agrees with the recorded block timeliness, this finding would be substantially weakened or moot. This uncertainty means the finding should be treated as a plausible analog requiring confirmation against the full body of `get_attestation_participation_flag_indices`, not a fully proven exploit.

### Recommendation
Gate the `payment.weight += ...` increment on an explicit check that the attester's vote (`data.index` / the specific participation flag being set) affirmatively corresponds to "payload present" for that slot, not merely on "this validator set some new flag." Equivalently, track weight only from votes that match the flag index tied to payload-timeliness approval, mirroring the fix recommended for the ERC1155 bug: only count contributions made through the intended, verified approval path.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual PoC (bounded by inability to execute code in this environment):
1. Proposer P builds a bid, gets it included; `builder_pending_payments[slot]` is created with `amount > 0`.
2. Over the course of the epoch, validators V1..Vn (chosen for high effective balance) submit their first attestation of the epoch specifically targeting this slot as `is_attestation_same_slot`, without necessarily affirming payload presence in the way `get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold` intends.
3. Because `had_no_participation` is true (their first attestation this epoch) and `will_set_new_flag` is true, `payment.weight` accrues their full effective balance regardless of the semantic content of their vote.
4. Once accumulated weight reaches `get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold(state)`, `process_builder_pending_payments` unconditionally schedules the withdrawal — releasing the builder's payment on a quorum that was not actually built from genuine "payload approved" votes.

This scenario should be validated against `test_builder_payment_weight_tracking*` in `tests/core/pyspec/eth_consensus_specs/test/gloas/block_processing/test_process_attestation.py` [3](#0-2)  and against the full definition of `get_attestation_participation_flag_indices` to confirm whether the flag actually encodes payload approval before this can be considered a confirmed, exploitable vulnerability rather than a plausible analog.

def process_builder_pending_payments(state: BeaconState) -> None:
    """
    Processes the builder pending payments from the previous epoch.
    """
    quorum = get_builder_payment_quorum_threshold(state)
    for payment in state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH]:
        if payment.weight >= quorum:
            state.builder_pending_withdrawals.append(payment.withdrawal)

    old_payments = state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:]
    state.builder_pending_payments[:SLOTS_PER_EPOCH] = old_payments
    new_payments = [BuilderPendingPayment.empty() for _ in range(SLOTS_PER_EPOCH)]
    state.builder_pending_payments[SLOTS_PER_EPOCH:] = new_payments
