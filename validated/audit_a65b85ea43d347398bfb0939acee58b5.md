This is a real, demonstrable bug. `createDstEscrow` performs no validation on the `parameters` bytes field of the caller-supplied `Immutables` struct [1](#0-0) , so an unprivileged caller can set `parameters` to any length they like while still correctly funding the clone with the real `amount`/`safetyDeposit` via `_deployEscrow`/`safeTransferFrom`.

`ImmutablesLib`'s calldata accessors gate each field on a strictly increasing length threshold (`0x20` for `protocolFeeAmountCd`, `0x40` for `integratorFeeAmountCd`, `0x60` for `protocolFeeRecipientCd`, `0x80` for `integratorFeeRecipientCd`) [2](#0-1) . A 65-byte (`0x41`) blob passes the two amount checks (`>= 0x40`) but fails any recipient check (`>= 0x60`/`>= 0x80`), reverting with `IndexOutOfRange`.

`EscrowDst._withdraw` — used by both `withdraw` and `publicWithdraw` — unconditionally calls the fee-amount and (when the decoded amount is non-zero) fee-recipient accessors before ever moving funds [3](#0-2) . If the attacker crafts the 65-byte blob so the decoded `integratorFeeAmount` (word at offset `0x20`) is non-zero — trivial, since it's fully attacker-controlled calldata — the call to `integratorFeeRecipientCd()` reverts every time, regardless of who calls or when. Since `publicWithdraw` just wraps `_withdraw` with a different modifier set [4](#0-3) , the public safety-valve is bricked identically to the private path.

Recovery is limited to `cancel()` (taker-only, after `DstCancellation`) which doesn't touch `parameters` at all and simply returns the deposited `amount` to `immutables.taker` [5](#0-4) , or `rescueFunds()`, also gated to the taker [6](#0-5) . Neither path can ever deliver the intended payout to the maker.

This is significant because `publicWithdraw`/`onlyAccessTokenHolder` exists specifically as a liveness backstop so that an uncooperative or malicious taker can't strand the maker's destination-side proceeds — any access-token holder is normally supposed to be able to force settlement in exchange for the safety deposit [7](#0-6) . A crafted `parameters` length permanently disables that backstop for the specific escrow instance, so the only exit path returns the destination-side deposit to the taker instead of the maker — matching the reported invariant break ("even the public path reverts on fee decoding").

### Title
Malformed `parameters` length in attacker-supplied `createDstEscrow` immutables permanently bricks `EscrowDst.withdraw`/`publicWithdraw` - (File: contracts/EscrowDst.sol, contracts/libraries/ImmutablesLib.sol)

### Summary
`BaseEscrowFactory.createDstEscrow` accepts an arbitrary, unvalidated `parameters` byte blob from any caller. By choosing a length between `0x40` and `0x80` bytes (e.g. 65 bytes) with a non-zero encoded fee amount, an unprivileged destination-escrow deployer can guarantee that `ImmutablesLib.integratorFeeRecipientCd`/`protocolFeeRecipientCd` always revert with `IndexOutOfRange` inside `EscrowDst._withdraw`, bricking both `withdraw` and `publicWithdraw` for that escrow instance regardless of secret validity or caller.

### Finding Description
`createDstEscrow` copies the caller-supplied `dstImmutables` (including `parameters`) directly into the deployed clone's salt/state with no format or length check [1](#0-0) . `ImmutablesLib`'s calldata decoders each independently gate on `parameters.length` thresholds without cross-checking consistency between the amount and recipient fields [2](#0-1) . `EscrowDst._withdraw` decodes amounts first and then unconditionally decodes the corresponding recipient whenever the amount is non-zero [8](#0-7) , so a length that satisfies the amount check but not the recipient check causes a deterministic revert on every future call to `_withdraw`, for both the private (`withdraw`) and public (`publicWithdraw`) entrypoints.

### Impact Explanation
Once deployed this way, no one — not the taker, not the maker, not any access-token holder using the public backstop — can ever complete `withdraw`/`publicWithdraw` on this escrow, even with the correct secret. The only paths available are `cancel()` (returns funds to the taker, not the maker) and `rescueFunds()` (taker-only). This freezes the maker's intended destination-chain proceeds for the entire withdrawal window and defeats the permissionless `publicWithdraw` liveness guarantee that exists precisely to prevent an uncooperative taker from stranding the maker.

### Likelihood Explanation
High. `createDstEscrow` is fully permissionless and payable by any address; the only requirement is funding the correct `amount`/`safetyDeposit`, which the attacker (as taker of their own crafted escrow) already controls. Crafting a 65-byte `parameters` blob with an arbitrary non-zero word requires no special privilege, race condition, or governance access.

### Recommendation
Validate `parameters` length/format at escrow-creation time in `createDstEscrow` (e.g., require `parameters.length == 0` or exactly `4 * 32` bytes matching the expected `abi.encode(protocolFeeAmount, integratorFeeAmount, protocolFeeRecipient, integratorFeeRecipient)` layout), or have `ImmutablesLib` decoders enforce a single consistent minimum length for all four fields together rather than independent staggered thresholds, so partial/malformed blobs are rejected atomically at creation rather than causing a permanent revert at withdrawal time.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `escrowFactory.createDstEscrow{value: safetyDeposit(+amount if native)}(immutables, srcCancellationTimestamp)` where `immutables.parameters` is a crafted 65-byte blob whose second 32-byte word (bytes `[32:64]`) is non-zero (this decodes as `integratorFeeAmount`), matching `test/integration/ResolverMock.t.sol`'s `_prepareDataDst` flow but substituting `parameters` [9](#0-8) .
2. Escrow deploys successfully and is funded with the real `dstToken` amount and safety deposit.
3. Warp past `DstWithdrawal`; call `dstClone.withdraw(secret, immutables)` with a correct secret — reverts with `ImmutablesLib.IndexOutOfRange` inside `integratorFeeRecipientCd()` [10](#0-9) .
4. Warp past `DstPublicWithdrawal`; call `dstClone.publicWithdraw(secret, immutables)` from any access-token holder — reverts identically [4](#0-3) .
5. Only `cancel()` after `DstCancellation` succeeds, returning the dst-side funds to the attacker/taker rather than the maker [5](#0-4) .
