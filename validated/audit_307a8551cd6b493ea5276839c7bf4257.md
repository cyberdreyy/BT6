**Analysis:**

The claimed broken binding would be: `ft.total_supply` (debited via `internal_withdraw`) == NEAR actually delivered to `account_id`. Tracing `near_withdraw`: [1](#0-0) 

The receiver of the `Promise::new(account_id).transfer(amount + 1)` is always `env::predecessor_account_id()` — i.e., the caller themselves, never an attacker-chosen third party. `internal_withdraw` (from `near-sdk-rs`/`near-contract-standards`, outside this repo) debits the caller's own `ft` balance and `total_supply` before the transfer is scheduled, with no `.then()` resolver — unlike `ft_resolve_transfer` used by `ft_transfer_call`.

Key points that break the exploit chain claimed in the question:
1. **The debited party and the transfer recipient are the same account.** Any failure of the outgoing transfer only harms the caller themselves (they lose wNEAR without receiving NEAR back); no other party benefits or is harmed, so there is no "funds moved to a party not entitled to it."
2. **A bare `Promise::transfer` to an already-existing account (the caller must exist to have signed the transaction) does not execute any receiver code and does not fail** under normal NEAR protocol operation — there is no function call, no gas limit for receiver logic, and no rejection path an unprivileged attacker can trigger deterministically. This is unlike the `ft_transfer_call`/`ft_resolve_transfer` case, which routes through `ft_on_transfer` on a receiver contract that can legitimately panic.
3. **Even in the hypothetical failure case**, `ft.total_supply` decreases while the NEAR balance remains locked in the wNEAR contract — this makes the contract's real balance sheet *more* over-collateralized, not under-collateralized. It does not allow any accounting value to diverge in a way that lets a third party "claim exceeding NEAR held," nor does it release locked tokens early, nor freeze funds belonging to someone else.

Since the attacker cannot direct the transfer to a victim, cannot force failure of a same-account native transfer, and even a failure would not produce a fund-extraction or third-party impact matching the Critical/High categories (no NEAR leaves the contract to an unauthorized party; no accounting divergence exploitable by another party), this does not meet the required impact bar.
