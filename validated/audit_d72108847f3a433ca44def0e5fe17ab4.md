### Title
Stale per-epoch instant-withdraw refcount is never cleared on funded claims, inflating default recovery basis and double-paying/leaking reserve — (`contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol`)

### Summary
`IdleCreditVault.requestInstantWithdraw` tracks instant receipts in two ledgers: the aggregate `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` and the per-epoch `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epoch]` / `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]` counters. The normal claim path `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` clears only the aggregate ledger (lines 387–392); the per-epoch refcount is cleared exclusively in `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` (lines 844–848). A funded instant receipt that is claimed normally therefore leaves a "leaked" per-epoch refcount — the exact analog of the CVE-2023-25399 refcount leak — which is later re-counted as claim basis during `finalizeDefaultRecovery` / `defaultPendingClaimBasis` when the same epoch defaults.

### Finding Description
Sequence (all unprivileged actions around honest manager/borrower calls):

1. Epoch `E` running, instant withdrawals enabled (`allowInstantWithdraw`/`instantWithdrawDelay` configured by manager). Attacker calls `requestInstantWithdraw(X, attacker)` via the CDO: `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[attacker][E] += X`, `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[E] += X`, `pendingInstantWithdraws += X` (lines 366–374).
2. CDO funds it via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` (`pendingInstantWithdraws -= X`, line 401) and the attacker calls `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` normally: `instantWithdrawsRequests[attacker] = 0` and `X` underlying paid — but `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[attacker][E]` and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[E]` are **not** decremented (lines 387–392).
3. Another user's instant request in epoch `E` stays unfunded, so `pendingInstantWithdraws > 0` at epoch end.
4. `stopEpoch` fails to pull borrower funds → `_handleBorrowerDefault` → `finalizeDefaultRecovery`. `defaultRecoveryEpoch = epochNumber = E` and `defaultInstantWithdrawsFinalized = pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` (lines 693–696).
5. `defaultPendingClaimBasis()` adds `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[E]`, which still includes the attacker's already-paid `X` (lines 644–648). `recoveryPrice = reserveAmount / totalBasis` is therefore diluted by phantom basis `X`, and the corresponding reserve slice is earmarked for a receipt that no longer exists.
6. After finalization, any call to `claimInstantWithdrawRequest(attacker)` executes `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`, which reads the stale entry `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[attacker][E] = X` and does `instantWithdrawsRequests[attacker] -= X` (line 848). Two outcomes, both harmful:
   - If the attacker's aggregate is `< X` (e.g., 0 after the normal claim), the subtraction underflows and the whole claim entry point reverts — and the reserve slice sized for the phantom claim can never be released, permanently locking part of `defaultRecoveryReserve` while `recoveryPrice` was lowered for every legitimate claimant.
   - If the attacker holds another instant receipt `Y > X` in a different epoch (so the aggregate covers the subtraction), the stale `X` is paid out again at `defaultRecoveryPrice` — a direct double-spend of the isolated recovery reserve at the expense of other defaulted-epoch claimants.

No guard prevents this: `_transferFundedClaim`/`_transferDefaultRecovery` only check balances, the `epochEndDate`/`epochNumber` gating in `_claimFundedWithdrawRequest` does not apply to instant receipts, and `_ensureDefaultRecoveryInitialized` only checks `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` once at upgrade.

### Impact Explanation
Direct theft of default recovery funds (double payment of `X * defaultRecoveryPrice / RECOVERY_FULL`) when the attacker holds a covering aggregate receipt, or permanent freezing of the reserve slice allocated to the phantom basis plus a diluted `defaultRecoveryPrice` for all other claimants when the subtraction underflows. Loss magnitude is bounded by the attacker's instant-withdraw size `X`, which is limited only by their deposited tranche value — an unprivileged lender can scale it to a large fraction of the epoch's instant queue.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires instant withdrawals enabled (a supported, configurable mode), at least one other unfunded instant receipt at epoch end, and a borrower default in the same epoch. Defaults are a designed-for state (`finalizeDefaultRecovery` exists precisely for this), and the attacker fully controls steps 1–2, so the preconditions need only one coinciding unfunded request — a common case whenever instant claims outpace funding.

### Recommendation
In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, clear the per-epoch ledgers in the same way `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` does — i.e., iterate/clear `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user]` entries and decrement `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]` for every epoch contributing to `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`. Alternatively, stop writing the per-epoch counters for receipts that can be claimed pre-default, or record each receipt's epoch list so normal claims decrement the matching epoch bucket. Additionally, make `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` clamp `claimBasis` to `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` so a stale entry can never underflow or over-pay.

Key assertions: `strategy.instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch(attacker, E)` remains `X` after the normal claim (the leaked refcount), `defaultPendingClaimBasis()` over-counts by `X`, and the post-default claim path either reverts (reserve permanently locked) or transfers `X * defaultRecoveryPrice / RECOVERY_FULL` twice (theft).

Caveat: the exact CDO entry points used to route `requestInstantWithdraw` and the ordering of `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` vs. claims should be confirmed against `IdleCDOEpochVariant` instant-withdraw helpers when writing the runnable test; the vault-side accounting flaw shown above is independent of those details.
