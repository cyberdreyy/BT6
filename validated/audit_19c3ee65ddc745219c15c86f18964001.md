## Finding

### Title
`getSupply`/`getLargestAccounts` RPC handlers compute non-circulating-supply scan and total-supply/second-scan from an actively-mutating "processed" `Bank`, yielding an internally inconsistent supply snapshot - (File: `rpc/src/rpc.rs`)

### Summary
The Balancer report is a case of combining two data points — a balance scan and a supply counter — that are read at different points in time from state that can mutate in between, producing an inconsistent, exploitable result. The equivalent pattern exists in Agave's `getSupply` and `getLargestAccounts` JSON-RPC handlers, which combine an account scan (`calculate_non_circulating_supply`) with a separately-read `capitalization()` counter (or a second scan) taken from the same `Bank`, but that `Bank` can still be actively mutating between the two reads when queried at `processed` commitment.

### Finding Description
`JsonRpcRequestProcessor::bank()` resolves `processed`/default commitment to the "heaviest slot" bank via `bank_forks.get(slot)` [1](#0-0) . This can be the still-unfrozen working bank that is actively being mutated as transactions continue to land (accounts stored, `capitalization` updated) — it is not a frozen, immutable snapshot.

In `get_supply`, the handler takes this `Arc<Bank>` once, then:
1. Spawns a blocking scan of stake-program accounts to compute `non_circulating_supply` via `calculate_non_circulating_supply` [2](#0-1) .
2. After that scan/await completes, reads `bank.capitalization()` as `total_supply` [3](#0-2) .

Because the underlying `Bank` object is the same evolving working bank, and steps 1 and 2 are not atomic with respect to each other, new transactions can land (mutating `capitalization` and storing/removing stake accounts) between the scan and the later `capitalization()` read. The result: `circulating = total_supply - non_circulating_supply.lamports` [4](#0-3)  can reflect two different points in the bank's mutation timeline — exactly the "balances vs supply desynchronization" pattern from the Balancer bug.

`get_largest_accounts` is an even closer analog: it performs the *same* non-circulating-supply scan (step 1) and then performs a **second, independent scan** — `bank.get_largest_accounts(...)` — in a separate `spawn_blocking` task [5](#0-4) . Two scans of the same mutating bank at different points in time, later combined/filtered together, is structurally identical to querying `getPoolTokens` and `totalSupply` at different points during a single mutating transaction.

The core helper `calculate_non_circulating_supply` itself performs a scan (`get_filtered_indexed_accounts`/`get_program_accounts`) and then a second pass summing `bank.get_balance(pubkey)` for the discovered pubkeys [6](#0-5)  — again two reads (account-set enumeration, then balance lookup) that are not atomic against a mutating bank.

### Impact Explanation
An RPC caller (unprivileged) querying `getSupply` or `getLargestAccounts` with `processed`/default commitment against a validator that is actively producing/replaying blocks can receive a supply/largest-accounts response that mixes account state from different points in the bank's execution, i.e., "account data returned" that does not correspond to any single consistent slot state. This matches the accepted impact category of "wrong-slot/fork/account data returned" from a single, low-rate, unprivileged query.

### Likelihood Explanation
Every call to these two RPC methods without an explicit `finalized`/`confirmed` commitment is exposed to this race, since the default/processed bank is the mutable working bank. No attacker action beyond issuing the RPC call during active block production is required, so likelihood is high for the "processed"-commitment case, though the practical severity is limited (informational RPC data, not a fund-movement or consensus primitive as in the DeFi analog).

### Recommendation
For `get_supply` and `get_largest_accounts`, either:
1. Restrict/clamp the effective commitment used for these two calls to at least `confirmed` (a frozen, immutable bank) before performing multi-step scans, or
2. Capture `total_supply = bank.capitalization()` and perform the account scan against a single frozen snapshot, ensuring both values are derived from the exact same immutable bank state, or
3. Explicitly document/return a slot bound / mark the response degraded when commitment is `processed`, so clients understand the returned supply figures are not atomic.

### Proof of Concept
Not directly demonstrable without a running cluster under load, but the race can be reasoned about directly from the code:
```rust
// rpc/src/rpc.rs
let bank = self.bank(config.commitment); // may be the actively mutating working bank
let non_circulating_supply = self.calculate_non_circulating_supply(&bank).await?; // scan #1, takes time
let total_supply = bank.capitalization(); // read #2, bank may have advanced meanwhile
``` [7](#0-6)  Repeatedly calling `getSupply` with default commitment during active leader block production against a busy validator would be expected to occasionally show `circulating + non_circulating != total_supply` observed at any single slot, analogous to the Balancer BPT price mismatch caused by unsynchronized balance/supply reads.
