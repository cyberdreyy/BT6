### Title
Instant-withdraw claims pay from unsegregated strategy balance and leave stale per-epoch accounting, draining funds earmarked for other receipts - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
Analogous to `recoverToken` moving tokens without decrementing `nextAuctionGoldAmount`, `IdleCreditVault.claimInstantWithdrawRequest` transfers underlying out of the strategy without checking that the payout is backed by funds actually collected for instant withdrawals, and it never clears the per-epoch receipt accounting (`instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch` / `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch`). The result is that an unfunded instant-withdraw requester can pull underlying that was collected (via `collectWithdrawFunds`) to back other users' funded normal/loss-adjusted receipts, leaving those claimants unable to claim.

### Finding Description
`requestInstantWithdraw` mints the user a receipt and increments `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epochNumber]`, `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` and `pendingInstantWithdraws` (lines 356-375). Funding for the queue arrives later through `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`, which pulls underlying from the CDO (lines 398-403).

`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` then pays out `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` in full via `_transferFundedClaim` (lines 380-393). Two accounting facts are never updated:

- `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[_user][epoch]` and `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epoch]` remain non-zero after the claim (only the defaulted-claim path at lines 842-856 clears them).
- The payout path `_transferFundedClaim` (lines 897-907) only ring-fences `defaultRecoveryReserve`. Underlying held by the strategy to back funded normal receipts (collected by `collectWithdrawFunds`, lines 411-430, including loss-adjusted receipts paid through `lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` in `_claimLossAdjustedWithdrawRequest`, lines 789-801) is unprotected.

So the strategy's tracked obligations ("how much of this balance belongs to whom") and the transferable balance diverge — the same class of bug as `nextAuctionGoldAmount` not being decremented when tokens left the TempleDAO auction.

### Impact Explanation
A user with an instant-withdraw request can claim it at any time the strategy holds *any* underlying, even before the borrower/CDO funds the instant queue. The claim consumes balance that was collected for funded normal withdraw receipts or loss-adjusted receipts. When those legitimate claimants later call `claimWithdrawRequest`, the strategy balance is insufficient and their claims revert — their receipts are permanently unpayable because `pendingWithdraws`/`lossRecoveryPriceByEpoch` accounting was already settled at `stopEpoch`. Loss is bounded by the strategy's collected-but-unclaimed underlying and is directly stolen by the instant claimant.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a KYC-passing lender (attacker) to hold an instant-withdraw receipt while the strategy holds underlying earmarked for other claims — a routine state between `collectWithdrawFunds` and user claims. No privileged cooperation is needed; the attacker simply calls the CDO's instant-claim path before their request is funded.

### Recommendation
Track the funded instant-withdraw balance separately (e.g., decrement `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch`/`instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch` in `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` and gate payout on funds collected via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`), and extend `_transferFundedClaim`'s reserve guard to cover all earmarked balances (funded pending receipts and loss-adjusted claims), not just `defaultRecoveryReserve`.

### Proof of Concept
Caveat: I could not fully verify the CDO-side entry point that invokes `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` within my available search budget. A Foundry fork PoC would: (1) user A requests a normal withdraw, epoch stops, `collectWithdrawFunds` funds it into the strategy; (2) attacker B (KYC'd) calls `requestInstantWithdraw` then `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` in the same epoch before `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` runs; (3) B receives A's earmarked underlying and A's subsequent `claimWithdrawRequest` reverts on insufficient balance.
