Notably, the SECURITY.md explicitly states: "Note: This does not exclude oracle manipulation/flash-loan attacks" — meaning flash-loan-based manipulation is explicitly in scope for this program. This supports treating the analog below as a valid finding.

### Title
Permissionless interest accrual samples a flash-loan-manipulable utilization ratio and applies it retroactively over the entire elapsed period - ([File: programs/marginfi/src/state/bank.rs])

### Summary
The original report describes a Yearn vault penalty (`_reportLoss`) that is computed from `_totalAssets()`, a value derived from the vault's current token balance, which an attacker can temporarily inflate or deflate within a single atomic transaction (flash loan → deposit → report → withdraw → repay) to bypass or reduce an economically important calculation. The marginfi analog is `Bank::accrue_interest`, which samples `total_assets` and `total_liabilities` (both derived from current share balances, i.e. the bank's live "book value") at the instant the (permissionless) accrual instruction executes, and then applies the resulting utilization-based interest rate to the *entire* elapsed `time_delta` since the last accrual — potentially a long idle period — rather than to a time-weighted average utilization.

### Finding Description
`accrue_interest` computes the instantaneous utilization inputs directly from the bank's share accounting: [1](#0-0) 

These two point-in-time values (`total_assets`, `total_liabilities`) are fed into `calc_interest_rate_accrual_state_changes` along with the *entire* `time_delta` since `last_update`, and the resulting rate is applied uniformly across that whole period: [2](#0-1) 

`get_asset_amount`/`get_liability_amount` are simple `shares * share_value` products — `total_asset_shares` changes immediately on any deposit/withdraw, while `share_value` itself does not change from deposits, so a deposit made moments before calling `accrue_interest` directly lowers utilization (`liabilities / assets`) for that call: [3](#0-2) 

`lending_pool_accrue_bank_interest` is explicitly permissionless — "anyone" can call it, and it can be freely composed with other instructions in the same transaction: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

The docs confirm the rate is only recomputed "any time a Bank has a balance change, or [when someone sends] a (permissionless) accrue interest instruction," i.e. it is a discrete point-sample model, not time-weighted: [6](#0-5) 

Because marginfi supports atomic, same-transaction borrow/deposit/withdraw/repay via its own flashloan mechanism (`ACCOUNT_IN_FLASHLOAN` defers health checks to the end of the transaction), and because Solana also has third-party flash-loan providers, a user can: (1) flash-borrow tokens, (2) deposit them into the target bank to artificially raise `total_assets` (lowering utilization), (3) invoke the permissionless `lending_pool_accrue_bank_interest` on that bank so the depressed-utilization rate is baked into `asset_share_value`/`liability_share_value` for the whole elapsed `time_delta`, (4) withdraw the deposit, (5) repay the flash loan — all in one atomic transaction: [7](#0-6) 

This is structurally identical to the reported bug class: an economically significant computation (here, the interest/utilization rate applied to *all* depositors' and borrowers' balances) depends on a bank-balance-derived value (`total_assets`) that can be transiently manipulated within a single atomic transaction, and the manipulated value is then durably committed to on-chain state (`asset_share_value`/`liability_share_value`), persisting after the manipulation is reversed.

### Impact Explanation
A large borrower in a bank that has gone a long time without an accrual (e.g., a low-traffic or newly re-enabled bank) can flash-inflate deposits immediately before triggering accrual, suppressing the interest rate charged against their own (and every other borrower's) outstanding debt for that entire elapsed period, at the direct expense of lenders in that bank who earn correspondingly less interest for the same period. The manipulation is durable: once `accrue_interest` commits the new `asset_share_value`/`liability_share_value`, the discount cannot be reversed even after the manipulator withdraws their flash-deposited liquidity. Conversely, the same lever could be used by a large depositor to *inflate* the perceived interest rate seen by third parties reading bank state (e.g., risk dashboards, other integrators) by depressing `total_assets` via a timed withdrawal before an accrual, which is the closer analog to Alice being misled about risk in the original report.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires: (a) locating a bank with a stale `last_update` (a meaningfully large `time_delta`), (b) enough flash-loan-able capital in the underlying mint to move utilization meaningfully, and (c) the manipulator (or an accomplice) holding/opening a debt position in that bank to benefit. All of the prerequisite building blocks (permissionless accrual, atomic flashloan/deposit/withdraw composition, and the direct dependence of utilization on live share balances) exist in the current code, and SECURITY.md explicitly does not exclude flash-loan attacks for this program. However, exploitation profitability depends on stale-accrual windows being large enough and on the elapsed-period rate delta being worth more than flash-loan fees plus the manipulator's own debt size — this bounds the practical likelihood to specific, less-frequently-accrued banks or unusually large positions.

### Recommendation
- Short term: Do not apply a single instantaneous utilization sample retroactively to the entire elapsed `time_delta`. Either (a) require/incentivize frequent accrual (e.g., via keeper crank with tight bounded intervals) to shrink the exploitable window, or (b) use a time-weighted-average utilization (TWAU), snapshotting utilization at each balance-changing instruction and integrating over time, similar to TWAP oracle designs.
- Long term: Audit all penalty/rate/limit computations (`accrue_interest`, `maybe_get_asset_weight_init_discount`, `get_remaining_deposit_capacity`, `socialize_loss`) for dependence on instantaneously-manipulable share/balance state, and add fuzzing/invariant tests (e.g., via the existing Trident fuzz harness) that specifically compose flashloan borrow/deposit/withdraw sequences around `lending_pool_accrue_bank_interest` to bound the maximum extractable value from utilization manipulation.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual sequence (not yet implemented against the test harness):
1. Attacker holds an existing borrow position in Bank X; `Bank.last_update` is stale (large `time_delta` has passed with no accrual).
2. In a single transaction: `LendingAccountStartFlashloan` → borrow a large amount of an external flash-loan-provided token, swap/acquire Bank X's mint, `lending_account_deposit` a large amount into Bank X (raising `total_asset_shares`/`total_assets`), call permissionless `lending_pool_accrue_bank_interest` on Bank X (which now computes a depressed utilization ratio and commits a lower interest rate over the full stale `time_delta` into `asset_share_value`/`liability_share_value`), `lending_account_withdraw` the deposited amount back out, repay the external flash loan, `LendingAccountEndFlashloan`.
3. Result: Attacker's outstanding liability in Bank X accrued interest at an artificially suppressed rate for the entire stale period, while genuine depositors in Bank X earned correspondingly less than they should have — with no lasting capital commitment from the attacker.

Note: I was not able to execute this against the test suite (e.g., `programs/marginfi/tests/user_actions/flash_loan.rs`) to empirically confirm the magnitude of extractable value in this session; a background Devin session with terminal access would be needed to write and run a concrete `solana-program-test`/Trident-based PoC that quantifies the interest-rate delta achievable and confirms it exceeds flash-loan fees.

5 in between), but the majority of banks use only 2 (0 at 0 UR, one in between, and a value at 100
UR).
