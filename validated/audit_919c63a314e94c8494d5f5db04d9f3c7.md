Confirmed: I found a concrete, exploitable analog of the fee-on-transfer "committed amount ≠ actually deducted amount" bug class in the Gloas fork's builder withdrawal path.

### Title
Builder pending withdrawal mints the full requested amount on the EL while the CL only debits `min(amount, balance)` — ([File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md])

### Summary
`get_builder_withdrawals` in `specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md` builds the `Withdrawal` object sent to the execution layer using the **raw requested** `withdrawal.amount` from `state.builder_pending_withdrawals`, without capping it to the builder's actual balance. `apply_withdrawals`, however, deducts only `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` from `state.builders[builder_index].balance`. Since the execution layer mints exactly the `Withdrawal.amount` value committed by the CL (this is the same value verified against the payload in `process_withdrawals`/envelope processing), a builder whose balance is insufficient at the time the withdrawal is scheduled will have EL-side Gwei minted to `fee_recipient` that is not backed by any CL-side balance debit of the same size.

### Finding Description
In `specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md`, `get_builder_withdrawals` (lines ~1802-1834): [1](#0-0) 

builds the `Withdrawal(... amount=withdrawal.amount)` directly from `state.builder_pending_withdrawals[*].amount`, i.e. the **requested** amount recorded when the builder's payment was queued in `process_execution_payload_bid` / `apply_parent_execution_payload`. No comparison with the builder's current balance is performed here.

`apply_withdrawals` (lines ~1920-1932) then applies the deduction: [2](#0-1) 

```python
if is_builder_index(withdrawal.validator_index):
    builder_index = convert_validator_index_to_builder_index(withdrawal.validator_index)
    builder_balance = state.builders[builder_index].balance
    state.builders[builder_index].balance -= min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)
```

This caps the CL-side deduction at the builder's balance, but the `Withdrawal.amount` field that was already embedded in `state.payload_expected_withdrawals` (and asserted against the execution payload / envelope) is the uncapped, requested amount. The test suite explicitly documents this asymmetry: [3](#0-2) 

In `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance_realistic_bounds`, a builder with balance `MIN_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT + 122` Gwei has a pending withdrawal requesting `MIN_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT + 123` Gwei. The test asserts the *withdrawal entry* still carries the full `123`-excess `withdrawal_amount` (the requested amount), while `builders[0].balance` goes to `0` — i.e., the deduction is capped by 1 Gwei short of what's committed to the EL.

This is the fee-on-transfer bug class in reverse: instead of the received amount being *less* than what the sender's balance-tracking assumes, here the recipient-side commitment (`Withdrawal.amount`, minted 1:1 on the EL) is *greater* than what the CL debits from the source (`builder.balance`), because the debit silently saturates via `min()` while the committed/paid amount does not.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the equality "Gwei paid out to `fee_recipient` == Gwei removed from the paying entity's (`builder`) balance." Every `Withdrawal` in `payload_expected_withdrawals` is guaranteed by consensus to be honored 1:1 by the execution layer (the withdrawal's `amount` field is minted at `fee_recipient` on the EL side, per EIP-4895/7002-style withdrawal semantics inherited from Capella/Electra and required to match the beacon block's execution payload). If the CL only actually debits `min(amount, balance)` from the builder while committing the un-capped `amount` to the EL, then Gwei is created out of nothing on the EL side beyond what was backed by the builder's CL balance — a direct violation of the "Gwei created" impact criterion (Critical: Gwei created, destroyed, or paid to a non-owner).

Because a builder can drive their own balance below their own pending withdrawal amount (e.g., by having multiple queued pending withdrawals executed in the same or prior blocks, or their balance being reduced via `is_active_builder`/exit-related balance flows before the pending withdrawal is swept), the shortfall is attacker-controllable and requires no coalition or client bug — only a builder queuing a withdrawal it knows it cannot fully cover by the time the sweep executes.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate-to-high: any builder that queues `builder_pending_withdrawals` faster than their balance can support (e.g., multiple large bids queued in quick succession, or balance reduced by intervening builder exits/penalties before the pending withdrawal item is processed) can trigger this deterministically and repeatably, since `get_builder_withdrawals` performs no balance check before embedding the requested amount into the committed `Withdrawal`.

### Recommendation
In `get_builder_withdrawals` (specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md), compute the withdrawal amount as `min(withdrawal.amount, state.builders[builder_index].balance)` at the point the `Withdrawal` object is constructed (mirroring what `get_pending_partial_withdrawals` does for validators via `get_balance_after_withdrawals`/`min(...)`), so the amount committed to the EL always matches what `apply_withdrawals` actually debits from `state.builders[*].balance`. Alternatively, remove the `min()` saturation in `apply_withdrawals` and instead assert `withdrawal.amount <= builder_balance` at withdrawal-construction time, causing an invalid state rather than silently minting uncovered Gwei.

### Proof of Concept
1. Builder `B` accumulates two `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` entries in `state.builder_pending_withdrawals` (e.g. via two large bids settled through `settle_builder_payment`/`apply_parent_execution_payload`), each requesting `X` Gwei, while `state.builders[B].balance == X` (only enough to cover one).
2. At the next `process_withdrawals` call, `get_builder_withdrawals` iterates `builder_pending_withdrawals` and emits two `Withdrawal` entries, each with `amount = X` (uncapped, per-entry, no running total check against balance) — see [1](#0-0) .
3. These two `Withdrawal(amount=X)` entries are placed into `state.payload_expected_withdrawals` and are required to be matched 1:1 by the execution payload (minting `X` to `fee_recipient` for each).
4. `apply_withdrawals` processes them sequentially: first entry debits `min(X, X) = X` from `builders[B].balance`, leaving it at `0`; second entry debits `min(X, 0) = 0` (per [2](#0-1) ).
5. Net result: EL mints `2X` Gwei to `fee_recipient` (as committed by the two `Withdrawal` entries), while CL only ever removed `X` Gwei from the builder's balance — `X` Gwei of unauthorized Gwei creation, matching the pattern verified by the repo's own test `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance_realistic_bounds`, which explicitly asserts the withdrawal's committed amount remains the full requested amount even when balance is insufficient to cover it.
