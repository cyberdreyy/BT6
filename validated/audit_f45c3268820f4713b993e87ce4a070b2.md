### Title
Pending operator commission loses beneficiary redirection after `switch_operator` because `distribute_internal` matches shares by `operator == shareholder`, not by the share's original owner - (File: `aptos-move/framework/aptos-framework/sources/staking_contract.move`)

### Summary
Your hypothesis about the exact mechanism (dust misrouted to `new_operator`'s beneficiary) is not what happens, but a related, real invariant break exists: any commission share still pending in the shared `distribution_pool` for `old_operator` at the moment `switch_operator` runs loses its beneficiary redirection once the contract is later processed under `new_operator`'s key. The funds still land at `old_operator`'s own account address (never `new_operator`'s beneficiary), but they bypass `old_operator`'s configured `beneficiary_for_operator`.

### Finding Description
`distribute_internal` redeems every shareholder's shares in `staking_contract.distribution_pool` and decides the payout recipient with a single check: `if (shareholder == operator) beneficiary_for_operator(operator) else shareholder` (the loop body itself is only partially visible in this index snapshot, but is confirmed by the `Distribute`/beneficiary event structs [4](#0-3)  and by `distribute_internal`'s signature requiring `acquires BeneficiaryForOperator` [5](#0-4) ). Every share added for commission is recorded with `recipient == operator` at `add_distribution` time inside `request_commission_internal` [6](#0-5) , i.e. the shareholder key stored in the pool is the operator address at the time commission was requested, not a stable "this share belongs to this beneficiary" marker.

`switch_operator` (lines 783-805) removes the `StakingContract` under `old_operator`, calls `distribute_internal(staker, old_operator, ...)` to flush already-inactive funds (correctly resolved against `old_operator`'s beneficiary because `operator == old_operator` at that call), then calls `request_commission_internal(old_operator, ...)` again to unlock any freshly-accrued commission. That second call adds a *new* share to the same `distribution_pool` keyed to `old_operator`, but this share is only *unlocked* (moved to `pending_inactive`), not yet paid out — it can't be paid until the stake pool's lockup ends. The `StakingContract` (with this pending share still inside it) is then reinserted into the map under `new_operator`.

Any later trigger of `distribute_internal` for this contract (via the permissionless `distribute` entry function, `unlock_stake`, `request_commission`, or `update_commision`) is now invoked with `operator == new_operator`. When the loop reaches the leftover share whose `shareholder == old_operator`, the comparison `shareholder == operator` is `old_operator == new_operator`, which is false, so the "else" branch fires and the funds are paid directly to `old_operator`'s raw account address instead of `beneficiary_for_operator(old_operator)`.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the beneficiary invariant described by `set_beneficiary_for_operator` — a beneficiary is meant to receive commission on the operator's behalf even after the operator no longer holds a given role in the contract [7](#0-6) . If `old_operator` set a beneficiary specifically because they do not want, or cannot receive, funds directly at their own operator key (e.g. custodial payout address, key-management separation), any commission still "in flight" (unlocked but not yet inactive) at the moment of `switch_operator` will silently be redirected away from that beneficiary and paid to `old_operator`'s own account instead. This is a real change in "who can withdraw ... commission" value, matching the Stake And Lockup review's decision standard, even though it does not let `new_operator` or any third party steal the funds outright.

### Likelihood Explanation
This triggers under entirely normal, unprivileged conditions with no attacker required: a staker calls `switch_operator` while `old_operator` has unpaid/unlocking commission and has previously called `set_beneficiary_for_operator`; afterward, anyone can call the permissionless `distribute` entry function once the lockup elapses, which naturally hits this mismatch. No malicious coordination or governance/operator compromise is needed — only the routine sequence of "operator switch while commission is unlocking" plus "beneficiary configured," both of which are supported, expected flows.

### Recommendation
Track share ownership independently of the *current* map key used to invoke `distribute_internal`. For example, resolve the beneficiary redirection by checking `beneficiary_for_operator(shareholder)` unconditionally for every shareholder that has ever been an operator of this pool (or store an explicit "this shareholder is an operator" tag when the share is added in `add_distribution`/`request_commission_internal`), rather than comparing `shareholder == operator` against whichever operator is currently associated with the contract. This makes the beneficiary redirection stable across `switch_operator` transitions.

### Proof of Concept
1. Staker creates a staking contract with `operator_1`, `operator_1` calls `set_beneficiary_for_operator(beneficiary_1)`.
2. Pool earns rewards; `operator_1`'s commission has been requested (unlocked) but has not yet crossed a lockup boundary into `inactive` (i.e., it's `pending_inactive`).
3. Staker calls `switch_operator(staker, operator_1, operator_2, new_commission_pct)`. This flushes already-inactive funds correctly to `beneficiary_1`, but the freshly-unlocking commission share (still `pending_inactive`, keyed to `operator_1`) is carried into the contract now stored under `operator_2`.
4. After lockup elapses, anyone calls `distribute(staker_address, operator_2)`.
5. Compare on-chain balances: `beneficiary_1` receives nothing for this leftover share; the payment instead lands directly in `operator_1`'s account, violating the expectation set by `set_beneficiary_for_operator`.

    fun request_commission_internal(
        operator: address,
        staking_contract: &mut StakingContract,
    ): u64 {
        // Unlock just the commission portion from the stake pool.
        let (total_active_stake, accumulated_rewards, commission_amount) =
            get_staking_contract_amounts_internal(staking_contract);
        staking_contract.principal = total_active_stake - commission_amount;

        // Short-circuit if there's no commission to pay.
        if (commission_amount == 0) {
            return 0
        };

        // Add a distribution for the operator.
        add_distribution(
            operator,
            staking_contract,
            operator,
            commission_amount
        );

        // Request to unlock the commission from the stake pool.
        // This won't become fully unlocked until the stake pool's lockup expires.
        stake::unlock_with_cap(commission_amount, &staking_contract.owner_cap);

        let pool_address = staking_contract.pool_address;
        emit(
            RequestCommission {
                operator,
                pool_address,
                accumulated_rewards,
                commission_amount
            }
        );

        commission_amount
    }
