### Title
Blacklisted/reverting `maker` address permanently blocks `EscrowDst.withdraw`/`publicWithdraw`, temporarily freezing resolver-committed funds - (File: contracts/EscrowDst.sol)

### Summary
`EscrowDst._withdraw` unconditionally pushes destination tokens to the `maker` address (and to `integratorFeeRecipient`/`protocolFeeRecipient`) taken verbatim from the immutables set at order-fill time [1](#0-0) . Because `maker` is derived from `order.receiver` (or `order.maker` if unset), which is fully controlled by the unprivileged order-creating maker, and the transfer uses `SafeERC20.safeTransfer`/`_uniTransfer` with no fallback on failure [2](#0-1) , a maker can supply a receiver address that a blacklist-capable token (e.g. USDC/USDT) will reject, or a contract with no acceptance logic is irrelevant for ERC20 (only native ETH cares about receive, blacklist tokens revert on `transfer`). This causes every call to `withdraw` and `publicWithdraw` to revert deterministically for the life of the escrow's withdrawal window.

### Finding Description
This is the direct analog of the Notional M-5 report: a semi-permissionless settlement/withdrawal action performs a hard ERC20 push-transfer to an externally-influenced address, and if that address is blacklisted by the token issuer, the transfer reverts and blocks the entire action for every caller, not just the intended recipient.

In `BaseEscrowFactory._postInteraction`, the destination-chain `maker` is fixed from `order.receiver` (falls back to `order.maker`) at order-fill time [3](#0-2) . This value, together with `integratorFeeRecipient`/`protocolFeeRecipient` extracted from `extraData`, is baked into the `Immutables`/`DstImmutablesComplement` and is immutable once `createDstEscrow` deploys the clone [4](#0-3) .

`EscrowDst.withdraw` (private, callable only by `taker`) and `publicWithdraw` (callable by any access-token holder after the public-withdrawal timelock) both funnel into `_withdraw`, which transfers integrator fee, protocol fee, and the remaining amount to `maker` in sequence, with no error handling [5](#0-4) . If any one of these three recipients cannot receive the token (blacklisted on USDC/USDT, or any revert-on-transfer condition), the whole `_withdraw` call reverts — for the resolver's private attempt and for every subsequent public caller — because the recipient addresses are fixed in the immutables and cannot be changed.

The only way out is `cancel()`, callable solely by `taker` after `DstCancellation` starts, which returns the locked tokens to the taker instead of the maker [6](#0-5) . Until that time elapses, the resolver's committed destination-chain capital (the swap `amount` plus `safetyDeposit`) is unconditionally locked and unusable, and the maker never receives their swap proceeds.

### Impact Explanation
This matches the bounty's **High** category: "temporary freezing of funds during the live swap lifecycle." An unprivileged maker, by setting `order.receiver`/`order.maker` (or colluding to make the protocol/integrator fee recipient) to a blacklisted USDC/USDT address, can force any resolver who fills that order to have their real capital (destination tokens + safety deposit) locked in `EscrowDst` for the full duration up to `DstCancellation`, with the swap incapable of completing normally. This is triggered purely by an unprivileged maker at the live order-fill entry point — no privileged resolver, governance, or malicious node/bridge assumption is required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: blacklisting requires token-issuer cooperation or a pre-blacklisted address, but a malicious/careless maker can trivially self-select (or be already) a blacklisted address, or (more maliciously) point `order.receiver` at a third-party address they know is blacklisted purely to grief a specific resolver's capital for the swap's lifetime. No special privileges are needed to construct and sign such an order.

### Recommendation
Do not let a single failing transfer in `_withdraw` block the entire withdrawal. Consider: (1) using a non-reverting/try-catch transfer pattern and crediting undeliverable amounts to an internal claimable balance instead of pushing them, or (2) allowing the taker/resolver to redirect an undeliverable `maker` payout to a rescue-style claim path after the normal withdrawal window, so a single bad recipient cannot indefinitely block fund release up to the cancellation timelock. At minimum, document/mitigate the griefing vector for resolvers before they commit capital by validating that `maker`/fee recipients are not known-blacklisted where feasible off-chain.

### Proof of Concept
1. Maker signs a Fusion order with `order.receiver` set to a USDC address already on Circle's blacklist (or one the maker expects to get blacklisted).
2. Resolver fills the order on the source chain (`EscrowSrc` deployed, maker's source tokens locked) and deploys `EscrowDst` via `createDstEscrow`, depositing destination USDC + safety deposit [4](#0-3) .
3. After the secret is revealed on the source chain (resolver withdraws maker's source tokens via `EscrowSrc.withdraw`), the resolver attempts `EscrowDst.withdraw(secret, immutables)`; the internal `IERC20(USDC).safeTransfer(maker, amount)` call reverts because `maker` is blacklisted [7](#0-6) .
4. Any other resolver/access-token holder calling `publicWithdraw` during the public window hits the same revert.
5. The resolver's destination-chain capital and safety deposit remain frozen until `DstCancellation` elapses, at which point they can only recover their own funds via `cancel()` — the maker's swap fails entirely despite having already lost their source-chain tokens to the resolver.
