Instant-withdraw claims are not isolated from matured normal-withdraw funding, letting an unfunded instant receipt drain underlying reserved for other claimants - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns the user's receipt tokens and transfers the vault's *entire* underlying balance basis for that receipt, with no check that the instant-withdraw funds were actually collected for the current epoch via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` and no accounting of the underlying balance as belonging to the instant bucket. `_transferFundedClaim` ring-fences only `defaultRecoveryReserve`, so matured normal withdraw receipts funded through `collectWithdrawFunds` sit in the same unprotected balance. An unprivileged KYC'd lender who holds an instant-withdraw receipt can claim against balance that was funded for normal withdraw requesters, stealing their payout and leaving them permanently undercollateralized.

### Finding Description
In `IdleCreditVault.sol`:

- `collectWithdrawFunds` pulls underlying from the CDO into the strategy to back matured normal receipts (`pendingWithdraws`), but that balance is not attributed to any bucket — it is just `underlyingToken.balanceOf(this)` (IdleCreditVault.sol:411-430).
- `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` similarly pulls underlying to back instant receipts and decrements `pendingInstantWithdraws` (IdleCreditVault.sol:398-403).
- `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` performs no funded check: it burns `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` receipt tokens and calls `_transferFundedClaim(_user, amount)`, which only verifies `balance - defaultRecoveryReserve >= amount` (IdleCreditVault.sol:381-393, 897-907).
- `pendingInstantWithdraws` is never decremented on claim, so the "funded vs unfunded" distinction exists only in `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`; the claim path is blind to it.

The analog to CVE-2017-12134 ("incorrect merge-ability calculation" — two buffers treated as mergeable when they are not) is that two distinct payout buckets — funded matured normal receipts and instant receipts — are treated as interchangeable against a single shared underlying balance, when the protocol's own accounting (`pendingWithdraws`, `pendingInstantWithdraws`, `withdrawsRequestsByEpoch`) treats them as separate obligations.

Attack trace (epoch mode: instant-enabled, e.g., APR-drop epoch):
1. Attacker (KYC'd AA lender) observes `lastEpochApr > unscaledApr + instantWithdrawAprDelta` during the buffer and calls `requestWithdraw`, which routes to `creditVault.requestInstantWithdraw` (IdleCDOEpochVariant.sol:761-768) — the CDO burns principal-side strategy tokens and the vault mints the receipt to the attacker.
2. At the next `stopEpoch`, the borrower funds matured normal withdraws via `collectWithdrawFunds`, leaving underlying in the vault backing victim receipts.
3. Instant funding for the new epoch is only collected at `startEpoch` through `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`. If the attacker's receipt is still unfunded — e.g., they front-run/claim before `startEpoch` funding completes, or the borrower funds partially — `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` still pays out of the vault balance.
4. The attacker receives underlying that belonged to normal-receipt claimants; those claimants later hit the `_transferFundedClaim` guard/balance shortfall and cannot withdraw their funded receipts — direct theft with quantified loss equal to the attacker's receipt amount.

### Impact Explanation
Direct theft of funded withdraw reserves: every underlying token a victim's matured receipt is owed becomes claimable by any instant-receipt holder while `allowInstantWithdraw` is on. Loss is bounded by the attacker's tranche position but requires no privileged role — only a normal deposit and an instant-withdraw-eligible epoch transition.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires an epoch in which instant withdrawals are enabled (APR drop beyond `instantWithdrawAprDelta`) and a claim executed while the vault holds underlying funded for normal receipts but instant funding has not been collected or is underfunded. The funding/claim ordering between `stopEpoch`, `startEpoch`, and `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` is manager/borrower-driven, so the window depends on call sequencing that honest privileged actors perform anyway; no attacker control over privileged calls is needed, only timing.

### Recommendation
Track funded instant and funded normal balances separately in the strategy (e.g., `fundedInstantClaims`, `fundedWithdrawClaims` incremented in `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`/`collectWithdrawFunds` and decremented on claim), and make `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`/`claimWithdrawRequest` transfer only from their own funded bucket rather than the aggregate vault balance. Extend the `_transferFundedClaim` guard to reserve all non-instant obligations, not just `defaultRecoveryReserve`.

### Proof of Concept
A Foundry fork PoC (harness exists in `test/foundry/IdleCreditVault.t.sol`):
1. Deploy `IdleCDOEpochVariant` + `IdleCreditVault` with `allowInstantWithdraw`, non-zero `instantWithdrawAprDelta`, KYC-attacker wallet via `isWalletAllowed`.
2. Victim deposits AA; attacker deposits AA. Run an epoch, `stopEpoch`, then lower APR so `lastEpochApr > unscaledApr + instantWithdrawAprDelta`.
3. Victim calls `requestWithdraw` (normal path, since APR drop makes them instant-eligible too — or use a second epoch where victim has a matured funded receipt); attacker calls `requestWithdraw` → routed to `requestInstantWithdraw`.
4. `stopEpoch` → borrower funds `pendingWithdraws` via `collectWithdrawFunds` (underlying now sits in vault for victim).
5. Before/without instant funding being collected (`pendingInstantWithdraws > 0`), attacker calls `cdoEpoch.claimInstantWithdrawRequest()`.
6. Assert `underlying.balanceOf(attacker)` increased by the receipt amount while vault balance drops below `withdrawsRequests[victim]`; victim's subsequent `claimWithdrawRequest` reverts on insufficient balance (theft confirmed).

Caveat: I was unable to fully trace `startEpoch`/`_afterStopEpochWithDuration` ordering for instant funding within the available search budget, so the exact window size (whether the protocol always collects instant funds atomically at `startEpoch` before any claim is possible) could not be confirmed from the indexed code alone; if `startEpoch` always funds instant receipts atomically and `epochEndDate` gating prevents claims before that, the attack surface narrows to the underfunded/partial-funding case.
