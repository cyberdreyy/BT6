### Title

ERC4626 share-price inflation drains programmable-borrower deposits while CDO NAV remains unimpaired - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol)

### Summary

`ProgrammableBorrower` deposits idle pool assets into an ERC4626 vault without a minimum-share check. An unprivileged vault user can pre-seed the vault and donate assets to inflate the share price, causing the programmable borrower to receive zero or economically negligible shares for the pool's deposit. The attacker then redeems their existing shares and extracts both the donation and the deposited pool principal.

Although `_vaultNetInterest()` observes the resulting vault loss, `totalInterestDueNow()` clamps the net result at zero. `IdleCDOEpochVariant` therefore mints no interest but also does not burn the strategy tokens representing the missing principal, leaving tranche prices and `getContractValue()` at par until a later close or withdrawal forces a default.

### Finding Description

During `startEpoch`, `ProgrammableBorrower.onStartEpoch()` deposits all on-hand underlying into the configured vault through `_depositToVault()`. The call uses `vault.deposit(_assetAmount, address(this))` without any `minSharesOut` protection or validation that the received shares economically represent the deposited assets.

An attacker can execute the standard ERC4626 first-depositor inflation sequence:

1. Deposit a minimal amount into the ERC4626 vault and receive one share.
2. Directly transfer a large amount of underlying to the vault, inflating `convertToAssets(1 share)`.
3. Let the honest manager call `IdleCDOEpochVariant.startEpoch()`.
4. `ProgrammableBorrower` deposits the CDO's pool balance and receives zero shares if the donation is at least the deposited amount.
5. The attacker redeems their one share and receives the donation plus the pool's deposit.

The accounting then records the missing deposit as a vault loss:

```solidity
uint256 earnedAssets = _currentVaultAssets() + epochWithdrawnFromVault;
uint256 principalAssets = epochStartVaultAssets + epochDepositedToVault;
int256 netDelta = bufferedVaultDelta + int256(earnedAssets) - int256(principalAssets);
```

from `contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol:337-346`.

However, `totalInterestDueNow()` returns only the positive remainder:

```solidity
uint256 totalGain = vaultInterest + borrowerInterestAccruedNow() + bufferInterest;
return totalGain > loss ? totalGain - loss : 0;
```

from `contracts/strategies/idle/ProgrammableBorrower.sol:330-334`.

At stop, `IdleCDOEpochVariant` uses that value as the interest amount and, in minted-interest mode, mints only `_grossInterest` strategy tokens. A `vaultLoss` larger than the epoch gain produces zero minted interest but leaves the principal-denominated strategy-token balance unchanged. The CDO therefore remains nominally solvent even though the programmable borrower no longer holds the corresponding assets.

### Impact Explanation

This is direct theft of pool principal by an unprivileged ERC4626 vault user.

For example, if the programmable borrower is about to deposit `1,000,000` underlying:

- attacker deposits `1` wei and receives one vault share;
- attacker donates `1,000,000` underlying directly to the vault;
- the programmable borrower deposits `1,000,000` and receives zero shares;
- attacker redeems their one share for approximately `2,000,001` underlying.

The attacker spends `1,000,001` and receives `2,000,001`, stealing the CDO deposit. Meanwhile, the CDO's strategy-token NAV continues to include the `1,000,000` principal because the vault loss only zeroes reported epoch interest and is never applied to principal during a normal `stopEpoch`.

The missing principal is eventually revealed when withdrawals or a close-pool stop require the programmable borrower to transfer funds it no longer has. That produces borrower-default handling and permanent LP loss through default recovery rather than immediate loss accounting.

### Likelihood Explanation

The attack requires the configured ERC4626 vault to be susceptible to share inflation when `ProgrammableBorrower` deposits. This is most likely when the vault is newly deployed or has a very small existing share supply relative to the programmable-borrower deposit.

No privileged role needs to misbehave. The attacker only needs to be an ordinary user of the external vault and to act before the vault position is established. Existing CDO donation skimming does not mitigate this because the donation is sent to the ERC4626 vault, not to `IdleCDOEpochVariant`. The `_onlyIdleCDO()` guards also do not help because the malicious manipulation occurs through the external vault's normal deposit and donation semantics.

### Recommendation

Apply minimum-share/slippage protection to every ERC4626 deposit, including the startup deposit in `onStartEpoch()` and redeposits in `_repay()`. `ProgrammableBorrower` should calculate the expected shares or require a `minSharesOut` parameter derived from the deposited assets and the vault's pre-donation exchange rate.

The vault should additionally be protected against first-depositor inflation using a recognized mitigation such as virtual shares/assets or an initial liquidity burn.

Accounting should also explicitly realize vault principal losses rather than clamping `totalInterestDueNow()` at zero. If `vaultLoss` exceeds current gains, the excess must be propagated to the CDO so strategy tokens or tranche NAV are reduced instead of remaining marked at par until a later liquidity failure.

### Proof of Concept

A Foundry PoC can use a standard ERC4626 implementation without inflation protection:

```solidity
function testVaultInflationDrainsEpochDeposit() external {
    uint256 depositAmount = 1_000_000e6;

    // Attacker becomes the first vault depositor.
    underlying.mint(attacker, depositAmount + 1);
    vm.startPrank(attacker);
    underlying.approve(address(vault), 1);
    vault.deposit(1, attacker);

    // Inflate the price of the attacker's single share.
    underlying.transfer(address(vault), depositAmount);
    vm.stopPrank();

    // Honest manager starts the epoch. ProgrammableBorrower deposits pool cash
    // through onStartEpoch -> _depositToVault().
    vm.prank(manager);
    cdo.startEpoch();

    // Attacker redeems the seeded share for donation + CDO deposit.
    vm.prank(attacker);
    vault.redeem(1, attacker, attacker);

    assertEq(vault.balanceOf(address(programmableBorrower)), 0);
    assertGt(underlying.balanceOf(attacker), depositAmount + 1);
    assertEq(programmableBorrower.totalInterestDueNow(), 0);

    // CDO principal remains represented by strategy tokens even though the
    // programmable borrower no longer owns the corresponding assets.
    assertEq(strategy.balanceOf(address(cdo)), depositAmount);
    assertEq(cdo.getContractValue(), depositAmount);
}
```

The final assertions demonstrate the broken solvency invariant: the programmable-borrower position was drained, while CDO NAV and tranche claims still report the stolen principal as fully backed.
