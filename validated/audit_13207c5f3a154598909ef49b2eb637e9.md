### Title
Byzantine current-tenure miner can indefinitely reset its own inactivity clock, wedging signers from ever falling back to the prior miner - (File: `stacks-signer/src/chainstate/mod.rs`)

### Summary
`SortitionState::is_timed_out` decides whether a tenure's miner has gone inactive by comparing `get_last_activity_time` (or, absent that, the burn-block receive time) against `block_proposal_timeout` [1](#0-0) . That activity timestamp is not tied to any signed/valid block: `check_latest_block_in_tenure` bumps `signer_db.update_last_activity_time(...)` any time the current miner sends a proposal that merely fails the "confirms enough blocks" check but is still within a leniency window, explicitly to "treat any attempt to reorg a locally accepted block as valid miner activity" or to give the miner "some extra buffer time" [2](#0-1) . This mirrors the `LendingAssetVault` bug class: a party (here, the same current miner who is supposed to be judged for inactivity) can unilaterally re-arm a shared timer that the protocol later checks equality/expiry against for someone else's benefit (the signer's ability to time out and fall back to the prior miner).

### Finding Description
`check_miner_inactivity` relies purely on `SortitionState::is_timed_out`, which is gated on `last_activity_time` for the *current* miner's tenure [3](#0-2) . If not timed out, the signer keeps treating the current miner as active and never reverts to the prior tenure's miner [4](#0-3) .

`update_last_activity_time` is invoked from `check_latest_block_in_tenure`, called for every incoming block proposal via `SortitionsView::check_proposal` / `confirms_latest_block_in_same_tenure` and `check_tenure_change_confirms_parent` [5](#0-4) . Two branches independently reset the clock on a *rejected* (non-higher / conflicting) proposal — one for a proposal at or below the last signed/globally-accepted block [6](#0-5) , and one for a proposal conflicting with a merely pre-committed (unsigned) block [7](#0-6) . Both are reachable with proposals the current, sole-slot miner can generate and gossip to StackerDB at will, without any signer signature and without needing agreement from any other party.

Because the very same current miner who is being evaluated for inactivity is the one whose proposal resets the activity clock, a stalled/malicious current miner can keep emitting a stream of stale/conflicting (but structurally valid) proposals — e.g., ones repeating the same or a lower chain length than the last signed block — purely to keep bumping `last_activity_time` forever, exactly as the frontrunning attacker in the report re-stamps `_lastDeposit[victim]` to defeat the freshness check the victim depends on.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the liveness guarantee that `check_miner_inactivity` / `is_timed_out` is supposed to provide: signers are meant to detect an unresponsive current miner and fail over to the previous tenure's miner so tenure production can continue [8](#0-7) . If the "current miner" is byzantine and keeps resetting its own activity timer with cheap, unsigned, invalid-height proposals, no signer will ever consider it timed out, and the signer set can never fall back — a signer is effectively wedged into perpetually waiting on a miner that will never produce a valid, signable block. This matches the specified High-impact class: "a signer wedged into never signing valid blocks... or acting on a stale reward set/threshold" (here: acting on a stale/never-expiring "miner is active" belief).

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium/High. It requires only the tenure's own single sortition-winning miner (a one-slot actor) to gossip proposals over StackerDB — no majority of signers, no other signer's key, no auth token, and no consensus assumptions are needed. The reset paths are explicitly reachable from ordinary conflicting/rejected proposals that the miner already has the material to construct (it already won the sortition and knows the tenure's consensus hash and chain state).

### Recommendation
Do not let the entity being monitored for inactivity (the current miner) refresh its own activity timer via proposals that are themselves rejected/invalid/conflicting. Base `last_activity_time` only on independently-verifiable, cost-bearing signals — e.g., only bump activity when a proposal is deemed valid enough to submit for node validation, or when the *canonical tip* (as reported by the node/tracked by the signer’s own state, not just any proposal) actually advances — rather than on the mere receipt of any proposal from the miner being evaluated. Consider decoupling the "extra buffer" leniency (`reorg_attempts_activity_timeout`) from a mechanism the same actor being timed out can trigger repeatedly.

### Proof of Concept
1. A current-tenure miner wins its sortition slot and mines/announces a first block, which signers sign (`last_signed_block` established).
2. The miner then stops making real progress but periodically gossips additional block proposals to StackerDB that either repeat/undercut the already-signed chain length, or conflict with a block signers have only pre-committed to (never fully signed).
3. Each such proposal is routed through `check_proposal` → `confirms_latest_block_in_same_tenure`/`check_tenure_change_confirms_parent` → `check_latest_block_in_tenure`, which detects the proposal doesn't confirm enough blocks but is still within `reorg_attempts_activity_timeout`/pre-commit freshness, and calls `update_last_activity_time` on the *current* tenure's consensus hash [9](#0-8) [10](#0-9) .
4. As long as the miner repeats step 2/3 more often than `block_proposal_timeout`, `SortitionState::is_timed_out` never returns `true` for this tenure [11](#0-10) , so `check_miner_inactivity` never reverts the active miner to the prior tenure [4](#0-3) , indefinitely wedging tenure progress on signers waiting for this miner.
