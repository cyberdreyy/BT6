Based on my research, I found a strong structural analog to the "unchecked transfer result" bug class in the Gloas fork's builder-withdrawal machinery. The root cause is that the beacon state's own accounting of a builder withdrawal amount can diverge from what is actually deducted from the builder's balance, while the diverged (larger) amount is still the one committed to and executed by the execution layer.

### Title
Uncapped builder withdrawal amount committed to the execution layer while the CL-side balance deduction is silently capped, allowing Gwei to be minted without backing - (File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md)

### Summary
`apply_withdrawals` in Gloas caps the actual `Builder.balance` deduction with `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` to avoid underflow, but the `Withdrawal.amount` field used to build `state.payload_expected_withdrawals` — which the execution layer is contractually required to execute verbatim — is never capped or corrected to match what was actually deducted. If a builder's balance is ever lower than a previously-committed pending withdrawal amount by the time that withdrawal is dequeued, the CL "silently succeeds" with a partial (or zero) internal deduction while still committing the full, uncapped amount for EL execution, functionally creating Gwei out of nothing.

Here `builder_balance` is read fresh and the deduction from `state.builders[builder_index].balance` is capped by `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` — this is the CL-side ledger update, and it never underflows or errors even if `withdrawal.amount` exceeds the available balance. However, the same `withdrawal` object (with its original, uncapped `amount`) is what gets placed into `state.payload_expected_withdrawals`: [2](#0-1) 

And this exact, uncapped list is precisely what the execution layer is required to execute — verified byte-for-byte against the payload: [3](#0-2) 

The committed amount for builder-pending withdrawals is fixed at bid-acceptance or payment-settlement time and is not re-checked against the builder's balance at the time the withdrawal is actually dequeued and executed: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

Note in particular the fallback branch at lines 1761-1770: if a bid's payment entry has already been evicted from `builder_pending_payments` (because settlement was delayed by 2+ epochs of missed blocks), the code appends a `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` directly from the *original, stale* `parent_bid.value`, with **no re-verification** that the builder still has sufficient balance to cover it. This is structurally identical to the reported bug: a fixed "transfer amount" is queued/committed once, and the flow proceeds to honor that commitment later without checking whether the underlying balance can still support it.

The only capping/verification that occurs is on the internal CL ledger (`state.builders[...].balance -= min(...)`), which is invisible to the EL. The EL performs the withdrawal using the full, asserted `amount` from `payload.withdrawals`, regardless of whether the CL had enough balance to back it. Because `decrease_balance` is not used for builders (which has the same saturate-at-zero behavior but that's exactly the problem — saturation on one side, no saturation on the committed payload on the other side), any gap between "committed withdrawal amount" and "actually available Builder.balance" becomes free Gwei minted at the destination address on the execution layer, with no corresponding deduction anywhere in consensus-layer accounting.

This exactly mirrors the reported bug class: a value-transfer instruction (`transferUsdc` in the report; `Withdrawal.amount` here) is dispatched/committed to without verifying that the underlying operation can actually be satisfied, and downstream accounting (fee_recipient credit in the report; `payload_expected_withdrawals` → EL execution here) proceeds unconditionally on the assumption that the transfer succeeded fully.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the fundamental total-supply invariant of the protocol: Gwei can be created that was never backed by any validator or builder balance, and paid to an address the block committed to (the `fee_recipient`/`execution_address` of the affected withdrawal) but did not actually own that balance. This falls squarely under the Critical impact bucket: "Gwei created, destroyed or paid to a non-owner." It can also manifest as a builder payment being effectively "doubled" (e.g., a builder's balance already swept in full via `get_builders_sweep_withdrawals`, and a stale, previously-queued `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` for the same builder later dequeued and paid out again at its original, uncapped, committed amount) — matching the High-impact category "a builder payment or withdrawal misdirected, doubled, or escaped."

### Likelihood Explanation
The path requires the builder's `Builder.balance` to drop below a previously committed withdrawal amount before that specific withdrawal is dequeued from `builder_pending_withdrawals`/settled via `settle_builder_payment`. Plausible triggers include: a builder's full-balance sweep withdrawal (`get_builders_sweep_withdrawals`, upon `builder.withdrawable_epoch <= current_epoch`) occurring before a stale, already-queued pending payment/withdrawal for the same builder is processed, or the "parent is older than previous epoch" fallback branch (lines 1761-1770) re-queuing a stale bid value with no fresh balance check. I was not able to fully trace the exact code of `get_builders_sweep_withdrawals`, `can_builder_cover_bid`, and `get_pending_balance_to_withdraw_for_builder` within the available tool budget to conclusively prove that no other invariant elsewhere in the spec prevents this exact race (e.g., a global constraint that always reserves builder balance against all outstanding pending payments/withdrawals). This should be verified by inspecting those three functions directly and by simulating the ordering of `apply_withdrawals` relative to sweep eviction across multiple blocks/epochs, ideally with a Devin session that has direct code execution to construct and run the scenario against the reference implementation.

### Recommendation
`apply_withdrawals` should not silently cap `state.builders[builder_index].balance` deduction while leaving the committed `Withdrawal.amount` uncapped for EL execution. Either: (1) recompute/cap `Withdrawal.amount` to `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` at the point the withdrawal is constructed and included in `payload_expected_withdrawals` (so CL commitment and CL deduction always match), or (2) enforce an invariant (checked at bid-acceptance and at every event that can reduce `Builder.balance`, including builder sweep eligibility and `initiate_builder_exit`) that a builder's balance can never be spent below the sum of all currently outstanding `builder_pending_payments` + `builder_pending_withdrawals` amounts owed by that builder.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual state trace (exact numeric reachability not fully verified against `can_builder_cover_bid`/sweep-eligibility helpers, which I could not inspect in full):
1. Builder `B` bids `amount = V` in block `N`; `can_builder_cover_bid` passes since `B.balance >= V + reserve` at that time. Payment queued in `builder_pending_payments`.
2. Blocks are missed for 2+ epochs (as already demonstrated feasible by the existing test `test_builder_payment_after_missed_epochs` at [6](#0-5) ), causing the pending-payment entry to be evicted and the fallback branch in `apply_parent_execution_payload` to append a `BuilderPendingWithdrawal(amount=V, fee_recipient=...)` directly, using the **stale, original bid value `V`**, with no re-check of `B`'s current balance.
3. Before this stale entry is dequeued, `B.withdrawable_epoch` elapses and `get_builders_sweep_withdrawals` produces a full-balance withdrawal draining `B.balance` to `B.execution_address`.
4. When the stale `BuilderPendingWithdrawal(amount=V)` is finally processed by `apply_withdrawals`, `builder_balance` reads `0`; the CL deducts `min(V, 0) = 0` (no state change), yet the `Withdrawal(amount=V)` object is still placed in `state.payload_expected_withdrawals` and, per `verify_execution_payload_envelope`, must be executed identically by the EL — crediting `fee_recipient` with `V` Gwei that was never actually present in or deducted from any consensus-layer balance.
