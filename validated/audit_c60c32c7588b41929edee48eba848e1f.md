This is a valid vulnerability. `rescueFunds()` in `BaseEscrow.sol` performs a completely generic transfer with no check that `token`/`amount` correspond to genuinely "stuck" assets rather than the escrow's own locked principal.

## Analysis

`rescueFunds` only enforces three things: caller is `immutables.taker`, the immutables hash resolves to this clone, and `block.timestamp >= timelocks.rescueStart(RESCUE_DELAY)`. It performs `_uniTransfer(token, msg.sender, amount)` with attacker-chosen `token`/`amount` — nothing restricts these to be assets other than `immutables.token`/`immutables.amount`. [1](#0-0) 

On `EscrowSrc`, the maker's principal is transferred back to the maker only through `_cancel()`, reachable via `cancel()` (taker-only, after `SrcCancellation`) or `publicCancel()` (anyone holding the access token, after `SrcPublicCancellation`) — both have no upper time bound, so they theoretically remain callable forever. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

However, `cancel`/`publicCancel` are optional actions that depend on someone actually calling them. The taker is precisely the party who is supposed to call `cancel()` and return the maker's principal, or let a public actor call `publicCancel()`. Because `rescueFunds` has no notion of "only rescue balance beyond the locked principal," a taker who simply refrains from calling `withdraw`/`cancel` (and no third-party access-token holder calls `publicCancel`) can wait until `RESCUE_DELAY` elapses and call `rescueFunds(immutables.token, immutables.amount, immutables)` themselves — the same `onlyCaller(taker)` gate that governs the legitimate `cancel()` also permits `rescueFunds()`, and there is nothing distinguishing "the maker's live principal" from "stray tokens" in the check.

The documentation states the intent is only to recover accidentally-stuck assets, but this intent is not enforced in code: [4](#0-3) 

Unit tests confirm the exact mechanics: after `RESCUE_DELAY`, `rescueFunds` transfers whatever `token`/`amount` is requested straight to the taker with no cross-check against the escrow's own committed balances: [5](#0-4) 

## Verdict

### Title
Unrestricted `rescueFunds` lets a rogue taker seize the maker's locked source principal instead of returning it via `cancel` - (File: contracts/BaseEscrow.sol)

### Summary
`BaseEscrow.rescueFunds` does not verify that the `token`/`amount` being rescued are surplus/stray assets distinct from the escrow's own committed principal (`immutables.token`/`immutables.amount`). Since only the `taker` (`onlyCaller(immutables.taker.get())`) can call it, and the taker is also the sole/primary party expected to voluntarily call `cancel()` to return the maker's funds, a taker can simply never call `withdraw`/`cancel`, wait for `RESCUE_DELAY` to elapse, and call `rescueFunds(immutables.token, immutables.amount, immutables)` to redirect the maker's original principal to themselves.

### Finding Description
`rescueFunds` is generic and caller-parameterized: [1](#0-0) 

There is no check comparing `amount`/`token` against the escrow's committed `immutables.amount`/`immutables.token`, and no check on the current escrow balance beyond what's requested. The only path returning the maker's principal is `EscrowSrc._cancel`, invoked by `cancel()` (taker-only) or `publicCancel()` (any access-token holder), both of which are optional, voluntary calls with no forced deadline before `RESCUE_DELAY`: [2](#0-1) 

A taker who withholds calling `cancel`/lets no watcher call `publicCancel` retains full unilateral control: once `RESCUE_DELAY` passes, they can extract the maker's principal via `rescueFunds` instead of returning it, converting what should be a maker refund into taker profit.

### Impact Explanation
This is a direct theft of the maker's principal token, matching the Critical bounty impact "direct theft of user funds, whether at-rest or in-motion." The taker is an unprivileged actor who only needed to fill the order (an ordinary, permissionless action) to gain the ability to call `rescueFunds`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high once `RESCUE_DELAY` elapses without a completed swap: the taker fully controls whether `cancel()` is called (nothing forces it), and `publicCancel` depends on a third watcher acting first, which is not guaranteed. A rational, dishonest taker who lost interest in completing the swap (e.g., unfavorable secret timing or price movement) has direct financial incentive to call `rescueFunds` instead of `cancel`, since it lets them keep the maker's principal in addition to their own safety deposit refund path.

### Recommendation
Restrict `rescueFunds` so it cannot draw down the escrow's own committed balances: require `token != immutables.token || amount <= (currentBalance - immutables.amount - immutables.safetyDeposit)` (adjusted per escrow side), or explicitly disallow rescuing `immutables.token` up to `immutables.amount` while the corresponding cancel/withdraw obligation is still outstanding. Alternatively, only allow rescue on tokens/amounts that are not part of the current active immutables, and/or gate `rescueFunds` to require the escrow's core obligation already discharged (e.g., a `resolved` flag set by prior `withdraw`/`cancel`).

### Proof of Concept
1. Maker signs order for partial fill (~25% band); Resolver/taker fills it via Limit Order Protocol, `EscrowFactory.postInteraction` deploys `EscrowSrc` funded with the maker's principal, per `test/unit/EscrowCancel.t.sol` fixtures such as `_prepareDataSrc`.
2. Taker deliberately never calls `withdraw` with the secret, and never calls `cancel()`; assume no other access-token holder calls `publicCancel()` before `RESCUE_DELAY`.
3. Warp past `srcTimelocks.publicCancellation` and further to `RESCUE_DELAY` (as in `test_RescueFundsSrc`/`test_RescueFundsSrcNative` patterns): [5](#0-4) 
4. Taker calls `srcClone.rescueFunds(immutables.token, immutables.amount, immutables)`.
5. Result: the maker's original principal (`MAKING_AMOUNT`) is transferred to the taker instead of being refunded via `cancel`, verified by the pre/post balance deltas.
