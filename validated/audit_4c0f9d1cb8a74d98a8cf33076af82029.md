## Title
Uncapped Builder Withdrawal Amount is Committed to the Execution Layer While the CL Only Debits the Available Balance - (File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md)

### Summary
`get_builder_withdrawals` copies `withdrawal.amount` from `state.builder_pending_withdrawals` verbatim into the `Withdrawal` object that is committed to the execution layer via `payload_expected_withdrawals`, with no cap against the builder's actual `balance`. The consensus-layer accounting function `apply_withdrawals`, however, only ever deducts `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` from `state.builders[builder_index].balance`. If a builder's balance is smaller than the sum of amounts already queued for it, the amount the block *commits* the EL to pay exceeds the amount actually debited from the builder's stake on the CL — the same "cap only truncates the internal bookkeeping while the externally observable amount is not truncated" pattern as the reported `RewardsClaimer` bug (there `maxClaimPerWallet` capped `userPendingRewards` inconsistently with what was actually paid/lost; here the discrepancy runs the other way, producing unbacked payment rather than lost reward).

### Finding Description
Compare the two code paths: [1](#0-0) 

`get_builder_withdrawals` builds each `Withdrawal` with `amount=withdrawal.amount` — the raw requested amount from `state.builder_pending_withdrawals`, never compared to `state.builders[builder_index].balance`. [2](#0-1) 

`apply_withdrawals` only reduces the builder's own balance by `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)`.

These two amounts are supposed to be equal (the withdrawal committed on-chain should equal what is actually available to withdraw), but nothing enforces that. The spec's own test suite documents this divergence directly: [3](#0-2) 

The test explicitly sets `builders[0].balance = 1 ETH` and a pending withdrawal of `5 ETH`, then asserts the committed `payload_expected_withdrawals` entry has `amount == 5 ETH` (the full requested amount) while `builders[0].balance` is merely zeroed out. Per the spec's `process_withdrawals` note, this committed withdrawal set is binding on the execution layer: *"any execution payload that has the corresponding block as parent beacon block is required to honor these withdrawals in the execution layer... the EL mints the full committed amount regardless"* [4](#0-3)  — a comment that is written specifically to justify *not* saturating validator withdrawals at zero (to avoid inflation), but the corollary is that any place where the committed amount is genuinely larger than the true backing balance (as with an under-funded builder) causes the EL to mint Gwei that was never actually staked/debited from anyone. The 4 ETH difference in the example is paid to `fee_recipient` without ever being subtracted from any account in `state` — new, unbacked Gwei is created and paid out, breaking the invariant that no execution-layer payment can exceed the Gwei actually held/committed by the paying party.

### Impact Explanation
This matches the Critical bucket "Gwei created ... or paid to a non-owner" / "a payload executed or paid that the block did not commit to" (here inverted: paid *more* than the entity behind the payment ever had). Builder balances are meant to back their bids/payments 1:1; if the committed withdrawal amount can exceed the builder's real balance, the chain instructs the EL to conjure ETH that is not backed by any deduction elsewhere in `state`, silently inflating total supply and breaking CL/EL balance parity — the same class of harm called out in the spec's own comment about "net supply inflation" for the validator-sweep case, but reachable here through builder withdrawals without any of the safeguards discussed for validators.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachability depends on whether `state.builder_pending_withdrawals` entries can ever exceed the builder's balance by the time they are dequeued. `process_execution_payload_bid` checks sufficiency (`get_pending_balance_to_withdraw_for_builder`) at the moment a *new* pending payment is queued, comparing against the balance *at that time*; I could not fully trace, within the remaining budget, whether this check accounts for balance already reserved by other in-flight `builder_pending_payments`/`builder_pending_withdrawals` for the same builder across multiple blocks/epochs (e.g., several bids maturing into payments before any of them are actually withdrawn, or reordering/queue-rotation edge cases such as `test_builder_payment_after_missed_epochs`). Because the invariant is enforced only at bid-acceptance time against a balance snapshot, and withdrawals are asynchronous and queued over many slots, it is plausible for cumulative pending amounts to exceed the balance by the time `apply_withdrawals` runs — but I was not able to conclusively prove or rule out this path with the available context. This should be treated as an unverified-but-plausible finding requiring deeper trace of `process_builder_pending_payments` and `process_execution_payload_bid`'s exact balance check.

### Recommendation
Cap the committed `Withdrawal.amount` in `get_builder_withdrawals` (and in the builder-sweep path, for consistency) to `min(withdrawal.amount, state.builders[builder_index].balance)`, matching what `apply_withdrawals` actually debits, so the amount committed to the EL never exceeds what is truly backed by the builder's balance. Additionally, verify/strengthen the balance-sufficiency check performed when a `BuilderPendingPayment`/`BuilderPendingWithdrawal` is enqueued so that it accounts for *all* other currently-pending payments/withdrawals for that builder, not just the balance snapshot at accept time.

### Proof of Concept
1. Register builder `B` with `balance = 1 ETH`.
2. Through the normal bid/payment quorum flow (`process_execution_payload_bid` → `process_builder_pending_payments`), get two payments queued against `B` that individually appear affordable versus the balance check at acceptance time, but which sum to `5 ETH` once both are converted into `state.builder_pending_withdrawals`.
3. At the epoch/slot when `process_withdrawals` runs, `get_builder_withdrawals` builds a `Withdrawal` (or set of withdrawals) totaling `5 ETH` for `B`, exactly as demonstrated by the spec test `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance` [3](#0-2) .
4. `apply_withdrawals` reduces `B.balance` from `1 ETH` to `0`, but the committed `payload_expected_withdrawals` entry — which the EL must honor — pays out the full `5 ETH` to `fee_recipient`, 4 ETH of which was never backed by any deduction in `state`.

def get_builder_withdrawals(
    state: BeaconState,
    withdrawal_index: WithdrawalIndex,
    prior_withdrawals: Sequence[Withdrawal],
) -> Tuple[Sequence[Withdrawal], WithdrawalIndex, Uint64]:
    withdrawals_limit = MAX_WITHDRAWALS_PER_PAYLOAD - 1
    assert len(prior_withdrawals) <= withdrawals_limit

    processed_count = Uint64(0)
    withdrawals: list[Withdrawal] = []
    for withdrawal in state.builder_pending_withdrawals:
        all_withdrawals = list(prior_withdrawals) + withdrawals
        has_reached_limit = len(all_withdrawals) >= withdrawals_limit
        if has_reached_limit:
            break

        builder_index = withdrawal.builder_index
        withdrawals.append(
            Withdrawal(
                index=withdrawal_index,
                validator_index=convert_builder_index_to_validator_index(builder_index),
                address=withdrawal.fee_recipient,
                amount=withdrawal.amount,
            )
        )
        withdrawal_index += 1
        processed_count += 1

    return withdrawals, withdrawal_index, processed_count

*Note*: This is modified to only take the `state` as parameter. Withdrawals are
deterministic given the beacon state, any execution payload that has the
corresponding block as parent beacon block is required to honor these
withdrawals in the execution layer. `process_withdrawals` must be called after
`process_parent_execution_payload` (which updates `state.latest_block_hash`) and
before `process_execution_payload_bid` as the latter function affects validator
balances.

*Note*: Unlike deposits (which are applied at the child's slot via
`apply_parent_execution_payload`), withdrawal balance deductions are applied
immediately via `apply_withdrawals`. Deferring the deduction to the child's slot
would break the total supply invariant: state transitions between the commitment
slot and the deduction slot (e.g., `process_pending_consolidations` at an epoch
boundary) can reduce a validator's balance below the committed withdrawal
amount, causing `decrease_balance` to saturate at zero. Since the execution
layer mints the full committed amount regardless, any CL-side saturation creates
a net supply inflation. As a consequence, `state.balances` reflects the
withdrawal deduction before the corresponding execution payload is confirmed,
creating a transient asymmetry with the EL state at `state.latest_block_hash`.
Off-chain consumers that require CL/EL balance consistency can reconstruct
pre-deduction balances by adding back `state.payload_expected_withdrawals`.

def test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance(spec, state):
    """
    Test builder withdrawal with insufficient balance.

    Input State Configured:
        - state.builders[0]: Builder exists with only 1 ETH balance
        - builder_pending_withdrawals: Contains 1 entry requesting 5 ETH
        - builders[0].balance: 1 ETH (insufficient for requested 5 ETH)

    Output State Verified:
        - payload_expected_withdrawals: Contains 1 withdrawal
        - withdrawal.amount: 5 ETH (requested amount)
        - builders[0].balance: 0 (deduction capped to available balance)
        - builder_pending_withdrawals: Reduced by 1 (processed even if capped)
        - next_withdrawal_index: Incremented by 1
    """
    builder_index = 0
    withdrawal_amount = spec.Gwei(5_000_000_000)
    available_balance = spec.Gwei(1_000_000_000)

    prepare_process_withdrawals(
        spec,
        state,
        builder_indices=[builder_index],
        builder_withdrawal_amounts={builder_index: withdrawal_amount},
        builder_balances={builder_index: available_balance},
    )

    pre_state = state.copy()
    yield from run_gloas_withdrawals_processing(spec, state)

    assert_process_withdrawals(
        spec,
        state,
        pre_state,
        withdrawal_count=1,
        builder_balances={builder_index: 0},
        builder_pending_delta=-1,
        withdrawal_index_delta=1,
        withdrawal_amounts_builders={builder_index: withdrawal_amount},
    )
