### Title
Pre-default unfunded instant-withdraw receipts escape the recovery haircut and are permanently frozen or overpay other claimants - (File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol)

### Summary
The TCP bug accepts an in-window but non-exact RST in SYN-RECEIVED instead of challenge-ACKing it — a request that falls inside an acceptable *range* is processed even though it does not *exactly* match the expected sequence number. The analog in `IdleCreditVault` is epoch-scoped claim matching: default finalization and `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` match only receipts recorded in `defaultRecoveryEpoch` (`epochNumber` at finalization), while `pendingInstantWithdraws` is an aggregate across *all* epochs. Instant receipts from earlier epochs that were never funded are inside the aggregate "window" but fail the exact-epoch match, so they are silently dropped from recovery accounting and then routed to a par payout path that can never succeed.

### Finding Description
`requestInstantWithdraw` records receipts per request epoch in `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[user][currentEpoch]` / `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[currentEpoch]` and in the aggregates `instantWithdrawsRequests[user]` and `pendingInstantWithdraws`. When the borrower underfunds the instant queue at `startEpoch`, `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` only reduces `pendingInstantWithdraws` by what was actually collected; the unfunded remainder persists indefinitely, but the receipt stays keyed to its original request epoch.

On default finalization, `defaultPendingClaimBasis()` adds `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` — only the current epoch's instant basis — whenever `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0`. `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve()` uses the same current-epoch-only key. Both ignore older-epoch receipts even though the `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0` condition may be true *solely because of those older receipts*. `finalizeDefaultRecovery` then computes `defaultRecoveryPrice` over a basis that excludes the stale instant claims, and sets `defaultInstantWithdrawsFinalized = true`.

After finalization, `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` calls `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest`, which clears only `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[user][defaultRecoveryEpoch]` (the exact-match check). An older-epoch unfunded receipt falls through to `instantWithdrawsRequests[user]` being burned and paid at par via `_transferFundedClaim`, which reverts unless `balance - defaultRecoveryReserve >= amount`. Since every underlying the strategy holds after finalization is counted inside `defaultRecoveryReserve` (prefunded reserve + recovered amount), the transfer reverts.

### Impact Explanation
Two harms, both accepted impact classes:

1. **Permanent freezing of user funds**: a lender whose instant receipt was requested in epoch N, partially funded at `startEpoch` (N+1), and still pending when the borrower defaults in a later epoch can never claim. Their claim always reverts in `_transferFundedClaim` (line 904), and no other path pays it, because their basis was never added to the recovery reserve. Loss = the entire unfunded receipt amount.
2. **Theft / mispriced recovery for other claimants**: because the stale basis is excluded from `defaultPendingClaimBasis`, `defaultRecoveryPrice` is computed over a smaller denominator than the true claim set. Every claimant who *does* match `defaultRecoveryEpoch` (and every post-default requester drawing from `defaultRecoveryReserve`) is paid against a recovery pool that was priced as if the frozen receipts did not exist — the reserve is divided more generously than fair pro-rata, enriching matching claimants (which can be the attacker) at the expense of the excluded users.

The guards do not prevent this: `_transferFundedClaim`'s reserve check (line 900-904) converts the overpay into a revert for the victim rather than routing them through the haircut path, and there is no catch-all mapping of aggregate pending instant basis into per-epoch recovery.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires (a) instant withdraws enabled and at least one epoch where the borrower underfunds the instant queue at `startEpoch` (the code explicitly anticipates this: "borrower-send funds that failed at epoch start"), and (b) a borrower default finalized while that remainder is still pending and at least one new current-epoch instant receipt exists or `pendingInstantWithdraws != 0`. Both are sequences around honest manager/borrower calls with unprivileged lenders as victims; an attacker only needs to hold a defaulted-epoch claim to benefit from the inflated `defaultRecoveryPrice`.

### Recommendation
- In `defaultPendingClaimBasis` / `_defaultPrefundedInstantReserve`, account for the full unfunded instant remainder, not only `instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[epochNumber]` — e.g., include `pendingInstantWithdraws` plus the already-prefunded portion regardless of request epoch.
- In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, route any `instantWithdrawsRequests[user]` remainder that was never funded through `defaultRecoveryPrice` instead of the par `_transferFundedClaim` path, or per-epoch iterate over all outstanding receipt epochs rather than only `defaultRecoveryEpoch`.
- The rule mirrors the kernel fix: process an exact match at par/recovery, but non-exact (older-epoch) receipts must get an explicit "challenge" path rather than being silently accepted into the funded-claim path.

### Proof of Concept
A Foundry PoC (fork of the existing `IdleCreditVault.t.sol` harness) would:

1. Deposit, `startEpoch`, `stopEpoch` so `epochNumber = 1`; enable `allowInstantWithdraw` with an APR drop.
2. `requestInstantWithdraw(X, victim)` in epoch 1 → `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch[victim][1] = X`.
3. `startEpoch` (epoch 2): borrower underfunds instant queue — `collectInstantWithdrawFunds(X - y)` leaves `pendingInstantWithdraws = y > 0`.
4. New epoch 2 instant request by attacker (`instantWithdrawClaimsByEpoch[2] = Z`).
5. Borrower defaults; `finalizeDefaultRecovery` → `defaultRecoveryEpoch = 2`, basis excludes `X`/`y`, `defaultRecoveryPrice` inflated.
6. `victim.claimInstantWithdrawRequest()` → `_claimDefaultedInstantWithdrawRequest` no-ops (epoch 1 ≠ 2), then `_transferFundedClaim` reverts `NotAllowed` → victim permanently frozen; attacker's epoch-2 claim pays against the inflated price.

Confidence is moderate on revert-vs-overpay branching (it depends on residual non-reserve balance in the strategy), but the epoch-mismatch exclusion itself follows directly from the code paths cited.
