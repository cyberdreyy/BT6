### Title
Unfunded instant-withdraw receipts are claimable at par, letting a requester drain other users' funded claim liquidity - ([File: contracts/strategies/idle/IdleCreditVault.sol])

### Summary

Directory traversal is a scope-escape bug: a request resolves a resource outside the directory it was confined to. The vault analog is a withdrawal receipt being paid outside the funding scope it was confined to. `requestInstantWithdraw` mints a receipt and records `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` / `pendingInstantWithdraws`, but `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` burns the receipt and pays the full recorded amount from the strategy's underlying balance without ever checking that the request was actually funded via `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`. The only isolation guard, `_transferFundedClaim`, protects `defaultRecoveryReserve` — not the funded-claims pool against unfunded instant receipts.

### Finding Description

- `requestInstantWithdraw` burns the CDO's strategy tokens, mints a 1:1 receipt to the user, and increments `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]`, `instantWithdrawsRequestsByEpoch`, and `pendingInstantWithdraws` (`IdleCreditVault.sol:356-375`).
- `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` is what actually funds instant receipts: it pulls underlying from the IdleCDO and decrements `pendingInstantWithdraws` (`IdleCreditVault.sol:398-403`).
- `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` then pays `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user]` in full through `_transferFundedClaim` (`IdleCreditVault.sol:380-392`). There is no check against `pendingInstantWithdraws` or any per-user funded marker — an unfunded receipt claims the same way as a funded one.
- `_transferFundedClaim` only subtracts `defaultRecoveryReserve` from the spendable balance (`IdleCreditVault.sol:897-907`). Every other underlying held by the strategy — including underlyings collected to satisfy *other* users' funded normal and instant receipts — is reachable by an unfunded instant claim.

So an instant-withdraw receipt "traverses" outside its funding scope into the pool of underlyings reserved for other claimants. Any underlying balance above `defaultRecoveryReserve` is within reach, and the receipt entitles the holder to it at par regardless of whether the borrower/CDO ever funded that request.

### Impact Explanation

Direct theft: a user who creates an instant withdraw request that is never (or only partially) funded can still claim the full `instantWithdrawsRequests` amount, spending underlyings that were collected for other users' matured normal withdraw receipts or earlier funded instant receipts. Those legitimate claimants are then left unable to claim — their one-receipt-one-payout invariant is broken and their claims are frozen or haircut to zero. Loss is bounded by the strategy's non-reserve underlying balance at claim time, i.e., up to the full funded-claims liquidity.

### Likelihood Explanation

Requires an unprivileged KYC'd lender holding tranche tokens, calling `requestInstantWithdraw` through the IdleCDO during a phase where instant withdrawals are enabled, and then triggering `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` before/without full funding of `pendingInstantWithdraws`. Whether the CDO entrypoint permits an early claim while `pendingInstantWithdraws` is still non-zero depends on gating in `IdleCDOEpochVariant` (e.g., `getInstantWithdrawFunds` / claim sequencing), which I could not fully verify within this session — if the CDO only invokes `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` after `collectInstantWithdrawFunds`, the strategy-level missing check is defense-in-depth rather than an exploitable path. The strategy itself contains no funding check, so any CDO path, queue path, or future call route that reaches the claim early is unsafe.

### Recommendation

In `claimInstantWithdrawRequest`, pay only the funded portion: track per-user funded instant receipts (or check `instantWithdrawsRequests[_user] <= instantWithdrawsRequests[_user] - pendingInstantWithdraws`-equivalent accounting), and revert or cap the claim when the request is still in the unfunded `pendingInstantWithdraws` bucket. Extend `_transferFundedClaim`-style isolation so unfunded receipts can never spend underlyings earmarked for funded claims.

### Proof of Concept

Conceptual Foundry fork sequence (standard epoch variant, instant withdrawals enabled):

```solidity
// Setup: epoch running, userA has a matured, funded normal withdraw receipt
// (borrower funded pendingWithdraws at stopEpoch; funds sit in the strategy).

// 1. Attacker (KYC'd LP) requests an instant withdraw mid-epoch.
vm.prank(attacker);
cdoEpoch.requestInstantWithdraw(trancheAmount, address(tranche));
// instantWithdrawsRequests[attacker] == amount; pendingInstantWithdraws == amount
// collectInstantWithdrawFunds is NOT called -> request remains unfunded.

// 2. Attacker claims immediately through the CDO claim path.
vm.prank(attacker);
cdoEpoch.claimInstantWithdrawRequest();

// 3. Assert: attacker received `amount` underlying, paid out of the strategy's
// balance that was earmarked for userA's funded receipt.
assertGt(underlying.balanceOf(attacker), balBefore);

// 4. userA's claim now reverts/underpays: insolvency of the funded-claims pool.
vm.prank(userA);
cdoEpoch.claimWithdrawRequest(); // pays less than owed or reverts on transfer
```

Exact reproducibility depends on whether `IdleCDOEpochVariant` gates `claimInstantWithdrawRequest` behind prior `collectInstantWithdrawFunds` funding; the strategy-level gap (`claimInstantWithdrawRequest` ignoring `pendingInstantWithdraws`, `IdleCreditVault.sol:380-392`) is confirmed in code.
