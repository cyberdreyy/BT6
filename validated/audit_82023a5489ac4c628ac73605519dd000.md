### Title
Uncapped `Withdrawal.amount` committed for insufficient-balance builder withdrawals creates unbacked Gwei - (File: specs/gloas/beacon-chain.md)

### Summary
`get_builder_withdrawals` and `get_builders_sweep_withdrawals` emit `Withdrawal` records whose `amount` is the *requested* value taken directly from `BuilderPendingWithdrawal.amount` or `builder.balance` at read time, while `apply_withdrawals` debits only `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` from the builder's actual balance. Because the execution layer is contractually required to credit exactly `withdrawal.amount` to `withdrawal.address` for every withdrawal in the committed payload, any case where the requested amount exceeds the builder's balance results in the EL crediting more Gwei to the recipient than was ever debited on the consensus layer, creating Gwei that is not backed by any decrease elsewhere in the state.

### Finding Description
`get_builder_withdrawals` constructs each `Withdrawal` straight from the pending request: [2](#0-1) 
There is no capping to `builder.balance` at this stage — the comment in the input-space report explicitly notes "Requested amount. Actual = `min(amount, builder.balance)`" is only applied later in `apply_withdrawals`: [3](#0-2) 
So the `Withdrawal.amount` placed into `state.payload_expected_withdrawals` (and thus into the execution payload that the block commits to) is the full, uncapped requested amount, while only `min(withdrawal.amount, builder_balance)` is subtracted from the builder's internal `balance` field. The test suite itself documents and accepts this divergence as intended behavior rather than as a bug to fix: `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance` explicitly asserts `withdrawal.amount == withdrawal_amount` (the full 5 ETH request) while `builders[0].balance` only drops from 1 ETH to 0: [4](#0-3) 

On the execution-layer side, withdrawals inherited from Capella/EIP-4895 are applied by crediting the recipient address with exactly `withdrawal.amount` — there is no re-derivation or capping performed by the EL; the EL trusts the CL's committed `Withdrawal.amount` values verbatim (see the shared `apply_withdrawals`/EIP-4895 semantics used since Capella: `decrease_balance(state, withdrawal.validator_index, withdrawal.amount)` for validators, and analogous unconditional crediting on the EL side). Since the CL's own internal debit is capped but the committed payload amount is not, the two sides of the "coin" (debit vs. credit) disagree by up to `withdrawal.amount - builder.balance`.

This is structurally the same bug class as the reported ERC20 fee-on-transfer issue: a contract/spec commits to transferring/crediting a stated amount, but the actual internal accounting change is smaller, and nothing reconciles the difference — except here the mismatch runs in the dangerous direction (external credit > internal debit) rather than the safe direction.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the Gwei conservation invariant: Gwei is credited to `fee_recipient` on the execution layer without a corresponding decrease anywhere in `state.builders[*].balance`, `state.balances[*]`, or any other ledger. This matches the "Critical" impact category: "Gwei created, destroyed or paid to a non-owner." A builder (or a proposer colluding by submitting a bid with `builder.balance` too small to cover it, or simply timing/racing a withdrawal after balance-decreasing operations) can have the network mint Gwei into an arbitrary `fee_recipient` address beyond what any account actually loses.

### Likelihood Explanation
Builder balances can legitimately fall below a pending withdrawal's requested amount through ordinary operation: a `BuilderPendingWithdrawal` is queued (e.g., from `settle_builder_payment` after a bid) with a fixed `amount`, and the builder's balance can subsequently be reduced by other bids, other pending withdrawals processed earlier in the same `apply_withdrawals` call, or exits — all before the queued withdrawal is swept. The `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance*` tests confirm this is a state the spec explicitly reaches and treats as valid output, not a rejected/invalid case, and it requires no external malicious peer — just a builder whose balance drops below a previously queued commitment.

### Recommendation
Cap the `amount` field of the `Withdrawal` object itself at the point of construction (in `get_builder_withdrawals` and `get_builders_sweep_withdrawals`) to `min(requested_amount, builder.balance)`, so that the value committed to the execution payload always matches the amount actually debited in `apply_withdrawals`. Alternatively, make `apply_withdrawals` assert `withdrawal.amount <= builder_balance` rather than silently capping the internal debit, so an under-collateralized withdrawal request fails loudly instead of producing a payload that pays out more than was backing it.

### Proof of Concept
1. Register builder `B` with `balance = 1 ETH`.
2. Queue `BuilderPendingWithdrawal(builder_index=B, amount=5 ETH, fee_recipient=X)` in `state.builder_pending_withdrawals` (e.g., via a bid settled by `settle_builder_payment`, or directly as in `test_builder_withdrawal_insufficient_balance`) [5](#0-4) .
3. Call `process_withdrawals(state)`. `get_builder_withdrawals` produces `Withdrawal(validator_index=convert(B), address=X, amount=5 ETH)` [2](#0-1) , which is committed into `state.payload_expected_withdrawals` via `update_payload_expected_withdrawals`.
4. `apply_withdrawals` runs `state.builders[B].balance -= min(5 ETH, 1 ETH)`, so `builder.balance` goes from 1 ETH to 0 [6](#0-5) .
5. The execution payload the proposer must build is required to include a withdrawal crediting `X` with the full `5 ETH` (per the committed `Withdrawal.amount`), yet the consensus layer only ever debited `1 ETH` from any account. `4 ETH` of unbacked Gwei is credited to `X` on the execution layer — a concrete Gwei-creation event with no corresponding debit anywhere in `state`.
